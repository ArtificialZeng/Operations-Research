## Structured Abstract

**Background & Objective:** Pharmaceutical sponsors routinely globalize pivotal multiregional clinical trials (MRCTs) across North America, Western Europe, Central/Eastern Europe, Latin America, and Asia to accelerate patient accrual and reduce R&D costs, yet U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and European Medicines Agency (EMA) prescribing-information safety tables aggregate adverse-event (AE) proportions across participating regions into single pooled summary rates. While prior single-disease evidence in rheumatoid arthritis documented substantially lower placebo-arm AE reporting in Russia and Eastern Europe (Keebler et al., 2020), pan-disease within-drug evidence remains sparse. Importantly, trial-level ClinicalTrials.gov summary registries cannot definitively separate true regional AE *incidence* differences from *ascertainment/reporting* differences without country-by-arm enrollment denominators, per-region follow-up exposure duration, and active-elicitation protocol details. We investigate (i) whether trial site geography systematically predicts registered non-serious AE reporting proportions within exact drug–sponsor programs, (ii) whether subjective-symptom versus objective laboratory/organ MedDRA System Organ Classes (SOCs) diverge in **absolute** arm-level rates as well as compositional shares, (iii) post-2016 regional reporting dynamics across the European Union border, (iv) uncensored post-February-24-2022 Russia–Ukraine site reallocation across all registered trials (`2017–2025`), and (v) downstream post-approval FDA labeling supplements, boxed warnings, and exploratory FAERS reporting profiles.

**Data & Methods:** We harvested the complete universe of `22,303` interventional industry-sponsored Phase 2 and Phase 3 studies with posted results on ClinicalTrials.gov v2, yielding `12,887` randomized industry trials (`11,265` completed) and a primary analytic panel of `2,771` completed placebo-controlled MRCTs (`1,842` Phase 3; `929` standalone Phase 2; `502` sponsors; `1,287` canonical molecules; 2005–2024), supplemented by `1,276` twin-matched non-placebo active-comparator MRCTs, `483` terminated placebo MRCTs, complete per-term arm-level MedDRA `otherEvents.stats` across all `2,771` panel trials, and a separate **uncensored all-registered cohort of `20,649` Phase 2/3 industry interventional trials started `2017–2025`** (`6,011` MRCTs including ongoing and non-result-posted trials) to eliminate post-2022 results-posting right-censoring. Using exact `Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor` (`N = 1,764`; Phase 3 `N = 1,282`), `Same-Molecule × Same-Primary-Condition` (`N = 876`), and triplet `Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor × Same-Primary-Condition` (`N = 838`) Twin-Trial fixed effects with connected-component full-rank pruning, finite-sample `t(G-1)` sponsor-clustered, molecule-clustered, two-way (`sponsor × molecule`) clustered, and Rademacher wild-cluster-bootstrapped (`B = 999`) inference, we estimate both `0 → 100%` endpoint contrasts and per-`+10 pp` site-share slopes alongside exact 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and 90% Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence bounds for null safety endpoints. We further link `697` named Phase-3 molecules to live `openFDA` `Drugs@FDA`, `drug/label`, and `FAERS` records (`N = 229` FDA-approved originator NDA/BLAs with strictly pre-approval Phase-3 MRCTs, 2005–2024).

**Results:** Within exact Phase-3 `Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor` Twin-Trial fixed effects (`N = 1,282`, `328` programs, `129` sponsors; 100% full rank), **trials with higher Eastern European EU site shares exhibit substantially lower registered non-serious AE reporting proportions**: `-24.80` percentage points (`pp`) in placebo arms (`SE = 5.72 pp, 95% CI [-36.11, -13.49 pp], p = 2.88e-5`; two-way `p = 2.63e-5`, wild bootstrap `p = 0.001`) and `-26.72 pp` in active arms (`SE = 5.72 pp, 95% CI [-38.04, -15.40 pp], p = 7.47e-6`; two-way `p = 6.70e-6`, wild bootstrap `p = 0.001`) across a `0 → 100%` site-share contrast—corresponding to **`-2.48 pp` (`95% CI [-3.61, -1.35 pp]`) and `-2.67 pp` (`95% CI [-3.80, -1.54 pp]`) per `+10 pp` increase in EU-East site share** (pooled Phase 2+3 `N = 1,764`: `-25.54 pp` / `-2.55 pp per +10 pp`, `p = 2.58e-5` and `-26.83 pp` / `-2.68 pp per +10 pp`, `p = 1.92e-6`). This gradient replicates inside exact `Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor × Same-Primary-Condition` triplets (`N = 838`: `-21.21 pp / -2.12 pp per +10 pp, p = 0.00301` placebo and `-19.96 pp / -2.00 pp per +10 pp, p = 0.00850` active; `p_WCB = 0.022 / 0.017`), standalone Phase-2 OOS Twin-Trial FE (`N = 320`: `-32.73 / -39.06 pp`), non-placebo active-comparator MRCTs (`N = 1,276`: `-25.01 pp, p = 0.000509`), strict `freq_thr == 5%` (`-28.62 / -31.97 pp`), `SYSTEMATIC_ASSESSMENT` controls (`-25.17 / -26.81 pp`), and 9/9 clinical therapeutic areas (`7/9 p < 0.05`). In contrast, Western Europe (`p = 0.475`), Latin America (`p = 0.553`), Asia (`p = 0.699`), SAEs (P3 placebo `+0.63 pp, 95% CI [-4.64, +5.91 pp], p = 0.813`, TOST 90% bound `±5.05 pp` [`±0.51 pp` per `+10 pp`]; pooled P2+P3 `-0.10 pp, 95% CI [-4.94, +4.75 pp]`, TOST `±4.16 pp`), within-trial risk differences (P3 `-1.92 pp, 95% CI [-7.69, +3.85 pp], p = 0.512`, TOST `±6.75 pp`), and all-cause mortality (P3 `N = 679`: `+1.78 pp, 95% CI [-7.64, +11.19 pp], p = 0.709`, TOST `±9.65 pp`) are statistically null within their reported equivalence bounds. Resolving the mechanical sum-to-100% constraint of MedDRA SOC compositional shares (`subj_soc_share = -22.03 pp, p = 0.000194`; `obj_soc_share = +23.67 pp, p = 3.87e-5`), **non-compositional absolute arm-level MedDRA SOC regressions (`N = 1,764`)** demonstrate that higher EU-East share sharply reduces **absolute** subjective-symptom reporting (summed affected-participant rate: `-0.726` placebo `95% CI [-1.153, -0.300], p = 0.000963`, `-0.789` active `95% CI [-1.227, -0.352], p = 0.000483`; peak single-term rate: `-9.58 pp, p = 0.00608` / `-10.80 pp, p = 0.000826`; `-13.90` subjective preferred terms, `p = 0.00614`) while **absolute objective laboratory/organ SOC rates stay statistically flat** (summed affected rate: `-0.074, 95% CI [-0.244, +0.096], p = 0.392` placebo / `-0.056, 95% CI [-0.252, +0.141], p = 0.575` active; peak single-term rate: `-1.23 pp, p = 0.643` / `-1.04 pp, p = 0.717`; `-2.33` terms, `p = 0.541`). In 3-era Twin-Trial FE, EU-East attenuation narrows in `2020–2024` (`+35.68 pp, p = 0.0157, p_WCB = 0.030` placebo; `+40.10 pp, p = 0.0185`, **borderline `p_WCB = 0.051`** active), whereas Non-EU Russia/Ukraine/Balkans show no Twin-Trial convergence (`-14.06 pp, p = 0.481` / `+2.87 pp, p = 0.863`) and retain a steep **`2020–2024` late-period cross-sectional slope (`-49.37 pp, 95% CI [-73.99, -24.74 pp], p = 0.000113`)**; however, joint ICH E6(R2)/E17 and FDAAA `42 CFR Part 11` horse-race specifications cannot cleanly distinguish the two overlapping 2017 regulatory drivers statistically (`eu × post_ich_2017 p = 0.528`, `eu × post_fdaaa_2017 p = 0.126`, though `eu × freq0 p = 0.909` rules out the 0%-threshold channel). On the Russia–Ukraine war, correcting post-2022 results-posting right-censoring (`187/398 = 46.98% → 0/43 = 0.00%`; primary results-posted OOS U.S. DiD `+1.083, 95% CI [-0.019, +2.185], p = 0.0541` crossing zero vs. wider `2012–16 → 2017–24` `+0.924, 95% CI [+0.398, +1.451], p = 0.000763`) via the **uncensored all-registered `2017–2025` MRCT cohort (`N = 6,011`)** shows true Russia/Ukraine participation dropped from `41.86%` (`270/645`) in 2019 to `3.76%` (`23/611`) in 2023 and `6.10%` (`43/705`) in 2025—with Russia falling to `0.00%` (`0/705`) while Ukraine partially resumed (`6.10%`)—and reveals that industry-wide displaced capacity (`within-sponsor RU/UA shift = -3.58 pp, p = 2.95e-29`, `N = 5,362`) shifted significantly to **Latin America (`+1.48 pp, p = 0.000109`)** and **Emerging Asia (`+1.37 pp, p = 0.000318`)**, while the uncensored sponsor-exposure U.S. DiD (`2017–19 → 2020–25`, `N = 2,596`) attenuates to a statistically non-significant **`+0.328` (`95% CI [-0.084, +0.739], p = 0.117`)**. Finally, in Gate #2 (`N = 229` approved drugs), high pivotal EE share predicts lower pre-approval registered active AEs (`-36.02 pp, p = 0.000457`, `-3.60 pp per +10 pp`), whereas post-approval FDA labeling supplements (`n_labeling_5yr = +1.16, 95% CI [-1.06, +3.39], p = 0.302`; Poisson IRR `1.500, 95% CI [0.844, 2.666], p = 0.167`, per `+10 pp` IRR `1.041 [0.983, 1.103]`) and boxed warnings (`+6.14 pp, 95% CI [-42.53, +54.80 pp], p = 0.803`) show no statistically significant association within wide drug-level equivalence margins, and exploratory FAERS spontaneous-reporting outcomes—which per FDA guidance cannot estimate true incidence rates or comparative risks—yield only a fragile, specification-sensitive `us_serious_share` shift (`p = 0.0462` main; `p = 0.061` with volume control; `p = 0.236` Sponsor FE; U.S./Non-U.S. LOR `p = 0.960`).

**Conclusions:** Across clinical medicine, trials with higher Eastern European EU site shares exhibit substantially lower registered non-serious AE reporting proportions (`-2.48 / -2.67 pp` per `+10 pp` EU-East site share; `-24.80 / -26.72 pp` across `0 → 100%`), concentrated in absolute subjective-symptom MedDRA SOCs while objective lab/organ SOC rates, SAEs, mortality, and within-trial randomized risk differences remain statistically null within their confidence bounds. Although trial-level summary registries cannot definitively separate true regional incidence from ascertainment without country-by-arm enrollment, follow-up exposure, and elicitation metadata, the converging objectivity gradient, absolute MedDRA SOC divergence, and post-2017 EU-border attenuation are strongly consistent with selective under-ascertainment of subjective symptoms. Extending regulatory disclosures beyond country-level enrollment shares (as in FDA Drug Trials Snapshots) to region-stratified baseline AE reporting tables and follow-up denominators would directly improve cross-trial tolerability comparability.

## 1. Introduction

Over the past two decades, pharmaceutical research and development (R&D) has undergone a structural operational transformation: pivotal Phase 2 and Phase 3 clinical trials that once recruited predominantly in the United States and Western Europe have migrated into multiregional clinical trials (MRCTs) spanning dozens of countries across Central and Eastern Europe, Latin America, and Asia (Glickman et al., 2009). Sponsors globalize trial footprints to tap treatment-naive patient pools, shorten accrual timelines, and lower per-patient clinical-operations costs. Yet while enrollment has globalized, regulatory safety communication remains largely pooled. Both U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) prescribing-information adverse-reaction tables and ClinicalTrials.gov summary safety panels aggregate adverse events (AEs) across participating countries into single headline summary proportions (e.g., "18% headache," "42% any non-serious AE").

This institutional architecture implicitly assumes **reporting and ascertainment invariance across clinical-site geographies**: that a patient randomized to the exact same molecule (or placebo) has a comparable probability of having a mild-to-moderate symptom elicited, recorded, and reported by investigators in Warsaw, Budapest, Moscow, Boston, and Munich. If clinical sites in specific offshoring hubs systematically under-ascertain or under-report subjective, non-serious symptoms—whether due to local clinical norms, physician–patient deference, translation friction, or differences in site-monitoring intensity—then pooled global safety tables can shift headline registered tolerability proportions downward purely through geographic site composition. Crucially, however, **trial-level ClinicalTrials.gov summary registries cannot definitively separate true regional AE *incidence* differences from *ascertainment/reporting* differences** without country-by-arm enrollment counts, per-region follow-up exposure time, and protocol-level active-elicitation details. Evaluating whether geographic reporting gaps reflect ascertainment rather than healthier enrolled populations therefore requires sharp within-molecule falsification tests across objective vs. subjective clinical endpoints.

Prior literature has documented the globalization of trials (Glickman et al., 2009) and highlighted the value of demographic and geographic participation reporting through FDA Drug Trials Snapshots (Dhruva & Redberg, 2017; U.S. FDA, 2024). Directly relevant to regional AE heterogeneity, **Keebler, Teng, Chia, Galanter, Peake, and Tuckwell (2020)** analyzed TransCelerate Biopharma individual-patient data across placebo and standard-of-care arms of rheumatoid arthritis trials and documented striking regional disparities: Week-12 AE reporting proportions were just `22%` in the Russian Federation and Eastern Europe (RFEE) compared with `53%` in the United States, `51%` in Asia, and `49%` in Latin America, persisting through Week 52 alongside regional variation in ACR responses. Yet beyond this foundational single-disease study in rheumatoid arthritis, pan-disease registry evidence applying within-molecule Twin-Trial identification, testing **absolute** (non-compositional) subjective vs. objective MedDRA SOC rates, evaluating post-2016 regulatory dynamics across the EU border, auditing geopolitical site reallocation on uncensored all-registered trials, bounding site-to-patient accrual error on the valid regional simplex, and linking pivotal trial geography to FDA labeling revisions and FAERS pharmacovigilance with explicit equivalence bounds remains absent.

In this study, we assemble the complete universe of `22,303` industry-sponsored interventional Phase 2 and Phase 3 trials with posted results on ClinicalTrials.gov v2, augment all `2,771` placebo-controlled MRCTs with complete arm-level per-term MedDRA `otherEvents.stats`, harvest a separate uncensored all-registered cohort of `20,649` Phase 2/3 industry trials started `2017–2025`, and execute a five-part forensic investigation:

1. **Exact Twin-Trial Identification Across All Therapeutic Areas (Dual `0 → 100%` & Per-`+10 pp` Scaling):** Matching sister trials testing the *exact same canonical molecule* conducted by the *exact same sponsor* (`Mol × Sponsor FE`, `N = 1,764`; Phase 3 `N = 1,282`) and *exact same primary indication* (`Mol × Sponsor × Condition FE`, `N = 838`) absorbs drug pharmacology, sponsor safety-coding pipelines, and disease indication. We report both full `0 → 100%` endpoint contrasts and realistic per-`+10 pp` site-share effects, verify finite-sample `t(G-1)` sponsor, molecule, two-way (`sponsor × molecule`), and Rademacher wild-cluster-bootstrap (`B = 999`) inference, and report exact 95% CIs and TOST 90% equivalence bounds for every null safety endpoint.
2. **Objectivity Gradient & Non-Compositional Absolute MedDRA SOC Rates:** Because MedDRA SOC compositional shares sum to ~100%, a decline in subjective SOC share mechanically increases objective SOC share even if absolute objective events are unchanged. We directly resolve this compositional identity by estimating separate Twin-Trial regressions on **absolute** arm-level affected-participant rates (`sum_aff_rate`), peak single-term proportions (`max_pct`), and preferred-term counts separately for Subjective-Symptom SOCs versus Objective Laboratory/Organ SOCs alongside SAEs, all-cause mortality, and within-trial risk differences (`oth_rd`).
3. **Post-2017 Regulatory Dynamics (`ICH E6[R2]/E17` vs. `FDAAA 42 CFR Part 11`):** Two overlapping regulatory regimes took effect in 2016–2018: ICH E6(R2) risk-based monitoring (Step 4 November 2016; EMA effective June 2017) alongside EMA/ICH E17 MRCT guidance (`EMA/CHMP/ICH/453276/2016`; Matsushima et al., 2024), and the U.S. FDAAA Final Rule (`42 CFR Part 11`, effective January 18, 2017; Zarin et al., 2016). We compare EU Eastern Europe against Non-EU Eastern Europe across `2017–2019` and `2020–2024`, transparently reporting borderline active-arm wild-bootstrap inference (`p_WCB = 0.051`), clearly distinguishing late-period cross-sectional slopes from Twin-Trial DiD changes, and acknowledging that joint horse-race models cannot cleanly isolate the two overlapping 2017 regulatory drivers statistically.
4. **Uncensored All-Registered Russia–Ukraine War Cohort (`2017–2025`) & Simplex-Valid Accrual Bounds:** Recognizing that results-posted post-2022 trials suffer severe right-censoring toward shorter completed studies (`HasResults` falls from `76.0%` in 2019 to `8.25%` in 2024 and `0.28%` in 2025), we pull all `20,649` registered Phase 2/3 industry trials (`2017–2025`, `6,011` MRCTs) regardless of results-posting status to measure true uncensored Russia/Ukraine exit and global site reallocation, alongside empirical per-site accrual calibration (`w ∈ [0.5, 3.0]`) and DoF-corrected errors-in-variables (EIV) bounds.
5. **Gate-2 Downstream FDA Labeling & Exploratory FAERS Audit (`openFDA`):** Linking `697` named Phase-3 molecules to live `openFDA` `Drugs@FDA`, `drug/label`, and `FAERS` (`N = 229` originator NDA/BLAs with strictly pre-approval Phase-3 MRCTs, 2005–2024), we report exact 95% CIs and TOST equivalence bounds for FDA labeling revisions and boxed warnings, and explicitly frame FAERS analyses under FDA's official limitations notice (spontaneous reporting systems lack exposed denominators and cannot estimate true incidence rates or comparative risks).

## 2. Institutional Setting & Conceptual Framework

### 2.1 Pooled Global Labeling, ClinicalTrials.gov Definitions, and Incidence vs. Ascertainment

Under FDA (`21 CFR 201.57`) and EMA guidelines, adverse reactions in Section 6 of approved drug labels are summarized primarily as pooled incidence proportions across pivotal Phase 3 trials. Under ClinicalTrials.gov Results Data Element Definitions (NLM/NIH, 2024), sponsors submit arm-level counts of participants affected by **Serious Adverse Events (SAEs)**—defined by objective regulatory criteria (death, life-threatening event, inpatient hospitalization or prolongation, persistent disability, or congenital anomaly)—**All-Cause Mortality**, and **Other (Not Including Serious) Adverse Events** exceeding a sponsor-designated reporting frequency threshold (`frequencyThreshold`, standardly `5%` or `0%`), together with an event-level `assessmentType` (`SYSTEMATIC_ASSESSMENT` vs. `NON_SYSTEMATIC_ASSESSMENT`) and MedDRA `organSystem` classification.

Because SAEs and deaths trigger mandatory expedited regulatory reporting (`15-day` IND safety reports / SUSARs) and objective hospital documentation, site-level discretion over SAE capture is tightly constrained. By contrast, whether mild-to-moderate headache, fatigue, nausea, insomnia, or pruritus enters the trial database depends jointly on underlying symptom occurrence ($\theta_{mar}$), follow-up exposure duration, active investigator questioning/diaries, patient cultural propensity to volunteer minor complaints, and clinical research associate (CRA) source-data verification ($\pi_r$). Let true regional symptom incidence for molecule $m$ in arm $a \in \{\text{placebo}, \text{active}\}$ be $\theta_{mar}$ and regional ascertainment probability be $\pi_r \in (0, 1]$. In a trial $i$ with patient shares $p_{ir}$ across regions $r$, the registered arm non-serious AE proportion is:

$$Y_{ia} \approx \sum_r p_{ir} \theta_{mar} \pi_r = \theta_{ma,\text{US}} \pi_{\text{US}} + \sum_{r \neq \text{US}} \underbrace{(\theta_{mar}\pi_r - \theta_{ma,\text{US}}\pi_{\text{US}})}_{\beta_r} p_{ir}.$$

This identity makes explicit a core identification boundary: **using trial-level summary registries alone, $\theta_{mar}$ (true regional incidence) and $\pi_r$ (regional ascertainment/reporting) enter multiplicatively and cannot be definitively separated without country-by-arm enrollment denominators, per-region follow-up duration, and active-elicitation protocol records.** Nevertheless, under the pure ascertainment hypothesis ($\theta_{mar} \approx \theta_{ma}$ with $\pi_r < \pi_{\text{US}}$ for subjective events), four testable predictions arise that distinguish reporting under-ascertainment from broadly healthier patient selection ($\theta_{mar} < \theta_{ma,\text{US}}$ across all endpoints):
1. **Parallel Arm Attenuation:** Both Placebo ($\beta_r^{\text{ctrl}} < 0$) and Active ($\beta_r^{\text{act}} < 0$) registered non-serious AE proportions shift downward together.
2. **Objectivity Gradient & Absolute MedDRA Divergence:** For objective endpoints where site discretion is constrained ($\pi_r^{\text{SAE}} \approx \pi_{\text{US}}^{\text{SAE}}$, $\pi_r^{\text{mort}} \approx \pi_{\text{US}}^{\text{mort}}$, and objective laboratory/organ MedDRA SOCs), $\beta_r$ should remain small and statistically null, while **absolute** affected-participant rates and term counts in subjective-symptom MedDRA SOCs should fall sharply without requiring any absolute increase in objective SOCs.
3. **Internal Contrast Preservation:** When $\theta_{m,\text{act}} - \theta_{m,\text{ctrl}}$ is small relative to baseline incidence ($\bar{Y}_{\text{ctrl}} \approx 46.8\%$, $\overline{\text{RD}} \approx 4.9\text{ pp}$), the within-trial randomized risk difference $\text{RD}_i = Y_{i,\text{act}} - Y_{i,\text{ctrl}}$ moves only modestly ($\Delta \text{RD} \approx (\theta_{\text{act}} - \theta_{\text{ctrl}})(\pi_r - \pi_{\text{US}}) \approx -1 \text{ to } -2\text{ pp}$), preserving internal randomized contrasts even when absolute reported proportions shift markedly.
4. **Jurisdictional Regulatory Convergence:** If harmonized centralized risk-based monitoring narrows reporting gaps, convergence should appear primarily inside jurisdictions adopting binding harmonization (EU Eastern Europe) rather than outside that perimeter (Non-EU Russia/Ukraine/Balkans).

### 2.2 Concurrent 2016–2018 Regulatory Regimes & the February 2022 Geopolitical Shock

Two overlapping regulatory regimes took effect around 2017:
- **ICH E6(R2) (November 9, 2016; EMA effective June 14, 2017) and ICH E17 (`EMA/CHMP/ICH/453276/2016`, Step 4 November 16, 2017; EMA effective June 2018):** ICH E6(R2) mandated risk-based monitoring (RBM) and centralized statistical quality systems targeting anomalous site reporting patterns, while ICH E17 established harmonized MRCT planning principles across intrinsic and extrinsic regional factors (Matsushima et al., 2024). Because Phase 2/3 protocols starting in 2017–2018 were typically drafted earlier, full operational embedding plausibly exhibits a 2–3 year implementation lag (`2020–2024`).
- **FDAAA Final Rule (`42 CFR Part 11`, effective January 18, 2017; Zarin et al., 2016):** Expanded mandatory ClinicalTrials.gov results submission and standardized AE assessment metadata (`SYSTEMATIC_ASSESSMENT` vs. `NON_SYSTEMATIC_ASSESSMENT`), accompanied by increased `0%` frequency-threshold reporting for trials reaching primary completion on or after January 18, 2017. Because these timelines overlap closely, statistical horse-race interactions between `start_year >= 2017` and `primaryCompletionDate >= 2017-01-18` face substantial collinearity and must be interpreted cautiously alongside the `freq_thr == 0%` channel and the EU-vs-Non-EU border contrast.

Finally, on **February 24, 2022**, Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine triggered immediate Western sponsor moratoria on new clinical-trial initiation in Russia and major operational disruption in Ukraine, creating a sharp exogenous supply-chain shock whose reallocation must be measured on **all registered trials** (including ongoing studies without posted results) to avoid severe post-2022 right-censoring.

## 3. Data & Empirical Strategy

### 3.1 ClinicalTrials.gov v2 Analytic Panels, Per-Term MedDRA Stats, and All-Registered War Cohort

We constructed three complementary, cryptographically verified ClinicalTrials.gov v2 datasets:

1. **Complete Results-Posted Universe (`N = 22,303` raw studies):** Queried `https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies` for all interventional industry-sponsored Phase 2 and/or Phase 3 trials with posted results across all statuses (23 gzipped JSON pages). Filtering to randomized industry trials with valid location metadata (`>= 1` site) and valid AE group denominators (`>= 20` at risk in `>= 2` arms) yields `12,887` randomized industry trials (`11,265` completed). Applying multiregional criteria (`start_year ∈ [2005, 2024]`, `enrollment >= 100`, `n_sites >= 5`, `n_countries >= 3`, explicit placebo and active arms) produces our core completed analytic panel of **`N = 2,771` placebo-controlled MRCTs** (`1,842` Phase 3 across `353` sponsors and `777` canonical molecules; `929` strictly disjoint standalone Phase 2), plus `3,356` all-status placebo MRCTs (including `483` `TERMINATED` trials) and `1,276` twin-matched non-placebo active-comparator MRCTs. Mean Phase-3 site shares are: U.S. `31.77%`, Western Europe/Dev `36.51%`, EU Eastern Europe `11.38%`, Non-EU EE/RU-UA `5.87%`, LatAm `5.22%`, Asia Emerging `7.17%`, and Other `2.08%`.
2. **Complete Per-Term Arm-Level MedDRA `otherEvents.stats` (`2,771 / 2,771` = `100.0%` panel coverage):** Because the initial bulk harvest omitted per-term `stats` arrays, we queried `otherEvents` with full arm-level `numAffected`, `numAtRisk`, and `numEvents` for every trial in the `2,771`-trial placebo MRCT panel (`data/processed/d30_meddra_soc_abs_rates.parquet`). Matching each `groupId` to placebo vs. active arms via our exact unit-tested `classify_event_group` parser yields **non-compositional absolute arm-level rates** separately for Subjective-Symptom SOCs (`Nervous System, GI, General Disorders [including CT.gov's abbreviated "General disorders"], Psychiatric, Musculoskeletal, Skin, Respiratory, Ear, Eye`) and Objective Laboratory/Organ SOCs (`Investigations, Blood/Lymphatic, Metabolism, Cardiac, Vascular, Renal, Hepatobiliary, Infections`; `96.3%` total term coverage):
   - **Summed Affected-Participant Rate (`sum_aff_rate`):** $\sum_{k \in \text{SOC}} (\text{numAffected}_{k,a} / \text{numAtRisk}_{k,a})$ — expected distinct reported preferred-term AE mentions per participant at risk in each SOC class (unbounded above by 1.0, completely free of any sum-to-100% compositional link between Subjective and Objective SOCs!).
   - **Peak Single-Term Affected Proportion (`max_pct`):** $\max_{k \in \text{SOC}} (\text{numAffected}_{k,a} / \text{numAtRisk}_{k,a}) \in [0, 1]$ — strict non-compositional lower bound on the fraction of arm participants experiencing at least one reported AE in that SOC class.
   - **Absolute Reported Preferred-Term Counts:** Trial-level `n_subj_terms`, `n_obj_terms` (and logs) plus arm-specific positive term counts `n_pos_terms` and serious-AE term counts (`n_serious_subj_terms`, `n_serious_obj_terms`).
3. **Uncensored All-Registered War Cohort (`2017–2025`, `N = 20,649` studies; `6,011` MRCTs):** To eliminate post-2022 results-posting right-censoring entirely, we queried ClinicalTrials.gov v2 for **all** Phase 2/3 industry interventional trials started `2017-01-01` through `2025-12-31`—**including recruiting, active-not-recruiting, ongoing, terminated, withdrawn, and completed studies without posted results** (`data/processed/ctgov_all_registered_war_2017_2025.parquet`). Filtering to multiregional trials (`n_sites >= 5`, `n_countries >= 3`, `enrollment >= 50`) yields **`N = 6,011` uncensored MRCTs** (`4,747` randomized; `611–789` MRCTs every single year from `2017` through `2025`, with `2,524` started on or after February 24, 2022).

**Parser & Classification Hygiene:** Four unit-tested safeguards ensure data quality: (i) precision arm classification separating genuine crossovers (`"Placebo -> Drug X"`, `"Placebo-Efgartigimod"`) and double-dummy active arms (`"Drug X + Placebo"`) from pure placebo and phase-labeled titles (`"Placebo - Treatment Phase"`, `"Placebo-Controlled Phase: Placebo"`); (ii) MeSH-verified backbone-aware canonical molecule normalization resolving single-substance internal codes (`CP-690,550 → tofacitinib`) and rejecting MeSH artifacts (`99.78%` Phase-3 coverage); (iii) case-insensitive complete MedDRA SOC mapping capturing `96.3%` of reported non-serious terms; and (iv) a mutually exclusive seven-region site-share simplex summing identically to `1.000` (with U.S. + Puerto Rico as omitted baseline).

### 3.2 Twin-Trial Fixed-Effects Identification, Dual Scaling & Equivalence Bounds

Our flagship specification absorbs exact **Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor** (`mol_sponsor`) fixed effects—and in stricter specifications, **Same-Molecule × Same-Primary-Condition** (`mol_cond`) and **Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor × Same-Primary-Condition** (`mol_sp_cond`) triplet fixed effects:

$$Y_{i} = \sum_{r \neq \text{US}} \beta_r S_{ir} + \mathbf{X}_i'\boldsymbol{\gamma} + \alpha_{m(i), s(i)} + \delta_{t(i)} + \phi_{\text{phase}(i)} + \varepsilon_i,$$

where $S_{ir}$ is trial $i$'s site share in region $r$ (U.S. omitted), $\mathbf{X}_i$ controls for `log(enrollment)`, `log(n_sites)`, `log(n_countries)`, exact duration `dur_yr`, `is_double_blind`, and `freq_thr` (for non-serious AE outcomes), $\alpha_{m(i), s(i)}$ is the exact drug–sponsor FE, $\delta_{t(i)}$ is start-year FE, and $\phi_{\text{phase}(i)}$ controls for phase group in pooled Phase 2+3 models. Because real-world regional site shares vary incrementally rather than strictly from `0%` to `100%` (mean EU-East share `11.38%`), we systematically report **both** the linear `0 → 100%` endpoint contrast ($\hat{\beta}_r$) **and** the per-`+10 pp` increase in site share ($0.10 \hat{\beta}_r$). Following Abowd–Kramarz–Margolis (AKM) connected-component pruning, every single model in Blocks A–E achieves **100% full design-matrix rank (`matrix_rank == n_params`)**, using finite-sample `t(G-1)` sponsor-clustered SEs (`use_t = True`), supplemented by molecule clustering, Cameron–Gelbach–Miller two-way clustering (`sponsor × molecule`), and Rademacher wild cluster bootstrapping (`B = 999`). For every null safety endpoint (`SAE`, `mortality`, `oth_rd`, and Gate-2 FDA labeling outcomes), we avoid claiming "exact zero" and instead report **exact 95% CIs** plus the **TOST 90% minimum symmetric equivalence margin** ($\Delta_{\text{TOST}} = \max(|CI_{90,\text{lo}}|, |CI_{90,\text{hi}}|)$, the smallest symmetric effect magnitude statistically ruled out at one-sided $\alpha = 0.05$).

### 3.3 Gate-2 Live `openFDA` Linkage & Official FAERS Limitations

We matched all `697` named Phase-3 canonical molecules against three live `openFDA` endpoints (`100%` disk-cached):
1. **`Drugs@FDA`:** Strictly queried `(application_number:NDA* OR application_number:BLA*)` with FDA 4-letter biologic nonproprietary suffix support (`-xxxx`, recovering 21 post-2017 approved biologics such as `risankizumab-rzaa`, `erenumab-aooe`, `tezepelumab-ekko`, `anifrolumab-fnia`), matching **`402 / 697` (`57.68%`)** approved originator drugs. Enforcing strict pre-approval temporal ordering (`start_date <= orig_approval_date`) yields **`N = 229` originator NDA/BLAs** approved `2005–2024` (`N = 190` with full 5-year follow-up; `N = 216` with 3-year follow-up).
2. **`drug/label`:** Matched by exact `openfda.application_number.exact` (`N = 205` in the primary cohort) to extract `boxed_warning` indicators and `warnings_and_cautions` / `adverse_reactions` lengths.
3. **`FAERS` (`drug/event`):** Queried strictly for **Primary-Suspect** reports (`patient.drug.drugcharacterization:1`) inside the United States (`N = 210` with `>= 20` reports) and globally (`366/366` = `100%` global query coverage; `N = 203` with `>= 20` both U.S. and Non-U.S. reports). **Critical FDA Regulatory Caveat:** Per the FDA's official FAERS limitations notice (`https://www.fda.gov/media/136729/download` and FDA FAERS Q&A), FAERS is a passive spontaneous reporting system that lacks exposed-patient denominators, suffers substantial under-reporting and stimulated/notoriety reporting biases, and **cannot be used to calculate true adverse-event incidence rates or comparative drug safety risks**. Accordingly, all FAERS regressions in Section 7 are interpreted strictly as exploratory post-marketing reporting-profile diagnostics rather than estimates of real-world safety incidence.

## 6. Quantifying and Bounding Site-Share vs. Patient-Accrual Attenuation

Because ClinicalTrials.gov enumerates *clinical sites* per country rather than *enrolled patients* per country, site share $s_{EE}$ can differ from true patient share $p_{EE}$ via (i) **systematic per-site accrual differences** (if EE sites enroll $w_{EE} > 1$ times more patients per site than U.S. sites, site share understates patient share and scales $|\hat{\beta}_{\text{site}}|$ above $|\beta_{\text{patient}}|$) and (ii) **idiosyncratic site-to-patient noise** ($s_{EE} = p_{EE} + \eta$, causing classical EIV attenuation toward zero).

We quantify both channels (**Table 4b** and **Figure 4C**):
1. **Direct Twin-Trial Accrual Velocity:** Regressing `log(enrollment / n_sites)` on regional site shares inside `Mol × Sponsor` FE (`N = 1,764`) shows Eastern European sites enroll **`1.354×`** more patients per site than U.S. sites (`γ_EE = +0.3031, SE = 0.1104, p = 0.00671`; **`EU-East: 1.451×, γ = +0.3724, p = 0.00265`**; `Non-EU RU/UA: 1.170×, p = 0.503`).
2. **Simplex-Preserving Nonlinear Patient-Share Calibration Across `w ∈ [0.5, 3.0]`:** Rescaling all regional shares jointly via $D_i(w) = w s_{i,EE} + (1 - s_{i,EE})$, $p_{i,EE}(w) = \frac{w s_{i,EE}}{D_i(w)}$, and $p_{i,r}(w) = \frac{s_{i,r}}{D_i(w)}$ ($\sum_r p_{ir}(w) \equiv 1.000$) yields an empirically calibrated patient-level slope at **`ŵ_EE = 1.354`** of **`-19.03 pp` (`-1.90 pp per +10 pp`, `SE = 4.87 pp, p = 0.000133`)** on Placebo and **`-22.42 pp` (`-2.24 pp per +10 pp`, `SE = 4.68 pp, p = 3.68e-6`)** on Active. Even at an extreme `w = 3.0`, patient-level slopes remain **`-15.27 pp` (`p = 0.000445`)** and **`-18.00 pp` (`p = 8.16e-5`)**.
3. **DoF-Corrected Classical EIV Envelope:** Residualizing `ee_sh` inside `Mol × Sponsor` FE (`df_resid = 1,276`, within-program SD `0.1098`) implies that random site-to-patient share noise $\sigma_\eta \in [0.02, 0.06]$ ($\lambda \in [0.967, 0.701]$) deattenuates true patient-level slopes upward in magnitude to `-21.66 to -29.86 pp` (Placebo) and `-25.51 to -35.16 pp` (Active).

2. **Post-Approval FDA Labeling Supplements and Boxed Warnings Are Statistically Null — With Explicit Equivalence Ranges:** Across `N = 190–229` drugs, post-approval labeling supplements (`n_labeling_5yr = +1.16, 95% CI [-1.06, +3.39], p = 0.302`, TOST `±3.03` [`±0.30` per `+10 pp`]; Poisson GLM IRR `1.500, 95% CI [0.844, 2.666], p = 0.167`, per `+10 pp` IRR `1.041 [0.983, 1.103]`; `n_labeling_3yr = -0.05, 95% CI [-1.46, +1.36], p = 0.948`, TOST `±1.23`; `log_labeling_per_yr = -0.021, 95% CI [-0.193, +0.152], p = 0.814`, TOST `±0.165`) and boxed warnings (`+6.14 pp, 95% CI [-42.53, +54.80 pp], p = 0.803`, TOST `±46.86 pp` [`±4.69 pp` per `+10 pp`]) show no statistically significant association. ## 8. Discussion, Policy Implications, and Limitations

### 8.1 Clinical, Regulatory, and Operations Management Implications

Our pan-disease registry evidence substantially extends Keebler et al.'s (2020) rheumatoid-arthritis IPD finding (`22%` RFEE vs. `53%` USA placebo AE reporting) across all major therapeutic domains while clarifying what summary registries can and cannot establish:
- **For Regulators (FDA & EMA) and Prescribers:** Because registered non-serious AE proportions decline by `-2.48 to -2.67 pp` per `+10 pp` increase in EU-East site share (`-24.80 to -26.72 pp` across `0 → 100%`) within exact sister trials of the same molecule—and enter FDA-approved dossiers at `-3.60 pp` per `+10 pp`—**pooled headline non-serious AE percentages in Section 6 prescribing labels are not directly comparable across drugs with markedly different regional site compositions**. While **FDA Drug Trials Snapshots** (`https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-approvals-and-databases/drug-trials-snapshots`; Dhruva & Redberg, 2017) publish trial-level demographic and geographic participation shares, **country-level enrollment shares alone resolve only the site-to-patient denominator mapping**. Separating true regional AE incidence from ascertainment requires regulators and sponsors to pair enrollment shares with **region-stratified baseline AE reporting tables (country/region × arm AE counts), regional follow-up exposure time, and active-elicitation protocol details** in Snapshots, EMA EPARs, and ClinicalTrials.gov results modules.
- **For Global R&D Quality Governance:** The divergence between `2020–2024` EU-East attenuation (placebo `p_WCB = 0.030`, active borderline `p_WCB = 0.051`) and the persistent `-49.37 pp` Non-EU cross-sectional slope suggests that operational standardization inside the EU narrowed pre-2017 reporting disparities over a 2–3 year lag, whereas mere `SYSTEMATIC_ASSESSMENT` checklist indicators alone did not (`ee × sys p = 0.630`). Because joint ICH + FDAAA specifications cannot isolate the two 2017 regulatory mechanisms statistically, direct audit studies of CRA monitoring intensity and ePRO/diary protocols remain essential.
- **For Pharmaceutical Supply-Chain Resilience:** Auditing all `20,649` registered Phase 2/3 industry trials (`2017–2025`) reveals that following Russia's total clinical-trial exit (`34.1% → 0.0%` by 2025) and Ukraine's sharp disruption and partial resumption (`22.3% → 2.95% → 6.10%`), global sponsors reallocated displaced trial share primarily into **Latin America (`+1.48 pp, p = 0.00011`)** and **Emerging Asia (`+1.37 pp, p = 0.00032`)**—not statistically significantly back to the United States (`+0.36 pp` overall, `p = 0.633`; uncensored OOS DiD `+0.328, 95% CI [-0.084, +0.739], p = 0.117`).

### 8.2 Limitations

1. **Trial-Level Registries Cannot Definitively Separate Incidence from Ascertainment:** Without country-by-arm enrollment denominators, per-region follow-up exposure duration, and active-elicitation protocol details, trial-level summary registries cannot conclusively separate true regional symptom incidence from reporting/ascertainment differences. Even augmenting ClinicalTrials.gov with country-level enrollment shares from FDA Drug Trials Snapshots resolves only the site-to-patient weighting problem; definitive separation requires individual-patient-data (IPD) repositories (e.g., Vivli, YODA, TransCelerate) containing patient-level country, arm, exposure duration, and MedDRA timestamps.
2. **Site Shares vs. Country-Level Patient Enrollment:** Our Twin-Trial accrual calibration (`ŵ_EE = 1.354`, bounding simplex-valid patient-level slopes at `-15.3 to -26.2 pp` across `w ∈ [0.5, 3.0]`) and DoF-corrected EIV envelope rule out mechanical accrual artifacts, but cannot replace exact country-level enrollment counts.
3. **Overlapping 2017 Regulatory Timelines & Borderline Wild Bootstrap:** Joint ICH E6(R2)/E17 and FDAAA `42 CFR Part 11` horse-race models cannot statistically separate the two overlapping 2017 reforms (`p = 0.528` vs. `p = 0.126`), and `2020–2024` active-arm EU-East convergence sits right at the 5% wild-bootstrap threshold (`p_WCB = 0.051`).
4. **Statistical Precision of Null Safety & Gate-2 Endpoints:** Exact 95% CIs and TOST bounds show that mortality (`N = 679–940`, TOST `±9.7 to ±12.1 pp` on `0→100%`) and drug-level FDA label outcomes (`N = 190–229`, boxed warning 95% CI `[-42.5, +54.8 pp]`; 5-yr labeling Poisson IRR 95% CI `[0.84, 2.67]`) rule out large effects rather than exact zero shifts. Furthermore, `Drugs@FDA` `LABELING` supplement counts do not isolate Section 6.1 adverse-reaction wording edits, and `FAERS` spontaneous reports cannot estimate true incidence rates or comparative risks (U.S. FDA, 2020).
5. **Post-2022 War Reduced-Form AE Power:** Although our new `20,649`-trial all-registered cohort eliminates right-censoring for *site reallocation*, post-2022 *AE outcomes* still exist only for the `133` completed placebo MRCTs with posted results (`94` post-war in OOS DiD), leaving reduced-form war-IV AE tests underpowered until the 2022–2025 Phase-3 cohort reports results.
