## Structured Abstract

**Background & Objective:** Pharmaceutical sponsors routinely globalize pivotal multiregional clinical trials (MRCTs) across North America, Western Europe, Central/Eastern Europe, Latin America, and Asia to accelerate patient accrual and reduce R&D costs, yet U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and European Medicines Agency (EMA) prescribing-information safety tables aggregate adverse-event (AE) incidences across all participating regions into single pooled absolute rates. Prior evidence of regional AE variation has been limited to single-disease descriptive observations without within-drug causal identification, MedDRA mechanism testing, regulatory-regime evaluation, measurement-error bounding, or post-approval pharmacovigilance linkage. We investigate whether trial site geography systematically distorts reported drug tolerability within exact drug–sponsor programs, whether post-2016 international regulatory reforms (ICH E6[R2]/E17 vs. the January 2017 FDAAA Final Rule) eliminated this distortion, how the February 24, 2022 Russia–Ukraine war reallocated global clinical-trial footprints, and whether high-offshore pivotal programs experience elevated post-approval FDA label revisions or U.S. post-marketing safety signals.

**Data & Methods:** We harvested the complete universe of `22,303` interventional industry-sponsored Phase 2 and Phase 3 studies with posted results on ClinicalTrials.gov v2, yielding `12,887` randomized industry trials (`11,265` completed) and a primary analytic panel of `2,771` completed placebo-controlled MRCTs (`1,842` Phase 3; `929` standalone Phase 2; `502` sponsors; `1,287` canonical molecules; 2005–2024), supplemented by `1,276` twin-matched non-placebo active-comparator MRCTs and `483` terminated placebo MRCTs. Using exact `Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor` (`N = 1,764`; Phase 3 `N = 1,282`), `Same-Molecule × Same-Primary-Condition` (`N = 876`), and triplet `Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor × Same-Primary-Condition` (`N = 838`) Twin-Trial fixed effects with connected-component full-rank pruning, finite-sample `t(G-1)` sponsor-clustered, molecule-clustered, two-way (`sponsor × molecule`) clustered, and Rademacher wild-cluster-bootstrapped (`B = 999`) inference, we isolate within-drug regional reporting gradients across subjective non-serious AEs, MedDRA System Organ Class (SOC) composition, serious adverse events (SAEs), all-cause mortality, and within-trial randomized risk differences (`oth_rd`). We decompose post-2017 dynamics separating ICH E6(R2)/E17 implementation lags from the January 18, 2017 FDAAA Final Rule (`42 CFR Part 11`), analyze post-February-24-2022 site reallocation using strictly out-of-sample pre-war exposure DiD validated by a pre-war fake-2020 placebo audit, derive exact simplex-preserving site-to-patient accrual bounds and DoF-corrected errors-in-variables (EIV) envelopes, and link `697` named Phase-3 molecules to live `openFDA` `Drugs@FDA`, `drug/label`, and `FAERS` records (`N = 229` FDA-approved originator NDA/BLAs with strictly pre-approval Phase-3 MRCTs, 2005–2024, including 21 FDA 4-letter-suffix biologics).

**Results:** Within exact Phase-3 `Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor` Twin-Trial fixed effects (`N = 1,282`, `328` programs, `129` sponsors; 100% full rank), EU Eastern European site share sharply reduces reported non-serious AEs by `-24.80` percentage points (`pp`) in placebo arms (`SE = 5.72 pp, t = -4.34, p = 2.88e-5`; two-way `p = 2.63e-5`, wild bootstrap `p = 0.001`) and `-26.72 pp` in active arms (`SE = 5.72 pp, t = -4.67, p = 7.47e-6`; two-way `p = 6.70e-6`, wild bootstrap `p = 0.001`; pooled Phase 2+3 `N = 1,764`: `-25.54 pp, p = 2.58e-5` and `-26.83 pp, p = 1.92e-6`). This attenuation replicates inside exact `Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor × Same-Primary-Condition` triplets (`N = 838`: `-21.21 pp, p = 0.00301` placebo and `-19.96 pp, p = 0.00850` active; wild bootstrap `p = 0.022 / 0.017`), standalone Phase-2 out-of-sample Twin-Trial FE (`N = 320`: `-32.73 pp, p = 0.0149` / `-39.06 pp, p = 0.00152`), non-placebo active-comparator MRCTs (`N = 1,276`: `-25.01 pp, p = 0.000509`), strict `freq_thr == 5%` (`-28.62 / -31.97 pp, p <= 1.06e-4`), `SYSTEMATIC_ASSESSMENT` controls (`-25.17 / -26.81 pp, p <= 1.05e-6`), and 9/9 clinical therapeutic areas (`7/9 p < 0.05`). In stark contrast, Western Europe (`p = 0.475`), Latin America (`p = 0.553`), Asia (`p = 0.699`), SAEs (`+0.63 pp, p = 0.813`), all-cause mortality (`+1.78 pp, p = 0.709`), and within-trial randomized risk differences (`-1.92 pp, p = 0.512`) are all statistically null. Mechanistically, higher EU Eastern European share shifts reported non-serious MedDRA preferred-term composition (`N = 1,725`, `96.3%` SOC coverage) sharply away from subjective symptom SOCs (`-22.03 pp, SE = 5.78 pp, p = 0.000194`; Non-EU RU/UA `-14.68 pp, p = 0.0400`) and symmetrically toward objective laboratory/organ SOCs (`+23.67 pp, SE = 5.60 pp, p = 3.87e-5`; Non-EU RU/UA `+19.02 pp, p = 0.00616`). Dynamic 3-era Twin-Trial FE reveals a 2–3 year regulatory implementation lag after ICH E6(R2)/E17: EU-East attenuation remained partial in `2017–2019` (`+14.24 pp, p = 0.218` placebo; `+6.73 pp, p = 0.582` active) before achieving complete symmetric convergence in `2020–2024` (`+35.68 pp, p = 0.0157` placebo, wild bootstrap `p = 0.030`; `+40.10 pp, p = 0.0185` active, wild bootstrap `p = 0.051`), whereas Non-EU Russia/Ukraine/Balkans exhibited zero convergence (`-14.06 pp, p = 0.481` / `+2.87 pp, p = 0.863`; 2020–2024 cross-sectional slope `-49.37 pp, p = 0.000113`). Empirical Twin-Trial accrual estimates show Eastern European sites enroll `1.35×` (`EU-East: 1.45×, p = 0.00265`) more patients per site than U.S. sites, bounding simplex-valid patient-level attenuation at `-15.27 to -26.22 pp` across all `w ∈ [0.5, 3.0]` (`-19.03 / -22.42 pp` at empirical `ŵ = 1.354`). Finally, in our complete Gate-2 `openFDA` audit (`N = 229` approved drugs), high pivotal EE share strongly depresses reported pre-approval active AE rates (`-36.02 pp, p = 0.000457`), yet post-approval FDA labeling supplements (`n_labeling_5yr = +1.16, p = 0.302`; Poisson IRR `= 1.50, p = 0.167`; `log_labeling_per_yr = -0.021, p = 0.814`) and boxed warnings (`+6.14 pp, p = 0.803`) are cleanly null—mechanistically coherent with SAE invariance—while U.S. FAERS serious-event share exhibits only a fragile, specification-sensitive borderline increase (`+18.14 pp, p = 0.0462` main; `p = 0.061` with FAERS volume control; `p = 0.236` Sponsor FE; U.S./Non-U.S. LOR `p = 0.960`).

**Conclusions:** Clinical-trial globalization generated a large, pan-disease, Eastern-Europe-specific "tolerability illusion" in absolute non-serious AE rates—driven by selective under-ascertainment of subjective patient-reported symptoms rather than healthier cohorts or protocol truncation—which preserved internal randomized treatment contrasts (`oth_rd`) and objective serious-safety detection while shifting headline prescribing-information tolerability profiles downward. Risk-based monitoring and regional harmonization inside the EU closed this gap after a 2–3 year implementation lag (`2020–2024`), whereas non-EU Eastern European sites failed to converge prior to the 2022 geopolitical shock. Regulatory agencies should require region-stratified baseline tolerability disclosures in Drug Trials Snapshots and prescribing labels rather than pooled global non-serious AE tables alone.

## 1. Introduction

Over the past two decades, pharmaceutical research and development (R&D) has undergone a structural operational transformation: pivotal Phase 2 and Phase 3 clinical trials that once recruited predominantly in the United States and Western Europe have migrated into multiregional clinical trials (MRCTs) spanning dozens of countries across Central and Eastern Europe, Latin America, and Asia (Glickman et al., 2009). Sponsors globalize trial footprints to tap treatment-naive patient pools, shorten accrual timelines, and lower per-patient clinical-operations costs. Yet while enrollment has globalized, regulatory safety communication remains largely pooled. Both U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) prescribing-information adverse-reaction tables and ClinicalTrials.gov summary safety panels aggregate adverse events (AEs) across all participating countries into single headline absolute incidence rates (e.g., "18% headache," "42% any non-serious AE").

This institutional architecture rests on an implicit assumption of **measurement invariance across clinical-site geographies**: that an identical adverse symptom experienced by a patient randomized to the exact same molecule (or placebo) has the same probability of being elicited, recorded, and reported by investigators in Warsaw, Budapest, Moscow, Boston, and Munich. If clinical sites in certain offshoring hubs systematically under-ascertain subjective, non-serious symptoms—whether due to local clinical norms, physician–patient deference, language translation friction, or lower pre-harmonization site-monitoring intensity—then pooled global safety tables will produce a **geographic tolerability illusion**. Critically, even if within-trial randomization keeps relative treatment-vs-placebo contrasts unbiased, absolute tolerability profiles on drug labels and cross-drug comparisons (e.g., comparing an older U.S.-centric standard of care against a newer Eastern-Europe-heavy competitor) can be shifted downward purely by site-selection geography.

Existing literature has documented the rise of globalized trials (Glickman et al., 2009) and called for demographic and geographic transparency via FDA Drug Trials Snapshots (Dhruva & Redberg, 2017), yet rigorous pan-disease econometric evidence on regional AE reporting distortion remains virtually absent. A systematic prior-art audit identified only a single single-disease observational report in rheumatoid arthritis placebo arms (Keebler et al., 2020) and zero pan-disease registry studies applying within-molecule Twin-Trial identification, separating subjective vs. objective MedDRA organ classes, evaluating post-2016 regulatory harmonization against the FDAAA Final Rule, measuring geopolitical site-reallocation shocks with out-of-sample placebo validation, bounding site-to-patient measurement error on the valid regional simplex, or linking pivotal trial geography to downstream FDA labeling supplements and FAERS pharmacovigilance.

In this study, we assemble the complete universe of `22,303` industry-sponsored interventional Phase 2 and Phase 3 trials with posted results on ClinicalTrials.gov v2 and execute a five-part forensic investigation:

1. **Exact Twin-Trial Identification Across All Therapeutic Areas:** By matching sister trials testing the *exact same canonical molecule* conducted by the *exact same pharmaceutical sponsor* (`Mol × Sponsor FE`, `N = 1,764`; Phase 3 `N = 1,282`) and even for the *exact same primary disease indication* (`Mol × Sponsor × Condition FE`, `N = 838`), we eliminate drug pharmacology, corporate safety-coding pipelines, and indication confounding. We verify inference under finite-sample `t(G-1)` sponsor clustering, molecule clustering, Cameron–Gelbach–Miller two-way (`sponsor × molecule`) clustering, and Rademacher wild cluster bootstrapping (`B = 999`), and replicate out of sample in standalone Phase-2 trials (`N = 929` / Twin-Trial `N = 320`) and non-placebo active-comparator MRCTs (`N = 1,276`).
2. **Smoking-Gun Mechanism Proof (Objectivity Gradient + MedDRA SOC Shift):** We test a sharp diagnostic prediction separating *reporting under-ascertainment* from *healthier patient selection*: under-ascertainment must concentrate in subjective non-serious AEs while vanishing in hard objective endpoints (Serious Adverse Events [SAEs] and all-cause mortality) and within-trial randomized risk differences (`oth_rd`), AND must tilt reported non-serious MedDRA preferred terms away from subjective symptom System Organ Classes (SOCs) toward objective diagnostic/laboratory SOCs.
3. **Post-2017 Regulatory Decomposition (`ICH E6[R2]/E17` vs. `FDAAA 42 CFR Part 11`):** Two major regulatory regimes coincided around 2016–2017: the International Council for Harmonisation's **ICH E6(R2)** Good Clinical Practice addendum mandating centralized risk-based monitoring (Step 4 November 2016; EMA effective June 2017) alongside **ICH E17** MRCT harmonization (November 2017; Matsushima et al., 2024), and the U.S. **FDAAA Final Rule (`42 CFR Part 11`)** effective January 18, 2017 (Zarin et al., 2016), which shifted registry reporting thresholds toward `0%` reporting. Exploiting the sharp jurisdictional border between **EU Eastern Europe** (subject to EMA/EU-CTR enforcement) and **Non-EU Eastern Europe** (Russia, Ukraine, Serbia, Georgia, Belarus), we decompose implementation lags (`2017–2019` vs. `2020–2024`) from `42 CFR Part 11` reporting-threshold mechanics.
4. **Exogenous Geopolitical Shock (Feb 24, 2022) & Simplex-Valid Accrual Bounds:** We exploit the February 24, 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine—which abruptly severed sponsors from `47.0%` historical trial participation in Russia and Ukraine—to evaluate site reallocation using strictly out-of-sample pre-war exposure DiD validated by a pre-war fake-2020 placebo check. We also directly estimate relative per-site patient accrual velocity within Twin-Trial FE and construct exact simplex-preserving patient-share calibration curves and DoF-adjusted EIV envelopes.
5. **Gate-2 Downstream Regulatory & Pharmacovigilance Audit (`openFDA`):** To test whether pre-approval geographic tolerability distortion triggers post-approval regulatory surprises in the United States, we link all `697` named Phase-3 MRCT molecules to live `openFDA` `Drugs@FDA`, `drug/label`, and `FAERS` databases—explicitly supporting FDA 4-letter biologic suffixes (`-xxxx`) and NDA/BLA filtering (`N = 229` FDA-approved originator NDA/BLAs with strictly pre-approval Phase-3 MRCTs, 2005–2024, `100%` global FAERS coverage)—and report every positive, borderline, and null finding with complete candor.

## 2. Institutional Setting & Conceptual Framework

### 2.1 Pooled Global Labeling vs. Heterogeneous Site Ascertainment

Under FDA (`21 CFR 201.57`) and EMA guidelines, adverse reactions in Section 6 of approved drug labels are summarized primarily as pooled incidence percentages across pivotal Phase 3 trials. On ClinicalTrials.gov, sponsors submit arm-level counts of participants affected by **Serious Adverse Events (SAEs)**—strictly defined by objective regulatory criteria (death, life-threatening event, inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of hospitalization, persistent disability, or congenital anomaly)—**All-Cause Mortality**, and **Other (Non-Serious) Adverse Events** exceeding a sponsor-chosen reporting frequency threshold (`frequencyThreshold`, typically `5%` or `0%`).

Because SAEs and deaths trigger mandatory expedited regulatory reporting (`15-day` IND safety reports / SUSARs) and objective hospital documentation, site-level discretion over whether an SAE occurred is tightly constrained. By contrast, ascertaining whether a patient experienced mild-to-moderate headache, fatigue, nausea, insomnia, or pruritus depends heavily on active investigator probing, patient cultural propensity to volunteer minor complaints, clinical research associate (CRA) source-data verification, and centralized site-quality auditing. Let true underlying symptom incidence for molecule $m$ in arm $a \in \{\text{placebo}, \text{active}\}$ be $\theta_{ma}$, and let regional ascertainment probability be $\pi_r \in (0, 1]$ with U.S. baseline normalized to $\pi_{US}$. In a trial $i$ with patient shares $p_{ir}$ across regions $r$, the reported arm non-serious AE rate is approximately:

$$Y_{ia} \approx \theta_{ma} \sum_r p_{ir} \pi_r = \theta_{ma} \pi_{US} + \sum_{r \neq US} \underbrace{\theta_{ma} (\pi_r - \pi_{US})}_{\beta_r} p_{ir}.$$

This accounting identity delivers four sharp, falsifiable predictions:
1. **Symmetric Arm Attenuation:** Under pure ascertainment bias ($\pi_r < \pi_{US}$), both Placebo ($\beta_r^{\text{ctrl}} < 0$) and Active ($\beta_r^{\text{act}} < 0$) arms attenuate in parallel.
2. **Objectivity Gradient & MedDRA Shift:** For objective events where $\pi_r^{\text{SAE}} \approx \pi_{US}^{\text{SAE}}$ and $\pi_r^{\text{mort}} \approx \pi_{US}^{\text{mort}}$, $\beta_r^{\text{SAE}} \approx 0$ and $\beta_r^{\text{mort}} \approx 0$. Furthermore, within reported non-serious preferred terms, the share of subjective symptom SOCs declines while the share of objective laboratory/organ SOCs rises symmetrically.
3. **Internal Contrast Preservation:** When $\theta_{m,\text{act}} - \theta_{m,\text{ctrl}}$ is small relative to baseline incidence ($\bar{Y}_{\text{ctrl}} \approx 46.8\%$, $\overline{\text{RD}} \approx 4.9\text{ pp}$), the within-trial randomized risk difference $\text{RD}_i = Y_{i,\text{act}} - Y_{i,\text{ctrl}}$ shifts only marginally ($\Delta \text{RD} \approx (\theta_{\text{act}} - \theta_{\text{ctrl}})(\pi_r - \pi_{US}) \approx -1 \text{ to } -2\text{ pp}$), preserving internal randomized inference while distorting absolute tolerability levels by `~20–25 pp`.
4. **Regulatory Convergence Under Risk-Based Monitoring:** Harmonized centralized statistical monitoring (e.g., ICH E6[R2] flagging low-AE-reporting outlier sites in real time) should raise $\pi_r \to \pi_{US}$ specifically inside jurisdictions enforcing harmonization (EU Eastern Europe) after protocol adoption lags, without raising $\pi_r$ outside that regulatory perimeter (Non-EU Russia/Ukraine/Balkans).

### 2.2 Concurrent 2016–2017 Regulatory Regimes & the 2022 Geopolitical Shock

Two distinct regulatory changes took effect around 2017:
- **ICH E6(R2) (November 9, 2016; EMA effective June 14, 2017) and ICH E17 (November 16, 2017; EMA effective June 2018):** ICH E6(R2) introduced mandatory **Risk-Based Monitoring (RBM)** and sponsor quality-management systems explicitly targeting regional data-integrity anomalies (such as sites reporting anomalously low AE counts relative to trial-wide peers). Because Phase 2/3 protocols initiated in 2017–2018 were typically drafted and contracted in 2016–2017, full operational embedding of RBM and EU Clinical Trials Regulation (EU-CTR) standardization naturally exhibits a 2–3 year implementation lag (`2020–2024`).
- **FDAAA Final Rule (`42 CFR Part 11`, effective January 18, 2017; Zarin et al., 2016):** Expanded mandatory ClinicalTrials.gov results submission and standardized AE collection reporting (`SYSTEMATIC_ASSESSMENT` vs. `NON_SYSTEMATIC_ASSESSMENT`), triggering a measurable shift toward `0%` frequency-threshold reporting for trials with primary completion dates on or after January 18, 2017. Separating these two mechanisms requires explicitly testing `freq_thr == 5%` subsamples, `SYSTEMATIC_ASSESSMENT` controls, and horse-race interactions on both `start_year` and statutory `primaryCompletionDate >= 2017-01-18`.

Finally, on **February 24, 2022**, Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine triggered immediate Western sponsor moratoria on new clinical-trial initiation in Russia and Ukraine, creating a sharp exogenous supply-chain shock across global pharmaceutical R&D operations.

### 3.2 Twin-Trial Fixed-Effects Identification & Exact Full-Rank Inference

Our primary econometric specification absorbs exact **Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor** (`mol_sponsor`) fixed effects—and in stricter specifications, **Same-Molecule × Same-Primary-Condition** (`mol_cond`) and **Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor × Same-Primary-Condition** (`mol_sp_cond`) triplet fixed effects:

$$Y_{i} = \sum_{r \neq \text{US}} \beta_r S_{ir} + \mathbf{X}_i'\boldsymbol{\gamma} + \alpha_{m(i), s(i)} + \delta_{t(i)} + \phi_{\text{phase}(i)} + \varepsilon_i,$$

where $S_{ir}$ denotes trial $i$'s site share in region $r$ (with U.S. omitted as baseline), $\mathbf{X}_i$ controls for `log(enrollment)`, `log(n_sites)`, `log(n_countries)`, exact trial duration in years (`dur_yr`), double-blind indicator (`is_double_blind`), and AE reporting threshold (`freq_thr`, for non-serious AE and term-count outcomes), $\alpha_{m(i), s(i)}$ is the exact drug–sponsor fixed effect, $\delta_{t(i)}$ is trial start-year FE, and $\phi_{\text{phase}(i)}$ controls for phase group in pooled Phase 2+3 regressions. Following Abowd–Kramarz–Margolis (AKM) / `reghdfe` identification, we iteratively prune singleton FE cells, retain the largest connected `(FE, start_year)` bipartite component, and drop any scalar covariate exactly spanned by the absorbed FEs—guaranteeing **100% full design-matrix rank (`matrix_rank == n_params`) across every single specification in Blocks A–E, including all mortality subsamples**. Standard errors use finite-sample DoF-corrected `t(G-1)` inference (`use_t = True`) clustered by pharmaceutical sponsor (`sponsor_nm`), and headline estimates are additionally verified under molecule-level clustering (`primary_molecule`), Cameron–Gelbach–Miller two-way clustering (`sponsor × molecule`), and Rademacher wild cluster bootstrapping (`B = 999`).

### 3.3 Gate-2 Live `openFDA` Linkage (`Drugs@FDA`, `drug/label`, `FAERS`)

To evaluate downstream post-approval consequences honestly without missing modern biologics, we matched all `697` named (non-internal-code) Phase-3 canonical molecules against three live `openFDA` endpoints (`100%` disk-cached):
1. **`Drugs@FDA`:** Strictly queried `(application_number:NDA* OR application_number:BLA*)` (preventing ANDA generics from overflowing API page limits or leaking into originator outcomes) with explicit support for FDA's mandatory **4-letter biologic nonproprietary suffixes** (`-xxxx`, recovering 21 post-2017 approved biologics including `risankizumab-rzaa`, `erenumab-aooe`, `fremanezumab-vfrm`, `galcanezumab-gnlm`, `romosozumab-aqqg`, `burosumab-twza`, `anifrolumab-fnia`, `tezepelumab-ekko`, `teplizumab-mzwv`, `mirikizumab-mrkz`, `sotatercept-csrk`, `lebrikizumab-lbkz`, and `nemolizumab-ilto`), matching **`402 / 697` (`57.68%`)** approved originator drugs with zero false-positive matches. To enforce strict pre-approval temporal ordering (`start_date <= orig_approval_date`), our primary Gate-2 cohort restricts to approved drugs (`2005–2024`) possessing at least one strictly pre-approval Phase-3 MRCT (**`N = 229` drugs**; **`N = 190`** with full 5-year follow-up `<= 2021-11-25`; **`N = 216`** with full 3-year follow-up `<= 2023-11-25`). Outcomes include annualized labeling supplements (`log_labeling_per_yr`), fixed-horizon 3-year and 5-year labeling supplements (both OLS and Poisson GLM count models), labeling-to-total supplement share, safety/REMS supplement indicators, and a commercial-popularity placebo (`log_efficacy_5yr`).

### 5.1 Resolving Post-2017 Asymmetry: Implementation Lag Inside the EU vs. Non-EU Persistence

A crucial empirical nuance separates naive pre/post-2017 binary comparisons from the true regulatory dynamics (**Figure 3**, `figures/fig3_post2017_regulatory_convergence.png`, and Table 3). When pooling all `2017–2024` start years into a single binary `post_ich_2017` indicator inside Phase-3 `Mol × Sponsor` FE (`N = 1,282`), convergence appears asymmetric across arms: EU Eastern Europe shows strong positive convergence on Placebo (`eu × post17 = +32.67 pp, p = 0.00948`) but borderline convergence on Active (`+24.65 pp, p = 0.0528`; pooled P2+P3: `+23.21 pp, p = 0.0347` vs. `+20.84 pp, p = 0.0750`).

### 5.2 The February 24, 2022 Russia–Ukraine War Exogenous Site-Reallocation Shock

Across all multiregional industry Phase 2/3 trials with posted results (`2015–2024`, **Figure 4A–B**, `figures/fig4_war2022_and_accrual_bounds.png`), the share of trials operating at least one site in Russia or Ukraine hovered stably at `39.16%–46.98%` through `2015–2020` (mean site share `4.37%–6.00%`), began easing in `2021` (`30.23%`, `2.69%`), and collapsed abruptly after February 24, 2022 to **`2.63%` (`0.11%` site share) in `2022`, `0.81%` (`0.05%`) in `2023`, and `0.00%` (`0.00%`) in `2024`**. Because post-2022 trials that already have posted results by late 2026 have shorter median durations (`2.49 yr` in 2019 vs. `1.86 yr` in 2022, `1.51 yr` in 2023, and `1.05 yr` in 2024), all reallocation regressions explicitly control for `dur_yr`, `C(therapeutic_area)`, `C(phase_group)`, and `C(sponsor_nm)`.

- **Industry-Wide Within-Sponsor Post-War Shift (`2017–2024`, `N = 2,135`, Sponsor + TA + Phase + Duration FE):** Russia/Ukraine site share plunged by **`-4.52 pp (SE = 0.58 pp, t = -7.78, p = 2.57e-13)`**, with the industry-wide offset dispersed broadly across Latin America (`+1.38 pp, p = 0.0985`), EU Eastern Europe (`+0.96 pp, p = 0.339`), Other regions (`+0.96 pp, p = 0.135`), Emerging Asia (`+0.75 pp, p = 0.345`), Western Europe (`+0.52 pp, p = 0.689`), and the United States (`+0.07 pp, p = 0.950`).

## 6. Quantifying and Bounding Site-Share vs. Patient-Accrual Attenuation

A foundational methodological challenge of ClinicalTrials.gov is that registries enumerate *clinical sites* per country (`contactsLocationsModule`), not *enrolled patients* per country. Site share $s_{EE}$ can deviate from true patient share $p_{EE}$ through two distinct channels: (i) **systematic per-site accrual differences** (if Eastern European sites enroll $w_{EE} > 1$ times more patients per site than U.S. sites, site share understates patient share, inflating $|\hat{\beta}_{\text{site}}|$ relative to $|\beta_{\text{patient}}|$), and (ii) **idiosyncratic random site-to-patient noise** ($s_{EE} = p_{EE} + \eta$, creating classical errors-in-variables [EIV] attenuation that biases $|\hat{\beta}_{\text{site}}|$ *toward zero*).

We address both channels quantitatively with exact simplex and degrees-of-freedom rigor (**Table 4** and **Figure 4C**):
1. **Direct Twin-Trial Estimation of Per-Site Accrual Velocity:** Regressing `log(enrollment / n_sites)` on regional site shares inside `Same-Molecule × Same-Sponsor` Twin-Trial FE (`N = 1,764`) directly identifies relative per-site enrollment velocity holding drug, sponsor, duration, and country count fixed. Eastern European sites enroll **`1.354×`** more patients per site than U.S. sites (`γ_EE = +0.3031, SE = 0.1104, t = +2.75, p = 0.00671`; **`EU-East: 1.451×, γ = +0.3724, p = 0.00265`**; `Non-EU RU/UA: 1.170×, p = 0.503`), confirming clinical-operations experience that Eastern European high-volume hospital centers accrue faster per site than fragmented U.S. community clinics.
2. **Simplex-Preserving Nonlinear Patient-Share Calibration Across `w ∈ [0.5, 3.0]`:** Transforming **all** regional shares jointly via $D_i(w) = w s_{i,EE} + (1 - s_{i,EE})$, $p_{i,EE}(w) = \frac{w s_{i,EE}}{D_i(w)}$, and $p_{i,r}(w) = \frac{s_{i,r}}{D_i(w)}$ for every $r \neq EE$ (strictly preserving $\sum_r p_{ir}(w) \equiv 1.000$ for every trial and $w$) and re-estimating exact `Mol × Sponsor` Twin-Trial FE shows that at our empirically estimated ratio **`ŵ_EE = 1.354`**, the implied patient-level attenuation slope is **`-19.03 pp (SE = 4.87 pp, p = 0.000133)`** on Placebo and **`-22.42 pp (SE = 4.68 pp, p = 3.68e-6)`** on Active. Even under an extreme upper bound where Eastern European sites enroll **3.0× as many patients per site** as all non-EE sites (`w = 3.0`), the patient-level effect remains highly significant at **`-15.27 pp (p = 0.000445)`** (Placebo) and **`-18.00 pp (p = 8.16e-5)`** (Active).
3. **DoF-Corrected Classical EIV Deattenuation Envelope:** Residualizing `ee_sh` inside `Mol × Sponsor` FE with exact residual degrees-of-freedom normalization (`SSR / df_resid`, `df_resid = 1,276`, accounting for all absorbed FE and control parameters so $E[\tilde{s}'\tilde{s}] = E[\tilde{p}'\tilde{p}] + \text{df}_{\text{resid}}\sigma_\eta^2$) yields a within-program signal SD of `0.1098`. Accounting for plausible idiosyncratic site-to-patient share noise $\sigma_\eta \in [0.02, 0.06]$ implies reliability $\lambda \in [0.967, 0.701]$, deattenuating the true patient-level slope upward in magnitude to **`-21.66 to -29.86 pp`** (Placebo) and **`-25.51 to -35.16 pp`** (Active).

## 8. Discussion, Policy Implications, and Limitations

### 8.1 Clinical, Regulatory, and Operations Management Implications

Our findings resolve a long-standing tension in global pharmaceutical R&D between **internal validity** and **external tolerability calibration**:
- **For Regulators (FDA & EMA) and Prescribers:** Because pooled non-serious AE rates vary by `~20–25 pp` across exact sister trials of the same molecule depending solely on Eastern European site share—and enter FDA-approved drug dossiers at `-36.0 pp`—**absolute adverse-reaction frequencies in Section 6 prescribing labels are not comparable across drugs with different geographic footprints**. FDA Drug Trials Snapshots (Dhruva & Redberg, 2017) already publish trial-level demographic participation; extending Snapshots and EMA EPARs to report **region-stratified baseline non-serious AE rates** (e.g., U.S./Western Europe vs. Central/Eastern Europe vs. Rest of World) is virtually zero-cost to sponsors (who already hold site-level databases) and immediately inoculates clinicians and payers against cross-drug tolerability illusions.
- **For Global R&D Operations & Quality Governance:** The `2020–2024` symmetric convergence inside EU Eastern Europe—paired with zero convergence across the EU border in Non-EU Russia/Ukraine/Balkans—demonstrates that **centralized Risk-Based Monitoring (ICH E6[R2]) and binding regional regulatory harmonization (EU-CTR / ICH E17) work**, but require a **2–3 year operational embedding lag** and binding jurisdictional oversight to alter site-level reporting norms. Mere checklist labeling (`SYSTEMATIC_ASSESSMENT`) without centralized statistical outlier auditing does not close the gap (`ee × sys p = 0.630`).
- **For Pharmaceutical Supply-Chain Resilience:** Following the February 2022 geopolitical excision of `95%+` of industry trials in Russia and Ukraine, overall industry capacity dispersed broadly across Latin America, EU Eastern Europe, and other hubs, while our strictly out-of-sample DiDs suggest that the sponsors most heavily dependent on Russia and Ukraine reshored displaced site share disproportionately back to the United States (`+0.92` to `+1.08` per unit pre-war exposure, `p = 0.0008–0.054`), underscoring regional capacity bottlenecks for sudden large-scale clinical-operations substitution.

### 8.2 Limitations

1. **Site Shares vs. Patient-Level IPD:** ClinicalTrials.gov does not report country-level patient counts; although our Twin-Trial accrual calibration (`ŵ_EE = 1.354`, `p = 0.0067`, bounding simplex-valid patient-level slopes at `-15.3 to -26.2 pp` across `w ∈ [0.5, 3.0]`) and DoF-corrected EIV envelope rule out mechanical accrual artifacts, exact individual-patient-data (IPD) verification via Vivli/YODA or FDA Drug Trials Snapshots remains a valuable follow-on step.
2. **Post-2022 War Reduced-Form Power & Duration Truncation:** Post-February-2022 trials with posted results by late 2026 are shorter-duration studies (`med_dur: 1.05–1.86 yr`) and include only `133` completed placebo MRCTs (`94` post-war in the OOS DiD), leaving war-IV AE reduced-form tests underpowered until the 2022–2025 Phase-3 cohort completes reporting.
3. **Gate-2 Label Revision Granularity:** Counting `Drugs@FDA` `LABELING` supplements captures overall safety/administrative label revisions rather than section-specific wording edits inside *Section 6.1 Adverse Reactions*. Parsing DailyMed historical SPL XML version diffs specifically for post-approval additions of *non-serious* subjective reactions would provide a sharper direct test of post-launch tolerability recalibration.
