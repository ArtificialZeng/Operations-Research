## Structured Abstract

**Objective.** To evaluate whether the geographic composition of multiregional clinical trials (MRCTs) is associated with registered non-serious adverse-event (AE) proportions within drug–sponsor development programs, and to audit the identification support, MedDRA reporting-threshold comparability, and clinical equivalence precision of summary trial registries.

**Study Design and Setting.** Cross-sectional within-program observational analysis of completed, randomized, industry-sponsored Phase 2 and Phase 3 placebo-controlled MRCTs (`start_year` 2005–2024, `>= 100` participants, `>= 5` sites, `>= 3` countries) with posted results in ClinicalTrials.gov v2 (`N = 2,771` trials; `1,842` Phase 3 across `353` sponsors and `777` canonical molecules). Primary models estimated arm-specific associations per `+10 percentage points (+10 pp)` increase in Eastern European European Union (EU-East) site share absorbing exact `drug × sponsor` and `start-year` fixed effects with sponsor-clustered finite-sample `t(G−1)` inference. We separated extensive (`share = 0%`) and intensive (`share > 0%`) margins across observed `[0%, 35%]` support, evaluated restricted cubic splines, computed Aronow–Samii effective regression weights and two-way alternative-region counterfactuals, recoded an archived per-term MedDRA snapshot (`210,424` reported cells; `166,260` pre-defined common-term panel cells) into four mutually exclusive censoring/missingness states with sharp Manski Frisch–Waugh–Lovell (FWL) bounds, audited AE observation time-frame completeness, and tested serious AEs (SAEs), within-trial risk differences (`RD`), and all-cause mortality against pre-specified clinical equivalence margins via two one-sided tests (TOST).

**Results.** In Phase 3 `drug × sponsor` fixed-effects models (`N = 1,282` trials across `328` programs), each `+10 pp` higher EU-East site share (replacing U.S. sites) was associated with **`-2.48 pp`** (`95% CI [-3.61, -1.35]`, `p = 2.88e-5`) in placebo arms and **`-2.67 pp`** (`95% CI [-3.80, -1.54]`, `p = 7.47e-6`) in active arms; two-way contrasts replacing North America (`-2.68 / -2.92 pp`), Western Europe EU/EEA (`-2.39 / -2.83 pp`), Latin America (`-3.02 / -3.41 pp`), and Emerging Asia (`-2.69 / -2.71 pp`) were consistent (`all p < 0.001`). Among all `1,842` Phase 3 trials, `504` (`27.36%`) had `0%` EU-East sites and only `25` (`1.36%`) had `>= 50%` (median `8.45%`, 95th percentile `35.29%`). Adding an explicit `I(share = 0)` indicator across `[0%, 35%]` (`N = 1,211`, `310` programs) yielded non-significant zero-share intercepts (`+1.55 pp, p = 0.565` placebo; `+1.95 pp, p = 0.495` active) and positive-share slopes of `-2.11 pp` (`[-4.24, +0.02], p = 0.052`) and `-2.50 pp` (`[-4.64, -0.36], p = 0.022`); restricting solely to positive-share `(0, 35%]` trials dropped `74` programs (`N = 870`, `236` programs) and attenuated estimates to `-1.14 pp` (`[-3.65, +1.37]`) and `-1.84 pp` (`[-4.42, +0.74]`). Restricted cubic splines showed no departure from linearity (`Wald p = 0.548 / 0.681`), and removing the top-10 (`32.0%` FWL weight) or top-20 (`47.5%` weight) weighted programs left estimates virtually unchanged (`-2.51 / -2.67 pp` and `-2.67 / -2.84 pp`). Auditing `210,424` reported MedDRA cells identified `278` (`0.13%`) missing/low-denominator cells and `69` (`0.03%`) missing-count cells; eliminating silent fallbacks and restricting to `2,402` trials with documented AE time frames (`369` [`13.32%`] missing) preserved both main (`-2.33 / -2.50 pp` in Phase 3) and paired subjective-minus-objective contrasts (`-0.0660 / -0.0743` per `+10 pp`, `p <= 0.00051`). Under four-state recoding of a pre-defined 15+15 common MedDRA panel, `69.04%` of `5%`-threshold cells were left-censored (`0.00%` in `0%`-threshold trials), making worst-case Manski bounds uninformative for summed multi-term panels (`[-0.41, +0.37]` to `[-0.59, +0.51]`) while sharp Manski FWL bounds on peak single-term rates (`72%–88%` point-identified) remained strictly negative (`Imbens–Manski 95% CI [-1.69, -0.17] pp` placebo, `[-1.79, -0.39] pp` active; active `Subj − Obj` `[-1.87, -0.04] pp`). SAEs (`+0.06 / +0.15 pp`) and within-trial RD (`-0.19 pp`) met formal TOST equivalence within pre-specified `±2.0 pp` and `±1.0 pp` margins (`all p_TOST <= 0.0032`), whereas all-cause mortality (`N = 679`, `+0.18 / +0.36 pp`) failed equivalence at `±0.5 pp` (`p_TOST = 0.249 / 0.370`).

**Conclusion.** Within the same drug and sponsor development program, higher Eastern European EU site share is negatively associated with the proportion of participants with registered non-serious adverse events in ClinicalTrials.gov. Summary registry records cannot disentangle true regional symptom occurrence from elicitation, follow-up, and coding processes; country-by-arm validation against clinical study reports (e.g., EMA Policy 0070) is required before drawing clinical safety inferences.

### What Is New?

- **Key findings:** Across `1,282` Phase 3 MRCTs within `328` exact drug–sponsor programs, each `+10 pp` higher Eastern European EU site share is associated with `-2.48 pp` (placebo) and `-2.67 pp` (active) lower registered non-serious AE proportions relative to the U.S. (`-2.39` to `-3.62 pp` relative to North America, Western Europe EU/EEA, Latin America, and Emerging Asia), while SAEs and within-trial risk differences satisfy formal TOST equivalence within `±1.0 pp` and `±2.0 pp` (`p_TOST <= 0.0032`) and all-cause mortality lacks precision for `±0.5 pp` equivalence.
- **What this adds to what is known:** Separating extensive (`0%`) and intensive (`>0%`) margins across observed `[0%, 35%]` support, auditing Aronow–Samii FWL program weights, and propagating four-state MedDRA threshold left-censoring via sharp Manski bounds shows that the registry association is not driven by high-share outliers, a handful of drug programs, or denominator fallback artifacts, but is attenuated and imprecise when restricted solely to positive `(0, 35%]` shares and loses point identification in summed multi-term panels under `5%` reporting thresholds.
- **What is the implication and what should change now:** Clinical epidemiologists and systematic reviewers pooling ClinicalTrials.gov non-serious AE tables across multiregional trials should treat geographic site mix, reporting thresholds (`0%` vs. `5%`), and missing AE observation time frames (`13.3%`) as methodological heterogeneity factors rather than assuming pooled summary proportions are geographically comparable; validating mechanisms requires country-by-arm extraction from public clinical study reports.

## 1. Introduction

Over the past two decades, pivotal Phase 2 and Phase 3 clinical trials have shifted from predominantly United States and Western European recruitment toward multiregional clinical trials (MRCTs) spanning Eastern Europe, Latin America, and Asia (Glickman et al., 2009). While trial recruitment has globalized, public safety tables in ClinicalTrials.gov and regulatory prescribing information typically pool adverse events (AEs) across all participating regions into single arm-level proportions (National Library of Medicine [NLM], 2024; U.S. Food and Drug Administration [FDA], 2024).

Pooling arm-level summaries across regions can obscure substantial heterogeneity in registered AE proportions. In an individual-patient-data (IPD) analysis of rheumatoid arthritis trials, Keebler et al. (2020) documented markedly lower non-serious AE reporting proportions in Russia and Eastern Europe (`22%`) compared with the United States (`53%`) within placebo and standard-of-care arms. Conversely, in an operational analysis of `585` clinical trials monitored on a centralized risk-based platform, de Viron et al. (2025) observed relatively narrow cross-regional dispersion in a site-level central-monitoring AE risk indicator. These two bodies of evidence differ fundamentally in unit of observation (site-level operational risk indicators versus pooled study-arm registry counts), censoring rules, and clinical-epidemiological estimand, leaving open how strongly geographic site composition is associated with publicly registered AE tables across therapeutic areas—and how much of that registry association survives rigorous audits of exposure support, reporting-threshold left-censoring, missing denominators, and missing AE observation windows.

This methodological study addresses a single focused question: **Within the same drug and sponsor development program, how is regional site composition associated with registered non-serious AE proportions in ClinicalTrials.gov, and what are the exact support limits, MedDRA threshold-censoring bounds, and clinical equivalence precision limits of registry-level evidence?** We intentionally restrict our primary claims to observable registry associations over empirically supported site shares (`+10 pp` increments across `[0%, 35%]`), reserve secondary temporal, geopolitical-footprint, and exploratory FDA/FAERS analyses for a separate Supplementary Appendix (`supplementary_d30_jce.pdf`), and specify a concrete clinical study report (CSR) validation protocol using EMA Policy 0070 to bridge registry audits to patient-level safety inference.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Data Sources, Cohort Eligibility, and Archived Per-Term API Snapshot

We assembled two cryptographically archived ClinicalTrials.gov REST API v2 datasets (full provenance in Appendix S5, Table S6):

1. **Results-Posted Trial Universe (`N = 22,303` raw studies):** Queried `https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies` for all industry-sponsored, interventional Phase 2 and/or Phase 3 trials with posted results across all overall statuses (`23` gzipped JSON pages; manifest in `data/raw_ctgov/ctgov_fetch_manifest.json`). Filtering to randomized trials with valid location metadata (`>= 1` site) and valid AE group denominators (`>= 20` participants at risk in `>= 2` arms) yielded `12,887` randomized industry trials (`11,265` completed). Applying multiregional eligibility criteria (`start_year` 2005–2024, `enrollment >= 100`, `n_sites >= 5`, `n_countries >= 3`, and at least one classified placebo and active arm) produced our primary completed analytic panel of **`N = 2,771` placebo-controlled MRCTs** (`1,842` Phase 3 trials across `353` sponsors and `777` canonical molecules; `929` strictly disjoint standalone Phase 2 trials), alongside `3,356` all-status placebo MRCTs (including `483` terminated studies) and `1,276` twin-matched non-placebo active-comparator MRCTs for sensitivity replication.
2. **Archived Per-Term MedDRA `otherEvents.stats` Snapshot (`2,771 / 2,771` trials = `100.0%`):** Because the initial bulk paginated harvest omitted per-term `stats` arrays, we queried ClinicalTrials.gov v2 (`filter.ids`, batches of `50` NCTs) for the complete `adverseEventsModule` (`frequencyThreshold`, `timeFrame`, `description`, `eventGroups`, `otherEvents` with full `term`, `organSystem`, `sourceVocabulary`, `assessmentType`, and per-arm `stats`) across all `2,771` panel trials and archived the verbatim JSONL response (`data/raw_ctgov/meddra_other_events_snapshot.jsonl.gz`, `5.86 MB` compressed, `82.81 MB` uncompressed; `SHA-256` `7eb1eefdeae3b188...`, fetched `2026-11-27T23:38:43Z`; `data/raw_ctgov/meddra_snapshot_manifest.json`). All MedDRA audits and regressions re-parse deterministically from this frozen archive.

Four unit-tested parsing rules ensured reproducible cohort construction (`tests/test_d30_pipeline.py`): (i) arm classification separating genuine crossovers (`"Placebo -> Drug X"`, `"Placebo-Efgartigimod"`) and double-dummy active arms (`"Drug X + Placebo"`) from pure placebo and phase-labeled placebo titles (`"Placebo - Treatment Phase"`); (ii) MeSH-verified, backbone-aware canonical molecule normalization resolving single-substance compound codes (`CP-690,550 -> tofacitinib`) and rejecting MeSH artifacts (`99.78%` Phase 3 coverage); (iii) case-insensitive MedDRA SOC mapping including ClinicalTrials.gov's abbreviated `"General disorders"` label (`96.3%` term coverage; `97.2%` of vocabulary-tagged terms explicitly coded as MedDRA) with canonical UK/US MedDRA spelling harmonization (`diarrhea -> diarrhoea`, `anemia -> anaemia`); and (iv) mutually exclusive regional site-share simplexes summing identically to `1.000`.

### 2.2 Regional Site-Share Simplex and Within-Program Estimation

Under ClinicalTrials.gov Results Data Element Definitions (NLM, 2024), sponsors report arm-level counts of participants affected by **Serious Adverse Events (SAEs)** (defined by objective regulatory criteria, distinct from expedited 7-day/15-day IND safety reporting under `21 CFR 312.32`), **All-Cause Mortality**, and **Other (Not Including Serious) Adverse Events** exceeding a sponsor-chosen reporting threshold (`frequencyThreshold`, typically `5%` or `0%`). Let $S_{ir}$ denote trial $i$'s site share in region $r$. Our primary 7-region simplex partitions all trial locations into: United States (including Puerto Rico, omitted reference), Eastern European EU member states (`eu_east_sh`: Poland, Hungary, Czechia, Romania, Bulgaria, Slovakia, Croatia, Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, Slovenia), Non-EU Eastern Europe / Russia–Ukraine (`ru_ua_sh`), Western Europe and Developed APAC/Canada (`we_sh`), Latin America (`latam_sh`), Emerging Asia (`asia_sh`), and Other (`other_sh`). To separate North America from Western Europe explicitly, we also estimate an 8-region simplex omitting North America (`na_sh = U.S. + Canada`) while entering Western Europe EU/EEA (`we_eu_sh`) and Developed APAC/Israel (`dev_apac_no_ca_sh`) separately.

Our primary specification absorbs exact **drug × sponsor** (`mol_sponsor`) and **start-year** fixed effects (plus `phase_group` fixed effects in pooled Phase 2+3 models):

$$Y_{ia} = \sum_{r \neq \text{ref}} \beta_r S_{ir} + \mathbf{X}_i'\boldsymbol{\gamma} + \alpha_{m(i),s(i)} + \delta_{t(i)} + \phi_{\text{phase}(i)} + \varepsilon_{ia},$$

where $Y_{ia}$ is the registered arm proportion (`ctrl_oth`, `act_oth`, `oth_rd = act_oth - ctrl_oth`, `ctrl_sae`, `act_sae`, `ctrl_mort`, `act_mort`) and $\mathbf{X}_i$ controls for `log(enrollment)`, `log(n_sites)`, `log(n_countries)`, exact study duration (`dur_yr`), `is_double_blind`, and `freq_thr` (for non-serious AE outcomes). Following Abowd–Kramarz–Margolis connected-component pruning, every reported model achieves **100% full design-matrix rank (`matrix_rank == n_params`)**. Standard errors are sponsor-clustered with finite-sample `t(G−1)` degrees of freedom (`use_t = True`), supplemented by molecule clustering, Cameron–Gelbach–Miller two-way clustering (`sponsor × molecule`), and restricted Rademacher wild cluster bootstrapping (`B = 999`; Cameron et al., 2008). Because real-world EU-East site shares lie almost entirely within `[0%, 35%]`, **all headline estimates are reported strictly per `+10 pp` increase in site share (`0.10 * beta`)**, withdrawing full `[0%, 100%]` extrapolation claims.

### 2.3 Extensive vs. Intensive Margin, Splines, Effective Weights, and Alternative-Region Counterfactuals

To audit whether linear slopes depend on zero-share trials, sparse high-share observations, or a handful of large drug programs, we conducted five diagnostic decompositions (`scripts/08_reporting_sensitivity.py`):

1. **Extensive vs. Intensive Decomposition:** We added an explicit zero-share indicator $1(S_{i,\text{EU-East}} = 0)$ alongside the linear slope $S_{i,\text{EU-East}}$ across both `[0%, 35%]` observed support (`N = 1,211`, `310` programs) and all Phase 3 trials (`N = 1,282`, `328` programs), separating an intercept shift at entry (`0%` vs. `>0%`) from the intensive gradient among exposed trials.
2. **Common-Drug-Program Comparison:** Restricting to strictly positive `(0, 35%]` shares drops `74` non-singleton programs (`310 -> 236`). To separate sample-composition change from zero-share leverage, we compared full `[0%, 35%]`, `[0%, 35%]` with $1(S = 0)$, and strictly positive `(0, 35%]` models inside the **exact same `236` shared programs** (`N = 1,047` vs. `N = 870`), plus the analogous comparison inside the `257` programs surviving `share > 0%` across all Phase 3 trials (`N = 1,129` vs. `N = 941`).
3. **Nonlinear Binned and Restricted Cubic Spline Models:** We estimated categorical site-share bins (`0%` reference, `(0, 10%]`, `(10, 20%]`, `(20, 35%]`, `>35%`) and 4-knot Harrell restricted cubic splines (knots at `0%`, `8%`, `17%`, `35%`), testing nonlinearity via cluster-robust joint Wald $F$-tests (`H0: nonlinear basis terms = 0`) and plotting pointwise differences from `0%` strictly over observed `[0%, 35%]` support.
4. **Effective FWL Regression Weights (Aronow & Samii, 2016):** Residualizing $S_{i,\text{EU-East}}$ against all covariates, other regional shares, start-year FE, and `mol_sponsor` FE yields exact unit leverage weights $w_i = \tilde{x}_i^2 / \sum_j \tilde{x}_j^2$ ($\sum_i w_i = 1$). We computed program-level weights $W_g = \sum_{i \in g} w_i$, effective program count $N_{\text{eff}} = 1 / \sum_g W_g^2$, top-10/top-20 program concentration, support-zone weight shares (`0%`, `(0, 35%]`, `>35%`), and **leave-top-10 / leave-top-20 weighted-program-out** re-estimations.
5. **Two-Way Alternative-Region Counterfactuals:** Using the exact cluster-robust covariance matrix, we evaluated pairwise simplex substitutions $0.10 \times (\hat{\beta}_{\text{EU-East}} - \hat{\beta}_k)$ contrasting `+10 pp` EU-East against `-10 pp` of every comparator region: United States, North America (`U.S. + Canada`), Western Europe (`EU/EEA only`), Western Europe/Dev, Latin America, Emerging Asia, Developed APAC/Israel, and Non-EU Eastern Europe/RU-UA.

### 2.4 Four-State MedDRA Threshold Audit, Sharp Manski FWL Bounds, and AE Time-Frame Completeness

Under ClinicalTrials.gov submission rules (NLM, 2024), any preferred term exceeding `frequencyThreshold` in *at least one arm* enters a trial's `otherEvents` table for all arms, whereas terms below the threshold in *all* arms are omitted (left-censored). Previous registry parsers risked bias when term-level `numAtRisk` was missing or `< 20` (silently falling back to arm totals) or when `numAffected` was missing (treating missing cells as zero).

We therefore audited every trial-arm-term cell in the frozen `2,771`-trial snapshot without silent fallbacks (`scripts/02b_fetch_meddra_soc_stats.py`) at two complementary levels:
- **Reported-Table Strict Audit (`210,424` cells):** Every cell in `otherEvents` was verified for explicit `numAtRisk >= 20` and valid `0 <= numAffected <= numAtRisk`, flagging missing/unreliable denominators and missing affected counts/omitted `groupId` entries explicitly. We compared legacy fallback vs. strict zero-fallback and zero-anomaly-trial-only paired contrasts (`Subjective SOC - Objective SOC`, and `Subjective SOC - pure Investigations SOC` excluding infections and symptom-bearing organ classes).
- **Pre-Defined Common MedDRA Panel & Four-State Recoding (`166,260` cells):** To prevent `5%`-threshold selection bias, we defined fixed 15-term Subjective-Symptom, 15-term Objective-SOC, and 15-term pure `Investigations` laboratory/vital-sign biomarker panels strictly by trial prevalence inside the `220` uncensored `0%`-threshold trials (Appendix S5 lists all 45 terms). Every `(trial, arm, term)` cell was classified into mutually exclusive states: **Observed Positive** (`naff > 0`), **True Zero** (`naff = 0` observed or absent under `freq_thr = 0%` with valid arm denominator), **Below-Threshold Left-Censored** (absent under `freq_thr = tau > 0`, placing the true rate strictly in $[0, \min(\tau, Y_{ia}^{\text{any\_oth}})]$), **Missing/Unreliable Denominator**, and **Missing Term**.
- **Exact Sharp Manski FWL Identification Bounds & Imbens–Manski 95% CIs:** Because any fixed-effects OLS slope is an exact linear functional of the outcome vector, $\hat{\beta}(Y) = \sum_{i=1}^N \omega_i Y_i$ with $\omega_i = \tilde{x}_i / \sum_j \tilde{x}_j^2$, minimizing and maximizing $\hat{\beta}(Y)$ over cell-level outcome intervals $Y_i \in [L_i, U_i]$ achieves exact sharp Manski worst-case bounds at $Y_i^{\min} = L_i\mathbf{1}(\omega_i \ge 0) + U_i\mathbf{1}(\omega_i < 0)$ and $Y_i^{\max} = U_i\mathbf{1}(\omega_i \ge 0) + L_i\mathbf{1}(\omega_i < 0)$! We computed sharp Manski intervals and finite-sample Imbens–Manski (2004) 95% confidence intervals (alongside uniform `0%`, `50%`, and `100%` threshold-bound imputations) for both (i) summed 15-term common panels and (ii) peak single-term SOC rates (`strict_soc_max_pct`, where any trial with an observed term `>= tau` is **point-identified** with $L_i = U_i$ identically!).
- **AE Observation Time-Frame Audit:** We extracted structured `adverseEventsModule.timeFrame` completeness across all `2,771` trials, tested whether missingness correlated with EU-East share, and re-estimated primary models strictly inside trials with documented AE time frames.

### 2.5 Pre-Specified Clinical Equivalence Margins and Formal TOST

Rather than treating statistically non-significant estimates (`p > 0.05`) as evidence of invariance, we pre-specified clinically grounded symmetric equivalence margins per `+10 pp` EU-East site share and conducted formal finite-sample Two One-Sided Tests (TOST; Lakens, 2017) using sponsor-clustered `t(G−1)` degrees of freedom:
- **Serious Adverse Events (`ctrl_sae`, `act_sae`) and Within-Trial Risk Difference (`oth_rd`):** Primary margin **`±2.0 pp`** and strict margin **`±1.0 pp`** per `+10 pp` EU-East site share (relative to mean baseline SAE incidence `11.15%–12.23%` and mean `oth_rd` `+4.94 pp`).
- **All-Cause Mortality (`ctrl_mort`, `act_mort`):** Primary margin **`±0.5 pp`** per `+10 pp` EU-East site share (relative to mean mortality `6.42%–6.54%`), with secondary `±1.0 pp` and wide `±1.5 pp` precision benchmarks. Equivalence at one-sided $\alpha = 0.05$ holds if and only if $p_{\text{TOST}} = \max(p_{\text{lower}}, p_{\text{upper}}) < 0.05$ (equivalently, the entire 90% CI lies inside `[−Δ, +Δ]`).

Four nuanced methodological conclusions emerge from **Table 2** and **Figure 2**:
1. **No Sharp Extensive-Margin Jump, Yet Substantial Positive-Only Uncertainty:** Restricting to `[0%, 35%]` (`N = 1,211`, `310` programs) leaves linear slopes unchanged (`-2.49 / -2.98 pp`). Adding the zero-share indicator $1(S_{i,\text{EU-East}} = 0)$ estimates small, statistically null intercept shifts at `0%` (`+1.55 pp, p = 0.565` placebo; `+1.95 pp, p = 0.495` active), while positive-share slopes remain `-2.11 pp` (`[-4.24, +0.02], p = 0.052`) and `-2.50 pp` (`[-4.64, -0.36], p = 0.022`; pooled P2+P3 `0–35%`: `-2.56 pp, p = 0.0088` and `-2.98 pp, p = 0.00067`). However, dropping all zero-share trials from `(0, 35%]` (`N = 870`, `236` programs) both removes `74` programs and forfeits `25.0%` of total FWL exposure leverage (plus `29.1%` from `>35%` shares). Even holding the **exact same `236` programs** constant, removing zero-share trials shifts estimates from `-2.15 / -2.74 pp` (`N = 1,047`, `p <= 0.011`) to `-1.14 / -1.84 pp` (`N = 870`, `95% CIs` crossing zero). Among all positive-share Phase 3 trials without the `35%` truncation (`N = 941`, `257` programs), slopes remain negative and significant (`-1.65 pp [-3.21, -0.09], p = 0.039`; `-1.84 pp [-3.47, -0.20], p = 0.028`). We therefore bound our conclusion to **compositional differences across observed `[0%, 35%]` support** and acknowledge wide uncertainty when isolating small positive-share increments alone.
2. **Smooth Monotonic Progression Without Statistical Nonlinearity:** Across categorical Phase 3 bins relative to `0%`, differences deepen monotonically (`-3.00`, `-5.09`, `-6.68`, `-11.09 pp` placebo; `-3.80`, `-5.22`, `-8.44`, `-11.63 pp` active), and 4-knot restricted cubic splines show no statistically significant departure from linearity (`Wald F(2,128) = 0.605, p = 0.548` placebo; `F = 0.385, p = 0.681` active; **Figure 2A**).
3. **Not Driven by a Few Dominant Programs:** Effective Aronow–Samii FWL weights across the `328` Phase 3 programs yield $N_{\text{eff}} = 43.7$ (`HHI = 0.0229`), with the top-1 (`dapagliflozin/AstraZeneca`, `11.11%`), top-5 (`22.65%`), top-10 (`32.00%`), and top-20 (`47.52%`) programs accounting for moderate concentration (**Figure 2C**). Crucially, dropping the **top-10 weighted programs** (`-2.51 / -2.67 pp`, `p <= 0.00083`) or even the **top-20 weighted programs** (`-2.67 / -2.84 pp`, `p <= 0.00308`) leaves the association slightly *larger* in magnitude, confirming broad cross-program generality rather than dominance by a few portfolios.

### 3.3 MedDRA Threshold Comparability, Four-State Audit, Sharp Manski Bounds, and AE Time Frames

Auditing all `210,424` reported trial-arm-term cells in the archived `2,771`-trial snapshot confirmed `162,338` (`77.15%`) valid positive cells and `47,739` (`22.69%`) valid true-zero cells, while uncovering `278` (`0.13%`) missing or unreliable (`< 20` or `naff > nrisk`) denominator cells (`11–31` trials), `69` (`0.03%`) missing affected-count or omitted-`groupId` cells (`2–3` trials), and `332` (`0.16%`) cells where term-level `numAtRisk` differed from arm-level `otherNumAtRisk`. Although eliminating silent fallbacks changed trial-level summed rates in only `0.47%–1.01%` of trials (with maximum single-trial discrepancies up to `1.16`), strict zero-fallback estimation and zero-anomaly-trial filtering (`N = 1,724`) produced virtually identical paired `Subjective − Objective` contrasts (**Table 3** and **Figure 3**, `figures/fig3_meddra_fourstate_and_manski_bounds.png`): `-0.0660 [-0.1028, -0.0293]` (`p = 5.10e-4`) in placebo arms and `-0.0743 [-0.1144, -0.0342]` (`p = 3.40e-4`) in active arms per `+10 pp` EU-East share (zero-anomaly trials: `-0.0625 / -0.0741`, `p <= 5.22e-4`; pure `Investigations` biomarker comparator excluding infections and symptom-bearing organ SOCs: `-0.0774 / -0.0847`, `p <= 8.69e-4`).

Recoding the pre-defined 15-subjective + 15-objective common preferred-term panel across all `2,771` trials (`166,260` trial-arm-term cells; **Figure 3A**) exposes the central methodological constraint of ClinicalTrials.gov's `5%` reporting rule:
- Inside **`0%`-threshold trials (`N = 220`, `13,200` cells)**: **`0` (`0.00%`)** cells are left-censored (`59.13%` observed positive, `39.51%` true zero, `1.36%` missing/unreliable denominator). Point estimates for paired `Subj − Obj` (`-0.0779 [-0.1618, +0.0060], p = 0.068` placebo; `-0.0442, p = 0.390` active) and common-panel `Subj − Investigations` (`-0.0512 [-0.1065, +0.0041], p = 0.069` placebo; `-0.0333, p = 0.275` active) remain negative while pure `Investigations` sits near zero (`-0.0035, p = 0.722`; `-0.0063, p = 0.556`), but the `0%`-threshold subset is small and statistically underpowered (all 95% CIs include zero).
- Inside **`5%`-threshold trials (`N = 2,006`, `120,360` cells)**: **`83,094` (`69.04%`)** cells are below-threshold left-censored (`29.26%` observed positive, `1.39%` true zero, `0.28%` missing/unreliable denominator, `0.03%` missing term).

Propagating threshold left-censoring via exact sharp Manski FWL bounds yields a decisive methodological contrast (**Table 3, Panel B**; **Figure 3C**):
- **Peak Single-Term Rates (`strict_soc_max_pct`) Resist Worst-Case Threshold Censoring:** Because `84.9%` (placebo) and `87.7%` (active) of trials already contain at least one reported subjective preferred term exceeding the reporting threshold (`81.6%` and `85.5%` inside strict `5%`-threshold trials), below-threshold omitted terms cannot alter the maximum single-term rate in those trials ($L_i = U_i$). Consequently, sharp Manski bounds on Subjective peak-term rates are remarkably tight and **strictly negative even under adversarial censoring**: `[-1.11, -0.75] pp` (`Imbens–Manski 95% CI [-1.69, -0.17] pp`) in placebo arms and `[-1.22, -0.90] pp` (`Imbens–Manski 95% CI [-1.79, -0.39] pp`) in active arms! Moreover, the paired `Subjective − Objective` peak-term contrast has strictly negative sharp Manski identification intervals in both arms (`[-1.21, -0.37] pp` placebo; `[-1.33, -0.60] pp` active), with active-arm **Imbens–Manski 95% CIs excluding zero** both overall (`[-1.87, -0.04] pp`) and inside the fixed `5%`-threshold stratum (`[-2.33, -0.01] pp`).
- **Multi-Term Summed Panels Are Uninformative Under Adversarial Manski Bounds:** In contrast, because `69.04%` of individual common-panel cells are censored below `5%`, only `2.3%–10.9%` of trials are fully point-identified across all 15 terms. While symmetric uniform imputation (`0%`, `50%`, `100%` of the threshold ceiling) yields negative paired `Subj − Obj` and `Subj − Investigations` slopes (e.g., active `Subj − Inv`: `-0.0474 / -0.0357 / -0.0239`, all `p <= 0.012`), allowing worst-case adversarial censoring (setting every censored cell to `5%` in high-EU-East trials and `0%` in low-EU-East trials) widens Manski bounds to `[-0.41, +0.37]` through `[-0.59, +0.51]`. Thus, **multi-term summed registry contrasts depend on non-adversarial censoring assumptions, whereas peak-term rates and overall trial-arm non-serious AE proportions (`ctrl_oth`, `act_oth`, which are uncensored at the participant level) remain robust**.
- **AE Observation Time-Frame Audit:** Structured `timeFrame` entries were missing in **`369 / 2,771` (`13.32%`)** pooled trials and **`238 / 1,842` (`12.92%`)** Phase 3 trials. Time-frame missingness was unrelated to EU-East site share (`+0.29 pp, 95% CI [-2.21, +2.80], p = 0.818`), and restricting strictly to trials with documented AE time frames preserved the primary association in both Phase 3 (`N = 1,110`: `-2.33 / -2.50 pp`, `p <= 0.0012`) and pooled Phase 2+3 (`N = 1,488`: `-2.70 / -2.70 pp`, `p <= 1.62e-4`; **Table 3, Panel C**).

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Reconciling Registry Associations With Central-Monitoring and IPD Studies

Our findings bridge two seemingly divergent strands of clinical-trials methodology (**Table 5**). Consistent with Keebler et al.'s (2020) IPD analysis of rheumatoid arthritis placebo arms, higher Eastern European EU site share is strongly and negatively associated with registered non-serious AE proportions across all major therapeutic domains. At the same time, de Viron et al. (2025) analyzed `585` trials monitored on CluePoints' centralized statistical monitoring platform and found relatively narrow geographic variation in a site-level AE reporting-rate risk indicator.

**Table 5. Methodological Comparison Across Study Designs and Pre-Specified EMA Policy 0070 Validation Roadmap**

| Design Dimension | Keebler et al. (2020) (*Rheumatology*) | de Viron et al. (2025) (*BMJ Open*) | Present Study (*ClinicalTrials.gov* v2) | Proposed CSR Validation (EMA Policy 0070) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Data Source & Scope** | Sponsor IPD from rheumatoid arthritis trials | Operational central-monitoring EDC database (`N = 585` trials) | Public ClinicalTrials.gov v2 registry (`N = 2,771` MRCTs; `1,842` Phase 3) | Stratified Phase 3 MRCTs linked by NCT to EMA Policy 0070 CSRs, protocols & CRFs |
| **Unit of Observation** | Individual participant (country × arm) | Clinical site operational key risk indicator (KRI) | Study-arm pooled summary proportion (site-share mix) | Country × arm subgroup within each trial (dual blinded extraction) |
| **Primary Estimand** | Regional placebo/SoC AE proportion & ACR response | Cross-regional dispersion of site-level AE reporting-rate KRI | Within-program association per `+10 pp` EU-East site share | Within-trial, same-risk-window regional AE proportion, RD & registry discrepancy |
| **Threshold / Truncation** | Uncensored patient-level AE records | Uncensored EDC operational event logs | `5%` (`72.4%` trials) or `0%` (`7.9%`) reporting threshold; `13.3%` missing `timeFrame` | Full CSR safety tables (`0%` cutoff) + verified person-time & elicitation method |
| **What It Can Identify** | Within-indication regional placebo AE differences | Operational outlier-site risk dispersion during monitoring | Program-level compositional registry association & TOST/Manski bounds | Direct separation of country enrollment, follow-up, elicitation, and event incidence |

Direct comparison explains why these designs need not contradict one another (**Table 5**). First, de Viron et al. (2025) evaluated standardized site-level central-monitoring risk scores across actively monitored EDC streams (where sponsor risk-based monitoring adjusts for protocol-level baselines and flags extreme site outliers), whereas ClinicalTrials.gov publishes pooled study-arm proportions aggregated after site-level elicitation, coding, and threshold truncation. Second, our `2,771`-trial registry cohort spans `2005–2024` across `502` sponsors and all therapeutic areas, whereas operational monitoring cohorts disproportionately reflect contemporary risk-based-monitored sponsors (notably, our Appendix S2 temporal analysis observes smaller EU-East registry gradients in `2020–2024`). Third, and most fundamentally, study-level registry regressions measure an *ecological site-composition association*: they cannot distinguish true regional differences in patient-reported symptom incidence, background care pathways, observation duration, or active vs. passive symptom elicitation.

### 4.2 Methodological Limitations of Summary Trial Registries

Four structural limitations strictly bound the inferences supported by ClinicalTrials.gov summary data:
1. **Incidence, Follow-Up, and Elicitation Remain Entangled:** Without country-by-arm enrollment counts, region-specific follow-up person-time, and protocol-level elicitation instruments (spontaneous open-ended queries vs. structured symptom checklists), registry associations **cannot be interpreted as proof of regional "underreporting."** Even our site-to-patient accrual calibration (`w_hat = 1.354`, Appendix S1) bounds only site-to-patient weighting, not within-country follow-up or baseline comorbidity differences.
2. **Positive-Share Precision Limits:** While adding an explicit zero-share indicator preserves negative slopes on `[0%, 35%]` (`-2.11 / -2.50 pp`), excluding all zero-share trials from `(0, 35%]` removes `74` programs and `25%` of FWL exposure weight, yielding attenuated, imprecise point estimates (`-1.14 / -1.84 pp`, 95% CIs crossing zero). Accordingly, our findings document compositional differences over observed `[0%, 35%]` support rather than a precisely estimated linear slope across small positive increments alone.
3. **MedDRA Left-Censoring and Broad SOC Heterogeneity:** As our four-state audit proves, `69.04%` of common-panel cells in `5%`-threshold trials are left-censored, rendering summed multi-term panels non-identified under adversarial Manski bounds even though peak single-term rates (`72%–88%` point-identified) retain strictly negative Manski intervals. Furthermore, broad MedDRA SOCs mix symptoms and measurements (e.g., `palpitations` inside Cardiac disorders), which is why we separately verified pure `Investigations` biomarker terms.
4. **Incomplete Structured Metadata and Mortality Power:** Structured AE observation time frames are absent in `13.32%` (`369/2,771`) of records, and all-cause mortality lacks precision to establish `±0.5 pp` equivalence (`N = 679` Phase 3).

### 4.3 Pre-Specified Clinical Study Report Validation Roadmap (EMA Policy 0070)

To determine whether the registry-level compositional differences documented here reflect true regional incidence heterogeneity, follow-up exposure differences, or elicitation/recording differences—and whether they materially affect clinical safety communication—we specify a four-stage external validation protocol leveraging EMA Policy 0070 public clinical data packages (`https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/human-regulatory-overview/marketing-authorisation/clinical-data-publication`; detailed extraction instrument in Appendix S5):

1. **Stratified Sampling & Deterministic NCT Linkage:** Draw a stratified sample of multiregional Phase 3 placebo-controlled trials from our `1,842`-trial cohort stratified by therapeutic area (Immune/Inflam, Oncology, Endocrine/Metabolic, Cardiovascular, Neuro/Psych, Infectious), sponsor tier, and EMA Policy 0070 public document availability, linking ClinicalTrials.gov NCT records deterministically to EMA CSR Module 5.3.5.1 packages, study protocols, and case report forms (CRFs).
2. **Dual Independent Hypothesis-Blinded Extraction:** Two trained reviewers blinded to study hypotheses independently extract, per trial and per region × treatment arm: (i) exact randomized and safety-set participant denominators; (ii) actual follow-up duration / person-years at risk and AE collection window (`timeFrame`); (iii) elicitation methodology from protocols/CRFs (active checklist/diary vs. open-ended investigator query); (iv) central-laboratory objective shift-table abnormalities (`Investigations`); and (v) pre-specified common symptom proportions (e.g., headache, nausea, fatigue, diarrhoea) at `0%` threshold. Inter-rater agreement (`Cohen's κ` / intraclass correlation) and adjudicated discrepancies are pre-logged.
3. **Field Availability & Public-Posting Selection Audit First:** Prior to outcome modeling, report exact field-level extractability rates for every variable and test whether EMA Policy 0070 availability correlates with EU-East site share, sponsor, or baseline registry AE rates (explicitly diagnosing public-posting selection bias).
4. **Same-Trial, Same-Risk-Window Comparison & Stopping Rule:** Within each validated trial and matched risk window, compare country-level affected-participant proportions, incidence rates per 100 person-years, within-trial arm risk differences, and exact numerical discrepancies against the pooled ClinicalTrials.gov table. **Pre-specified interpretive rule:** If publicly accessible CSRs yield only country-level enrollment denominators without country-by-arm event tables, follow-up person-time, and elicitation metadata, conclusions remain explicitly bounded to registry-level compositional differences rather than clinical safety claims.

## 5. Conclusion

Within the same drug and sponsor development program, higher Eastern European EU site share is negatively associated with the proportion of participants with registered non-serious adverse events in ClinicalTrials.gov (`-2.48 pp` placebo and `-2.67 pp` active per `+10 pp` site share across Phase 3 trials; consistent against North America, Western Europe, Latin America, and Emerging Asia). Extensive/intensive decomposition, restricted cubic splines, effective FWL weight audits, four-state MedDRA recoding with sharp Manski bounds on peak-term rates, AE time-frame audits, and pre-specified TOST equivalence tests (`±1.0–2.0 pp` met for SAEs and within-trial RD; `±0.5 pp` unmet for mortality) clarify both the robustness and the exact boundary conditions of trial-registry safety summaries. Disentangling underlying event incidence from ascertainment processes requires country-by-arm validation against clinical study reports and protocols.
