# Exact transportation-basis certificates on public CAP101

This fixed-layout recourse pilot uses [OR-Library CAP101](https://people.brunel.ac.uk/~mastjjb/jeb/orlib/files/cap101.txt), with raw SHA-256 `7f1d0e32808b8c1bd9e3f49ee42dd758abb5a157da63ed52182f22bd14919ebe`. The instance has 25 facilities and 50 customers. Each listed transportation cost covers the customer's entire nominal demand, so divide it by that demand to obtain the original rational unit cost. The source capacities and opening costs are retained. [OR-Library format](https://people.brunel.ac.uk/~mastjjb/jeb/orlib/capinfo.html)

Use seed 20261124 for 1,500 independent normal demand vectors with the source means and customer standard deviations selected uniformly between 10% and 30% of the mean. Clip to zero and round to thousandths before any optimization. Use the first 250 vectors as a nested lighter workload. The two open sets are all facilities and the fewest capacities reached in ascending fixed-cost order until supply covers 110% of the largest sampled total demand. Break facility ties by increasing source ID. Freeze both sets against the same 1,500 sampled demands. This is an external source-scale pilot with new draws under the published generation model. [Bodur et al., Section 4.4](https://jrluedtke.github.io/papers/bodur-etal-str-benders-ijoc-2017.pdf)

Compare ordinary persistent dual-simplex recourse in HiGHS and CLP to HiGHS with at most 24 basis attempts. Use the same independent scenarios, opening sets, exact costs and total-demand traversal order. The first attempt is at the rounded barycenter. Each subsequent attempt solves the middle remaining scenario in total-demand order and screens the other remaining scenarios. Uncertified requests still receive an LP solve. Record the first-tree hit fraction, overall fraction of LP calls avoided with an exact tree certificate, all LP call counts, wall and CPU times, and objective agreement. Neither the fixed-layout recourse kernel nor the hits add new first-stage decisions.

Use a zero-cost dummy customer for unused open capacity. Interpret the optimal simplex columns as a tree, reject bases that lack a spanning tree or whose reduced costs fail an exact integer check, and recover shipments from signed subtree sums. Demand, capacity, flow signs, and original-cost primal–dual equality are checked with integer arithmetic for any accepted scenario. Measured time includes all basis checks and fallbacks. The pilot warrants larger integration only if recourse on the 1,500-scenario capacity-cover case is at least twice as fast as both ordinary implementations, with no objective discrepancy beyond `max(1e-6, 1e-9 × objective)` and no failed certificate.

Common-basis bunching and trickling down are established techniques. This pilot asks how much exact network structure and batching buy on a released instance, with the LP solver's own basis updates as the control.
