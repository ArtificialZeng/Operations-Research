## Abstract

Utility-scale battery storage reached 56.6 GW (156.0 GWh) across 36 U.S. balancing authorities by October 2026, yet its operational carbon impact remains contested. Linking 49.77 TWh of reconciled battery discharge across 14 metered grids to hourly fossil smokestack emissions from the EPA Continuous Emissions Monitoring System, we show that Average Emission Factors (-3.75 Mt CO2) and marginal emission factors (+3.69 Mt CO2) imply opposite signs under zero curtailment absorption—a +7.44 Mt CO2 counterfactual accounting discrepancy reversing sign in 9 of 14 grids. Joint 14-day block bootstraps confirm this discrepancy persists across curtailment-absorption scenarios and within an exact 18-month overlap. In California, accounting for cross-region imports leaves net emissions direction undetermined (-0.80 to +1.34 kg/MWh), while local transmission congestion (84.9%) limits incremental abatement. In Texas, marginal emissions remain 532 kg/MWh even above 60% renewable penetration as coal enters the margin.

Grid-scale lithium-ion batteries are widely subsidized on the premise that shifting midday solar electricity to evening peaks cuts power-sector carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions. Earlier engineering and economic studies established theoretically and through dispatch simulations that storage round-trip energy losses and coal-marginal charging can instead increase operational emissions (Carson & Novan, 2013; Hittinger & Azevedo, 2015; Fisher & Apt, 2017; Fares & Webber, 2017), and subsequent operational and equilibrium models underscored that decarbonization depends on absorbing otherwise curtailed renewables, transmission constraints, and long-run investment feedback (Arbabzadeh et al., 2019; Butters et al., 2025; Dupont et al., 2026). In parallel, empirical power-sector studies demonstrated that consumption-weighted Average Emission Factors (AEFs) systematically diverge from Marginal Emission Factors (MEFs) estimated from measured generating units when evaluating load-shifting technologies (Graff Zivin et al., 2014; Holland et al., 2016; Callaway et al., 2018; de Chalendar et al., 2019; Holland et al., 2022).

Until recently, commercial battery fleets were too small for national empirical evaluation, confining most evidence to simulations or early single-market pilots. By October 2026, however, U.S. utility-scale battery energy storage systems (BESS) expanded to 56,578.3 MW (156,003.2 MWh; mean duration 2.76 h) across 36 balancing authorities (BAs), including 19,702.9 MW (34.8%) outside California and Texas. Simultaneously, Form EIA-930 began reporting hourly storage dispatch in July 2024 alongside standalone 5-minute California Independent System Operator (CAISO) battery and curtailment logs and hourly U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Continuous Emissions Monitoring System (CEMS) smokestack CO2 records covering 99.94% of U.S. fossil stack emissions.

Here we move from simulated scenarios to commercial operational data to evaluate when large-scale batteries reduce operational CO2, when competing carbon-accounting frameworks reverse the estimated sign, and where transmission congestion constrains incremental abatement. Across three explicitly delineated samples (Supplementary Box 1)—a core 14-BA carbon-accounting panel (`2024-07` to `2026-09`, 49.77 TWh discharged), a CAISO curtailment subsample (`2024-07` to `2025-12`, 13,170 hours), and a supporting 49-BA operational gas-displacement panel (`2019-01` to `2026-10`, 3,359,080 BA-hours)—we quantify the fleet-wide AEF-versus-MEF accounting discrepancy, validate marginal emissions independently without shared fuel-slope assumptions, test cross-regional mechanisms in America's second-largest storage market (ERCOT), and derive an empirical congestion-constrained carbon break-even frontier.

## Counterfactual carbon accounting across 14 U.S. grids

Evaluating real-world battery emissions first required reconciling five reporting discrepancies in public EIA-930 telemetry across 52,816.8 GWh of full-window discharge (Methods; Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table 1): replacing CAISO's unphysical `Other+Geothermal` battery proxy (round-trip efficiency, RTE = 1.076) and aligning a 2-year +5,553 MW EIA-930 versus CAISO/CEMS midday natural-gas discrepancy using standalone 5-minute CAISO `fuelsource.csv` (true RTE = 0.863; lag-0 CEMS correlation $r = 0.9962$); recovering battery charging routed into `Unknown Energy Storage` in `ERCO` (7,224.4 GWh; RTE = 0.778) and `AZPS` (5,690.4 GWh; RTE = 0.875); filtering a single 2,668 MW outlier in `FPL`; and confirming 0% pumped-hydro leakage across `SOCO`, `DUK`, `MISO`, and `CPLE`. Across 13 clean alternating-current-coupled grids, physical RTEs lie tightly between 0.778 and 0.886 (fleet-weighted 0.845). Diurnal charging profiles diverge sharply by region: Southwestern solar fleets (`AZPS`, `PNM`, `TEPC`, `CISO`, `IID`) draw 71.1%–83.1% of charging energy during midday solar hours (10:00–16:00) and only 0.9%–5.8% overnight (23:00–06:00), whereas fossil-heavy Midwest and Southeast fleets draw over half of charging energy overnight (`MISO` 56.7%, `SOCO` 56.4%, `DUK` 50.6%).

It is critical to separate physical stack metering from counterfactual attribution: EPA CEMS directly measures hourly fossil generator smokestack emissions, whereas battery-induced net CO2 change relative to a no-storage baseline is estimated by our counterfactual displacement model (Methods). Linking reconciled hourly dispatch across 49.77 TWh in the 27-month EPA CEMS overlap (`2024-07` to `2026-09`) to 24-hour bilateral-trade-adjusted CEMS MEFs (with exact 24x24 date-clustered covariances; Fig. 1 and Table 1) exposes a stark directional divergence. Without renewable curtailment absorption ($\alpha_b = 0$), a consumption-weighted AEF counterfactual implies -75.3 kg CO2/MWh (-3.75 Mt CO2) of abatement, whereas the CEMS-MEF marginal displacement counterfactual estimates +74.2 kg CO2/MWh (+3.69 Mt CO2; import-only weight check: +72.5 kg/MWh). The resulting **+7.44 Mt CO2** (+149.5 kg/MWh) figure is strictly the **counterfactual accounting discrepancy** between two model-based estimates—not directly metered battery exhaust—with the two frameworks assigning opposite signs in 9 of 14 grids (and disagreeing by +2.55 Mt across the 12 clean non-CAISO grids).

Analytical decomposition (Methods) clarifies both the accounting discrepancy and the thermodynamic threshold for abatement. First, the AEF-versus-MEF gap is driven primarily by a +217.1 kg/MWh charging wedge—amplified by round-trip losses ($1/\eta_b$) because infra-marginal midday solar and nuclear generation depress average grid intensity far below the thermal margin—offset only partially by a +67.6 kg/MWh evening discharge wedge. Second, discharging 1 MWh yields net operational abatement ($\Delta\text{CO}_{2,b} < 0$) if and only if the fraction of charging energy absorbing genuinely curtailed zero-carbon electricity exceeds $\alpha_b^* = 1 - \eta_b\,\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} / \text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}$. Across all 14/14 grids, point estimates satisfy $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} / \text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} < 1/\eta_b$, yielding strictly positive break-even thresholds ($\alpha_b^* \in [0.6\%, 24.8\%]$). In 4/14 grids (`CISO`, `MISO`, `IID`, `CPLE`), charging MEF equals or exceeds discharging MEF outright ($\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} \ge \text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}}$); in the remaining 10 grids (including `ERCO`, `AZPS`, `PNM`, and `SOCO`), discharging MEF slightly exceeds charging MEF, yet the diurnal carbon spread remains too narrow to overcome physical round-trip losses. Without curtailment adjustments, unadjusted CEMS-MEF net emissions correlate positively with overnight charging share ($r = +0.413$) and only weakly with midday solar share ($r = -0.170$), led by coal- and gas-marginal grids (`MISO` +175.1, `ERCO` +142.5, `CPLE` +118.9, `SOCO` +74.8, `DUK` +44.3 kg/MWh).

## Curtailment sensitivity, multi-grid uncertainty, and independent MEF validation

Because the +7.44 Mt CO2 baseline assumes zero curtailment absorption ($\alpha_b = 0$), we evaluate sensitivity across non-zero absorption rates while propagating multi-day temporal correlation jointly across all 14 grids, bilateral neighbors, and California's curtailment regimes via a $B = 1,000$ 14-day contiguous moving-block bootstrap (Fig. 2a and Table 2, Panel A). To eliminate temporal extrapolation from California's 2024–2025 curtailment archive into 2026, we estimate every scenario identically on both the full 27-month CEMS window (49.77 TWh) and the exact 18-month same-window overlap (`2024-07` to `2025-12`, 26.05 TWh, AEF = -1.58 Mt). Under uniform curtailment absorption across all 14 grids, fleet-wide CEMS-MEF net CO2 crosses zero at $\alpha_{\text{fleet}}^* = 18.9\%$, yet the counterfactual discrepancy relative to naive AEF remains strictly positive across the entire spectrum—from +7.42 Mt (14d 95% CI [+7.11, +7.78]) at $\alpha = 0\%$ to +4.49 Mt ([+4.20, +4.82]) at $\alpha = 15\%$ and +3.51 Mt ([+3.22, +3.84]) at $\alpha = 20\%$. Under realistic hybrid scenarios substituting California's curtailment-stratified empirical system estimate for `CISO` while sweeping non-California absorption from 0% to 15%, fleet net CO2 declines from +1.81 Mt ([+1.41, +2.21]) at 0% to +0.26 Mt ([-0.14, +0.64]) at 15%—spanning zero at 15%, so fleet-wide net increases cannot be claimed at high non-California absorption. Crucially, however, the accounting discrepancy versus naive AEF remains tightly bounded well above zero in both windows: **+5.56 to +4.01 Mt** ([+3.60, +4.39]) in the full window and **+2.40 to +1.72 Mt** ([+1.49, +1.93]) in the exact same-window overlap.

Because our eGRID 2023 rev2 peaker cross-check ($r = 0.977$, MAD = 9.8 kg/MWh) shares EIA-930 dispatchable-generation slopes with our main MEF estimator, we conduct three genuinely independent validations (Fig. 2b and Supplementary Table 4). First, directly regressing hourly EPA CEMS smokestack $\Delta\text{CO}_2$ on hourly metered battery dispatch $\Delta\text{BESS}_{\text{MW}}$ (controlling for $\Delta\text{Load}$, $\Delta\text{Solar}$, $\Delta\text{Wind}$, $\Delta\text{Imports}$, and Year-Month x Hour fixed effects) directly measures how fossil stacks respond to actual battery cycling without using dispatchable-load slopes: stack emissions fall by **271.3 $\pm$ 4.2 kg/MWh** in `CISO` ($t = -64.6$), **499.3 $\pm$ 3.4 kg/MWh** in `ERCO` ($t = -146.3$), **222.3 $\pm$ 22.5 kg/MWh** in `AZPS`, and **618.6 $\pm$ 84.4 kg/MWh** in `MISO`—closely matching both our direct $\partial\text{CO}_2/\partial\text{Load}$ slopes (+275.3, +494.3, +214.5, +557.4 kg/MWh) and our 24-hour CEMS MEFs. Second, pure EPA CEMS internal stack ramp slopes ($\Delta\text{CO}_{2,\text{CEMS}} / \Delta\text{GrossLoad}_{\text{CEMS}}$, zero EIA-930 inputs) confirm elevated fossil marginal intensity across thermal grids (`CISO` 376, `ERCO` 516, `AZPS` 597, `MISO` 636 kg/MWh). Third, applying official EPA eGRID 2023 rev2 BA Non-Baseload CO2 output rates—an independent capacity-factor-weighted EPA metric using zero hourly regressions—to each grid's physical round-trip loss floor yields +89.6 kg/MWh (+4.46 Mt CO2) across the 14 grids, independently reproducing the sign reversal relative to AEF (while lower cross-grid correlation $r = 0.327$ reflects eGRID Non-Baseload omitting interstate trade and hydro margins in `PGE` and `AZPS`).

## California curtailment, cross-region imports, and multi-day uncertainty

California's public 5-minute curtailment archive (`2024-07` to `2025-12`, 13,170 matched hours, 4,347.2 GWh curtailed) illustrates how verified renewable curtailment and cross-region imports jointly alter marginal storage estimates (Fig. 3 and Table 2, Panel B). Stratifying strictly on consecutive interior regime hours (requiring both $t$ and $t-1$ inside the same bin to remove transition-boundary leakage) shows monotonic marginal stack declines: when curtailment exceeds 2,000 MW, California's **in-state domestic** CEMS MEF declines **83%** (160.6 to 26.8 kg/MWh; alternative observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$ specification: -83%) and **import-adjusted system-wide** CEMS MEF declines **60%** (268.8 to 107.0 kg/MWh; observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$: -62%), while marginal un-curtailment climbs from 0.0% to 62.4%. Hourly battery charging responds directly to curtailment ($\partial\text{Chg}/\partial\text{Curt} = +0.1385$, $t = +8.95$; system curtailment $+0.1539$, local congestion $+0.1322$).

Yet 84.9% of CAISO curtailment (3,691.3 of 4,347.2 GWh) reflects local transmission congestion rather than system-wide oversupply, and 55.0% of battery charging occurs during light 1–500 MW congestion hours where marginal un-curtailment is only 2.5%. Consequently, charging-weighted empirical marginal absorption is $\hat{\alpha}_{\text{unspill}}^{\text{emp}} = 14.1\%$ (14d 95% CI [9.7%, 19.0%]; CEMS MEF collapse 16.7%, 14d CI [11.9%, 20.8%]), below California's break-even threshold $\alpha_{\text{CISO}}^* = 19.9\%$. Propagating diurnal, neighbor-import, curtailment-regime, cycling-weight, and multi-day weather uncertainty jointly via $B = 1,000$ contiguous block bootstraps yields a curtailment-stratified system-wide balance of **-0.80 kg CO2/MWh** (14d 95% CI [-14.20, +12.58], SE = 6.85, $p = 0.920$; 7d [-13.24, +9.83]; 1d [-10.39, +8.15]) under our main potential-net-load specification and **+1.34 kg CO2/MWh** (14d 95% CI [-21.86, +30.51], SE = 13.78, $p = 0.764$) under the alternative observed-load ($\Delta\text{Load}$) specification (without asserting strict exogeneity of observed load changes). Ignoring interstate imports gives a strictly negative domestic balance across all block lengths (-30.64 kg/MWh, 14d CI [-42.78, -17.48]; observed $\Delta\text{Load}$: -30.67, 14d CI [-49.02, -8.54]). Thus, **accounting for cross-region imports, California's net emissions direction remains undetermined**, while naive AEF's -104.7 kg/MWh abatement claim is decisively ruled out.

## Congestion-constrained carbon break-even frontier and cross-regional validation

To answer a broader planning question—under what grid conditions incremental storage expansion stops delivering operational decarbonization—we combine California's observed hourly curtailment curve with cross-regional empirical stratification in ERCOT, MISO, and AZPS (Fig. 4). Sweeping incremental storage capacity scale $\kappa \in [0.1\times, 3.0\times]$ (1.7–52.0 GW added on top of California's 17.3 GW fleet, holding the empirical hourly charging profile fixed as a short-run boundary) across four local-congestion access shares ($\tau_{\text{local}} = 0\%, 25\%, 50\%,$ and $100\%$) traces a **Congestion-Constrained Carbon Break-Even Frontier** (Fig. 4a). Under current transmission constraints accessing only system-wide curtailment ($\tau_{\text{local}} = 0\%$), incremental headroom falls from 7.5% at $\kappa = 0.1\times$ to 2.8% at $1.0\times$, remaining net carbon-increasing at every scale (+39.2 to +59.3 kg/MWh). Unlocking 25% of local congestion via targeted co-siting or transmission upgrades yields net abatement only up to $\kappa \approx 0.25\times$ (~4.3 GW; +0.4 kg/MWh) before turning carbon-positive (+40.4 kg/MWh at $1.0\times$); unlocking 50% extends abatement to $\kappa \approx 0.47\times$ (~8.1 GW); and even a frictionless zero-congestion ceiling ($\tau_{\text{local}} = 100\%$) saturates below $\kappa = 1.0\times$ (headroom falling from 40.3% at $0.25\times$ to 18.2% at $1.0\times$ and 10.1% at $2.0\times$, turning +5.4 to +30.7 kg/MWh) without concurrent renewable expansion.

Cross-regional interior stratification across hourly renewable penetration ((Solar + Wind) / Load) demonstrates why high renewable penetration alone does not guarantee storage abatement (Fig. 4b). In California (`CISO`, zero coal), 85.2% of battery charging occurs above 60% renewable penetration, where domestic net-load CEMS MEF declines to 69.9 $\pm$ 3.4 kg/MWh. In Texas (`ERCO`, 9.92 TWh—the second-largest U.S. storage fleet), 73.6% of charging likewise occurs above 40% renewables (28.4% above 60%), yet domestic CEMS MEF does not fall: it remains 501.8 kg/MWh at 20–40%, 495.4 kg/MWh at 40–60%, and rises to **532.4 $\pm$ 7.9 kg/MWh** above 60% renewables. Fuel-stack decomposition inside CEMS reveals the mechanism: pushing wind and solar past 60% in ERCOT displaces gas peakers and forces **coal units onto the operating margin**, doubling coal's marginal contribution from 151 kg/MWh (<20%) to **279 kg/MWh** (>60%, 52% of total MEF). A similar coal margin persists across all regimes in `MISO` (496–508 kg/MWh, coal share 271–308 kg/MWh). Finally, supporting 49-BA operational regressions (Methods; Supplementary Figs. 2–3 and Supplementary Tables 2–3) show that conditioning on solar and demand shifts, +1% $\text{BESS}/d_{19}$ is associated with a -0.3352 reduction in evening-minus-midday natural-gas generation ($p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.004$; Drop CISO & ERCO -0.3810; Nuclear placebo -0.0005, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.291$), contrasted transparently against the solar-confounded uncontrolled baseline (`No Controls` = -0.0579, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.576$). Even where batteries demonstrably displace evening gas peakers, operational decarbonization fails unless charging avoids fossil margins.

## Discussion
Evaluating commercial storage operations across 49.77 TWh demonstrates that carbon-accounting conventions—not merely engineering assumptions—govern whether today's U.S. battery fleet appears to abate or increase emissions. Under carbon-blind incentives such as U.S. Internal Revenue Code Section 48E and average-emission Scope 2 protocols, storage earns identical credit whether charging from overnight coal margins in `MISO` (+175 kg/MWh), coal-marginal high-wind hours in `ERCO` (+142 kg/MWh), or curtailed midday solar in `CISO`. Across identical dispatch records, replacing AEF counterfactuals with generator-stack CEMS MEFs closes a +7.44 Mt CO2 accounting discrepancy (+4.01 Mt even under 15% non-California curtailment absorption and +1.72 Mt strictly inside the 18-month same-window overlap). Policy designs aimed at operational decarbonization therefore require locational marginal emissions signals paired with transmission expansion or co-sited renewable relief—not undifferentiated storage deployment mandates.

Several limitations bound these findings and define priorities for future research. First, public 5-minute renewable curtailment logs across 2024–2025 are uniform only for CAISO; extending interval-level curtailment stratification to ERCOT, MISO, and international markets (such as Great Britain and Australia) as high-resolution curtailment records become publicly standardized will test global generalizability. Second, our congestion-constrained frontier holds the empirical hourly charging shape fixed and weights cross-region imports using average bilateral trade shares; coupling nodal optimal power flow, dynamic price-responsive dispatch feedback, and marginal bilateral power-flow elasticities is needed for long-horizon siting studies. Third, wide 14-day block-bootstrap intervals for import-adjusted California net emissions ([-14.20, +12.58] and [-21.86, +30.51] kg/MWh) establish directional indeterminacy rather than equivalence-tested zero impact. Fourth, our supporting 49-BA gas-displacement regressions are observational conditional associations whose sub-component wild bootstrap $p$-values sit between 0.075 and 0.130 (Supplementary Table 3), and classifying the administrative cause of EIA-930's 2024–2025 California midday natural-gas discrepancy requires internal EIA revision metadata. Finally, our accounting evaluates short-run operational dispatch holding generation capacity fixed; long-run storage-induced renewable investment and carbon pricing (Butters et al., 2025; Dupont et al., 2026) can yield larger lifecycle abatement than short-run operations alone, though short-run operations cannot be credited with AEF-implied savings without verified curtailment absorption.

## Methods

### Empirical samples and data sources

We assemble five public U.S. grid and emissions archives across three delineated samples (Supplementary Box 1): (1) hourly Form EIA-930 operations (`2019-01` to `2026-10`, 4,285,392 rows; 3,359,080 rows across $G = 49$ balanced BAs with $\ge 72$ months of non-null demand and 2019 mean demand $d_{19} \ge 100$ MW) plus 2025–2026 bilateral `EIA930_INTERCHANGE` flows; (2) monthly Form EIA-860M generator inventories through October 2026; (3) official CAISO 5-minute `fuelsource.csv` telemetry (1066/1066 days, `2023-12-01` to `2026-10-31`, aggregated to 25,569 hours); (4) official CAISO 5-minute `Production and Curtailments` workbooks (`2024-01` to `2025-12`, decomposing 4,347.2 GWh overlap curtailment into 656.0 GWh System and 3,691.3 GWh Local congestion); and (5) hourly EPA CEMS smokestack emissions (`2024-07-01` to `2026-09-30`, 1,043,187 BA-hours, 3,506.3 Mt CO2) from Catalyst PUDL `v2026.11.0`, mapped to BAs and fuel classes (`COAL`, `NGCC`, `NGCT`, `OIL`) via EPA eGRID 2023 rev2 (`PLNT23`, `BA23`) and EIA-860M (16,316 plants, 99.94% of U.S. CEMS CO2).

### Telemetry reconciliation and EIA-930 audit

Physical telemetry reconciliation resolves five anomalies before carbon accounting (Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table 1): (i) splicing CAISO `fuelsource.csv` across 25,566 hours (`2023-12` to `2026-10`) reconciles a 2-year +5,553 MW EIA-930 versus CAISO/CEMS midday natural-gas discrepancy (peaking at +7,639 MW in 2025-10 and converging to 36.8 MW in 2026, raising CEMS lag-0 correlation from $r = 0.835$ to $r = 0.9962$) and replaces CAISO's unphysical `Other+Geothermal` battery column (RTE = 1.076) with standalone battery metering (RTE = 0.863); (ii) summing `Battery + Other + Unknown Energy Storage` recovers utility-scale charging in `ERCO` (7,224.4 GWh; RTE 1.49 to 0.778) and `AZPS` (5,690.4 GWh; undefined to 0.875); (iii) a deterministic physical bound ($|\text{BESS}| \le 1.25 \times \text{EIA-860M nameplate}$) filters one 2,668 MW glitch in `FPL` (2025-08-27 HE 6), while reconciling `DUK` (53.5 MW operating plus 18.8 MW 2026 under-construction BESS); (iv) pumped-hydro correlation audits verify 0/4 leakage across `SOCO`, `DUK`, `MISO`, and `CPLE`, while flagging DC-coupled `FPL` (RTE = 0.949) separately and excluding four discharge-only reporters (`NEVP`, `ISNE`, `SWPP`, `PSCO`) from two-way carbon accounting; and (v) cross-correlation audits verify exact 0-hour UTC timestamp alignment between CEMS hour-beginning and EIA-930/CAISO hour-ending clocks ($r = 0.991$–$0.996$ across major thermal grids).

### Formal carbon-arbitrage identities and congestion frontier

For BA $b$ with physical RTE $\eta_b \in (0,1)$, charging- and discharging-weighted marginal emission factors $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}, \text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} > 0$, and curtailed-renewable charging fraction $\alpha_b \in [0,1]$, discharging 1 MWh induces net counterfactual CO2 change $\Delta \text{CO}_{2,b}(\alpha_b) = (1 - \alpha_b)\,\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} / \eta_b - \text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}}$, yielding net operational abatement if and only if $\alpha_b > \alpha_b^* \equiv 1 - \eta_b\,\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} / \text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}$ (verified to numerical error 5.7e-14 across all 14 grids). The AEF-versus-MEF accounting discrepancy at $\alpha_b = 0$ decomposes identically into charging and discharging wedges: $\text{Bias}_b = (\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} - \text{AEF}_{b,\text{chg}})/\eta_b - (\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} - \text{AEF}_{b,\text{dis}})$ (identity error 5.7e-14). Because observed operator curtailment $q_{b,t}^{\text{obs}}$ represents post-battery residual curtailment, it cannot bound historical absorption of installed fleets; however, for an incremental expansion $\Delta c_{b,t} = \kappa c_{b,t}$ added on top of today's fleet with local-congestion access fraction $\tau_{\text{local}} \in [0,1]$, effective accessible spill $q_{b,t}^{\text{eff}}(\tau_{\text{local}}) = q_{b,t}^{\text{sys}} + \tau_{\text{local}} q_{b,t}^{\text{loc}}$ strictly bounds incremental curtailment absorption at $\rho_b^{\text{res}}(\kappa, \tau_{\text{local}}) = \sum_t \min(\kappa c_{b,t}, q_{b,t}^{\text{eff}}) / \sum_t \kappa c_{b,t}$, defining the Congestion-Constrained Carbon Break-Even Frontier in Fig. 4a. Storage duration scaling (Proposition 1; 4-hour vs. 1-hour ratio = 4.0x, error 0.0e+00) predicts energy capacity (`BESS_MWh`), not power (`BESS_MW`), governs evening gas displacement.

### EPA CEMS marginal emission factors and 24x24 covariance

For every metered BA and bilateral neighbor, we estimate 24-hour domestic CEMS MEF vectors ($\text{MEF}_{b,h}^{\text{dom}}$, $h = 1,\dots,24$) by first-differencing hourly stack CO2 on dispatchable domestic generation ($\Delta\text{Disp}_{b,t}$, omitting solar, wind, and BESS) within Year-Month x Hour fixed effects after a deterministic 8x MAD extreme-ramp glitch mask. Joint 24-hour estimation yields exact 24x24 date-clustered covariance matrices $V_b^{\text{dom}}$ capturing arbitrary within-day cross-hour correlation. Bilateral-trade-adjusted system MEFs weight domestic and neighbor 24-hour curves by hour-specific marginal domestic versus net-import shares (with exact propagation of actual neighbor covariance matrices $V_j^{\text{dom}}$). Consumption-weighted Naive AEFs use incoming-import-only bilateral weights (`imp_weight`) and full CAISO `fuelsource.csv` supply. Independent cross-checks use 24-hour marginal fuel shares multiplied by BA-specific EPA eGRID 2023 rev2 coal, CCGT, and CT peaker emission factors.

### Independent MEF validation and cross-regional stratification

To validate MEFs without relying on EIA-930 dispatchable-generation slopes, we compute three independent benchmarks (Supplementary Table 4): (1) official EPA eGRID 2023 rev2 BA annual CO2 Non-Baseload output emission rates (`BA23`/`PLNT23`, zero hourly regressions) evaluated on each grid's physical round-trip loss floor $\text{NB}_b (1/\eta_b - 1)$; (2) pure EPA CEMS internal stack ramp slopes regressing $\Delta\text{CO}_{2,\text{CEMS}}$ solely on $\Delta\text{GrossLoad}_{\text{CEMS}}$ within Year-Month x Hour fixed effects; and (3) direct hourly battery-dispatch regressions of $\Delta\text{CO}_{2,\text{CEMS}}$ on metered $\Delta\text{BESS}_{\text{MW}}$ controlling for $\Delta\text{Load}$, $\Delta\text{Solar}$, $\Delta\text{Wind}$, $\Delta\text{Imports}$, and Year-Month x Hour fixed effects with date-clustered standard errors. Cross-regional renewable-penetration stratification (`CISO`, `ERCO`, `AZPS`, `MISO`) partitions consecutive interior hours ($t$ and $t-1$ in the same bin: `<20%`, `20–40%`, `40–60%`, `>60%` of `(Solar+Wind)/Load`) and regresses total and coal-only CEMS $\Delta\text{CO}_2$ on potential net load within Year-Month x Hour fixed effects with the same 8x MAD guard.

### Multi-day contiguous moving-block bootstraps

To propagate diurnal covariance, neighbor-import MEF uncertainty across all 10 CAISO bilateral neighbors, curtailment-regime slope variance, hourly battery cycling weights and RTE, and multi-day synoptic weather autocorrelation jointly, we implement contiguous moving-block bootstraps ($B = 1,000$) at block lengths of $L = 1, 7,$ and $14$ days. For each draw, contiguous $L$-day blocks are sampled with replacement across the 549 overlap days (`2024-07` to `2025-12`) and 273 post-overlap CEMS days (`2026-01` to `2026-09`) to preserve exact sample lengths, simultaneously re-estimating all neighbor 24-hour CEMS MEFs, CAISO interior curtailment slopes (both main potential net load and alternative observed $\Delta\text{Load}$), and hourly cycling weights. Our 14-BA fleet sensitivity bootstrap extends this exact 14-day block resampling simultaneously across all 14 metered grids and their bilateral neighbors in both the full 27-month window (49.77 TWh) and the exact 18-month same-window overlap (26.05 TWh).

### Supporting 49-BA diurnal gas-displacement regressions

Supporting Sample 1 regressions (Supplementary Figs. 2–3 and Supplementary Tables 2–3) regress the monthly evening-minus-midday natural-gas spread normalized by 2019 mean demand ($d_{19,b}$) on installed $\text{BESS}_{b,t}/d_{19,b}$ across $G = 49$ BAs ($N = 4,589$ BA-months), weighted by pre-boom baseline demand $d_{19,b}$ to prevent tiny zero-gas hydroelectric districts (`DOPD`, 21.3% equal-weighted FWL leverage vs. 1.01% WLS) from distorting national slopes. Inference uses BA-clustered CR1, two-way clustering (Cameron et al., 2011; Abadie et al., 2023), exact LSDV-equivalent restricted wild cluster bootstraps under Rademacher and Webb 6-point weights ($B = 999$; Cameron et al., 2008; MacKinnon & Webb, 2018; Roodman et al., 2019; Webb, 2023), and 1,000-permutation randomization inference, alongside distributed 6-month timing tests, daily `BA x Year-Month FE` solar-swing dispatch (139,497 BA-days), MWh-versus-MW duration horse-races, and nuclear placebos.
