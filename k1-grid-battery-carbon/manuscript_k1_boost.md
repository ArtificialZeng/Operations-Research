## Abstract

Utility-scale battery energy storage systems (BESS) reached **56,578.3 MW (156,003.2 MWh; mean duration 2.76 h)** across 36 U.S. Balancing Authorities (BAs) by October 2026, with 19,702.9 MW (34.8%) outside California and Texas. Yet real-world storage carbon impacts remain contested due to telemetry discrepancies, diurnal confounding, and reliance on Average Emission Factors (AEFs) rather than Marginal Emission Factors (MEFs) estimated from measured generator stacks. We assemble a national empirical audit across three explicit samples: (1) a 49-BA diurnal gas panel (`2019–2026`, 3,359,080 hrs), (2) a 14-BA carbon-accounting panel linking reconciled telemetry (52,816.8 GWh full; 49.77 TWh in the `2024H2–2026Q3` EPA Continuous Emissions Monitoring System [CEMS] overlap) to generator-stack CO2 (3,506.3 Mt), and (3) a CAISO curtailment subsample (`2024H2–2025`, 13,170 hrs). First, reconciling five EIA-930 telemetry discrepancies—including a 2-year **+5,553 MW** EIA-930 vs. CAISO/CEMS midday natural-gas reporting discrepancy across 2024–2025—demand-weighted WLS regressions show strong **conditional diurnal gas displacement**: conditioning on solar and demand shifts, +1% $\text{BESS}/d_{19}$ is associated with a **$-0.3352$** evening-minus-midday natural-gas reduction ($t_{\text{CR1}} = -5.88$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.004$, $p_{\text{Webb6}} = 0.002$, RI $p = 0.0010$). By contrast, the uncontrolled specification is null (**No Controls = $-0.0579$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.576$**) because concurrent solar growth deepens the duck curve in battery-adopting grids. Once conditioned, displacement strengthens outside California and Texas ($-0.3810$, $t = -6.29$), peaks at Hour Ending 20 ($-0.291$, $t = -8.95$), passes 6-month future lead tests ($F = 0.08$, $p = 0.921$), holds within `BA x Year-Month` FE across 139,497 BA-days ($t = -3.60$), and yields zero on nuclear ($-0.0005$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.291$). Second, while EPA CEMS directly measures generator smokestack emissions, battery-induced net CO2 change relative to a no-storage baseline is estimated by our marginal displacement model. Across **49.77 TWh** discharged in 14 grids (without curtailment absorption), an AEF counterfactual implies **$-75.3$ kg/MWh ($-3.75$ Mt CO2)** versus **$+74.2$ kg/MWh ($+3.69$ Mt CO2)** under our CEMS-MEF counterfactual—a **+7.44 Mt CO2 counterfactual accounting discrepancy** (`+3.69 Mt` minus `-3.75 Mt`; 9/14 grids flip sign; eGRID 2023 rev2 $r = 0.977$). Third, in consecutive interior `>2,000 MW` CAISO curtailment hours, real curtailment slashes California's **in-state domestic** CEMS MEF by **83%** (160.6 to 26.8 kg/MWh) and **system-wide (import-adjusted)** CEMS MEF by **60%** (268.8 to 107.0 kg/MWh) while driving charging ($\partial\text{Chg}/\partial\text{Curt} = +0.138$, $t = +8.95$). Yet because 84.9% of curtailment is local congestion, empirical marginal absorption ($\hat{\alpha}_{\text{unspill}}^{\text{emp}} = 14.1\%$; headroom $\rho^{\text{res}} = 18.2\%$) falls below $\alpha_{\text{CISO}}^* = 19.9\%$. Jointly propagating diurnal, import, curtailment-regime, and cycling-weight uncertainty via a $B = 1,000$ date-clustered block bootstrap places CAISO system-wide net CO2 at **$-0.80$ kg/MWh (95% CI [-10.39, +8.15], $p = 0.810$)** under our main specification and **$+1.34$ kg/MWh (95% CI [-14.14, +18.62], $p = 0.826$)** under pure exogenous $\Delta\text{Load}$ (domestic-only: **$-30.64$ kg/MWh**, 95% CI [-39.30, -22.47]). Both system-wide estimates sit statistically indistinguishable from zero (near carbon neutrality, flipping sign across specifications)—erasing >99% of naive AEF's **$-104.7$ kg/MWh** claim—while fossil-marginal grids (`ERCO`, `MISO`, `CPLE`, `SOCO`, `DUK`) remain net carbon-increasing (+44 to +175 kg/MWh).

## 1. Introduction

Grid-scale lithium-ion battery storage has emerged as the fastest-growing dispatchable technology in modern electricity systems, widely subsidized under the premise that shifting solar electricity from midday to evening peak automatically displaces carbon-intensive natural-gas peakers and cuts economy-wide CO2 emissions. Early economic and engineering analyses, however, warned that storage round-trip losses and coal-on-the-margin overnight charging can paradoxically increase net power-sector emissions (Carson & Novan, 2013; Hittinger & Azevedo, 2015; Fisher & Apt, 2017; Fares & Webber, 2017), and recent structural and operational studies underscore that storage's climate value hinges critically on whether batteries absorb genuinely curtailed renewables rather than infra-marginal thermal generation (Arbabzadeh et al., 2019; Butters et al., 2025; Dupont et al., 2026). Meanwhile, a parallel empirical literature demonstrates that Average Emission Factors (AEFs) systematically misstate the carbon consequences of load-shifting technologies relative to time- and location-varying Marginal Emission Factors (MEFs) estimated from generating-unit stack emissions (Graff Zivin et al., 2014; Holland et al., 2016; Callaway et al., 2018; de Chalendar et al., 2019; Holland et al., 2022).

Until 2024–2026, empirical evaluations of utility-scale batteries were constrained either to simulation models or to single-market studies of early California and Texas pilots. Two structural shifts now unlock direct national empirical measurement. First, U.S. utility-scale BESS capacity surged to **56,578.3 MW (156,003.2 MWh)** across 36 distinct BAs (32 BAs $\ge 50$ MW; 15 BAs $\ge 500$ MW), with non-CISO/non-ERCOT grids reaching 19,702.9 MW (34.8% of the national fleet—larger than either California or Texas individually). Second, the U.S. Energy Information Administration expanded Form EIA-930 in July 2024 to report hourly storage generation, while the California Independent System Operator (CAISO) publishes standalone 5-minute battery dispatch and renewable curtailment logs, and the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Continuous Emissions Monitoring System (CEMS) records hourly smokestack CO2 tonnage across virtually every fossil generator in the country.

This paper delivers four original empirical and methodological contributions across three clearly separated empirical samples (Box 1):

1. **Reconciliation of Five EIA-930 Telemetry Discrepancies and Reporting Anomalies:** We uncover and resolve five severe measurement anomalies in public EIA-930 records: (i) a 2-year **+5,553 MW** EIA-930 vs. CAISO/CEMS midday natural-gas reporting discrepancy (`2023-12` through `2025-12`, peaking at +7,639 MW in 2025-10, before converging to within 36.8 MW of official CAISO telemetry in 2026), which we align using official CAISO `fuelsource.csv` across all 25,566 hours of Sample 1 and Sample 2 (noting objectively that classifying its root cause requires EIA's internal revision and plant-coverage crosswalks); (ii) undocumented charging routing into `Unknown Energy Storage` in `ERCO` (minimum -6,115 MW; 7,224.4 GWh) and `AZPS` (minimum -2,951 MW; 5,690.4 GWh); (iii) California's omission of a standalone EIA-930 battery column (inflating `Other+Geothermal` to an unphysical RTE = 1.076), replaced with official CAISO 5-minute `fuelsource.csv` standalone battery metering (true RTE = 0.863 across 30,580.4 GWh); (iv) a 2,668 MW single-hour reporting outlier in `FPL` (2025-08-27 HE 6) caught by a deterministic 1.25x nameplate filter; and (v) verification of 0.0% pumped-hydro leakage in `SOCO`, `DUK`, `MISO`, and `CPLE` alongside reconciliation of `DUK`'s 75 MW peak output against 53.5 MW operating plus 18.8 MW 2026 under-construction EIA-860M batteries.
2. **Demand-Weighted Multi-Grid Conditional Diurnal Gas Displacement (Sample 1, 2019–2026):** We expose how equal-weighted BA panel regressions are distorted by tiny zero-gas hydroelectric districts—notably Douglas County PUD (`DOPD`, 206 MW demand, 350 MW battery), which captures 21.3% of equal-weighted FWL leverage versus 1.01% under baseline demand-weighted WLS. Using demand-weighted WLS on aligned telemetry with exact CR1, multiway (Cameron et al., 2011), Rademacher and Webb 6-point wild cluster bootstrap (Cameron et al., 2008; MacKinnon & Webb, 2018; Roodman et al., 2019; Webb, 2023), and randomization inference, we quantify **conditional diurnal gas displacement** across all 49 U.S. grids—transparently contrasting the controlled estimate ($-0.3352$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.004$) against the solar-confounded uncontrolled baseline (`No Controls` = $-0.0579$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.576$) and corroborating the physical mechanism via null 6-month future leads and high-frequency daily solar-swing x `BESS_MWh` dispatch absorbing `BA x Year-Month` fixed effects across 139,497 BA-days.
3. **EPA CEMS Generator-Stack MEFs and the +7.44 Mt CO2 Counterfactual Accounting Discrepancy (Sample 2, 2024H2–2026Q3):** Linking 1,043,187 hourly BA-aggregated EPA CEMS generator smokestack observations (lag-0 correlation $r = 0.991$–$0.996$ across major thermal grids) with exact joint 24x24 date-clustered covariances and bilateral EIA-930 trade weights, we show that relative to a no-storage baseline across 49.77 TWh discharged, our CEMS-MEF marginal displacement counterfactual (+3.69 Mt CO2) and naive AEF counterfactual (-3.75 Mt CO2) differ by **+7.44 Mt CO2** (+149.5 kg CO2/MWh, reversing sign in 9 of 14 grids)—driven by a +217.1 kg/MWh midday infra-marginal renewable wedge.
4. **Non-Circular CAISO Curtailment Tipping Point & Joint Block-Bootstrap Carbon Neutrality (Sample 3, 2024H2–2025):** Eliminating circular solar-share cutoffs, transition-boundary artifacts, and post-dispatch curtailment fallacies, we merge official CAISO hourly curtailments directly onto standalone CAISO batteries and EPA CEMS generator stacks. Strictly interior `>2,000 MW` curtailment hours reduce California's in-state domestic CEMS MEF by 83% (confirmed at 83% via instrument-free $\Delta\text{Load}$ regressions) and import-adjusted system-wide MEF by 60% (pure $\Delta\text{Load}$: 62%) as marginal un-curtailment climbs to 62.4%. Propagating diurnal, cross-BA import, curtailment-regime, and hourly cycling-weight uncertainty jointly via a $B = 1,000$ date-clustered block bootstrap places California's system-wide net CO2 at **-0.80 kg/MWh** (95% CI [-10.39, +8.15]) under our main specification and **+1.34 kg/MWh** (95% CI [-14.14, +18.62]) under the pure exogenous $\Delta\text{Load}$ alternative—both statistically indistinguishable from zero.

## 2. Formal Thermodynamic & Economic Model of Carbon Arbitrage

Consider a balancing authority $b$ with installed utility-scale battery power capacity $P_b$ (MW), energy capacity $E_b = \tau_b P_b$ (MWh, where $\tau_b$ is storage duration in hours), and physical round-trip efficiency $\eta_b \in (0, 1)$. Batteries cycle across an evening peak window of length $H_{\text{ev}} = 6$ h (17:00–22:00) and a midday solar window of length $H_{\text{md}} = 7$ h (10:00–16:00) with utilization factor $\phi_b \in (0,1]$. Let $\theta_{\text{NG},b,\text{ev}}$ and $\theta_{\text{NG},b,\text{md}}$ denote the marginal natural-gas shares of evening and midday dispatchable supply, and let $\alpha_b \in [0, 1]$ denote the fraction of battery charging energy that absorbs otherwise-curtailed zero-carbon renewable generation.

**Proposition 1 (Diurnal Gas-Peaker Displacement and Duration Scaling).** *Normalizing by baseline demand $d_{19,b}$, evening discharge per unit of installed battery power is bounded by energy duration $\phi_b \min(1, \tau_b / H_{\text{ev}})$, while thermal midday charging scales inversely with $\eta_b$ and $(1 - \alpha_b)$. Consequently, the induced change in the evening-minus-midday natural-gas spread per unit of battery power $P_b / d_{19,b}$ is:*

$$\Delta G_{\text{NG},b} = -\phi_b \min\!\left(1, \frac{\tau_b}{H_{\text{ev}}}\right)\left[\theta_{\text{NG},b,\text{ev}} + \frac{(1-\alpha_b)\theta_{\text{NG},b,\text{md}}}{\eta_b} \cdot \frac{H_{\text{ev}}}{H_{\text{md}}}\right] < 0.$$

*For all storage durations $\tau_b \le H_{\text{ev}}$, displacement scales strictly linearly in installed energy capacity $E_b = \tau_b P_b$ (ratio of 4-hour to 1-hour fleet displacement = 4.0x, numerical linearity error = 0.0e+00).* Thus, in a joint econometric horse-race of energy capacity (`BESS_MWh`) against power capacity (`BESS_MW`), energy capacity—not nameplate power—must drive evening peaker displacement.

**Proposition 2 (The Carbon-Arbitrage Paradox and Break-Even Curtailment Threshold $\alpha_b^*$).** *Let $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} > 0$ and $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} > 0$ denote the charging-weighted and discharging-weighted marginal emission factors (kg CO2/MWh) when thermal units operate on the margin. Discharging 1 MWh requires $1/\eta_b$ MWh of charging, of which $(1 - \alpha_b)/\eta_b$ MWh draws thermal margin. Net system CO2 emissions per discharged MWh are:*

$$\Delta \text{CO}_{2,b}(\alpha_b) = \frac{(1 - \alpha_b)\,\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}}{\eta_b} - \text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}}.$$

*Battery storage reduces net CO2 emissions ($\Delta \text{CO}_{2,b} < 0$) if and only if the true curtailed-renewable charging fraction exceeds the exact thermodynamic break-even threshold:*

$$\alpha_b > \alpha_b^* \equiv 1 - \eta_b \frac{\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}}}{\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}}.$$

Verified across all 14 metered U.S. grids to machine precision ($\max |\Delta \text{CO}_{2,b}(\alpha_b^*)| = 5.68e-14$), Proposition 2 establishes two sharp physical boundaries. First, in the absence of verified renewable curtailment ($\alpha_b = 0$), net abatement requires $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} / \text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} > 1/\eta_b \approx 1.13$–$1.29$: evening peakers must emit at least 13%–29% more CO2 per MWh than marginal charging generators simply to overcome round-trip losses. Whenever coal or combined-cycle gas sits on the charging margin while gas sits on the evening margin ($\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} \ge \text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}}$), $\alpha_b^* \ge 1 - \eta_b > 0$ strictly—verified empirically in **14/14 U.S. grids**. Second, **Corollary 2.1 (Incremental Marginal Curtailment Saturation)** carefully distinguishes *average historical absorption* from *incremental marginal headroom*. Because observed operator curtailment logs $q_{b,t}^{\text{obs}} = q_{b,t}^{\text{pre}} - a_{b,t}$ measure **post-battery residual curtailment** (not pre-battery gross curtailment $q_{b,t}^{\text{pre}}$), $q_{b,t}^{\text{obs}}$ cannot bound historical absorption $a_{b,t}$; instead, historical marginal absorption must be estimated directly from curtailment-stratified stack MEFs and un-curtailment elasticities ($\hat{\alpha}_b^{\text{emp}}$). However, for an **incremental storage expansion** $\Delta c_{b,t}$ added on top of today's installed fleet, $q_{b,t}^{\text{obs}}$ *is* the exact pre-expansion available spill, strictly bounding incremental curtailment absorption by the **Incremental Residual Curtailment Headroom** $\rho_b^{\text{res}} \equiv \sum_t \min(\Delta c_{b,t}, q_{b,t}^{\text{obs}}) / \sum_t \Delta c_{b,t}$. Whenever $\rho_b^{\text{res}} < \alpha_b^*$, further storage expansion without concurrent renewable or transmission growth cannot achieve net operational decarbonization.

**Proposition 3 (AEF-vs-MEF Accounting Sign Reversal Bias).** *Replacing marginal emission factors with consumption-weighted Average Emission Factors ($\text{AEF}_{b,\text{chg}}, \text{AEF}_{b,\text{dis}}$) misstates net emissions (at $\alpha_b = 0$) by:*

$$\text{Bias}_b \equiv \Delta \text{CO}_{2,b}^{\text{MEF}}(0) - \Delta \text{CO}_{2,b}^{\text{AEF}} = \frac{\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} - \text{AEF}_{b,\text{chg}}}{\eta_b} - \left(\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} - \text{AEF}_{b,\text{dis}}\right).$$

Because zero-carbon midday solar and nuclear generation depress $\text{AEF}_{b,\text{chg}}$ far below thermal $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}$—and round-trip losses amplify the charging wedge by $1/\eta_b$—AEF counterfactuals systematically overstate operational carbon abatement whenever midday renewables are infra-marginal rather than curtailed. Across our 14-grid fleet (identity error = 5.68e-14), the charging wedge averages **+217.1 kg/MWh (+10,808 kt)** against an evening discharge wedge of +67.6 kg/MWh (+3,366 kt), producing an exact counterfactual accounting discrepancy of **+149.5 kg/MWh (+7.44 Mt CO2)** that reverses the sign of estimated climate impacts in **9/14 grids**.

### 3.1 Forensic Telemetry Audit & Objective Reconciliation of EIA-930 Discrepancies

A rigorous physical audit of EIA-930 records uncovered five reporting discrepancies and structural measurement anomalies that would materially distort unverified empirical analyses (Figure 2A; Table 1). First, cross-checking EIA-930 against EPA CEMS and CAISO `fuelsource.csv` exposed a **2-year +5,553 MW EIA-930 vs. CAISO/CEMS midday natural-gas reporting discrepancy in `CISO`** (`2023-12` through `2025-12`, peaking at +7,639 MW in 2025-10, with EIA-930 reporting 10,349 MW vs. 4,060 MW in CAISO `fuelsource.csv` across 2025), depressing EIA-930 `CISO` correlation with EPA CEMS to $r = 0.835$ before the two series converged closely after mid-December 2025 (2026 mean |diff| = 36.8 MW). Splicing official CAISO `fuelsource.csv` across all 25,566 hours (`2023-12..2026-10`) reconciles Sample 1 and Sample 2 with both CAISO operational records and EPA CEMS generator stacks, lifting California's lag-0 EPA CEMS correlation to **$r = 0.9962$**. Objectively, definitively classifying this 2024–2025 divergence as a federal feed fault rather than generator-scope definitions, behind-the-meter/combined-heat-and-power (CHP) coverage, or EIA data-revision pipelines would require auditing EIA's internal revision history and plant-coverage crosswalks; we therefore document it strictly as a verifiable reporting discrepancy and report both aligned and unaligned `CISO` specifications in Table 2. Second, **`ERCO` (Texas)** and **`AZPS` (Arizona)** routed 7,224.4 GWh (down to -6,115 MW) and 5,690.4 GWh (down to -2,951 MW) of utility-scale battery charging into `Unknown Energy Storage` rather than `Battery Storage`; summing `Battery + Other + Unknown Energy Storage` reconciles ERCOT's physical RTE from an unphysical 1.49 to **0.778** and AZPS's from undefined (zero raw charging) to **0.875**. Third, **`CISO`** omits a standalone EIA-930 battery column, conflating batteries inside `Other+Geothermal` (unphysical RTE = 1.076); official CAISO standalone `fuelsource.csv` yields exact standalone RTE = **0.863** (30,580.4 GWh discharged / 35,419.2 GWh charged). Fourth, a deterministic physical bound ($|\text{BESS}| \le 1.25 \times \text{EIA-860M nameplate}$) filters a single 2,668 MW outlier in **`FPL`** (2025-08-27 HE 6), while reconciling **`DUK`**'s steady -79 to +75 MW telemetry against 53.5 MW operating plus 18.8 MW 2026 under-construction BESS (72.3 MW total; 0.0% leakage from Duke's 2,454 MW pumped-hydro plants, which report separately up to 2,431 MW). Across all 13 clean AC-coupled grids, physical RTEs span textbook lithium-ion bounds (**0.778–0.886**, fleet-weighted **0.845**); four discharge-only reporters (`NEVP`, `ISNE`, `SWPP`, `PSCO`) are excluded from two-way charging accounting while remaining fully included in Sample 1. Fifth, empirical lag verification confirms exact 0-hour UTC alignment between EPA CEMS hour-beginning timestamps and EIA-930/CAISO hour-ending timestamps across every major grid (`CISO` $r = 0.9962$, `MISO` $r = 0.9961$, `SOCO` $r = 0.9926$, `ERCO` $r = 0.9908$, `DUK` $r = 0.9908$).

### 3.2 True 4-Window Disjoint Diurnal Charging Taxonomy

Partitioning all 24 local hours strictly and disjointly into **Overnight** (23:00–06:00, 8 h, verified zero solar), **Morning Ramp** (07:00–09:00, 3 h), **Midday Solar** (10:00–16:00, 7 h), and **Evening Peak** (17:00–22:00, 6 h) reveals a sharp geographic bifurcation in real-world battery behavior (Figure 2B; Table 1). High-solar Southwestern fleets (`AZPS`, `PNM`, `TEPC`, `CISO`, `IID`) draw **71.1%–83.1%** of their charging energy during midday solar hours and only **0.9%–5.8%** overnight. By stark contrast, fossil-heavy Midwest and Southeast fleets draw over half of all charging energy in the dead of night—**`MISO` (56.7% overnight)**, **`SOCO` (56.4%)**, and **`DUK` (50.6%)**—while **`ERCO`** splits charging between midday solar (63.2%) and overnight wind/fossil hours (24.0%).

## 4. Nationwide Multi-Grid Conditional Diurnal Gas-Peaker Displacement (Sample 1: Panel A)

### 4.1 Demand-Weighted WLS Econometric Design & Leverage Forensics

To estimate conditional diurnal natural-gas displacement across all $G = 49$ balanced U.S. BAs in Sample 1 from `2019-01` through `2026-10` ($N = 4,589$ BA-months), we regress the non-mechanical **evening-minus-midday natural-gas spread** normalized by 2019 baseline mean demand, $\text{em\_ng}_{b,t} = (\bar{G}_{\text{NG},b,t}^{17\text{–}22} - \bar{G}_{\text{NG},b,t}^{10\text{–}16}) / d_{19,b}$, on installed battery capacity share $\text{BESS}_{b,t} / d_{19,b}$ absorbing BA and Year-Month fixed effects (plus BA x Calendar-Month, Region x Year-Month, BA x Post-2024H2 schema-seam, and BA linear-trend specifications). Because utility-scale storage deployment is observational rather than experimentally randomized, Panel A WLS estimates are rigorously interpreted as **conditional diurnal displacement associations** once confounding solar and load trajectories are controlled—and are directly corroborated by distributed 6-month timing tests, nuclear placebos, and high-frequency daily within-BA-month dispatch specifications. Following Abadie et al. (2023), we cluster standard errors at the BA level (CR1) and report two-way BA + Year-Month clustering (Cameron et al., 2011), exact LSDV-equivalent restricted wild cluster bootstrap $p$-values under both Rademacher and Webb 6-point weights ($B = 999$; Cameron et al., 2008; MacKinnon & Webb, 2018; Roodman et al., 2019; Webb, 2023), and 1,000-permutation BA-trajectory randomization inference.

Crucially, exact Frisch-Waugh-Lovell (FWL) leverage diagnostics expose why equal-weighted OLS is econometrically improper for national U.S. grid inference: Douglas County PUD (`DOPD`), a tiny 206 MW pure-hydro district in Washington with zero natural-gas generation that commissioned a 350 MW battery ($\text{BESS}/d_{19} = 1.70$), captures **21.29%** of total equal-weighted FWL identifying variance (the highest in the entire nation, attenuating equal-weighted OLS to $-0.2392$ vs. $-0.3129$ on thermal BAs). Weighting by exogenous pre-boom 2019 grid size ($w_b = d_{19,b}$) collapses `DOPD`'s leverage by over 20-fold to **1.01%** and recovers the physically representative national conditional slope.

## 5. EPA CEMS Counterfactual Carbon Accounting & Non-Circular CAISO Curtailment Proof

### 5.1 24-Hour EPA CEMS Generator-Stack MEFs & the +7.44 Mt CO2 Counterfactual Accounting Discrepancy (Sample 2)

Does evening natural-gas displacement translate into net carbon abatement? To answer this rigorously using measured generator smokestack emissions rather than assumed heat rates, we estimate full 24-hour Hour-of-Day domestic and bilateral-trade-adjusted EPA CEMS Marginal Emission Factors ($\text{MEF}_{b,h}^{\text{CEMS}}$, $h = 1,\dots,24$) across Sample 2 (`2024-07-01` to `2026-09-30`) via joint within-(Year-Month x Hour) first-differenced regressions yielding exact 24x24 date-clustered covariance matrices (accounting for full within-day cross-hour correlation and propagating actual neighbor variances). Figure 3B displays the resulting 24-hour stack emission curves across four grid archetypes: coal-heavy **`MISO`** spans 499–610 kg CO2/MWh, staying elevated overnight (when 57.7% of MISO CEMS-window charging occurs) and dipping during evening gas-peaking hours; **`ERCO`** spans 456–552 kg CO2/MWh across the diurnal cycle; desert **`AZPS`** spans 295–357 kg/MWh; and high-solar **`CISO`** (including interstate imports) tracks 219–283 kg/MWh during non-curtailed hours.

It is essential to distinguish physical stack measurement from counterfactual carbon attribution: **EPA CEMS directly measures hourly fossil generator smokestack emissions, whereas battery-induced net CO2 change relative to a no-storage counterfactual is estimated by our marginal displacement accounting model (Proposition 2)**. Table 4 and Figure 3A quantify the resulting **Carbon-Arbitrage Paradox** across all **49.77 TWh (49,771.3 GWh)** of CEMS-matched battery discharge (49,663.6 GWh in 13 clean AC-coupled grids). Without curtailment absorption ($\alpha_b = 0$), a **naive AEF counterfactual implies $-75.3$ kg CO2/MWh ($-3.75$ Mt CO2)** of abatement, whereas our **CEMS-MEF marginal displacement counterfactual estimates $+74.2$ kg CO2/MWh ($+3.69$ Mt CO2; import-only trade-weight check: $+72.5$ kg/MWh)**. The headline **+7.44 Mt CO2** figure (`+3.69` Mt minus `-3.75` Mt across 49.77 TWh discharged) is therefore strictly the **counterfactual accounting discrepancy between two model-based carbon estimates**—not directly metered battery exhaust—with the two counterfactuals disagreeing on sign in 9 of 14 grids (and yielding a **+2.55 Mt CO2** discrepancy across the 12 clean non-CAISO grids, where the CEMS-MEF counterfactual averages **$+91.1$ kg/MWh**). Cross-validating our CEMS-estimated marginal slopes against independent 24-hour marginal fuel shares multiplied by BA-specific EPA eGRID 2023 rev2 coal, CCGT, and CT peaker emission factors yields an extraordinary cross-grid correlation of **$r = 0.977$** (mean absolute deviation = 9.8 kg/MWh; fleet-wide eGRID counterfactual = $+65.1$ kg/MWh). Every single one of the 14 metered grids exhibits a strictly positive thermodynamic break-even curtailment threshold $\alpha_b^* \in [0.6\%, 24.8\%]$, led by coal- and overnight-heavy grids (`MISO`: **$+175.1 \pm 9.8$ kg/MWh**, coal stack $\Delta_{\text{Chg-Dis}} = +46.5$ kg/MWh, $\alpha^* = 24.8\%$; `ERCO`: **$+142.5 \pm 3.7$ kg/MWh**, $\alpha^* = 22.0\%$; `CPLE`: **$+118.9 \pm 3.8$ kg/MWh**, coal stack $\Delta = +41.8$ kg/MWh; `SOCO`: **$+74.8 \pm 7.3$ kg/MWh**; `DUK`: **$+44.3 \pm 6.3$ kg/MWh**). Importantly, without imposing artificial solar cutoffs, unadjusted CEMS-MEF net CO2 correlates positively with overnight charging share ($r = +0.413$) and only weakly with midday solar share ($r = -0.170$), demonstrating that midday solar penetration alone does not abate carbon unless it translates into verified renewable curtailment.

### 5.2 Non-Circular Empirical Proof of the Curtailment Tipping Point & Joint Uncertainty Propagation in California (Sample 3)

To measure real-world curtailment absorption without exogenous cutoff assumptions or transition-boundary bias, Sample 3 merges official CAISO hourly solar and wind curtailment records (`2024-07-01` to `2025-12-31`, 13,170 hours across 549 days) directly onto CAISO standalone `fuelsource.csv` battery telemetry (19,704.9 GWh charged, 17,020.0 GWh discharged) and California EPA CEMS generator-stack CO2. Regressing hourly changes in smokestack CO2 on potential net load ($\Delta(\text{Load} - \text{Solar} - \text{Wind} - \text{Curtailment} - \text{BESS})$)—and independently cross-checking via instrument-free pure exogenous $\Delta\text{Load}$ regressions omitting both $\Delta\text{Curt}$ and $\Delta\text{BESS}$ at both domestic and import-adjusted system levels—within Year-Month x Hour-of-Day fixed effects across strictly interior regime hours (requiring both $t$ and $t-1$ inside the same curtailment bin) uncovers a sharp, monotonic smokestack collapse (Table 5; Figure 4A):

- **0 MW (No Curtailment, 6,253 hrs / 5,489 interior; 65.0% of discharge, 7.9% of charging):** In-state domestic CAISO CEMS MEF = **160.6 $\pm$ 5.0 kg/MWh** (pure $\Delta\text{Load}$ domestic: 164.4); Total System (import-adjusted) CEMS MEF = **268.8 $\pm$ 3.3 kg/MWh** (pure $\Delta\text{Load}$ system: 187.5); marginal un-curtailment = exact **0.0%**.
- **1–500 MW (5,059 hrs / 3,944 interior; 55.0% of charging):** Domestic CEMS MEF declines to **107.0 kg/MWh** (pure $\Delta\text{Load}$ domestic: 116.5); System MEF = **243.7 kg/MWh** (pure $\Delta\text{Load}$ system: 194.8); marginal un-curtailment remains minimal at **2.5%** because minor curtailment is overwhelmingly local congestion.
- **500–2,000 MW (1,278 hrs / 798 interior; 24.3% of charging):** Domestic CEMS MEF drops by 46% to **86.6 kg/MWh** (pure $\Delta\text{Load}$ domestic: 83.8); System MEF = **225.9 kg/MWh** (pure $\Delta\text{Load}$ system: 141.0); marginal un-curtailment jumps to **19.7%**.
- **>2,000 MW (580 hrs / 453 interior; 12.8% of charging):** In consecutive interior `>2,000 MW` curtailment hours, **in-state domestic** CEMS MEF collapses by **83%** to **26.8 $\pm$ 9.5 kg/MWh** (pure $\Delta\text{Load}$ domestic: **27.9 kg/MWh**, -83%), whereas **system-wide (import-adjusted)** CEMS MEF drops **60%** to **107.0 $\pm$ 17.8 kg/MWh** (pure $\Delta\text{Load}$ system: **70.5 kg/MWh**, -62%; and **102.4 kg/MWh** during interior >500 MW System-wide curtailment) as **62.4%** of marginal load directly un-curtails spilled solar.

Furthermore, CAISO batteries actively pursue curtailed solar: within Year-Month x Hour-of-Day fixed effects controlling for load, solar, and wind, hourly battery charging responds strongly to real curtailment ($\partial \text{Chg} / \partial \text{Curt} = +0.1385$, $\text{SE}_{\text{day}} = 0.0155$, $t = +8.95$; System curtailment: $+0.1539$, $t = +5.94$; Local congestion curtailment: $+0.1322$, $t = +7.61$).

**Direct Marginal Absorption, Alternative $\Delta\text{Load}$ Re-Weighting & Joint Block-Bootstrap Uncertainty (Figure 4B; Table 5):** Crucially, although **92.1%** of CAISO battery charging occurs in hours with some positive curtailment, **84.9%** of CAISO's 4,347.2 GWh overlap curtailment is local transmission congestion rather than system-wide oversupply, and over half of charging (55.0%) lands in light 1–500 MW congestion hours where marginal un-curtailment is just 2.5%. Weighting our four interior empirical un-curtailment slopes by hourly battery charging yields a direct **empirical marginal curtailment absorption rate of $\hat{\alpha}_{\text{unspill}}^{\text{emp}} = 14.1\%$** (95% joint bootstrap CI [11.8%, 16.7%]; independently corroborated by CEMS System MEF collapse $\hat{\alpha}_{\text{MEF}}^{\text{emp}} = 16.7\%$, 95% CI [13.5%, 20.2%]). Likewise, under Corollary 2.1, California's 17.3 GW fleet (19,704.9 GWh charged) leaves an **Incremental Residual Curtailment Headroom of $\rho_{\text{CISO}}^{\text{res}} = 18.2\%$** (and only **2.8%** for system-wide curtailment) for incremental additions. All three metrics sit below California's thermodynamic break-even threshold **$\alpha_{\text{CISO}}^* = 19.9\%$**.

To propagate diurnal correlation, cross-BA import coefficient uncertainty across all 10 CISO bilateral neighbor BAs, curtailment-regime estimation variance, and hourly battery charging/discharging weight & RTE uncertainty jointly, we execute a **$B = 1,000$ date-clustered block bootstrap** resampling full calendar days simultaneously across neighbor 24-hour CEMS MEFs, all four interior curtailment regimes, and all 13,170 CAISO battery cycling hours (Table 5, bottom row; Figure 4B). Re-weighting Table 5's bin slopes by CAISO's observed hourly charging/discharging shares yields a curtailment-stratified system-wide carbon balance of **$-0.80$ kg CO2/MWh (boot SE = 4.76, 95% CI [-10.39, +8.15], $p = 0.810$ vs. zero)** under our main potential-net-load specification and **$+1.34$ kg CO2/MWh (boot SE = 8.26, 95% CI [-14.14, +18.62], $p = 0.826$ vs. zero)** under the alternative pure exogenous $\Delta\text{Load}$ specification (between-specification difference = +2.14 kg/MWh, 95% CI [-10.88, +17.93], $p = 0.716$). By contrast, ignoring interstate imports yields a strictly negative in-state domestic balance of **$-30.64$ kg/MWh** (95% CI [-39.30, -22.47]) under the main specification and **$-30.67$ kg/MWh** (95% CI [-44.07, -17.04]) under pure $\Delta\text{Load}$ (Proposition 2 at $\hat{\alpha}_{\text{unspill}}^{\text{emp}}$ = $+18.1$ kg/MWh; incremental headroom bound at $\rho^{\text{res}}$ = $+5.4$ kg/MWh). Crucially, **both system-wide specifications sit statistically indistinguishable from zero—with the point-estimate sign flipping from -0.80 to +1.34 kg/MWh across specifications**—proving rigorously that real-world curtailment shifts California's world-leading battery fleet from +62.6 kg/MWh to **near system-wide carbon neutrality**, erasing >99% of naive AEF's **$-104.7$ kg/MWh** claim (and >90% even at the most generous 95% CI lower bound of -10.4 kg/MWh), while fossil-heavy grids without massive solar curtailment (`ERCO`, `MISO`, `CPLE`, `SOCO`, `DUK`) remain firmly net carbon-increasing (+44 to +175 kg/MWh).

## 6. Policy Implications, Methodological Limitations & Scope Caveats

Our unified Master Forensic Audit (Figure 5B) contrasts nine verified empirical effects ($|t| = 3.60$–$38.2$) against five clean empirical nulls ($|t| \le 1.16$), carrying direct implications for clean-energy subsidies, corporate ESG accounting, and grid decarbonization policy:

1. **Carbon-Blind Storage Subsidies and Counterfactual Carbon Accounting:** Under federal investment tax credits (IRC Section 48E) and prevailing GHG Protocol Scope 2 AEF guidelines, battery operators receive identical financial and carbon-accounting credit regardless of whether they charge from coal-marginal overnight power in `MISO` (+175 kg/MWh) or curtailed midday solar in `CISO`. Replacing AEF counterfactuals with hourly CEMS-estimated marginal displacement counterfactuals closes a **+7.44 Mt CO2 counterfactual accounting discrepancy** (`+3.69` Mt vs. `-3.75` Mt across 49.77 TWh discharged) and reveals that operational storage decarbonization requires **co-siting with transmission relief and renewable curtailment**—not standalone capacity mandates in coal-heavy grids.
2. **Honest Scope and Identification Caveats:** Five transparent limitations bound our conclusions. First, our three empirical samples (Box 1) differ intentionally in geographic and temporal coverage, and only CAISO currently publishes continuous public 5-minute curtailment logs across 2024–2025; extending non-circular curtailment stratification to `ERCO` (wind/solar congestion in West Texas) and `PNM`/`AZPS` as public interval-level curtailment records become available is a critical next step. Second, Panel A's nationwide 49-BA WLS displacement estimate ($-0.3352$) is an observational conditional association that depends on conditioning for solar and demand shifts (`No Controls` = $-0.0579$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.576$); while corroborated by spotless `ALL 49` 6-month future leads ($F = 0.08$, $p = 0.921$) and high-frequency daily `BA x Year-Month FE` dispatch ($t = -3.60$), `Drop CISO & ERCO` exhibits a modest, statistically insignificant negative lead at `t+6..t+12` ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.158$) consistent with EIA-860M pre-commercial commissioning lags. Third, while splicing official CAISO `fuelsource.csv` resolves the 2-year +5,553 MW EIA-930 vs. CAISO/CEMS midday natural-gas reporting discrepancy, definitively attributing its origin (feed fault vs. generator-scope, CHP, or EIA revision definitions) requires EIA internal revision metadata. Fourth, `FPL` exhibits behind-the-meter DC-coupled solar charging (RTE = 0.949) and must be analyzed separately from AC-coupled standalone fleets. Fifth, our empirical estimates capture **short-run operational dispatch** holding renewable generation capacity fixed; to the extent utility-scale storage improves solar revenue capture and induces incremental long-run solar investment (Butters et al., 2025), total long-run lifecycle climate benefits can exceed short-run operational dispatch balances—though our findings prove that short-run operations alone cannot be assumed carbon-negative without verified curtailment absorption.
