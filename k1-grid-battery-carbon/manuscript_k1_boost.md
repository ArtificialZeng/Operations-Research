# Counterfactual Carbon Accounting and Diurnal Gas-Peaker Displacement of U.S. Utility-Scale Battery Storage (2019–2026)

**Target Format:** *Nature Energy* (Analysis) | Secondary Venues: *Environmental Science & Technology* / *Nature Communications* / *JEEM*  
**Data Universe:** Direct Hourly EPA CEMS Smokestack CO2 (1,043,187 BA-hours, 99.94% plant-to-BA CO2 match rate); EPA eGRID 2023 rev2; Official Standalone CAISO 5-Minute Telemetry (`fuelsource.csv`, 1066/1066 days, 0 missing); Official CAISO Hourly Renewable Curtailments (2024–2025, 4,347.2 GWh overlap); EIA-930 Hourly Grid Operations (4,285,392 total rows; 3,359,080 hourly rows across $G = 49$ balanced U.S. Balancing Authorities, 2019-01 to 2026-10); EIA-860M Monthly Fleet (56,578.3 MW / 156,003.2 MWh across 36 BAs).

## Abstract

Utility-scale battery energy storage systems (BESS) reached **56,578.3 MW (156,003.2 MWh; mean duration 2.76 h)** across 36 U.S. Balancing Authorities (BAs) by October 2026, yet real-world operational carbon accounting for storage remains widely contested. Evaluating the same reconciled physical charging and discharging records under competing emission-factor frameworks across three delineated samples—a core 14-BA carbon-accounting panel (`2024H2–2026Q3`, **49.77 TWh** discharged; 52,816.8 GWh full telemetry), a California curtailment subsample (`2024H2–2025`, 13,170 hrs), and a 49-BA operational dispatch panel (`2019–2026`, 3,359,080 hrs)—we document a directional divergence in counterfactual carbon estimates. First, linking metered storage dispatch to hourly fossil generator smokestack emissions from the EPA Continuous Emissions Monitoring System (CEMS; 3,506.3 Mt CO2), **Average Emission Factors (AEFs) and CEMS-estimated Marginal Emission Factors (MEFs) yield opposite signs for battery carbon impacts on the identical 49.77 TWh dispatch record**. Without curtailment absorption ($\alpha = 0$), an AEF counterfactual implies **$-75.3$ kg/MWh ($-3.75$ Mt CO2)** of abatement, whereas a CEMS-MEF marginal displacement counterfactual estimates **$+74.2$ kg/MWh ($+3.69$ Mt CO2)**—a **+7.44 Mt CO2 counterfactual accounting discrepancy** (`+3.69` Mt minus `-3.75` Mt; 9/14 grids reverse sign; EPA eGRID 2023 rev2 $r = 0.977$). Curtailment sensitivity confirms this accounting gap persists even with substantial curtailment absorption: applying California's curtailment-adjusted estimate to `CISO` and allowing 0%–15% absorption across all other 13 grids leaves a **+5.59 to +4.04 Mt CO2** discrepancy relative to AEF. Second, California's public curtailment archive illustrates how renewable curtailment and cross-region imports reshape marginal estimates. In consecutive interior `>2,000 MW` curtailment hours, California's **in-state domestic** CEMS MEF falls **83%** (160.6 to 26.8 kg/MWh) and **import-adjusted system-wide** CEMS MEF falls **60%** (268.8 to 107.0 kg/MWh), while charging responds to curtailment ($\partial\text{Chg}/\partial\text{Curt} = +0.138$, $t = +8.95$). Yet because 84.9% of curtailment reflects local transmission congestion, empirical marginal absorption ($\hat{\alpha}_{\text{unspill}}^{\text{emp}} = 14.1\%$) and incremental residual headroom ($\rho^{\text{res}} = 18.2\%$; system-only 2.8%) sit below $\alpha_{\text{CISO}}^* = 19.9\%$. Propagating diurnal, neighbor-import, curtailment-regime, and cycling-weight uncertainty via a conservative **$B = 1,000$ 14-day contiguous block bootstrap** places CAISO system-wide net CO2 at **$-0.80$ kg/MWh (14d 95% CI [-14.20, +12.58]; 7d [-13.24, +9.83]; 1d [-10.39, +8.15])** under our main specification and **$+1.34$ kg/MWh (14d 95% CI [-21.86, +30.51])** under an alternative observed-load ($\Delta\text{Load}$) specification, whereas in-state domestic estimates remain strictly negative (**$-30.64$ kg/MWh**, 14d 95% CI [-42.78, -17.48]). Thus, **accounting for cross-region imports, California's net emissions direction remains undetermined**, while naive AEF's **$-104.7$ kg/MWh** abatement claim is cleanly rejected and coal- or gas-marginal grids (`ERCO`, `MISO`, `CPLE`, `SOCO`, `DUK`) remain net carbon-increasing (+44 to +175 kg/MWh). Third, as operational dispatch context across 49 BAs after reconciling five EIA-930 telemetry discrepancies (including a 2-year **+5,553 MW** EIA-930 vs. CAISO/CEMS midday gas reporting discrepancy), demand-weighted WLS shows **conditional diurnal natural-gas displacement** (**$-0.3352$**, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.004$; Drop CISO/ERCO **$-0.3810$**; Nuclear placebo **$-0.0005$**), contrasted transparently against the solar-confounded uncontrolled baseline (**No Controls = $-0.0579$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.576$**)—confirming that evening gas displacement alone does not guarantee net operational carbon abatement.

## 1. Introduction

Grid-scale lithium-ion battery storage is expanding rapidly across electricity markets under policies that assume shifting midday solar power to evening peaks reliably cuts power-sector CO2 emissions. Prior modeling and simulation studies demonstrated that round-trip energy losses and coal-marginal overnight charging can instead raise operational emissions (Carson & Novan, 2013; Hittinger & Azevedo, 2015; Fisher & Apt, 2017; Fares & Webber, 2017), while recent operational and equilibrium analyses emphasize that storage's climate value hinges on absorbing genuinely curtailed renewables and long-run investment feedback (Arbabzadeh et al., 2019; Butters et al., 2025; Dupont et al., 2026). In parallel, an extensive empirical literature shows that Average Emission Factors (AEFs) diverge systematically from Marginal Emission Factors (MEFs) estimated from measured generator stacks when evaluating load-shifting technologies (Graff Zivin et al., 2014; Holland et al., 2016; Callaway et al., 2018; de Chalendar et al., 2019; Holland et al., 2022).

Because utility-scale storage remained small until recently, prior work relied primarily on engineering dispatch simulations, theoretical models, or early single-market observations. Two developments now permit a direct multi-grid empirical accounting comparison using observed commercial dispatch. First, U.S. utility-scale BESS capacity reached **56,578.3 MW (156,003.2 MWh)** across 36 BAs by October 2026 (19,702.9 MW, or 34.8%, outside California and Texas). Second, Form EIA-930 began reporting hourly storage dispatch in July 2024 alongside CAISO's standalone 5-minute battery and curtailment logs and hourly EPA CEMS smokestack CO2 records covering virtually all U.S. fossil generators.

Rather than claiming the theoretical possibility of storage-induced emissions increases—which prior literature established—this Analysis makes a focused empirical and methodological contribution centered on **counterfactual carbon accounting from commercial storage operations** across three delineated samples (Box 1):

1. **Fleet-Wide AEF-vs-CEMS-MEF Counterfactual Accounting Comparison (Centerpiece — Sample 2, 2024H2–2026Q3):** Linking 1,043,187 hourly BA-aggregated EPA CEMS generator smokestack observations (lag-0 correlation $r = 0.991$–$0.996$ across major thermal grids) with exact joint 24x24 date-clustered covariances and bilateral EIA-930 trade weights across 49.77 TWh of actual battery discharge in 14 grids, we show that AEF (-3.75 Mt CO2) and CEMS-MEF (+3.69 Mt CO2) counterfactuals disagree by **+7.44 Mt CO2** (+149.5 kg/MWh) and reverse sign in 9/14 grids—a divergence driven by a +217.1 kg/MWh midday infra-marginal renewable wedge that persists across curtailment-absorption sensitivity scenarios (Table 4b).
2. **Curtailment, Cross-Region Imports, and Saturation in California (Sample 3, 2024H2–2025):** Merging official CAISO hourly curtailments directly onto standalone CAISO batteries and EPA CEMS stacks illustrates how real curtailment and interstate imports shift marginal estimates. Consecutive interior `>2,000 MW` curtailment hours lower California's in-state domestic CEMS MEF by 83% (observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$: 83%) and import-adjusted system MEF by 60% (observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$: 62%) as marginal un-curtailment climbs to 62.4%. Yet local transmission congestion (84.9% of curtailment) constrains absorption and creates rapid diminishing incremental headroom as fleet size expands. Under conservative $B = 1,000$ 14-day contiguous block bootstraps, CAISO system-wide net CO2 spans zero across both main (**-0.80 kg/MWh**, 14d 95% CI [-14.20, +12.58]) and alternative observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$ (**+1.34 kg/MWh**, 14d 95% CI [-21.86, +30.51]) specifications—establishing that **once cross-region imports are included, the net emissions direction remains undetermined**, while decisively excluding naive AEF's -104.7 kg/MWh abatement claim.
3. **Reconciliation of Five EIA-930 Telemetry Discrepancies:** We document and reconcile five measurement anomalies in public EIA-930 records: (i) a 2-year **+5,553 MW** EIA-930 vs. CAISO/CEMS midday natural-gas reporting discrepancy (`2023-12` through `2025-12`, peaking at +7,639 MW in 2025-10, before converging to 36.8 MW in 2026), aligned via official CAISO `fuelsource.csv` across 25,566 hours; (ii) charging routed into `Unknown Energy Storage` in `ERCO` (7,224.4 GWh) and `AZPS` (5,690.4 GWh); (iii) California's omission of a standalone EIA-930 battery column (RTE = 1.076 vs. standalone 0.863); (iv) a 2,668 MW single-hour outlier in `FPL` (2025-08-27 HE 6); and (v) verification of 0.0% pumped-hydro leakage in `SOCO`, `DUK`, `MISO`, and `CPLE` alongside `DUK`'s 75 MW output reconciliation.
4. **Operational Dispatch Context Across 49 U.S. Grids (Sample 1, 2019–2026):** As supporting operational context explaining why gas displacement and carbon accounting diverge, we quantify **conditional diurnal natural-gas displacement** using baseline demand-weighted WLS (eliminating `DOPD`'s 21.3% equal-weighted FWL leverage distortion), contrasting the controlled slope ($-0.3352$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.004$) transparently against the uncontrolled baseline (`No Controls` = $-0.0579$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.576$) and reporting both CR1 and finite-cluster wild bootstrap inference across timing and daily dispatch checks.

## 2. Formal Thermodynamic & Economic Model of Carbon Arbitrage

Consider a balancing authority $b$ with installed utility-scale battery power capacity $P_b$ (MW), energy capacity $E_b = \tau_b P_b$ (MWh, where $\tau_b$ is storage duration in hours), and physical round-trip efficiency $\eta_b \in (0, 1)$. Batteries cycle across an evening peak window of length $H_{\text{ev}} = 6$ h (17:00–22:00) and a midday solar window of length $H_{\text{md}} = 7$ h (10:00–16:00) with utilization factor $\phi_b \in (0,1]$. Let $\theta_{\text{NG},b,\text{ev}}$ and $\theta_{\text{NG},b,\text{md}}$ denote the marginal natural-gas shares of evening and midday dispatchable supply, and let $\alpha_b \in [0, 1]$ denote the fraction of battery charging energy that absorbs otherwise-curtailed zero-carbon renewable generation.

**Proposition 1 (Diurnal Gas-Peaker Displacement and Duration Scaling).** *Normalizing by baseline demand $d_{19,b}$, evening discharge per unit of installed battery power is bounded by energy duration $\phi_b \min(1, \tau_b / H_{\text{ev}})$, while thermal midday charging scales inversely with $\eta_b$ and $(1 - \alpha_b)$. Consequently, the induced change in the evening-minus-midday natural-gas spread per unit of battery power $P_b / d_{19,b}$ is:*

$$\Delta G_{\text{NG},b} = -\phi_b \min\!\left(1, \frac{\tau_b}{H_{\text{ev}}}\right)\left[\theta_{\text{NG},b,\text{ev}} + \frac{(1-\alpha_b)\theta_{\text{NG},b,\text{md}}}{\eta_b} \cdot \frac{H_{\text{ev}}}{H_{\text{md}}}\right] < 0.$$

*For all storage durations $\tau_b \le H_{\text{ev}}$, displacement scales strictly linearly in installed energy capacity $E_b = \tau_b P_b$ (ratio of 4-hour to 1-hour fleet displacement = 4.0x, numerical linearity error = 0.0e+00).* Thus, in a joint econometric horse-race of energy capacity (`BESS_MWh`) against power capacity (`BESS_MW`), energy capacity—not nameplate power—must drive evening peaker displacement.

**Proposition 2 (The Carbon-Arbitrage Paradox and Break-Even Curtailment Threshold $\alpha_b^*$).** *Let $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} > 0$ and $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} > 0$ denote the charging-weighted and discharging-weighted marginal emission factors (kg CO2/MWh) when thermal units operate on the margin. Discharging 1 MWh requires $1/\eta_b$ MWh of charging, of which $(1 - \alpha_b)/\eta_b$ MWh draws thermal margin. Net system CO2 emissions per discharged MWh are:*

$$\Delta \text{CO}_{2,b}(\alpha_b) = \frac{(1 - \alpha_b)\,\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}}{\eta_b} - \text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}}.$$

*Battery storage reduces net CO2 emissions ($\Delta \text{CO}_{2,b} < 0$) if and only if the true curtailed-renewable charging fraction exceeds the exact thermodynamic break-even threshold:*

$$\alpha_b > \alpha_b^* \equiv 1 - \eta_b \frac{\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}}}{\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}}.$$

Verified across all 14 metered U.S. grids to machine precision ($\max |\Delta \text{CO}_{2,b}(\alpha_b^*)| = 5.68e-14$), Proposition 2 establishes two sharp physical boundaries. First, in the absence of verified renewable curtailment ($\alpha_b = 0$), net abatement requires $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} / \text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} > 1/\eta_b \approx 1.13$–$1.29$: evening peakers must emit at least 13%–29% more CO2 per MWh than marginal charging generators simply to overcome round-trip losses. Whenever coal or combined-cycle gas sits on the charging margin while gas sits on the evening margin ($\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} \ge \text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}}$), $\alpha_b^* \ge 1 - \eta_b > 0$ strictly—verified empirically in **14/14 U.S. grids**. Second, **Corollary 2.1 (Incremental Marginal Curtailment Saturation)** carefully distinguishes *average historical absorption* from *incremental marginal headroom*. Because observed operator curtailment logs $q_{b,t}^{\text{obs}} = q_{b,t}^{\text{pre}} - a_{b,t}$ measure **post-battery residual curtailment** (not pre-battery gross curtailment $q_{b,t}^{\text{pre}}$), $q_{b,t}^{\text{obs}}$ cannot bound historical absorption $a_{b,t}$; instead, historical marginal absorption must be estimated directly from curtailment-stratified stack MEFs and un-curtailment elasticities ($\hat{\alpha}_b^{\text{emp}}$). However, for an **incremental storage expansion** $\Delta c_{b,t}$ added on top of today's installed fleet, $q_{b,t}^{\text{obs}}$ *is* the exact pre-expansion available spill, strictly bounding incremental curtailment absorption by the **Incremental Residual Curtailment Headroom** $\rho_b^{\text{res}} \equiv \sum_t \min(\Delta c_{b,t}, q_{b,t}^{\text{obs}}) / \sum_t \Delta c_{b,t}$. Whenever $\rho_b^{\text{res}} < \alpha_b^*$, further storage expansion without concurrent renewable or transmission growth cannot achieve net operational decarbonization.

**Proposition 3 (AEF-vs-MEF Accounting Sign Reversal Bias).** *Replacing marginal emission factors with consumption-weighted Average Emission Factors ($\text{AEF}_{b,\text{chg}}, \text{AEF}_{b,\text{dis}}$) misstates net emissions (at $\alpha_b = 0$) by:*

$$\text{Bias}_b \equiv \Delta \text{CO}_{2,b}^{\text{MEF}}(0) - \Delta \text{CO}_{2,b}^{\text{AEF}} = \frac{\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} - \text{AEF}_{b,\text{chg}}}{\eta_b} - \left(\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} - \text{AEF}_{b,\text{dis}}\right).$$

Because zero-carbon midday solar and nuclear generation depress $\text{AEF}_{b,\text{chg}}$ far below thermal $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}$—and round-trip losses amplify the charging wedge by $1/\eta_b$—AEF counterfactuals systematically overstate operational carbon abatement whenever midday renewables are infra-marginal rather than curtailed. Across our 14-grid fleet (identity error = 5.68e-14), the charging wedge averages **+217.1 kg/MWh (+10,808 kt)** against an evening discharge wedge of +67.6 kg/MWh (+3,366 kt), producing an exact counterfactual accounting discrepancy of **+149.5 kg/MWh (+7.44 Mt CO2)** that reverses the sign of estimated climate impacts in **9/14 grids**.

### 3.1 Telemetry Audit & Objective Reconciliation of EIA-930 Discrepancies

A physical audit of EIA-930 records uncovered five reporting discrepancies and measurement anomalies that would materially distort unverified empirical analyses (Figure 2A; Table 1). First, cross-checking EIA-930 against EPA CEMS and CAISO `fuelsource.csv` exposed a **2-year +5,553 MW EIA-930 vs. CAISO/CEMS midday natural-gas reporting discrepancy in `CISO`** (`2023-12` through `2025-12`, peaking at +7,639 MW in 2025-10, with EIA-930 reporting 10,349 MW vs. 4,060 MW in CAISO `fuelsource.csv` across 2025), depressing EIA-930 `CISO` correlation with EPA CEMS to $r = 0.835$ before the two series converged closely after mid-December 2025 (2026 mean |diff| = 36.8 MW). Splicing official CAISO `fuelsource.csv` across all 25,566 hours (`2023-12..2026-10`) reconciles Sample 1 and Sample 2 with both CAISO operational records and EPA CEMS generator stacks, lifting California's lag-0 EPA CEMS correlation to **$r = 0.9962$**. Objectively, classifying this 2024–2025 divergence as a federal feed fault rather than generator-scope definitions, behind-the-meter/combined-heat-and-power (CHP) coverage, or EIA data-revision pipelines would require auditing EIA's internal revision history and plant-coverage crosswalks; we therefore document it strictly as a verifiable reporting discrepancy and report both aligned and unaligned `CISO` specifications in Table 2. Second, **`ERCO` (Texas)** and **`AZPS` (Arizona)** routed 7,224.4 GWh (down to -6,115 MW) and 5,690.4 GWh (down to -2,951 MW) of utility-scale battery charging into `Unknown Energy Storage` rather than `Battery Storage`; summing `Battery + Other + Unknown Energy Storage` reconciles ERCOT's physical RTE from an unphysical 1.49 to **0.778** and AZPS's from undefined (zero raw charging) to **0.875**. Third, **`CISO`** omits a standalone EIA-930 battery column, conflating batteries inside `Other+Geothermal` (unphysical RTE = 1.076); official CAISO standalone `fuelsource.csv` yields exact standalone RTE = **0.863** (30,580.4 GWh discharged / 35,419.2 GWh charged). Fourth, a deterministic physical bound ($|\text{BESS}| \le 1.25 \times \text{EIA-860M nameplate}$) filters a single 2,668 MW outlier in **`FPL`** (2025-08-27 HE 6), while reconciling **`DUK`**'s steady -79 to +75 MW telemetry against 53.5 MW operating plus 18.8 MW 2026 under-construction BESS (72.3 MW total; 0.0% leakage from Duke's 2,454 MW pumped-hydro plants, which report separately up to 2,431 MW). Across all 13 clean AC-coupled grids, physical RTEs span expected lithium-ion bounds (**0.778–0.886**, fleet-weighted **0.845**); four discharge-only reporters (`NEVP`, `ISNE`, `SWPP`, `PSCO`) are excluded from two-way charging accounting while remaining fully included in Sample 1. Fifth, empirical lag verification confirms exact 0-hour UTC alignment between EPA CEMS hour-beginning timestamps and EIA-930/CAISO hour-ending timestamps across every major grid (`CISO` $r = 0.9962$, `MISO` $r = 0.9961$, `SOCO` $r = 0.9926$, `ERCO` $r = 0.9908$, `DUK` $r = 0.9908$).

### 3.2 True 4-Window Disjoint Diurnal Charging Taxonomy

Partitioning all 24 local hours strictly and disjointly into **Overnight** (23:00–06:00, 8 h, verified zero solar), **Morning Ramp** (07:00–09:00, 3 h), **Midday Solar** (10:00–16:00, 7 h), and **Evening Peak** (17:00–22:00, 6 h) reveals a sharp geographic bifurcation in real-world battery behavior (Figure 2B; Table 1). High-solar Southwestern fleets (`AZPS`, `PNM`, `TEPC`, `CISO`, `IID`) draw **71.1%–83.1%** of their charging energy during midday solar hours and only **0.9%–5.8%** overnight. By stark contrast, fossil-heavy Midwest and Southeast fleets draw over half of all charging energy in the dead of night—**`MISO` (56.7% overnight)**, **`SOCO` (56.4%)**, and **`DUK` (50.6%)**—while **`ERCO`** splits charging between midday solar (63.2%) and overnight wind/fossil hours (24.0%).

## 4. Nationwide Multi-Grid Conditional Diurnal Gas-Peaker Displacement (Sample 1: Panel A)

### 4.1 Demand-Weighted WLS Econometric Design & Leverage Forensics

To estimate conditional diurnal natural-gas displacement across all $G = 49$ balanced U.S. BAs in Sample 1 from `2019-01` through `2026-10` ($N = 4,589$ BA-months), we regress the non-mechanical **evening-minus-midday natural-gas spread** normalized by 2019 baseline mean demand, $\text{em\_ng}_{b,t} = (\bar{G}_{\text{NG},b,t}^{17\text{–}22} - \bar{G}_{\text{NG},b,t}^{10\text{–}16}) / d_{19,b}$, on installed battery capacity share $\text{BESS}_{b,t} / d_{19,b}$ absorbing BA and Year-Month fixed effects (plus BA x Calendar-Month, Region x Year-Month, BA x Post-2024H2 schema-seam, and BA linear-trend specifications). Because utility-scale storage deployment is observational rather than experimentally randomized, Panel A WLS estimates are rigorously interpreted as **conditional diurnal displacement associations** once confounding solar and load trajectories are controlled—and are directly corroborated by distributed 6-month timing tests, nuclear placebos, and high-frequency daily within-BA-month dispatch specifications. Following Abadie et al. (2023), we cluster standard errors at the BA level (CR1) and report two-way BA + Year-Month clustering (Cameron et al., 2011), exact LSDV-equivalent restricted wild cluster bootstrap $p$-values under both Rademacher and Webb 6-point weights ($B = 999$; Cameron et al., 2008; MacKinnon & Webb, 2018; Roodman et al., 2019; Webb, 2023), and 1,000-permutation BA-trajectory randomization inference.

Crucially, exact Frisch-Waugh-Lovell (FWL) leverage diagnostics expose why equal-weighted OLS is econometrically improper for national U.S. grid inference: Douglas County PUD (`DOPD`), a tiny 206 MW pure-hydro district in Washington with zero natural-gas generation that commissioned a 350 MW battery ($\text{BESS}/d_{19} = 1.70$), captures **21.29%** of total equal-weighted FWL identifying variance (the highest in the entire nation, attenuating equal-weighted OLS to $-0.2392$ vs. $-0.3129$ on thermal BAs). Weighting by pre-boom 2019 baseline grid size ($w_b = d_{19,b}$) reduces `DOPD`'s leverage by over 20-fold to **1.01%** and recovers the physically representative national conditional slope.

### 4.2 Headline Conditional Displacement Estimates, Uncontrolled Baseline, Robustness & High-Frequency Mechanism

Table 2 and Figure 1 document stable conditional evening gas-peaker displacement on aligned national telemetry while transparently highlighting the role of solar confounding. In our primary demand-weighted WLS specification (`ALL 49 BAs`, `BA + YM FE`, conditioning on diurnal demand, renewable generation, and thermal/renewable capacity shares), a +1 percentage-point expansion in $\text{BESS}/d_{19}$ is associated with a **$-0.3352$** reduction in the evening-minus-midday natural-gas spread ($\text{SE} = 0.0570$, $t_{\text{CR1}} = -5.88$, $t_{\text{2-way}} = -5.84$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.004$, $p_{\text{Webb6}} = 0.002$, $p_{\text{RI}} = 0.0010$). Crucially, stripping out all covariates yields a small, statistically insignificant uncontrolled association (**No Controls = $-0.0579$**, $\text{SE} = 0.0997$, $t_{\text{CR1}} = -0.58$, $p_{\text{CR1}} = 0.564$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.576$, $p_{\text{Webb6}} = 0.606$). The economic mechanism behind this contrast is transparent: utility-scale solar expansion simultaneously deepens the evening-minus-midday duck curve (positive confounding on `em_ng`, as seen in the positive `solar_cap_share` slope) in the exact high-solar BAs that deploy battery storage, offsetting the unconditional two-way FE spread until solar and demand shifts are conditioned on. Contrasting the unaligned EIA-930 CISO feed ($-0.4487$, $\text{SE} = 0.0706$) further shows that leaving the 2024–2025 California midday natural-gas discrepancy unresolved overstates the controlled point estimate by 0.1135 while inflating standard errors. Decomposing against the zero-solar overnight baseline (23:00–06:00) confirms both physical limbs of battery arbitrage: evening gas falls by **$-0.1582$** ($t = -5.04$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.003$) while midday gas rises by **$+0.2481$** ($t = +3.79$). Total dispatchable fossil plus net imports shifts by **$-0.5632$** ($t = -8.01$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.001$, $p_{\text{RI}} = 0.0010$). Supplementary operational checks reinforce this pattern while also highlighting where finite-cluster wild bootstrap inference is conservative:

## 5. EPA CEMS Counterfactual Carbon Accounting & Non-Circular CAISO Curtailment Proof

### 5.1 24-Hour EPA CEMS Generator-Stack MEFs & the +7.44 Mt CO2 Counterfactual Accounting Discrepancy (Sample 2)

Does evening natural-gas displacement translate into net carbon abatement? To answer this rigorously using measured generator smokestack emissions rather than assumed heat rates, we estimate full 24-hour Hour-of-Day domestic and bilateral-trade-adjusted EPA CEMS Marginal Emission Factors ($\text{MEF}_{b,h}^{\text{CEMS}}$, $h = 1,\dots,24$) across Sample 2 (`2024-07-01` to `2026-09-30`) via joint within-(Year-Month x Hour) first-differenced regressions yielding exact 24x24 date-clustered covariance matrices (accounting for full within-day cross-hour correlation and propagating actual neighbor variances). Figure 3B displays the resulting 24-hour stack emission curves across four grid archetypes: coal-heavy **`MISO`** spans 499–610 kg CO2/MWh, staying elevated overnight (when 57.7% of MISO CEMS-window charging occurs) and dipping during evening gas-peaking hours; **`ERCO`** spans 456–552 kg CO2/MWh across the diurnal cycle; desert **`AZPS`** spans 295–357 kg/MWh; and high-solar **`CISO`** (including interstate imports) tracks 219–283 kg/MWh during non-curtailed hours.

It is essential to distinguish physical stack measurement from counterfactual carbon attribution: **EPA CEMS directly measures hourly fossil generator smokestack emissions, whereas battery-induced net CO2 change relative to a no-storage counterfactual is estimated by our marginal displacement accounting model (Proposition 2)**. Table 4 and Figure 3A quantify the resulting **counterfactual accounting divergence** across all **49.77 TWh (49,771.3 GWh)** of CEMS-matched battery discharge (49,663.6 GWh in 13 clean AC-coupled grids). Without curtailment absorption ($\alpha_b = 0$), a **naive AEF counterfactual implies $-75.3$ kg CO2/MWh ($-3.75$ Mt CO2)** of abatement, whereas our **CEMS-MEF marginal displacement counterfactual estimates $+74.2$ kg CO2/MWh ($+3.69$ Mt CO2; import-only trade-weight check: $+72.5$ kg/MWh)**. The headline **+7.44 Mt CO2** figure (`+3.69` Mt minus `-3.75` Mt across 49.77 TWh discharged) is strictly the **counterfactual accounting discrepancy between two model-based carbon estimates**—not directly metered battery exhaust—with the two counterfactuals disagreeing on sign in 9 of 14 grids (and yielding a **+2.55 Mt CO2** discrepancy across the 12 clean non-CAISO grids, where the CEMS-MEF counterfactual averages **$+91.1$ kg/MWh**). Cross-validating our CEMS-estimated marginal slopes against independent 24-hour marginal fuel shares multiplied by BA-specific EPA eGRID 2023 rev2 coal, CCGT, and CT peaker emission factors yields a close cross-grid correlation of **$r = 0.977$** (mean absolute deviation = 9.8 kg/MWh; fleet-wide eGRID counterfactual = $+65.1$ kg/MWh). Every single one of the 14 metered grids exhibits a strictly positive break-even curtailment threshold $\alpha_b^* \in [0.6\%, 24.8\%]$, led by coal- and overnight-heavy grids (`MISO`: **$+175.1 \pm 9.8$ kg/MWh**, coal stack $\Delta_{\text{Chg-Dis}} = +46.5$ kg/MWh, $\alpha^* = 24.8\%$; `ERCO`: **$+142.5 \pm 3.7$ kg/MWh**, $\alpha^* = 22.0\%$; `CPLE`: **$+118.9 \pm 3.8$ kg/MWh**, coal stack $\Delta = +41.8$ kg/MWh; `SOCO`: **$+74.8 \pm 7.3$ kg/MWh**; `DUK`: **$+44.3 \pm 6.3$ kg/MWh**). Table 4b evaluates sensitivity when $\alpha > 0$: the uniform fleet break-even is 18.9%, and under a realistic hybrid scenario applying CAISO's empirical curtailment-stratified balance (-0.80 kg/MWh) to California while allowing 0%–15% absorption across all other 13 grids, the fleet CEMS-MEF estimate remains net-positive (+1.84 to +0.29 Mt CO2) and the discrepancy relative to naive AEF remains **+5.59 to +4.04 Mt CO2**. Without imposing artificial solar cutoffs, unadjusted CEMS-MEF net CO2 correlates positively with overnight charging share ($r = +0.413$) and only weakly with midday solar share ($r = -0.170$), indicating that midday solar penetration alone does not abate carbon unless it translates into verified renewable curtailment.

### 5.2 Empirical Curtailment Stratification, Cross-Region Imports, and Saturation in California (Sample 3)

To examine how real-world renewable curtailment and cross-region imports modify marginal estimates without imposing solar-share cutoffs or transition-boundary bias, Sample 3 merges official CAISO hourly solar and wind curtailment records (`2024-07-01` to `2025-12-31`, 13,170 hours across 549 days) directly onto CAISO standalone `fuelsource.csv` battery telemetry (19,704.9 GWh charged, 17,020.0 GWh discharged) and California EPA CEMS generator-stack CO2. Regressing hourly changes in smokestack CO2 on potential net load ($\Delta(\text{Load} - \text{Solar} - \text{Wind} - \text{Curtailment} - \text{BESS})$)—and cross-checking via an alternative observed-load ($\Delta\text{Load}$) specification omitting $\Delta\text{Curt}$ and $\Delta\text{BESS}$ from the RHS at both domestic and import-adjusted system levels—within Year-Month x Hour-of-Day fixed effects across strictly interior regime hours (requiring both $t$ and $t-1$ inside the same curtailment bin) reveals a steep, monotonic decline in marginal stack emissions (Table 5; Figure 4A):

- **0 MW (No Curtailment, 6,253 hrs / 5,489 interior; 65.0% of discharge, 7.9% of charging):** In-state domestic CAISO CEMS MEF = **160.6 $\pm$ 5.0 kg/MWh** (observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$ domestic: 164.4); Total System (import-adjusted) CEMS MEF = **268.8 $\pm$ 3.3 kg/MWh** (observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$ system: 187.5); marginal un-curtailment = **0.0%**.
- **1–500 MW (5,059 hrs / 3,944 interior; 55.0% of charging):** Domestic CEMS MEF declines to **107.0 kg/MWh** (observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$ domestic: 116.5); System MEF = **243.7 kg/MWh** (observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$ system: 194.8); marginal un-curtailment remains minimal at **2.5%** because minor curtailment is overwhelmingly local congestion.
- **500–2,000 MW (1,278 hrs / 798 interior; 24.3% of charging):** Domestic CEMS MEF drops by 46% to **86.6 kg/MWh** (observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$ domestic: 83.8); System MEF = **225.9 kg/MWh** (observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$ system: 141.0); marginal un-curtailment rises to **19.7%**.
- **>2,000 MW (580 hrs / 453 interior; 12.8% of charging):** In consecutive interior `>2,000 MW` curtailment hours, **in-state domestic** CEMS MEF declines by **83%** to **26.8 $\pm$ 9.5 kg/MWh** (observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$ domestic: **27.9 kg/MWh**, -83%), whereas **system-wide (import-adjusted)** CEMS MEF declines **60%** to **107.0 $\pm$ 17.8 kg/MWh** (observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$ system: **70.5 kg/MWh**, -62%; and **102.4 kg/MWh** during interior >500 MW System-wide curtailment) as **62.4%** of marginal load directly un-curtails spilled solar.

Furthermore, CAISO batteries actively pursue curtailed solar: within Year-Month x Hour-of-Day fixed effects controlling for load, solar, and wind, hourly battery charging responds to real curtailment ($\partial \text{Chg} / \partial \text{Curt} = +0.1385$, $\text{SE}_{\text{day}} = 0.0155$, $t = +8.95$; System curtailment: $+0.1539$, $t = +5.94$; Local congestion curtailment: $+0.1322$, $t = +7.61$).

**Direct Marginal Absorption, Congestion-Constrained Saturation Frontier & Multi-Day Block-Bootstrap Uncertainty (Figure 4B; Table 5):** Although **92.1%** of CAISO battery charging occurs in hours with some positive curtailment, **84.9%** of CAISO's 4,347.2 GWh overlap curtailment is local transmission congestion rather than system-wide oversupply, and over half of charging (55.0%) lands in light 1–500 MW congestion hours where marginal un-curtailment is just 2.5%. Weighting our four interior empirical un-curtailment slopes by hourly battery charging yields a direct **empirical marginal curtailment absorption rate of $\hat{\alpha}_{\text{unspill}}^{\text{emp}} = 14.1\%$** (14d 95% CI [9.7%, 19.0%]; CEMS System MEF collapse $\hat{\alpha}_{\text{MEF}}^{\text{emp}} = 16.7\%$, 14d 95% CI [11.9%, 20.8%]). Sweeping incremental storage expansion $\kappa$ on top of California's current 17.3 GW fleet against the observed 13,170-hour residual curtailment curve defines a **congestion-constrained incremental saturation frontier**: at $\kappa = 0.25\times$ current charging, total residual curtailment headroom is **40.3%** (implied net -64.2 kg/MWh if local congestion were accessible) but only **5.3%** under system-wide curtailment (+45.8 kg/MWh); by $\kappa = 1.0\times$, even total headroom falls below break-even $\alpha_{\text{CISO}}^* = 19.9\%$ to **18.2%** (2.8% system-only); and by $\kappa = 2.0\times$, headroom falls to **10.1%** (1.6% system-only, implied net +30.7 to +57.7 kg/MWh).

To propagate diurnal correlation, cross-BA import coefficient uncertainty across all 10 CISO bilateral neighbor BAs, curtailment-regime variance, hourly battery cycling-weight/RTE uncertainty, and multi-day synoptic weather autocorrelation jointly, we execute **$B = 1,000$ contiguous moving-block bootstraps** at block lengths of 1 day, 7 days, and 14 days (Table 5, bottom rows; Figure 4B). Under the conservative **14-day contiguous block bootstrap**, CAISO's curtailment-stratified system-wide balance is **$-0.80$ kg CO2/MWh (SE = 6.85, 14d 95% CI [-14.20, +12.58], $p = 0.920$; 7d [-13.24, +9.83]; 1d [-10.39, +8.15])** under our main specification and **$+1.34$ kg CO2/MWh (SE = 13.78, 14d 95% CI [-21.86, +30.51], $p = 0.764$; 7d [-17.90, +26.27]; 1d [-14.14, +18.62])** under the alternative observed-load ($\Delta\text{Load}$) specification (between-specification difference = +2.14 kg/MWh, 14d 95% CI [-15.09, +27.46]). By contrast, ignoring interstate imports yields a strictly negative in-state domestic balance across all block lengths: **$-30.64$ kg/MWh** (14d 95% CI [-42.78, -17.48]) under the main specification and **$-30.67$ kg/MWh** (14d 95% CI [-49.02, -8.54]) under observed $\Delta\text{Load}$. Crucially, failing to reject zero across wide intervals (`[-14.2, +12.6]` and `[-21.9, +30.5]` kg/MWh) does not establish equivalence to zero; rather, **accounting for cross-region imports, the net emissions direction in California remains undetermined**, while cleanly ruling out naive AEF's **$-104.7$ kg/MWh** abatement claim (ruling out abatement stronger than -14.2 / -21.9 kg/MWh at the 95% level). Meanwhile, fossil-marginal grids without comparable solar curtailment (`ERCO`, `MISO`, `CPLE`, `SOCO`, `DUK`) remain net carbon-increasing (+44 to +175 kg/MWh).

## 6. Policy Implications, Methodological Limitations & Scope Caveats

Figure 5B summarizes cluster-robust $|t|$-statistics across all nine empirical estimates ($|t| = 3.60$–$38.2$) alongside five placebo and lead tests ($|t| \le 1.16$). Together, our 14-BA counterfactual comparison and California curtailment breakdown carry direct implications for storage incentives, Scope 2 carbon accounting, and grid planning:

1. **Carbon-Blind Storage Subsidies and Counterfactual Carbon Accounting:** Under federal investment tax credits (IRC Section 48E) and prevailing GHG Protocol Scope 2 AEF guidelines, battery operators receive identical carbon-accounting credit regardless of whether they charge from coal-marginal overnight power in `MISO` (+175 kg/MWh) or curtailed midday solar in `CISO`. Across the same 49.77 TWh of actual commercial battery discharge, AEF and CEMS-MEF counterfactuals diverge by **+7.44 Mt CO2** (`+3.69` Mt vs. `-3.75` Mt; remaining +5.59 to +4.04 Mt even under hybrid curtailment sensitivity), indicating that operational storage decarbonization depends on **co-siting with transmission relief and verifiable renewable curtailment** rather than capacity additions in fossil-marginal grids.
2. **Methodological Limitations and Extensions:** Six transparent limitations bound our conclusions. First, our three empirical samples (Box 1) differ intentionally in geographic and temporal coverage, and public 5-minute curtailment archives are currently uniform across `2024–2025` only for CAISO; replicating curtailment stratification in ERCOT, MISO, and international markets (e.g., Great Britain or Australia) is an important next step as interval-level curtailment records expand. Second, cross-region import emissions are weighted using average bilateral EIA-930 trade shares (robust to two-way vs. incoming-only weights); incorporating marginal bilateral power-flow elasticities and nodal transmission constraints is a valuable extension when nodal unit-dispatch records are available. Third, California's system-wide net CO2 14-day block bootstrap intervals span zero (`[-14.20, +12.58]` and `[-21.86, +30.51]` kg/MWh), establishing that **accounting for cross-region imports, the net emissions direction remains undetermined** rather than proving exact carbon balance. Fourth, Sample 1's 49-BA WLS displacement estimate ($-0.3352$) is an observational conditional association (`No Controls` = $-0.0579$, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.576$), and several Table 3 sub-component wild bootstrap $p$-values sit between 0.075 and 0.130; we therefore treat Sample 1 as supportive operational context rather than quasi-experimental identification. Fifth, while splicing official CAISO `fuelsource.csv` reconciles the 2-year +5,553 MW EIA-930 vs. CAISO/CEMS midday natural-gas discrepancy, attributing its administrative origin requires EIA internal revision metadata, and `FPL` (DC-coupled RTE = 0.949) is flagged separately from AC-coupled fleets. Sixth, our empirical accounting evaluates **short-run operational dispatch** holding renewable capacity fixed; long-run storage-induced renewable entry and carbon pricing interactions (Butters et al., 2025; Dupont et al., 2026) can yield larger lifecycle benefits, though short-run operations cannot be credited with large AEF-implied abatement absent verified curtailment absorption.
