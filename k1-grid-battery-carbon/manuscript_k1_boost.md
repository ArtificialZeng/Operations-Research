## Abstract

Utility-scale battery storage reached 56.6 GW (156.0 GWh) across 36 U.S. balancing authorities by October 2026, yet operational carbon-accounting frameworks remain contested. Linking 49.77 TWh of reconciled discharge across 14 metered grids (78.5% in California and Texas) to EPA Continuous Emissions Monitoring System stack records, we quantify counterfactual model discrepancies and verifiable error boundaries. Under zero curtailment absorption, Average Emission Factors (-3.75 Mt CO2) and marginal emission factors (+3.69 Mt CO2) disagree by +7.44 Mt CO2, reversing sign in 9/14 grids. Unconditional season-stratified 7/14/28-day UTC-day bootstraps, leave-BA-out audits, and out-of-sample holdouts (pooled R2 = 0.94) confirm this discrepancy persists across curtailment scenarios. In California, cross-region imports leave net direction undetermined (-0.80 to +1.34 kg/MWh) while local congestion bounds fixed-profile scenarios; in Texas, matched stress tests show coal enters the margin above 60% renewables (529 kg/MWh).

Grid-scale lithium-ion batteries are widely subsidized on the premise that shifting midday solar electricity to evening peaks cuts power-sector carbon dioxide (CO2) emissions. Earlier engineering and economic studies established theoretically and through dispatch simulations that storage round-trip energy losses and coal-marginal charging can instead increase operational emissions (Carson & Novan, 2013; Hittinger & Azevedo, 2015; Fisher & Apt, 2017; Fares & Webber, 2017), and subsequent operational and equilibrium models underscored that decarbonization depends on absorbing otherwise curtailed renewables, transmission constraints, and long-run investment feedback (Arbabzadeh et al., 2019; Butters et al., 2025; Dupont et al., 2026). In parallel, empirical power-sector studies demonstrated that consumption-weighted Average Emission Factors (AEFs) systematically diverge from Marginal Emission Factors (MEFs) estimated from measured generating units when evaluating load-shifting technologies (Graff Zivin et al., 2014; Holland et al., 2016; Callaway et al., 2018; de Chalendar et al., 2019; Holland et al., 2022).

Until recently, commercial battery fleets were too small for national empirical evaluation, confining most evidence to simulations or early single-market pilots. By October 2026, however, U.S. utility-scale battery energy storage systems (BESS) expanded to 56,578.3 MW (156,003.2 MWh; mean duration 2.76 h) across 36 balancing authorities (BAs), including 19,702.9 MW (34.8%) outside California and Texas. Simultaneously, Form EIA-930 began reporting hourly storage dispatch in July 2024 alongside standalone 5-minute California Independent System Operator (CAISO) battery and curtailment logs and hourly U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) Continuous Emissions Monitoring System (CEMS) smokestack CO2 records achieving a 99.94% plant-to-BA CO2 match rate within the sampled EPA CEMS fossil archive.

Here we contribute a commercial-scale counterfactual carbon-accounting model comparison and verifiable error-boundary assessment grounded in observed U.S. grid dispatch rather than synthetic dispatch assumptions. Across three explicitly delineated samples (Supplementary Box 1)—a core 14-BA carbon-accounting panel (`2024-07` to `2026-09`, 49.77 TWh discharged), a CAISO curtailment subsample (`2024-07` to `2025-12`, 13,170 hours), and a supporting 49-BA operational gas-displacement panel (`2019-01` to `2026-10`, 3,359,080 BA-hours)—we quantify when AEF and MEF frameworks reverse sign, propagate full unconditional uncertainty across multi-week blocks and bilateral trade weights, evaluate out-of-sample predictive accuracy and hourly lead-lag timing, stress-test high-renewable coal margins in ERCOT, and delineate fixed-profile BA-level abatement bounds (while explicitly reserving nodal security-constrained dispatch replay and exogenous outage instruments for future work).

## Counterfactual carbon accounting across 14 U.S. grids

Evaluating real-world battery emissions first required reconciling five reporting discrepancies in public EIA-930 telemetry across 52,816.8 GWh of full-window discharge (Methods; Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table 1): replacing CAISO's unphysical `Other+Geothermal` battery proxy (round-trip efficiency, RTE = 1.076) and aligning a 2-year +5,553 MW EIA-930 versus CAISO/CEMS midday natural-gas discrepancy using standalone 5-minute CAISO `fuelsource.csv` (true RTE = 0.863; lag-0 CEMS correlation $r = 0.9962$); recovering battery charging routed into `Unknown Energy Storage` in `ERCO` (7,224.4 GWh; RTE = 0.778) and `AZPS` (5,690.4 GWh; RTE = 0.875); filtering a single 2,668 MW outlier in `FPL`; and confirming 0% pumped-hydro leakage across `SOCO`, `DUK`, `MISO`, and `CPLE`. Across 13 clean alternating-current-coupled grids, physical RTEs lie tightly between 0.778 and 0.886 (fleet-weighted 0.845). Diurnal charging diverges sharply by region: Southwestern solar fleets (`AZPS`, `PNM`, `TEPC`, `CISO`, `IID`) draw 71.1%–83.1% of charging energy during midday solar hours (10:00–16:00) and 0.9%–5.8% overnight (23:00–06:00), whereas fossil-heavy Midwest and Southeast fleets charge predominantly overnight (`MISO` 56.7%, `SOCO` 56.4%, `DUK` 50.6%).

Physical stack metering must be separated from counterfactual attribution: EPA CEMS directly measures hourly fossil generator smokestack emissions, whereas battery-induced net CO2 change relative to a no-storage baseline is estimated by our counterfactual displacement model (Methods). Linking reconciled hourly dispatch across 49.77 TWh in the 27-month EPA CEMS overlap (`2024-07` to `2026-09`) to 24-hour bilateral-trade-adjusted CEMS MEFs (with exact 24x24 date-clustered covariances; Fig. 1 and Table 1) exposes a stark directional divergence. Without renewable curtailment absorption ($\alpha_b = 0$), a consumption-weighted AEF counterfactual implies -75.3 kg CO2/MWh (-3.75 Mt CO2) of abatement, whereas the CEMS-MEF marginal counterfactual estimates +74.2 kg CO2/MWh (+3.69 Mt CO2; import-only weight check: +72.5 kg/MWh). The resulting **+7.44 Mt CO2** (+149.5 kg/MWh) figure is strictly the **counterfactual accounting discrepancy** between two model-based estimates—not directly metered battery exhaust—with opposite signs in 9 of 14 grids. Crucially, observed U.S. storage volume is highly concentrated: `CISO` and `ERCO` account for 78.5% of fleet discharge (`CISO` alone contributes 65.5% of the baseline Mt discrepancy). Leave-one-BA-out audits confirm sign reversal in all 14/14 sub-fleets (Supplementary Table 5), with discrepancy remaining **+2.57 Mt** (unconditional 14d CI [+2.30, +2.83]) excluding `CISO`, **+5.81 Mt** excluding `ERCO`, and **+0.93 Mt** ([+0.80, +1.04], +87.1 kg/MWh) across the remaining 12 grids (10.69 TWh, 21.5% of discharge). Aggregate Mt magnitudes are thus bounded to this observed 14-market U.S. fleet and historical dispatch.

Analytical decomposition (Methods) clarifies both the accounting discrepancy and the thermodynamic threshold for abatement. First, the AEF-versus-MEF gap is driven primarily by a +217.1 kg/MWh charging wedge—amplified by round-trip losses ($1/\eta_b$) because infra-marginal midday solar and nuclear generation depress average grid intensity far below the thermal margin—offset only partially by a +67.6 kg/MWh evening discharge wedge. Second, discharging 1 MWh yields net operational abatement ($\Delta\text{CO}_{2,b} < 0$) if and only if curtailed zero-carbon charging exceeds $\alpha_b^* = 1 - \eta_b\,\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} / \text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}$. All 14/14 grids satisfy $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} / \text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} < 1/\eta_b$ ($\alpha_b^* \in [0.6\%, 24.8\%]$): in 4/14 grids (`CISO`, `MISO`, `IID`, `CPLE`), $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} \ge \text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}}$, while in the remaining 10 grids discharging MEF slightly exceeds charging MEF without overcoming physical efficiency losses. Unadjusted CEMS-MEF net emissions correlate positively with overnight charging ($r = +0.413$) and weakly with midday solar ($r = -0.170$), led by coal- and gas-marginal grids (`MISO` +175.1, `ERCO` +142.5, `CPLE` +118.9, `SOCO` +74.8, `DUK` +44.3 kg/MWh).

## Unconditional uncertainty, out-of-sample validation, and measurement cross-checks

Conditional block bootstraps that hold AEF, discharge volumes, and bilateral trade weights fixed understate total uncertainty. We therefore retain our conditional 14-day intervals alongside a **full unconditional season-stratified (DJF/MAM/JJA/SON) joint UTC-day moving-block bootstrap** ($B = 1,000$; $L = 7, 14, 28$ days; Fig. 2a and Table 2, Panel A) that simultaneously re-estimates on every resampled block: exact hourly consumption-weighted AEF, 24-hour CEMS MEFs and import shares, hourly cycling and unfixed BA discharge volumes, exact same-window bilateral trade weights (`2024-07` to `2026-09` and `2024-07` to `2025-12` daily UTC interchange), and CAISO curtailment regimes. Reconciling Table 1 (`+7.44` Mt) against our conditional v4 baseline (`+7.42` Mt) shows the `0.024` Mt gap reflects untrimmed `d_disp+d_imp` versus 8x-MAD `d_need` import shares (`-0.025` Mt) and `|BESS|>0.5` MW filtering (`+0.002` Mt), whereas updating Table 1 to exact same-window trade weights shifts the discrepancy by only `+0.0017` Mt to **+7.44 Mt** (Supplementary Table 5). Unconditional resampling widens the baseline 14-day discrepancy CI by 1.85x relative to matched conditional intervals—to [+6.97, +7.98] Mt (`7d` [+7.02, +7.88]; `28d` [+6.90, +8.14])—yet every scenario across both full (`49.77 TWh`) and exact 18-month same-window (`26.03 TWh`, avoiding 2026 extrapolation) samples keeps the discrepancy well above zero. Under hybrid scenarios applying California's empirical curtailment estimate to `CISO` and sweeping non-California absorption from 0% to 15%, fleet net CO2 spans zero at 15% (+0.29 [-0.06, +0.57] Mt full; +0.16 [-0.03, +0.33] Mt same-window), while the discrepancy relative to AEF remains strictly positive at **+4.03 Mt** (`14d` [+3.62, +4.35]; `28d` [+3.66, +4.40]) full and **+2.20 Mt** (`14d` [+1.95, +2.44]; `28d` [+1.97, +2.52]) same-window.

To test predictive transferability without circular in-sample reuse, we perform temporal holdout validation (estimating 24-hour CEMS MEFs strictly on `2024-07` to `2025-09` and predicting held-out `2025-10` to `2026-09` hourly $\Delta\text{CO}_{2,\text{CEMS}}$) and 9-quarter leave-quarter-out cross-validation against pre-specified acceptance criteria (Methods; Fig. 2b and Supplementary Table 6). Across the held-out year, out-of-sample $R^2$ reaches **0.938** pooled (0.761 discharge-weighted, calibration slope 0.872), led by `ERCO` (0.967), `MISO` (0.945), and `CISO` (0.759), with noisier domestic predictability in heavily interconnected `AZPS` (0.385); applying training MEFs to held-out dispatch predicts a +4.23 Mt discrepancy versus +4.38 Mt actual (3.5% error). Across all 9 held-out quarters, mean major-grid out-of-sample $R^2$ is 0.952 with 9/9 sign preservation. Complementary measurement and order-of-magnitude cross-checks (Supplementary Table 4) corroborate the scale of CEMS MEFs while clarifying identification boundaries: (1) distributed $k = -3\dots+3$ h lead-lag regressions of $\Delta\text{CO}_{2,\text{CEMS}}$ on metered $\Delta\text{BESS}_{\text{MW}}$ concentrate **94.6%–96.1%** of response at contemporaneous $k = 0$ in `CISO` (-270.6 $\pm$ 4.4), `ERCO` (-495.6 $\pm$ 3.7), and `AZPS` (-221.7 $\pm$ 22.7 kg/MWh) while revealing small $k = -1$ anticipation ($\le 2.4\%$ of $k = 0$, $F = 10.3$ in `CISO`, $5.8$ in `ERCO`) and substantial simultaneity in small-fleet `MISO` (47.3% at $k = 0$), alongside direct contemporaneous import displacement $\partial\text{Imports}/\partial\text{BESS} = -0.675$ MW/MW in `CISO` versus $-0.022$ in islanded `ERCO`; (2) pure CEMS internal stack ramp slopes confirm high combustion intensity (`CISO` 376, `ERCO` 516, `AZPS` 597, `MISO` 636 kg/MWh, sharing CEMS stack data); and (3) official EPA eGRID 2023 rev2 Non-Baseload rates evaluated on $(1/\eta_b - 1)$ provide a flat-MEF order-of-magnitude floor of +89.6 kg/MWh (+4.46 Mt; positive by construction whenever $\text{eGRID} > 0$ and $\eta_b < 1$, rather than an independent sign test).

## California curtailment, cross-region imports, and multi-day uncertainty

California's public 5-minute curtailment archive (`2024-07` to `2025-12`, 13,170 matched hours, 4,347.2 GWh curtailed) illustrates how verified renewable curtailment and cross-region imports jointly alter marginal storage estimates (Fig. 3 and Table 2, Panel B). Stratifying strictly on consecutive interior regime hours (requiring both $t$ and $t-1$ inside the same bin to remove transition-boundary leakage) shows monotonic marginal stack declines: above 2,000 MW curtailment, California's **in-state domestic** CEMS MEF declines **83%** (160.6 to 26.8 kg/MWh; alternative observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$: -83%) and **import-adjusted system-wide** CEMS MEF declines **60%** (268.8 to 107.0 kg/MWh; observed-$\Delta\text{Load}$: -62%), while marginal un-curtailment climbs from 0.0% to 62.4%. Hourly charging responds directly to curtailment ($\partial\text{Chg}/\partial\text{Curt} = +0.1385$, $t = +8.95$; system $+0.1539$, local congestion $+0.1322$).

Yet 84.9% of CAISO curtailment (3,691.3 of 4,347.2 GWh) reflects local transmission congestion rather than system-wide oversupply, and 55.0% of battery charging occurs during light 1–500 MW congestion hours where marginal un-curtailment is only 2.5%. Consequently, charging-weighted empirical marginal absorption is $\hat{\alpha}_{\text{unspill}}^{\text{emp}} = 14.1\%$ (14d 95% CI [9.7%, 19.0%]; CEMS MEF collapse 16.7%, 14d CI [11.9%, 20.8%]), below California's break-even threshold $\alpha_{\text{CISO}}^* = 19.9\%$. Propagating diurnal, neighbor-import, curtailment-regime, cycling-weight, and multi-day weather uncertainty jointly via $B = 1,000$ contiguous block bootstraps yields an import-adjusted system balance of **-0.80 kg CO2/MWh** (unstratified 14d CI [-14.20, +12.58], SE = 6.85, $p = 0.920$; 7d [-13.24, +9.83]; 1d [-10.39, +8.15]; season-stratified unconditional UTC-day 14d/28d: -0.66 [-12.47, +6.49] / [-11.67, +6.68]) under potential net load and **+1.34 kg CO2/MWh** (14d CI [-21.86, +30.51], $p = 0.764$) under observed $\Delta\text{Load}$ (without asserting strict exogeneity of load changes). Ignoring interstate imports gives a strictly negative domestic balance (-30.64, 14d CI [-42.78, -17.48]; observed $\Delta\text{Load}$: -30.67, [-49.02, -8.54]). Thus, consistent with CAISO's -0.675 MW/MW import offset, **accounting for cross-region imports, California's net emissions direction remains undetermined**, while naive AEF's -104.7 kg/MWh claim is ruled out.

## Upper-bound accounting scenarios and ERCOT cross-regime stress test

To bound how local congestion limits incremental storage abatement—without claiming nodal-deliverability capacity guarantees—we combine CAISO's observed hourly residual curtailment curve with a cross-regime renewable-penetration stress test in ERCOT, MISO, and AZPS (Fig. 4). Holding CAISO's empirical hourly charging profile fixed and omitting nodal locational marginal prices (LMPs), dynamic state-of-charge (SOC) re-optimization, and binding transmission shadow prices, sweeping incremental scale $\kappa \in [0.1\times, 3.0\times]$ (1.7–51.8 GW atop CAISO's 17.3 GW fleet) across four local-congestion access shares ($\tau_{\text{local}} = 0\%, 25\%, 50\%, 100\%$) defines **fixed-profile BA-level upper-bound accounting scenarios** (Fig. 4a). Accessing only system-wide curtailment ($\tau_{\text{local}} = 0\%$) leaves incremental storage net carbon-increasing at every scale (+39.2 to +59.3 kg/MWh; headroom 7.5% to 2.8%). Unlocking 25% of local congestion yields an upper-bound break-even of $\kappa = 0.25\times$ (4.3 GW); 50% extends the upper bound to $0.46\times$ (8.0 GW); and even a frictionless zero-congestion ceiling ($\tau_{\text{local}} = 100\%$) saturates at $0.88\times$ (15.2 GW; turning +5.4 to +30.7 kg/MWh) without concurrent renewable expansion.

Because public interval-level ERCOT curtailment is absent from Form EIA-930, we frame Texas (`ERCO`, 9.92 TWh) as a **cross-regime renewable-penetration stress test** across hourly `(Solar + Wind) / Load` regimes (Fig. 4b). In zero-coal `CISO`, 85.2% of charging occurs above 60% renewables, where domestic net-load CEMS MEF declines to 69.9 $\pm$ 3.4 kg/MWh. In `ERCO`, 73.6% of charging likewise occurs above 40% renewables (28.4% above 60%), yet domestic CEMS MEF does not fall: it remains 501.8 kg/MWh at 20–40%, 495.4 kg/MWh at 40–60%, and rises to **532.4 $\pm$ 7.9 kg/MWh** above 60% renewables as coal's marginal contribution doubles from 151 to **279 kg/MWh**. Crucially, matching strictly within identical `Season x Hour x Load-Quartile` cells (120 common-support cells, reweighting `<40%` to the exact `>60%` cell distribution; Supplementary Table 6) confirms that ERCOT's high-renewable coal margin is not a diurnal, seasonal, or load-level artifact: MEF rises from 486.3 to **533.8 kg/MWh** (+47.5 $\pm$ 12.9; coal 206.9 to 290.2, +83.3 $\pm$ 16.2), persists under `Season x Hour x Load-Decile` FE (528.8, coal 286.3 vs. `CISO` 65.6 kg/MWh), and holds specifically during ERCOT midday solar hours (`10:00–16:00`, 535.5 $\pm$ 8.4, coal 291.2). Supporting 49-BA regressions (Methods; Supplementary Figs. 2–3 and Supplementary Tables 2–3) show that conditioning on solar and demand shifts, +1% $\text{BESS}/d_{19}$ is associated with a -0.3352 reduction in evening-minus-midday gas generation ($p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.004$; Drop CISO & ERCO -0.3810; Nuclear placebo -0.0005, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.291$), contrasted transparently against the solar-confounded uncontrolled baseline (`No Controls` = -0.0579, $p_{\text{Rad}} = 0.576$).

## Discussion
Evaluating 49.77 TWh of commercial U.S. storage operations demonstrates that carbon-accounting conventions—and the geographic boundaries of trade and congestion—govern whether observed battery dispatch appears to abate or increase emissions. Building on earlier simulation and equilibrium analyses (Hittinger & Azevedo, 2015; Arbabzadeh et al., 2019; Butters et al., 2025; Dupont et al., 2026), our empirical contribution is a verifiable commercial-scale comparison of competing carbon-accounting models and their geographic error boundaries across observed market dispatch. Under carbon-blind incentives such as U.S. Internal Revenue Code Section 48E and average-emission Scope 2 protocols, storage earns identical credit whether charging on overnight coal margins in `MISO` (+175 kg/MWh), high-renewable coal margins in `ERCO` (+142 kg/MWh), or curtailed midday solar in `CISO`. Across identical historical dispatch records, switching from AEF to generator-stack CEMS-MEF accounting reveals a +7.44 Mt CO2 discrepancy (+4.03 Mt unconditional at 15% non-California absorption; +2.20 Mt strictly inside the 18-month overlap; +0.93 Mt across the 12 non-CAISO/ERCOT grids). Operational decarbonization policies therefore require locational marginal emission signals paired with targeted transmission relief or co-sited renewable curtailment absorption rather than undifferentiated storage deployment mandates.

Clear methodological boundaries define the scope of these findings and priorities for future work. First, aggregate national volumes are concentrated in California and Texas (78.5% of observed discharge), so our empirical totals apply strictly to these 14 metered U.S. markets and historical dispatch rather than universal future grids. Second, our analysis is a reduced-form counterfactual accounting comparison rather than a nodal security-constrained economic dispatch (SCED) or optimal power flow (OPF) replay; our CAISO capacity scenarios (Fig. 4a) represent fixed-profile BA-level upper bounds omitting nodal LMPs, SOC trajectories, and binding constraint shadow prices, and direct $\Delta\text{BESS}$ regressions share CEMS data and dispatch simultaneity (as quantified by our lead-lag tests). Pairing nodal SCED replay with exogenous forced-outage or transmission-line instruments is a key next step. Third, public interval-level curtailment records are currently uniform only for CAISO; verifying ERCOT's high-renewable coal margin directly against SCED marginal-resource logs and binding transmission constraints remains essential as market disclosures expand. Fourth, California's import-adjusted multi-day block-bootstrap CIs ([-14.20, +12.58] and [-21.86, +30.51] kg/MWh) establish directional indeterminacy rather than equivalence-tested zero impact, and our supporting 49-BA gas-displacement regressions remain observational conditional associations (with sub-component wild bootstrap $p$-values between 0.075 and 0.130). Finally, we evaluate short-run operational dispatch holding generation capacity fixed; long-run storage-induced renewable investment and carbon pricing (Butters et al., 2025; Dupont et al., 2026) can deliver larger lifecycle reductions than short-run operations alone, though short-run dispatch should not be credited with AEF-implied savings absent verified curtailment absorption.

## Methods

### Empirical samples and data sources

We assemble six public U.S. grid and emissions archives across three delineated samples (Supplementary Box 1 and `REPRODUCIBILITY.md`): (1) hourly Form EIA-930 balance operations (`2019-01` to `2026-10`, 4,285,392 rows; 3,359,080 rows across $G = 49$ balanced BAs with $\ge 72$ months of non-null demand and 2019 mean demand $d_{19} \ge 100$ MW); (2) hourly Form EIA-930 bilateral `EIA930_INTERCHANGE` flows covering the complete 27-month CEMS window (`2024-07-01` to `2026-09-30`, aggregated to daily UTC bilateral flows across all 14 BAs); (3) monthly Form EIA-860M generator inventories through October 2026; (4) official CAISO 5-minute `fuelsource.csv` telemetry (1066/1066 days, `2023-12-01` to `2026-10-31`, 25,569 hours); (5) official CAISO 5-minute `Production and Curtailments` workbooks (`2024-01` to `2025-12`, decomposing 4,347.2 GWh overlap curtailment into 656.0 GWh System and 3,691.3 GWh Local congestion); and (6) hourly EPA CEMS smokestack emissions (`2024-07-01` to `2026-09-30`, 1,043,187 BA-hours, 3,506.3 Mt CO2) from Catalyst PUDL `v2026.11.0`, mapped to BAs and fuel classes (`COAL`, `NGCC`, `NGCT`, `OIL`) via EPA eGRID 2023 rev2 (`PLNT23`, `BA23`) and EIA-860M (16,316 plants, achieving a 99.94% plant-to-BA CO2 match rate within the sampled EPA CEMS fossil archive).

### Telemetry reconciliation and EIA-930 audit

Physical telemetry reconciliation resolves five anomalies before carbon accounting (Supplementary Fig. 1 and Supplementary Table 1): (i) splicing CAISO `fuelsource.csv` across 25,566 hours (`2023-12` to `2026-10`) reconciles a 2-year +5,553 MW EIA-930 versus CAISO/CEMS midday natural-gas discrepancy (peaking at +7,639 MW in 2025-10 and converging to 36.8 MW in 2026, raising CEMS lag-0 correlation from $r = 0.835$ to $r = 0.9962$) and replaces CAISO's unphysical `Other+Geothermal` battery column (RTE = 1.076) with standalone battery metering (RTE = 0.863); (ii) summing `Battery + Other + Unknown Energy Storage` recovers utility-scale charging in `ERCO` (7,224.4 GWh; RTE 1.49 to 0.778) and `AZPS` (5,690.4 GWh; undefined to 0.875); (iii) a deterministic physical bound ($|\text{BESS}| \le 1.25 \times \text{EIA-860M nameplate}$) filters one 2,668 MW glitch in `FPL` (2025-08-27 HE 6), while reconciling `DUK` (53.5 MW operating plus 18.8 MW 2026 under-construction BESS); (iv) pumped-hydro correlation audits verify 0/4 leakage across `SOCO`, `DUK`, `MISO`, and `CPLE`, while flagging DC-coupled `FPL` (RTE = 0.949) separately and excluding four discharge-only reporters (`NEVP`, `ISNE`, `SWPP`, `PSCO`) from two-way carbon accounting; and (v) cross-correlation audits verify exact 0-hour UTC timestamp alignment between CEMS hour-beginning and EIA-930/CAISO hour-ending clocks ($r = 0.991$–$0.996$ across major thermal grids).

### Formal carbon-arbitrage identities and fixed-profile upper-bound scenarios

For BA $b$ with physical RTE $\eta_b \in (0,1)$, charging- and discharging-weighted marginal emission factors $\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}, \text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} > 0$, and curtailed-renewable charging fraction $\alpha_b \in [0,1]$, discharging 1 MWh induces net counterfactual CO2 change $\Delta \text{CO}_{2,b}(\alpha_b) = (1 - \alpha_b)\,\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} / \eta_b - \text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}}$, yielding net operational abatement if and only if $\alpha_b > \alpha_b^* \equiv 1 - \eta_b\,\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} / \text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}}$ (verified to numerical error 5.7e-14 across all 14 grids). The AEF-versus-MEF accounting discrepancy at $\alpha_b = 0$ decomposes identically into charging and discharging wedges: $\text{Bias}_b = (\text{MEF}_{b,\text{chg}} - \text{AEF}_{b,\text{chg}})/\eta_b - (\text{MEF}_{b,\text{dis}} - \text{AEF}_{b,\text{dis}})$ (identity error 5.7e-14). Because observed operator curtailment $q_{b,t}^{\text{obs}}$ represents post-battery residual curtailment, it cannot bound historical absorption of installed fleets; however, for an incremental expansion $\Delta c_{b,t} = \kappa c_{b,t}$ holding the empirical BA-aggregate charging shape fixed (omitting nodal LMPs, SOC constraints, and binding shadow prices), effective accessible spill $q_{b,t}^{\text{eff}}(\tau_{\text{local}}) = q_{b,t}^{\text{sys}} + \tau_{\text{local}} q_{b,t}^{\text{loc}}$ defines the fixed-profile BA-level upper-bound absorption $\rho_b^{\text{res}}(\kappa, \tau_{\text{local}}) = \sum_t \min(\kappa c_{b,t}, q_{b,t}^{\text{eff}}) / \sum_t \kappa c_{b,t}$ and exact break-even roots in Fig. 4a. Storage duration scaling (Proposition 1; 4-hour vs. 1-hour ratio = 4.0x, error 0.0e+00) predicts energy capacity (`BESS_MWh`), not power (`BESS_MW`), governs evening gas displacement.

### EPA CEMS marginal emission factors, 24x24 covariance, and Table 1-to-Table 2 bridge

For every metered BA and bilateral neighbor, we estimate 24-hour domestic CEMS MEF vectors ($\text{MEF}_{b,h}^{\text{dom}}$, $h = 1,\dots,24$) by first-differencing hourly stack CO2 on dispatchable domestic generation ($\Delta\text{Disp}_{b,t}$, omitting solar, wind, and BESS) within Year-Month x Hour fixed effects after a deterministic 8x MAD extreme-ramp mask. Joint 24-hour estimation yields exact 24x24 date-clustered covariance matrices $V_b^{\text{dom}}$ capturing within-day cross-hour correlation. Bilateral-trade-adjusted system MEFs weight domestic and neighbor 24-hour curves by hour-specific marginal domestic versus net-import shares estimated from 8x-MAD-trimmed net dispatchable need ($\Delta\text{Need} = \Delta\text{Demand} - \Delta\text{Solar} - \Delta\text{Wind} - \Delta\text{BESS}$), propagating actual neighbor covariance matrices $V_j^{\text{dom}}$. Consumption-weighted Naive AEFs use incoming-import-only bilateral weights (`imp_weight`) and full CAISO `fuelsource.csv` supply evaluated hour-by-hour on matched hours (`|BESS| > 0.5` MW). Reconciling Table 1 (`+7.4417` Mt) against our conditional v4 Table 2 estimator (`+7.4179` Mt) confirms the `0.0238` Mt gap arises solely from the conditional script using untrimmed $\Delta\text{Disp} + \Delta\text{Imp}$ (`-0.0247` Mt) and unfiltered `>0.0` MW BESS hours (`+0.0019` Mt); our `v5` unconditional engine (`scripts/03c_k1_v5_unconditional_audit.py`) matches Table 1's exact estimator and updates bilateral trade weights to exact same-window UTC interchange (`+7.4434` Mt full, `+3.9505` Mt 18-month same-window).

### Unconditional season-stratified UTC-day block bootstraps and leave-BA-out audits

To remove conditioning on fixed AEF, fixed historical discharge totals, and fixed 2025–2026 trade weights, we synchronize all 14 metered grids, all bilateral neighbors, daily UTC bilateral interchange flows, and CAISO curtailments on common physical UTC days (`2024-07-01` to `2026-09-30`, 822 UTC days; overlap `2024-07-01` to `2025-12-31`, 549 UTC days). We draw $B = 1,000$ contiguous moving-block resamples at block lengths $L = 7, 14,$ and $28$ days strictly stratified by meteorological season (`DJF`, `MAM`, `JJA`, `SON`, sampling contiguous $L$-day blocks strictly within contiguous seasonal spells so no block crosses year or season seams). On every resampled block, we simultaneously re-estimate same-window bilateral trade weights (`weight` and `imp_weight`), 24-hour domestic CEMS MEFs for all 14 BAs and neighbors, marginal domestic/import shares, exact hour-by-hour consumption-weighted AEF, hourly cycling and unfixed total discharge volumes, CAISO interior curtailment slopes, and Leave-CISO / Leave-ERCO / Leave-CISO-&-ERCO sub-fleet portfolios. We retain our earlier conditional 14-day intervals alongside the unconditional $L = 7, 14, 28$-day intervals in Table 2 and Supplementary Table 5.

### Out-of-sample validation, lead-lag diagnostics, and ERCOT matched stress test

We pre-specify four quantitative out-of-sample validation criteria: (1) temporal holdout (`2024-07..2025-09` training predicting held-out `2025-10..2026-09` hourly $\Delta\text{CO}_{2,\text{CEMS}}$) pooled and discharge-weighted $R^2 > 0.70$; (2) discharge-weighted calibration slope within $[0.85, 1.15]$; (3) held-out counterfactual discrepancy prediction error $< 10\%$; and (4) 9/9 sign preservation across 9-quarter leave-quarter-out cross-validation (`2024Q3..2026Q3`). All four criteria pass (Supplementary Table 6). To diagnose simultaneity versus contemporaneous stack displacement transparently, we estimate distributed $k = -3\dots+3$ h lead-lag regressions of $\Delta\text{CO}_{2,\text{CEMS},t}$ and $\Delta\text{Imports}_t$ on $\Delta\text{BESS}_{t-k}$ controlling for $\Delta\text{Load}_t$, $\Delta\text{Solar}_t$, $\Delta\text{Wind}_t$, and `YM x Hour FE` with date-clustered standard errors and joint Wald $F$-tests on future leads ($k = -3, -2, -1$). Earlier benchmarks (direct $-\partial\text{CO}_2/\partial\text{BESS}$, pure CEMS internal stack slopes $\Delta\text{CO}_2/\Delta\text{GrossLoad}$, and EPA eGRID 2023 rev2 Non-Baseload rates times $(1/\eta_b - 1)$, which is positive by algebraic construction when $\text{eGRID} > 0, \eta_b < 1$) are explicitly evaluated as measurement and order-of-magnitude cross-checks. For ERCOT's cross-regime renewable-penetration stress test (and `CISO` contrast), we supplement `YM x Hour FE` interior bins (`<20%`, `20–40%`, `40–60%`, `>60%`) with (a) fine-cell `Season x Hour x Load-Quartile` and `Season x Hour x Load-Decile` FE, (b) exact common-support `Season x Hour x Load-Quartile` cell-matched reweighting comparing `<40%` versus `>60%` renewables on identical cells, and (c) separate Midday Solar (`10:00–16:00`) versus Overnight (`23:00–06:00`) checks.

### Supporting 49-BA diurnal gas-displacement regressions

Supporting Sample 1 regressions (Supplementary Figs. 2–3 and Supplementary Tables 2–3) regress the monthly evening-minus-midday natural-gas spread normalized by 2019 mean demand ($d_{19,b}$) on installed $\text{BESS}_{b,t}/d_{19,b}$ across $G = 49$ BAs ($N = 4,589$ BA-months), weighted by pre-boom baseline demand $d_{19,b}$ to prevent tiny zero-gas hydroelectric districts (`DOPD`, 21.3% equal-weighted FWL leverage vs. 1.01% WLS) from distorting national slopes. Inference uses BA-clustered CR1, two-way clustering (Cameron et al., 2011; Abadie et al., 2023), exact LSDV-equivalent restricted wild cluster bootstraps under Rademacher and Webb 6-point weights ($B = 999$; Cameron et al., 2008; MacKinnon & Webb, 2018; Roodman et al., 2019; Webb, 2023), and 1,000-permutation randomization inference, alongside distributed 6-month timing tests, daily `BA x Year-Month FE` solar-swing dispatch (139,497 BA-days), MWh-versus-MW duration horse-races, and nuclear placebos.
