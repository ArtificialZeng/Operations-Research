## Abstract

Large-scale assessments of river deoxygenation rely predominantly on daytime grab samples collected during afternoon field hours (e.g., Water Quality Portal and GEMStat archives) or optical satellite retrievals constrained to sun-synchronous mid-morning overpass times (Landsat ~10:00–11:00 local solar time). Yet aquatic ectotherms experience acute physiological oxygen stress near nocturnal and early-morning oxygen minima ($DO_{\min}$), when photosynthesis ceases and community oxygen demand persists. Analyzing a screened continental network of 397 dam-, lock-, lake-, canal-, estuary-, swamp-, and salinity-filtered free-flowing freshwater U.S. monitoring stations spanning 38 states, 17 of 18 contiguous U.S. HUC-2 watershed basins, and 1,446,764 continuous daily observations (2005–2025)—representing monitored free-flowing reaches rather than a random census of all streams—we document a pervasive **diurnal thermal decoupling** in observed short-term temperature sensitivity. Controlling within each station for Year $\times$ Month fixed effects and nonlinear discharge ($\log Q, (\log Q)^2$; primary flow-controlled specification $N = 324$ stations, 607,735 warm-season river-days), daily minimum dissolved oxygen ($DO_{\min}$) declines at $-0.2319\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$—**25.1% faster** than barometric- and water-vapor-adjusted Benson–Krause physical equilibrium solubility ($-0.1855\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$; excess slope $-0.0465$, HUC-2 clustered $p = 6.34 \times 10^{-8}$, State $p = 2.40 \times 10^{-10}$)—whereas daytime $DO_{\max}$ declines **15.6% slower** than equilibrium solubility ($-0.1566\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$; buffering $+0.0289$, HUC-2 $p = 0.0070$). Warming-associated short-term daily temperature anomalies thus widen the diurnal oxygen chasm by $d(DO_{\min} - DO_{\max})/dT = -0.0754\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$ (87.3% of stations negative; 16 of 17 HUC-2 basins negative; HUC-2 $p = 7.70 \times 10^{-9}$, State $p = 1.98 \times 10^{-8}$, cluster bootstrap $p = 0.0002$), remaining invariant to exact diurnal $T_{\min}/T_{\max}$ solubility, specific conductance, turbidity, 2-km reach deduplication, and geospatial restriction via the 62,226-dam USACE National Inventory of Dams (intensifying to $-0.0795$ beyond 5 km of $\ge 500\text{ acre-ft}$ dams and $-0.0903\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$ beyond 5 km of any dam). Because short-term observational temperature sensitivities capture coupled metabolic, reaeration, irradiance, and subdaily hydrologic co-variation, they establish net diurnal divergence rather than a direct causal partition of dark respiration from physical gas exchange. Across 139 multi-year subdaily USGS stations (92,800 warm-season river-days, 2021–2024), we evaluate **both** full-day 24-hour minima ($DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$) and site-day-specific **astronomical sunset-to-sunrise nocturnal minima** ($DO_{\min,\text{night}}$, computed via $\cos(\omega_0) = -\tan(\phi)\tan(\delta)$). Overall, **67.0%** of daily minima fall strictly inside astronomical darkness (**85.3%** including the 2-hour post-sunrise metabolic lag), and on episodic hypoxic cycles **72.6%–75.0%** of first threshold-crossings ($<5$ and $<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) initiate during astronomical night (median onset 22:30–23:30 LST), persisting continuously for a median of **8.0–11.0 h day$^{-1}$** ($<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) and **7.0–9.0 h day$^{-1}$** ($<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$). Afternoon grab-sample windows (13:00–16:00 local clock) underestimate short-term temperature sensitivity by **53.4%** against $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ (**51.6%** against strict astronomical $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$, $p = 4.13 \times 10^{-6}$; 72.8% under instantaneous grab temperature; 31.3%–33.5% after discharge control) and miss **65.3%–72.6%** of $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ (**61.9%–69.3%** of strict astronomical $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$) hypoxic days ($<5$ to $<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$). Restricting in-situ sensors to the **overpass-time (Landsat-window, 10:00–11:00 LST)** isolates the pure temporal sampling bias of sun-synchronous satellites (excluding optical retrieval error, cloud masking, and 16-day revisit intervals): it misses **52.8%–67.8%** of $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ (**49.0%–66.0%** of strict $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$) hypoxic days alongside **31.0%** (**28.4%** nocturnal) slope attenuation. Miss rates remain robust or *increase* under $\ge 90\%$ completeness ($\ge 86/96$ 15-min intervals day$^{-1}$: 63.7%–75.2% afternoon, 50.2%–71.4% overpass against $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$) and equal-site weighting (74.5%–74.7% afternoon, 68.6%–70.0% overpass against $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$). A well-conditioned physical transfer function (all variance inflation factors $<4.0$) anchored by the daytime supersaturation hinge eliminates **95.8%–97.6%** of mean concentration bias, cuts RMSE by **45.0%–56.8%** (matching or outperforming a 21-feature nonlinear spline benchmark), and recovers **69.7%–86.0%** of missing temperature sensitivity strictly out-of-sample across held-out HUC-2 basins. Under compound heatwave $\times$ low-flow droughts ($N = 319$), daytime $DO_{\max}$ shows zero significant decline ($+0.0555\text{ mg L}^{-1}$, $p = 0.605$) and daytime $<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ hypoxia shifts insignificantly ($+0.62\text{ pp}$, 95% CI $[-0.27, +1.51]\text{ pp}$, $p = 0.161$), whereas nocturnal $DO_{\min}$ drops $-0.8542\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ ($p = 2.19 \times 10^{-6}$) with a $+9.90\text{ pp}$ hypoxia surge (95% CI $[+4.83, +14.97]\text{ pp}$, $p = 7.72 \times 10^{-4}$; bounded night-minus-day gap $+9.28\text{ pp}$, 95% CI $[+4.89, +13.68]\text{ pp}$, $p = 3.83 \times 10^{-4}$) and super-additive $\text{Hot} \times \text{Dry}$ interactions across all thresholds ($<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$: $+2.25\text{ pp}$, 95% CI $[+0.85, +3.65]\text{ pp}$, $p = 0.0036$; $<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$: $+0.62\text{ pp}$, 95% CI $[+0.13, +1.12]\text{ pp}$, $p = 0.0173$; $<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$: $+0.52\text{ pp}$, 95% CI $[+0.08, +0.95]\text{ pp}$, $p = 0.0227$). Split-half baseline diurnal amplitude explains 47.5% of cross-site decoupling (Spearman $r = -0.676$, $p = 1.81 \times 10^{-37}$). Over 2005–2025 ($N = 194$), Clean Water Act abatement masks average secular nocturnal decline, yet faster-warming rivers ($\ge +0.25^\circ\text{C decade}^{-1}$) reverse those gains ($r = +0.295$, $p = 2.96 \times 10^{-5}$). Independent replication across 65 screened English stations (UK Environment Agency, 33,019 warm-season days, 19 1° grid clusters) reproduces diurnal decoupling ($-0.0947\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$, $p = 1.98 \times 10^{-4}$), excess $DO_{\min}$ sensitivity ($-0.0389$, $p = 0.0085$), daytime buffering ($+0.0557$, $p = 0.0355$), and exact 15-minute astronomical-night miss rates (55.6%–62.8% afternoon, 48.0%–54.0% overpass) outside U.S. jurisdiction.

## 1. Introduction

Dissolved oxygen ($DO$) governs the metabolic habitability, biogeochemical cycling, and biodiversity of flowing freshwaters (Odum, 1956; Bernhardt et al., 2018; Battin et al., 2023). Because oxygen solubility decreases nonlinearly with water temperature (Benson and Krause, 1984) while ectotherm oxygen demand rises steeply with warming (Pörtner, 2010), climate-driven river deoxygenation threatens freshwater ecosystems worldwide (van Vliet et al., 2023), paralleling widespread oxygen loss in oceans (Breitburg et al., 2018) and temperate lakes (Jane et al., 2021).

Three recent large-scale syntheses frame current understanding of river deoxygenation and hypoxia, each with distinct scope and observational constraints. First, **Zhi et al. (2023, *Nature Climate Change*)** analyzed nearly 800 U.S. and European rivers largely from routine grab-sample archives (Water Quality Portal and GEMStat) alongside watershed modeling, demonstrating that rivers are warming and deoxygenating faster than oceans and linking oxygen loss to temperature-driven solubility decline, land-use nutrient regimes, and urbanization. Second, **Blaszczak et al. (2023, *Limnology and Oceanography Letters*)** synthesized continuous high-frequency dissolved oxygen records across global streams and rivers, establishing the geographic prevalence, episodic nature, and hydrologic/thermal correlates (low discharge and warm temperatures) of lotic hypoxia. Third, **Guan et al. (2026, *Science Advances*)** coupled sun-synchronous Landsat optical imagery with stacked machine learning across 21,439 global river reaches (1985–2023), reporting sustained deoxygenation in 78.8% of global rivers ($-0.045\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ decade}^{-1}$) and attributing 62.7% of long-term decline to physical solubility, 22.7% to heatwaves, and ~12% to ecosystem metabolism.

Both routine grab-sample syntheses (Zhi et al., 2023) and sun-synchronous optical satellite products (Guan et al., 2026) share an underquantified temporal constraint: **they observe rivers almost exclusively during daylight hours**. Field monitoring crews overwhelmingly sample during working hours—concentrated in late morning and afternoon (13:00–16:00 local civil clock time)—whereas sun-synchronous Landsat 5–9 sensors overpass mid-latitude reaches within a narrow mid-morning window (~10:00–11:00 local mean solar time). Flowing waters, however, exhibit pronounced 24-hour oxygen cycles shaped by daytime gross primary production ($GPP$), continuous ecosystem respiration ($ER$), and physical air–water gas exchange (Odum, 1956; Hall and Hotchkiss, 2017; Appling et al., 2018). Because heterotrophic respiration typically exhibits higher thermal activation energy ($E_R \approx 0.65\text{ eV}$) than photosynthesis ($E_P \approx 0.32\text{ eV}$; Brown et al., 2004; Allen et al., 2005; Demars et al., 2011; Yvon-Durocher et al., 2012), short-term warm anomalies are expected to diverge asymmetrically between daytime maxima ($DO_{\max}$) and nocturnal/pre-dawn minima ($DO_{\min}$)—although in observational daily records, temperature-correlated shifts in reaeration, irradiance, and depth co-occur with metabolic rates and cannot be causally isolated without explicit metabolism-reaeration modeling (Appling et al., 2018).

If daily oxygen extrema decouple across the 24-hour cycle under warm conditions, daytime archives suffer a systematic **24-hour observational clock-bias** comprising two distinct errors: (i) **temperature-sensitivity attenuation**, whereby afternoon daytime buffering masks the steeper short-term temperature sensitivity of $DO_{\min}$, and (ii) **hypoxia-threshold blindness**, whereby single daytime or mid-morning overpass-time measurements miss sustained nocturnal excursions below chronic ($5.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$), acute stress ($3.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$), and severe hypoxic ($2.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) thresholds. Importantly, evaluating in-situ continuous sensors restricted to `10:00–11:00` local mean solar time isolates the **pure overpass-time (Landsat-window) sampling bias** of sun-synchronous optical platforms; actual remote-sensing error additionally encompasses optical retrieval uncertainty, clear-sky cloud masking, and 16-day revisit intervals, which are outside the scope of a purely temporal clock audit.

Building directly upon—while remaining distinct from—Zhi et al. (2023) and Blaszczak et al. (2023), this study makes **four specific, non-overlapping contributions**:
1. **Quantified continental diurnal decoupling in a rigorously dam- and salinity-screened monitoring network:** Across **397 screened free-flowing freshwater USGS stations** spanning 38 states and 17 HUC-2 basins (1,446,764 station-days, 2005–2025; **324 stations** in the primary flow-controlled specification), audited geospatially against all 62,226 dams in the USACE National Inventory of Dams (NID), we quantify short-term within-month temperature sensitivity of daily $DO_{\min}$ versus $DO_{\max}$ against barometric- and water-vapor-adjusted Benson–Krause solubility, compound heatwave $\times$ low-flow drought probability shifts with bounded night-minus-day contrasts, uniform discharge-controlled split-half diurnal-amplitude stratification, and 20-year secular trend confounding.
2. **Exact astronomical sunset-to-sunrise nocturnal window accounting, crossing timing, duration, and 24-hour clock-bias dissection:** Across **139 multi-year subdaily USGS stations** (92,800 warm-season river-days, 2021–2024), we reconstruct the complete 24-hour clock-bias curve, contrast afternoon grab-sample windows (13:00–16:00) against the **overpass-time (Landsat-window, 10:00–11:00 LST)** benchmark against **both** full-day $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ and exact site-day astronomical sunset-to-sunrise $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$, quantify first threshold-crossing solar timing and continuous hypoxia duration hours, and verify stability under $\ge 90\%$ 24-hour completeness ($\ge 86/96$ 15-min intervals day$^{-1}$) and equal-site versus site-day weighting.
3. **Cross-basin out-of-sample transfer-function bias correction:** We develop and validate an orthogonalized, well-conditioned transfer function (all VIF $<4.0$) under strict 17-fold Leave-One-HUC2-Basin-Out (LOHUC2) cross-validation and 2024 temporal holdout—benchmarked against a 21-feature nonlinear piecewise-spline model—demonstrating operational nocturnal drawdown correction from daytime grab or overpass-window observations without target-basin nocturnal calibration.
4. **Independent, spatially clustered trans-Atlantic replication:** Across **65 screened, 2-km-deduplicated free-flowing English monitoring stations** (UK Environment Agency Hydrology API, 33,019 warm-season river-days, 19 1° hydrometric grid clusters), we replicate diurnal thermal decoupling, both solar and UK civil BST afternoon biases, overpass-window sampling bias, and raw 15-minute astronomical sunset-to-sunrise hypoxia miss rates outside U.S. Clean Water Act jurisdiction.

## 2. Data Architecture, Purification Audit, and Methods

### 2.1 Screened Continental USGS Continuous Network, Hydrologic Purification, and USACE NID Linkage

We queried the official U.S. Geological Survey National Water Information System (`waterservices.usgs.gov`) across all 48 contiguous U.S. states plus the District of Columbia (49 jurisdictions) for every station reporting daily continuous dissolved oxygen (`parameterCd=00300`), retrieving expanded site metadata (`siteOutput=expanded`: `site_tp_cd`, coordinates, altitude `alt_va`, hydrologic unit code `huc_cd`, drainage area `drain_area_va`, and timezone `tz_cd`) and complete series catalogs (`seriesCatalogOutput=true`). Because continuous USGS DO probes preferentially instrument mid-to-large monitored rivers and regulated or water-quality-priority watersheds, the resulting sample constitutes a **screened continental free-flowing freshwater monitoring subset** rather than an unweighted random census of all U.S. streams.

Out of 1,577 discovered daily-value DO gauges nationwide, 565 stations met our pre-locked metadata eligibility criteria—applied strictly before inspecting any DO observations—requiring complete daily maximum (`00001`), minimum (`00002`), and mean (`00003`) dissolved oxygen plus daily mean water temperature (`00010`), with $\ge 1,000$ valid catalog days overlapping 2005–2025. To eliminate physical confounding from impounded, regulated tailrace, water-control, lentic, artificial conveyance, wetland-swamp, or coastal-tidal regimes, we enforced a transparent multi-stage exclusion audit (`data/processed/excluded_sites_audit.csv`), removing **148 stations**:
- **Dam, tailrace, lock-and-dam, powerhouse, water-control structure, and treatment-plant gauges ($n = 60$):** including lock channels (e.g., Des Plaines River Lock Channel, IL), numbered lock pools (Monongahela L&D2 Lower Pool, PA), Corps of Engineers structures (Caloosahatchee River at S-79 Franklin Lock & Dam and Reedy Creek at S-40, FL), John H. Kerr Dam / Buggs Island tailrace (`02079500`, VA), abbreviated treatment-plant reaches (`05406479` Cross Plains, WI; `03431091` Omohundro Water Plant, TN), and all four forensic dam/outflow stations flagged in prior pilot screening (`0212880025`, `03220510`, `421401121480900`, `03036000`).
- **Non-stream USGS site types ($n = 46$):** strictly enforcing `site_tp_cd == "ST"`, removing 35 tidal streams (`ST-TS`), 6 canals (`ST-CA`), 3 estuaries (`ES`), and 2 lakes (`LK`).
- **Lakes, reservoirs, impoundments, and springheads ($n = 35$):** e.g., upstream/downstream pools, backwaters, and karst spring vents.
- **Klamath / Link River / Keno lake-outflow system ($n = 16$):** hyper-eutrophic Upper Klamath Lake outflow gauges with lentic bloom advection.
- **Estuarine, coastal, marina, freshwater-tidal, and swamp reaches ($n = 15$)**—including Delaware River at Chester, PA (`01477050`, tidal freshwater estuary), Waccamaw River at Conway Marina (`02110704`, SC), and coastal swamps—plus **canals/ditches ($n = 13$)** and 2 invalid HUC-2 entries (several excluded stations triggered multiple rules).

Fetching full 2005–2025 daily continuous DO (max/min/mean), water temperature (max/min/mean), discharge (`00060`), specific conductance (`00095`), and turbidity (`63680`) for all 417 eligible stations yielded 403 stations passing physical quality control ($0 \le DO_{\min} \le DO_{\text{mean}} \le DO_{\max} \le 25\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ within $0.15\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ rounding tolerance, diurnal swing $\le 22\text{ mg L}^{-1}$, $-2 \le T \le 42^\circ\text{C}$, $\ge 1,000$ paired days, and $\ge 400$ May–October warm-season days across $\ge 3$ years).

A final **water-chemistry salinity screen** (`median SpCond > 5,000 uS/cm`, approximately $>2.7\text{ PSU}$) caught **6 unflagged marine/estuarine stations** classified by USGS as inland `ST` without tidal naming—including three South Carolina gauges at seawater salinity (`02176640`, `02176603`, `02176585`; $47{,}100\text{–}48{,}500\text{ }\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}$), plus Arroyo Colorado, TX ($22{,}200\text{ }\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}$), New River at Jacksonville, NC ($17{,}300\text{ }\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}$), and Broadkill River, DE ($6{,}590\text{ }\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}$). Removing these 6 brackish gauges yields our final screened subset of **397 free-flowing freshwater CONUS monitoring stations** across **38 states/jurisdictions** and **17 of 18 HUC-2 basins** (only HUC-08 Lower Mississippi lacked a qualifying inland station after filtering), totaling **1,446,764 station-days** (771,486 warm-season days). Of these, **324 stations** (36 states, 17 HUC-2 basins) provide $\ge 400$ warm-season days of co-located continuous discharge $Q$ passing full-rank checks (**primary discharge-controlled Spec 2**), **323** have exact daily $T_{\min}$ and $T_{\max}$, **315** have continuous specific conductance, **133** have turbidity, **311** pass a secondary ultra-strict zero-infrastructure flag, and **194** provide long-term $\ge 10$-warm-season secular records.

Because station names alone can omit an upstream dam located several kilometers away, we geospatially linked every gauge to the complete official **U.S. Army Corps of Engineers National Inventory of Dams (NID; 62,226 CONUS dams)** using exact spherical great-circle distances (`cKDTree` in 3D ECEF coordinates). Among the 324 primary flow-controlled stations, **242** lie $>5\text{ km}$ from any NID dam $\ge 500\text{ acre-ft}$, **247** lie $>10\text{ km}$ from any major reservoir $\ge 5{,}000\text{ acre-ft}$, and **141** lie $>5\text{ km}$ from *any* NID structure of any size (including small farm ponds). We also evaluate a strictly **2-km reach-deduplicated** subsample ($N = 318$).

### 2.2 Exact Altitude-Adjusted and Diurnal Benson–Krause Thermodynamics

Following USGS TWRI Book 9 and Benson and Krause (1984), sea-level freshwater oxygen saturation at temperature $T_K = T_C + 273.15$ is:

$$DO_{\text{sat,1atm}}(T_C) = \exp\left(-139.34411 + \frac{1.575701 \times 10^5}{T_K} - \frac{6.642308 \times 10^7}{T_K^2} + \frac{1.243800 \times 10^{10}}{T_K^3} - \frac{8.621949 \times 10^{11}}{T_K^4}\right)$$

To account for lower atmospheric pressure in mountain watersheds (e.g., HUC-14 Upper Colorado and HUC-10 Missouri gauges at 1,500–2,500 m elevation), we compute exact barometric- and water-vapor-adjusted saturation at station elevation $z$ (meters, converted from USGS `alt_va`, clamped at $0\text{ m}$ when sea-level or missing):

$$DO_{\text{sat,alt}}(T_C, z) = DO_{\text{sat,1atm}}(T_C) \cdot \frac{P_{\text{atm}}(z) - P_{wv}(T_K)}{1 - P_{wv}(T_K)}, \quad P_{\text{atm}}(z) = (1 - 2.25577 \times 10^{-5} z)^{5.25588}$$

where $P_{wv}(T_K) = \exp(11.8571 - 3840.70/T_K - 216961/T_K^2)$ in atm. Because water temperature also oscillates diurnally, we additionally compute exact pre-dawn solubility $DO_{\text{sat,alt}}(T_{\min}, z)$ and afternoon peak solubility $DO_{\text{sat,alt}}(T_{\max}, z)$ across all 323 stations with daily temperature extrema to verify that diurnal temperature swings do not artifactually generate excess slopes.

### 2.3 Full-Rank Econometric Identification and Multi-Level Spatial Cluster Inference

Warm-season (May–October) within-station short-term temperature sensitivity is estimated by absorbing every station-specific Year $\times$ Month cell ($\alpha_{i,ym}$, dropping cells with $<5$ days), controlling for inter-annual climate shifts, secular nutrient policy changes, sensor recalibrations, and seasonal photoperiod/phenology. Our primary flow-controlled specification (Spec 2, $N = 324$) adds flexible nonlinear discharge controls ($\log Q_{it}$ and $(\log Q_{it})^2$) to condition on daily hydrodynamic dilution, residence time, and reaeration velocity:

$$Y_{it} = \alpha_{i,ym} + \beta_i T_{it} + \gamma_{1i} \log Q_{it} + \gamma_{2i} (\log Q_{it})^2 + \varepsilon_{it}$$

where $Y_{it} \in \{DO_{\text{sat,alt}}, DO_{\max}, DO_{\text{mean}}, DO_{\min}, DO_{\min} - DO_{\max}, DO_{\min} - DO_{\text{sat,alt}}, DO_{\max} - DO_{\text{sat,alt}}, DO_{\text{mean}} - DO_{\min}\}$. Every within-station Frisch–Waugh–Lovell (FWL) demeaned design matrix is verified for full rank and well-conditioned singular values ($\sigma_{\min} > 10^{-8}\sigma_{\max}$), and verified numerically against explicit dummy-variable OLS to $<10^{-10}$. We emphasize that $\beta_i$ measures **observed short-term (within-month) temperature sensitivity of daily DO extrema**, capturing net coupled responses of ecosystem respiration, daytime primary production, temperature-correlated solar irradiance/cloudiness, and unmodeled subdaily reaeration or depth dynamics—not a direct causal partition of respiration from physical gas exchange.

Because proximate stations share synoptic weather, geology, and regional land use, standard station-level $t$-tests overstate precision. We therefore conduct primary inference using CR1 cluster-robust standard errors with finite-sample $t(G - 1)$ critical values (Cameron and Miller, 2015) clustered separately at two spatial hierarchies: **HUC-2 watershed basins** ($G = 17$) and **U.S. states** ($G = 36$), complemented by 5,000-replication pairs cluster bootstraps and conservative equal-cluster-mean $t$ and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. We further confirm equivalence with a single double-precision (`float64`) pooled 607,735-river-day panel regression (Spec 6) absorbing all 21,155 Station $\times$ Year $\times$ Month fixed effects simultaneously with exact $(G/(G-1)) \cdot (N-1)/(N - K_{\text{FE}} - k)$ CR1 degrees-of-freedom scaling.

### 2.4 Subdaily 24-Hour Clock Census, Exact Astronomical Night Geometry, Transfer Function, and UK EA Replication

To resolve the continuous 24-hour observational clock-bias, we deterministically selected 140 active 2021–2024 screened USGS stations balanced round-robin across all 17 populated HUC-2 basins and 25 states via SHA-256 hash ordering of `site_no` (zero outcome peeking). Fetching four warm seasons (May–October 2021–2024) of subdaily (`00300` DO and `00010` temperature; 114 stations at 15-min, 4 at 5-min, 12 at 30-min, 8 at 60-min, and 1 at ~15-min cadence) yielded **139 valid screened stations** (99.3% yield, **92,800 complete warm-season river-days**, baseline requiring $\ge 20$ distinct observed hours of the 24-hour cycle). Using exact USGS timezone codes (`tz_cd`) and station longitude, every subdaily timestamp is converted to both **local civil clock time** (governing afternoon field grab-sampling windows: 13:00–16:00 wide and 13:30–15:30 core) and **local mean solar time (LMST)**, $\text{LST} = (\text{UTC} + \text{lon}/15^\circ) \bmod 24$ (governing the sun-synchronous **overpass-time [Landsat-window, 10:00–11:00 LST]** benchmark and solar irradiance). We compute both hourly slopes $dDO(h)/dT$ and within-station hourly excess slopes $d(DO(h) - DO_{\text{sat,alt}})/dT$ across all 24 solar hours. Importantly, restricting in-situ probes to `10:00–11:00` LST isolates the **pure temporal overpass-time sampling bias** of sun-synchronous optical platforms; actual satellite remote-sensing error additionally includes optical retrieval uncertainty, cloud-cover masking, and 16-day revisit gaps not modeled here.

**Exact Astronomical Sunset-to-Sunrise Nocturnal Window, Crossing Timing, Duration, and Completeness/Weighting Sensitivity:** Because the full 24-hour minimum ($DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$) can occasionally occur after sunrise or during daytime storms/advection, we compute exact **site-day astronomical sunrise and sunset solar hours** across all 139 stations ($92{,}800$ site-days) from station latitude $\phi$ and day-of-year $\text{DOY}$ using the standard 3-harmonic Spencer–Iqbal solar declination series $\delta(\gamma)$ ($\gamma = 2\pi(\text{DOY}-1)/365$) and geometric sunset hour-angle equation (Iqbal, 1983):

$$\cos(\omega_0) = -\tan(\phi)\tan(\delta), \quad t_{\text{sunrise}} = 12 - \frac{\omega_0}{15^\circ}, \quad t_{\text{sunset}} = 12 + \frac{\omega_0}{15^\circ}$$

Across our 139 CONUS stations ($27^\circ\text{N}\text{–}49^\circ\text{N}$, May–October), astronomical sunrise spans 04:00–07:06 LST (mean 05:19), sunset spans 16:54–20:00 LST (mean 18:41), and astronomical night averages 10.63 h. For every site-day we define strict nocturnal oxygen minimum $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$ strictly over astronomical darkness ($t_{\text{solar}} < t_{\text{sunrise}}$ or $t_{\text{solar}} \ge t_{\text{sunset}}$) alongside full-day $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ (15-min and hourly solar bins). To eliminate calendar-midnight boundary splitting when measuring hypoxia onset and duration, we additionally construct all **91,495 consecutive site-day pairs** into unbroken midday-to-midday diurnal cycles ($\text{noon}_d \to \text{noon}_{d+1}$, where astronomical darkness is strictly $[t_{\text{sunset},d}, 24) \cup [0, t_{\text{sunrise},d+1})$ paired against day $d+1$'s `10:00–11:00` overpass and `13:00–16:00` afternoon windows), computing: (i) exact **first threshold-crossing solar hour** ($<5$, $<4$, $<3$, $<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) and share initiating strictly during astronomical darkness; (ii) **exact continuous hypoxia duration (hours day$^{-1}$)**; and (iii) **24-hour completeness and weighting sensitivity**, tightening completeness to $\ge 90\%$ of 96 15-min intervals ($\ge 86$ intervals day$^{-1}$; $N = 120$ stations, $78{,}405$ site-days) and cadence-normalized $\ge 22/24$ solar hours ($92{,}365$ site-days, 99.5%), while comparing pooled **site-day weighting** against **equal-site weighting** (unweighted average across hypoxic stations, verified also at $\ge 5$ hypoxic days per station).

For bias correction, we formulate an orthogonalized nocturnal-drawdown transfer function predicting $\Delta_{\text{noc}} = DO_{\text{obs}}(h) - DO_{\min} \ge 0$ strictly from observable daytime/overpass variables using exact altitude- and vapor-adjusted saturation $DO_{\text{sat,alt}}(T_{\text{obs}}, z)$: signed saturation excess $E = DO_{\text{obs}} - DO_{\text{sat,alt}}$, the nonlinear **daytime supersaturation hinge** $S_+ = \max(0, E)$, centered water temperature $T_{20} = T_{\text{obs}} - 20^\circ\text{C}$, $T_{20}^2$, $S_+ \cdot T_{20}$, seasonal harmonics ($\sin/\cos(2\pi \cdot \text{DOY}/365.25)$), latitude, longitude, and elevation. Dropping collinear raw $DO_{\text{sat,alt}}$ reduces maximum VIF from $>1{,}100$ to **3.09–4.00** (condition number **4.04–4.30**) and centers the supersaturation slope at $20^\circ\text{C}$. We validate strictly out-of-sample via **17-fold Leave-One-HUC2-Basin-Out (LOHUC2) spatial cross-validation** and **2021–2023 $\to$ 2024 temporal holdout**, benchmarking against an expanded **21-feature nonlinear piecewise-spline + interaction model** on identical folds.

Finally, to test external validity outside U.S. Clean Water Act waters, we queried the UK Environment Agency Hydrology API (`environment.data.gov.uk/hydrology/id/stations`), discovering 1,912 continuous DO stations across England. Applying an identical metadata filter—requiring multi-year paired subdaily DO ($\text{mg L}^{-1}$) and temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$), deduplicating probes within **2.0 km**, and systematically excluding **67** tidal Thames/Tyne/Witham estuary gauges, Windermere/Esthwaite/Ennerdale lake outflows, coastal outlets, weirs, dams, locks, reservoirs, sewage treatment/CSO outfalls, mine adits, wetland dykes, and groundwater boreholes (`GW_`)—yielded a **screened English subset of 65 independent free-flowing monitoring stations** spanning **33,019 complete warm-season river-days** ($\ge 20$ observed hours/day across 2–6 warm seasons). We compute spatially clustered UK inference via CR1 $t(G-1)$ SEs and 5,000-rep bootstraps across **19 1° $\times$ 1° hydrometric grid cells** (verified across **54 river-name clusters**), evaluating both solar (13:00–16:00 LST) and UK civil summer (13:00–16:00 BST) afternoon windows, the `10:00–11:00` LST overpass window, and exact raw **15-minute astronomical sunset-to-sunrise** nocturnal minima.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Continental Diurnal Thermal Decoupling and Hypoxia Blindness

Across our 397 screened free-flowing freshwater CONUS monitoring stations (1,446,764 station-days), daily minimum hypoxia is widespread yet largely hidden from daily-mean or daytime-maximum summaries. Stations recorded **103,272 days below the EPA chronic criterion ($DO_{\min} < 5.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$)** across 318 stations (80.1% of the screened network), of which **55.2%** were missed by daily-mean $DO_{\text{mean}}$ and **75.5%** were missed by daytime $DO_{\max}$ (78.1% missed during warm months May–October). Severe thresholds show identical masking: **54.7%–55.0%** of $<3.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ (26,917 days, 221 stations) and $<2.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ (12,570 days, 160 stations) hypoxic days are missed by $DO_{\text{mean}}$, and **72.1%–72.9%** are missed by $DO_{\max}$.

Crucially, short-term temperature anomalies do not shift the diurnal oxygen cycle uniformly downward—they **pull daytime maxima and daily minima apart** (Fig. 1, Table 1). In our primary flow-controlled specification (Spec 2: Year $\times$ Month FE $+ \log Q + (\log Q)^2$, $N = 324$ stations, 36 states, 17 HUC-2 basins), altitude- and vapor-adjusted Benson–Krause equilibrium solubility declines at $-0.1855\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$ (100.0% negative). Daily minimum $DO_{\min}$ declines at **$-0.2319\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$** (99.4% negative; HUC-2 $p = 1.49 \times 10^{-15}$, State $p = 8.05 \times 10^{-28}$), running **25.1% faster** than equilibrium solubility (excess slope **$-0.0465\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$**; 89.5% of stations negative; HUC-2 $t = -9.41, p = 6.34 \times 10^{-8}$; State $t = -8.76, p = 2.40 \times 10^{-10}$; bootstrap $p = 0.0002$). Conversely, daytime $DO_{\max}$ declines at **$-0.1566\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$**, running **15.6% slower** than equilibrium solubility ($DO_{\max} - DO_{\text{sat}} = +0.0289\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$; HUC-2 $t = +3.09, p = 0.0070$; State $p = 0.0093$; bootstrap $p = 0.0002$). Together, these opposing departures from physical solubility open a continental diurnal decoupling slope of **$d(DO_{\min} - DO_{\max})/dT = -0.0754\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$** (median $-0.0562$, 87.3% negative; HUC-2 95% CI $[-0.0900, -0.0608]$, $t = -10.95, p = 7.70 \times 10^{-9}$; State $t = -7.22, p = 1.98 \times 10^{-8}$; equal-HUC2-mean $-0.0631, p = 2.93 \times 10^{-5}$).

Three quantitative findings distinguish the afternoon grab-sample and sun-synchronous overpass-time sampling regimes:
1. **Pre-dawn solar time (05:00–07:00 LST) is unbiased for short-term temperature sensitivity:** its slope ($-0.2124$) differs from $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ ($-0.2143$) by only 0.9% (HUC-2 $p = 0.467$).
2. **Afternoon grab samples (13:00–16:00 civil clock; typical of WQP/GEMStat and Zhi et al., 2023) suffer severe dual distortion:** apparent temperature sensitivity flattens from $-0.2143$ to $-0.1000\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$—an underestimation of **53.4%** (**54.1%** in the 13:30–15:30 core; **51.6%** against strict astronomical $DO_{\min,\text{night}} = -0.2064$, HUC-2 $t = +6.82, p = 4.13 \times 10^{-6}$)—affecting **93.5% (130/139) of stations** (HUC-2 $t = +6.86, p = 3.81 \times 10^{-6}$; State $p = 5.69 \times 10^{-7}$). When $DO(h)$ is regressed on **instantaneous grab-sample water temperature $T(h)$**—mirroring how routine grab-sample models pair $DO$ and $T$ from the same field visit—slope attenuation reaches **72.8%–74.7%** ($-0.0583$ to $-0.0541\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$, HUC-2 $p = 1.15 \times 10^{-6}$), because warmer sunny afternoons jointly raise $T(h)$ and daytime photosynthetic $DO$. Even after conditioning on nonlinear discharge ($N = 116$), afternoon sampling underestimates temperature sensitivity by **33.5%** against $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ ($p = 5.12 \times 10^{-4}$) and **31.3%** against strict astronomical $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$ ($p = 8.76 \times 10^{-4}$). Simultaneously, afternoon $DO$ sits **$+1.64\text{ mg L}^{-1}$** above $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ (HUC-2 $p = 3.00 \times 10^{-9}$), missing **65.3%** ($<5$), **66.7%** ($<4$), **72.6%** ($<3$), and **68.8%** ($<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) of daily hypoxic episodes.
3. **Overpass-time (Landsat-window, 10:00–11:00 LST) sampling isolates severe hypoxia blindness alongside intermediate slope attenuation:** Restricting in-situ sensors to the 10:00–11:00 LST sun-synchronous overpass window misses **52.8%** ($<5$), **57.9%** ($<4$), **67.8%** ($<3$), and **65.3%** ($<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) of daily hypoxic days (mean concentration bias $+1.17\text{ mg L}^{-1}$, HUC-2 $p = 3.29 \times 10^{-9}$), while underestimating temperature sensitivity by **31.0%** against $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ (**28.4%** against strict astronomical $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$, HUC-2 $p = 1.13 \times 10^{-6}$; 33.1% instantaneous $T(h)$; 19.6% discharge-controlled vs. $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ and 16.9% vs. $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$, $p = 5.94 \times 10^{-4}$). Head-to-head within the same 139 stations, afternoon grab sampling incurs significantly steeper slope attenuation than the 10:00–11:00 overpass window ($+0.0479\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$, HUC-2 $t = +4.90, p = 1.60 \times 10^{-4}$).

**Strict Astronomical Sunset-to-Sunrise Audit, Onset Timing, Continuous Duration, and Completeness/Weighting Sensitivity (Table 2b):** Auditing every site-day against exact astronomical darkness ($\text{sunset} \to \text{sunrise}$) confirms that full-day clock-bias findings are neither artifacts of daytime storm troughs nor artifacts of subdaily data gaps or chronically hypoxic site dominance:
- **Nocturnal timing of minima and onset:** Across all 92,800 warm-season river-days, **67.0%** of daily oxygen minima occur strictly inside astronomical darkness, and **85.3%** occur within astronomical darkness plus the 2-hour post-sunrise metabolic lag (when low solar elevation and riparian shading keep respiration above production). Among hypoxic days, **53.7%** ($<5$), **61.4%** ($<3$), and **67.8%** ($<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) of daily minima occur strictly inside astronomical night (**76.6%**, **82.3%**, and **82.0%** including the 2-hour post-sunrise lag). Along unbroken consecutive midday-to-midday cycles ($91{,}495$ pairs), **72.6%** ($<5$), **75.0%** ($<3$), and **63.9%** ($<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) of episodic (non-midday-persistent) first threshold-crossings initiate strictly during astronomical darkness, with median onset at **22:30 LST** (IQR 19:30–02:30) for $<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ and **23:30 LST** for $<3$ (IQR 20:30–01:30) and $<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ (IQR 18:30–02:30).
- **Sustained ecological exposure duration:** Hypoxic episodes are multi-hour nocturnal events rather than brief sensor excursions, persisting continuously for a mean of **12.2 h day$^{-1}$** (median **11.0 h**, IQR 5–19 h) across all $<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ cycles and **8.5 h day$^{-1}$** (median **8.0 h**, IQR 4–12 h) on episodic overnight-onset cycles; **10.1 h** all / **7.7 h** episodic (medians 9.0 / 7.0 h) at $<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$; and **9.4 h** all / **6.5 h** episodic (medians 7.0 / 5.0 h) at $<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ (`tables/table2d_nocturnal_crossing_duration.csv`).
- **Strict astronomical night, $\ge 90\%$ completeness, and equal-site weighting sensitivity:** Evaluating miss rates strictly against astronomical $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$ yields **61.9% / 69.3% / 63.7%** (afternoon) and **49.0% / 66.0% / 60.4%** (overpass window) across $<5 / <3 / <2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$—rising to **63.6% / 71.9% / 68.0%** (afternoon) and **50.5% / 68.0% / 62.8%** (overpass) under true consecutive overnight continuity ($\text{sunset}_d \to \text{sunrise}_{d+1}$). Tightening 24-hour completeness to **$\ge 90\%$ of 96 15-min intervals** ($\ge 86$ intervals day$^{-1}$; $N = 120$ stations, $78{,}405$ site-days) slightly *increases* both slope attenuation (**56.1%** $24\text{h}$ / **54.3%** astro night afternoon; **32.6%** / **29.7%** overpass) and miss rates (**67.0%–77.7%** $24\text{h}$ and **63.7%–75.2%** astro night afternoon; **54.2%–72.8%** $24\text{h}$ and **50.2%–71.4%** astro night overpass). Most notably, switching from pooled site-day weighting to **equal-site weighting** (removing the influence of chronically 24-hour-hypoxic stations where daytime probes do detect hypoxia) *raises* miss rates by 8–19 percentage points to **74.5%–74.7%** (afternoon) and **68.6%–70.0%** (overpass) against strict astronomical $DO_{\min,\text{night}}$ (**77.3%–81.1%** and **71.6%–76.3%** against $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$; remaining 67.8%–73.1% afternoon and 60.2%–65.7% overpass even when requiring $\ge 5$ hypoxic days per station).

### 3.3 Out-of-Sample Bias-Correction Transfer Function

Can existing daytime grab-sample and mid-morning overpass archives be corrected to reconstruct nocturnal oxygen minima in unmonitored watersheds? Evaluating our orthogonalized, well-conditioned 10-feature transfer function (maximum VIF **3.09–4.00**, condition number **4.04–4.30**) under **17-fold Leave-One-HUC2-Basin-Out (LOHUC2) spatial cross-validation** (Fig. 2 Panel C; `tables/table2b_transfer_function_oos.csv`) demonstrates strong generalization using **zero nocturnal training data from the held-out basin**:
- **Afternoon Grab Core (13:30–15:30; 92,755 river-days; 13:00–16:00 wide virtually identical at 92,787 days):** eliminates **97.55%** of mean concentration bias ($+1.664 \to -0.041\text{ mg L}^{-1}$; wide 97.51%), cuts out-of-sample RMSE by **56.78%** ($2.341 \to 1.012\text{ mg L}^{-1}$; wide $2.312 \to 1.009\text{ mg L}^{-1}$; 2024 temporal holdout $2.295 \to 0.886\text{ mg L}^{-1}$), achieves out-of-sample $R^2 = 0.700$ on $DO_{\min}$ ($R^2 = 0.622$ on drawdown $\Delta_{\text{noc}}$), **recovers 85.98% of the missing temperature-sensitivity slope** ($-0.0984 \to -0.1981$ vs. true $-0.2144\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$; wide 85.86%), and raises $<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ hypoxia recall from **34.6% to 56.0%** ($<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$: $27.1\% \to 48.8\%$) while maintaining **97.9%–99.5% specificity** and **76.8%–78.5% precision** (81.1% at $<4\text{ mg L}^{-1}$).
- **Overpass-Time Correction (Landsat-Window, 10:00–11:00 LST; 92,477 river-days):** eliminates **95.83%** of mean temporal bias ($+1.179 \to -0.049\text{ mg L}^{-1}$), cuts RMSE by **45.01%** ($1.668 \to 0.917\text{ mg L}^{-1}$; 2024 holdout $1.637 \to 0.819\text{ mg L}^{-1}$), achieves $R^2 = 0.754$ on $DO_{\min}$ ($R^2 = 0.396$ on $\Delta_{\text{noc}}$), **recovers 69.72% of slope bias** ($-0.1479 \to -0.1942$ vs. $-0.2143$), and boosts $<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ hypoxia recall from **47.2% to 67.8%** ($<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$: $32.2\% \to 52.6\%$) at **98.0%–99.5% specificity** and **76.8%–80.5% precision** (81.8% at $<4\text{ mg L}^{-1}$).

Benchmarking against an expanded **21-feature nonlinear piecewise-spline + interaction model** on the identical 17 HUC-2 folds confirms that physical parsimony outperforms overparameterized flexibility: on afternoon samples the spline trims RMSE by only $0.005\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ ($1.006$ vs. $1.012$) while achieving *lower* slope-bias recovery (82.7% vs. 86.0%), and on the 10:00–11:00 overpass window our 10-feature physical hinge strictly **outperforms** the 21-feature spline in both held-out RMSE ($0.917$ vs. $0.923\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) and slope recovery (69.7% vs. 68.5%). Centered at $20^\circ\text{C}$, the total marginal overnight drawdown above saturation ($\beta_{E} + \beta_{S_+}$) is **$+1.088\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ per $\text{mg L}^{-1}$ of daytime supersaturation** in afternoon samples ($\beta_{E} = -0.023$, $\beta_{S_+} = +1.111$, $\beta_{S_+ \cdot T_{20}} = -0.0092$) and **$+1.080\text{ mg L}^{-1}$** in the 10:00–11:00 overpass window ($\beta_{E} = +0.010$, $\beta_{S_+} = +1.070$, $\beta_{S_+ \cdot T_{20}} = -0.0175$)—indicating that virtually 100% of daytime oxygen supersaturation dissipates overnight alongside an ~8%–9% undersaturation overshoot at warm temperatures.

Two findings stand out:
1. **Clean Water Act nutrient abatement masks average CONUS secular widening, but rapid warming reverses it:** Across 194 long-record screened CONUS stations (2005–2025), warm-season water temperatures rose significantly ($+0.228^\circ\text{C decade}^{-1}$, HUC-2 $p = 0.0034$, State $p = 3.20 \times 10^{-5}$), lowering physical solubility by $-0.0416\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ decade}^{-1}$ ($p = 0.0042$). Yet average raw $DO_{\min}$ ($+0.0729, p = 0.210$) and diurnal swing ($-0.0366, p = 0.664$) did not worsen on average; rather, $DO_{\min} - DO_{\text{sat}}$ *improved* by $+0.1144\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ decade}^{-1}$ (HUC-2 $p = 0.0270$, State $p = 0.0169$), consistent with ongoing Clean Water Act wastewater/BOD abatement (Keiser and Shapiro, 2019). Crucially, cross-site warming rates strongly govern secular trajectories: **faster-warming stations exhibit significantly steeper nocturnal $DO_{\min}$ declines (Spearman $r = -0.3290, p = 2.80 \times 10^{-6}$), wider secular diurnal swings ($r = +0.2951, p = 2.96 \times 10^{-5}$), and deeper nocturnal plunges ($r = +0.2775, p = 8.94 \times 10^{-5}$)**, whereas daytime $DO_{\max}$ shows no decline ($r = +0.0954, p = 0.186$). In slow-warming/cooling rivers ($n = 101$, mean $-0.113^\circ\text{C/dec}$), recovery lifts $DO_{\min}$ by $+0.1653\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ decade}^{-1}$ ($p = 0.0037$) and shrinks diurnal swing ($-0.165\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ decade}^{-1}$). In rapidly warming rivers ($\ge +0.25^\circ\text{C/dec}, n = 93$, mean $+0.598^\circ\text{C/dec}$), warming neutralizes those gains ($DO_{\min} = -0.0275\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ decade}^{-1}, p = 0.673$) while widening diurnal swings ($+0.103\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ decade}^{-1}$).
2. **Complete, spatially clustered trans-Atlantic replication in England (UK Environment Agency, $N = 65$ screened 2-km-deduplicated stations, 19 1° grid clusters, 33,019 warm-season river-days):** Despite cooler maritime climates (steeper equilibrium solubility slope $-0.2214\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$) and distinct European water governance, English stations replicate every core signature of the CONUS results (Fig. 5 Panel B):
   - Daily minimum $DO_{\min}$ declines at $-0.2604\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$ (98.5% of 65 stations negative, 1°-grid $p = 5.68 \times 10^{-13}$)—**17.6% faster** than equilibrium solubility ($-0.0389$, 1°-grid $p = 0.0085$, exact $T_{\min}$ solubility $-0.0358$, grid $p = 0.0135$).
   - Daytime $DO_{\max}$ declines at $-0.1657\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$—**25.2% slower** than equilibrium solubility ($+0.0557$, 1°-grid $p = 0.0355$, exact $T_{\max}$ solubility $+0.0571$, grid $p = 0.0404$).
   - Diurnal thermal decoupling is **$-0.0947\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$** (1°-grid CR1 $t = -4.65, p = 1.98 \times 10^{-4}$, bootstrap $p = 0.0002$; 54 river-system clusters $p = 3.46 \times 10^{-6}$).
   - Afternoon sampling underestimates short-term temperature sensitivity by **29.1%** in local solar time (13:00–16:00 LST; 1°-grid $p = 5.25 \times 10^{-4}$; $+1.47\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ bias; 63.0%–66.4% of $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ hypoxia missed) and **27.5%** in UK civil summer clock time (13:00–16:00 BST; 1°-grid $p = 6.21 \times 10^{-4}$; $+1.44\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ bias; 61.3%–64.9% missed). The **overpass-time (Landsat-window, 10:00–11:00 LST)** underestimates slope by **15.1%** (1°-grid $p = 0.0025$), overestimates DO by **$+1.18\text{ mg L}^{-1}$**, and misses **50.4%** ($<5$), **52.7%** ($<3$), and **58.4%** ($<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) of $DO_{\min,24\text{h}}$ hypoxic days.
   - **Exact raw 15-minute UK astronomical sunset-to-sunrise verification:** Across all 33,019 warm-season days, **58.8%** of raw 15-minute daily minima fall strictly inside astronomical darkness (**74.4%** within night + 2 h post-sunrise lag), strict astronomical $dDO_{\min,\text{night}}/dT = -0.2470\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$ (1°-grid $p = 1.10 \times 10^{-12}$; afternoon attenuation **25.2%**, overpass attenuation **10.5%**), and strict astronomical-night $<5 / <3 / <2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ miss rates remain severe at **56.7% / 58.5% / 62.8%** (afternoon solar; **55.6% / 56.5% / 61.1%** BST civil) and **48.0% / 48.4% / 54.0%** (overpass window 10:00–11:00 LST).
   - Overlaying exact within-station excess sensitivity $d(DO(h) - DO_{\text{sat}})/dT$ across all 24 solar hours (Fig. 5 Panel B) shows closely aligned trans-Atlantic phase symmetry: both networks peak above zero in daytime buffering at 14:00–15:00 LST and trough strictly below zero at 05:00–06:00 LST. An exploratory UK multi-year probe ($\ge 5$ warm seasons, $N = 10$ stations) shows rapid recent warming ($+1.34^\circ\text{C decade}^{-1}$) alongside an underpowered null on secular $DO_{\min}$ ($-0.156, p = 0.62$) and swing ($-0.008, p = 0.98$), confirming that the UK continuous sensor archive is too young for decadal trend detection.

## 4. Discussion and Implications for Global River Monitoring

Our findings clarify how observational timing reconciles prior global river deoxygenation syntheses and defines a distinct niche relative to foundational studies. **Zhi et al. (2023)** established widespread multi-decadal warming and deoxygenation across nearly 800 U.S. and European rivers largely from routine grab-sample archives, while **Blaszczak et al. (2023)** mapped global continuous-sensor hypoxia prevalence, episode durations, and low-flow/high-temperature drivers across streams and rivers. Neither study, however, reconstructed the continuous 24-hour observational clock-bias curve, quantified miss rates against strict astronomical sunset-to-sunrise windows under completeness/site-weighting controls, or provided a cross-basin validated transfer function to correct daytime archives. Similarly, when **Guan et al. (2026)** attributed 62.7% of global river deoxygenation to physical solubility and only ~12% to ecosystem metabolism using sun-synchronous Landsat (~10:30 LST) retrievals, their platform sampled near the mid-morning diurnal crossover where daytime buffering begins offsetting excess nocturnal sensitivity. Evaluated at $DO_{\min}$ in our screened CONUS and English networks, short-term temperature sensitivity runs **17.6%–25.1% steeper** than equilibrium solubility on average—and **42.2% steeper** in eutrophic stations ($-0.2623$ vs. $-0.1844\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$).

Equally consequential is the **asymmetry between afternoon grab-sample and mid-morning overpass-time sampling biases**. Afternoon grab samples (13:00–16:00 civil clock) suffer pronounced *double jeopardy*: they miss **61.9%–72.6%** of hypoxic days (and **74.5%–81.1%** under equal-site weighting) while cutting apparent short-term temperature sensitivity roughly in half (51.6%–53.4% under daily mean temperature; 72.8% under instantaneous grab temperature). By contrast, our **overpass-time (Landsat-window, 10:00–11:00 LST)** benchmark isolates the pure temporal component of sun-synchronous satellite sampling: it preserves a larger share of the temperature slope (still attenuated by 16.9%–31.0%), yet remains blind to **49.0%–67.8%** of hypoxic episodes (**68.6%–76.3%** under equal-site weighting). Because actual optical remote-sensing products (e.g., Guan et al., 2026) additionally incur optical retrieval noise, cloud masking (preferentially sampling clear, bright days with elevated photosynthesis), and 16-day revisit intervals, our 10:00–11:00 in-situ window should be interpreted strictly as a **pure temporal sampling benchmark** rather than a full end-to-end satellite error budget. Critically, our 17-fold Leave-One-HUC2-Basin-Out transfer function demonstrates that historical grab-sample and overpass-time archives need not be discarded: leveraging the physically transparent daytime supersaturation hinge ($S_+ = \max(0, DO_{\text{obs}} - DO_{\text{sat,alt}})$, matching or beating a 21-feature nonlinear spline benchmark) eliminates 96%–98% of mean temporal bias and recovers 70%–86% of missing temperature sensitivity without nocturnal training data from the target watershed.

Finally, our compound-extreme and secular-trend audits carry direct monitoring implications. During compound heatwave $\times$ low-flow droughts, daytime $DO_{\max}$ records show no significant oxygen decline ($+0.0555\text{ mg L}^{-1}, p = 0.605$) and no significant $<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ hypoxia surge ($+0.62\text{ pp}$, 95% CI $[-0.27, +1.51]\text{ pp}, p = 0.161$), completely obscuring a **$+9.90\text{ pp}$** nocturnal hypoxia surge (95% CI $[+4.83, +14.97]\text{ pp}$; bounded night-minus-day gap **$+9.28\text{ pp}$**, 95% CI $[+4.89, +13.68]\text{ pp}$, $p = 3.83 \times 10^{-4}$) driven by super-additive Hot $\times$ Dry interactions across $<5$, $<3$, and $<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$. Because episodic nocturnal hypoxia persists continuously for a median of 5–8 hours (and 7–11 hours across all hypoxic cycles), regulatory compliance programs relying on daytime grab sampling risk systematically overlooking ecologically lethal multi-hour nocturnal oxygen troughs—especially in warming watersheds outside Clean Water Act-style point-source abatement.

## 5. Limitations and Honest Journal-Tier Calibration

Scientific rigor requires explicitly bounding what this study establishes:
1. **Short-term observational temperature sensitivity vs. causal metabolic partitioning and multi-decadal attribution:** Our primary within-station specification (Year $\times$ Month FE $+ \log Q + (\log Q)^2$) isolates **short-term (within-month) temperature sensitivity of daily DO extrema**, not a direct causal separation of heterotrophic respiration ($ER$) from physical gas-exchange reaeration ($k_{O_2}$), solar irradiance, or subdaily flow/depth variability. Because warm anomalies co-vary with clear skies, lower viscosity/reaeration regimes, and shallow nighttime boundary layers, formal partitioning of $GPP$, $ER$, and $k_{O_2}$ requires explicit process-based inverse metabolism modeling (e.g., Appling et al., 2018). Likewise, 20-year CONUS secular means are confounded upward by Clean Water Act point-source BOD abatement (transparently decomposed by warming rate), and the UK EA continuous sensor archive (2–6 years) is too short for decadal trend detection.
2. **Screened monitoring subsets vs. random stream censuses, and northern-hemisphere geography:** Our 397 CONUS USGS stations (324 flow-controlled; 139 subdaily) and 65 English UK EA stations constitute **screened free-flowing freshwater continuous-monitoring subsets**, not probability-weighted censuses of all streams (small headwaters and unmonitored agricultural ditches are underrepresented). Moreover, public multi-year subdaily continuous DO networks remain sparse across tropical ($20^\circ\text{S}\text{–}20^\circ\text{N}$) basins, so extrapolating our transfer function to tropical rivers requires local sensor validation.
3. **Pure overpass-time temporal sampling bias vs. full satellite remote-sensing error:** Our 10:00–11:00 LST in-situ benchmark isolates the **pure temporal overpass-time (Landsat-window) sampling bias** of sun-synchronous optical platforms. It does **not** jointly model optical water-leaving reflectance retrieval uncertainty, clear-sky cloud-masking selection bias, or 16-day revisit sampling error, which should be combined with our temporal benchmark in future end-to-end satellite validation studies.
4. **Lack of continental co-located chlorophyll-$a$/PAR budgets, UK discharge pairing, and network-traced dam regulation:** Continuous USGS gauges rarely co-locate multi-year nitrate, chlorophyll-$a$, and PAR sensors with 15-year DO records (our turbidity probe returned an honest null because FNU conflates sediment and phytoplankton). Our UK EA replication lacks one-to-one co-located daily discharge for every DO probe, and while USACE NID Euclidean dam-distance filtering ($>5$ to $>10\text{ km}$) verifies that decoupling strengthens away from all 62,226 national dams, full NHDPlus network-traced degree-of-regulation (DOR) remains a valuable future refinement.

**Honest Journal-Tier Calibration:** With the screened 397-station CONUS network (324 flow-controlled) audited against the 62,226-dam USACE NID, exact astronomical sunset-to-sunrise nocturnal window accounting with first-crossing timing, continuous duration hours, and $\ge 90\%$ completeness/equal-site sensitivity across 139 subdaily stations, an orthogonalized 17-fold LOHUC2 transfer function outperforming nonlinear splines on overpass-window data, bounded super-additive compound Hot $\times$ Dry hypoxia contrasts, and spatially clustered 65-station UK EA replication (including raw 15-minute astronomical-night verification), this manuscript is competitive as a **stretch submission** to *Nature Climate Change* or *PNAS* and sits squarely in the **primary high-probability editorial sweet spot** of *Nature Water*, *Science Advances*, or *Nature Communications*.
