## Abstract

Global assessments of river deoxygenation rely predominantly on daytime grab samples collected during afternoon field hours (e.g., Water Quality Portal and GEMStat archives) or sun-synchronous satellite retrievals locked to mid-morning overpasses (Landsat ~10:30 local solar time). Yet aquatic ectotherms experience physiological oxygen stress at pre-dawn nocturnal minima ($DO_{\min}$), when photosynthesis ceases and temperature-accelerated community respiration peaks. Analyzing a continental census of 397 systematically dam-, lock-, lake-, canal-, estuary-, swamp-, and salinity-filtered free-flowing U.S. rivers spanning 38 states, 17 of 18 contiguous U.S. HUC-2 watershed basins, and 1,446,764 continuous daily observations (2005–2025), we uncover a pervasive **diurnal thermal decoupling**: controlling within each river for Year $\times$ Month fixed effects and nonlinear discharge ($\log Q, (\log Q)^2$; $N = 324$ rivers, 607,735 warm-season river-days), nocturnal $DO_{\min}$ declines at $-0.2319\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$—**25.1% faster** than barometric- and water-vapor-adjusted Benson–Krause physical solubility ($-0.1855\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$; biological excess $-0.0465$, HUC-2 clustered $p = 6.34 \times 10^{-8}$, State $p = 2.40 \times 10^{-10}$)—whereas daytime $DO_{\max}$ declines **15.6% slower** than solubility ($-0.1566\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$; photosynthetic buffering $+0.0289$, HUC-2 $p = 0.0070$). Consequently, warming widens the diurnal oxygen chasm by $d(DO_{\min} - DO_{\max})/dT = -0.0754\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$ (87.3% of rivers negative; 16 of 17 HUC-2 basins negative; HUC-2 $p = 7.70 \times 10^{-9}$, State $p = 1.98 \times 10^{-8}$, cluster bootstrap $p = 0.0002$), remaining invariant to exact diurnal $T_{\min}/T_{\max}$ solubility, specific conductance, turbidity, 2-km reach deduplication, and geospatial restriction via the 62,226-dam USACE National Inventory of Dams (intensifying to $-0.0795$ beyond 5 km of $\ge 500\text{ acre-ft}$ dams and $-0.0903\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$ beyond 5 km of any dam). Across 139 multi-year 15-minute USGS stations (92,800 warm-season river-days, 2021–2024), reconstructing the complete 24-hour clock-bias curve reveals that afternoon grab-sample windows (13:00–16:00 local clock) underestimate true nocturnal warming sensitivity by **53.4%** (72.8% under instantaneous grab-sample temperature; 33.5% after discharge control, $p = 5.12 \times 10^{-4}$) and miss **65.3%–72.6%** of pre-dawn hypoxic days ($<5$ to $<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$), whereas mid-morning Landsat overpasses (10:00–11:00 local solar time) suffer severe hypoxia-threshold blindness (**52.8%–67.8%** missed) alongside moderate slope attenuation (**31.0%**; 19.6% discharge-controlled). A well-conditioned, physically interpretable transfer function (all variance inflation factors $<4.0$) anchored by the daytime supersaturation hinge ($S_+ = \max(0, DO_{\text{obs}} - DO_{\text{sat,alt}})$, marginal drawdown $+1.08\text{–}1.09\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ per $\text{mg L}^{-1}$ supersaturation at $20^\circ\text{C}$) eliminates **95.8%–97.6%** of mean concentration bias, cuts RMSE by **45.0%–56.8%** (matching or beating a 21-feature nonlinear piecewise-spline benchmark), and recovers **69.7%–86.0%** of missing warming sensitivity strictly out-of-sample across held-out HUC-2 basins. Under compound heatwave $\times$ low-flow droughts ($N = 319$), daytime $DO_{\max}$ shows zero significant decline ($+0.0555\text{ mg L}^{-1}$, $p = 0.605$) while nocturnal $DO_{\min}$ collapses by $-0.8542\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ ($p = 2.19 \times 10^{-6}$) with super-additive biological deficit ($-0.0349\text{ mg L}^{-1}$, $p = 0.0288$) and super-additive hypoxia surges across all thresholds ($<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$: $+2.25\text{ pp}$ interaction, $p = 0.0036$; $+9.90\text{ pp}$ total, $16.0\times$ daytime; $<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$: $+0.62\text{ pp}$, $p = 0.0173$; $<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$: $+0.52\text{ pp}$, $p = 0.0227$). Uniform discharge-controlled split-half productivity explains 47.5% of cross-river decoupling (Spearman $r = -0.676$, $p = 1.81 \times 10^{-37}$). Over 2005–2025 ($N = 194$ long-record rivers), Clean Water Act nutrient abatement masks average secular nocturnal deoxygenation, yet faster-warming rivers ($\ge +0.25^\circ\text{C decade}^{-1}$) reverse CWA nocturnal gains and widen diurnal swings ($r = +0.295$, $p = 2.96 \times 10^{-5}$). Independent replication across 65 purified English rivers (UK Environment Agency, 33,019 warm-season river-days, 19 1° hydrometric grid clusters) reproduces diurnal decoupling ($-0.0947\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$, grid-clustered $p = 1.98 \times 10^{-4}$), nocturnal excess ($-0.0389$, $p = 0.0085$), daytime buffering ($+0.0557$, $p = 0.0355$), and afternoon/Landsat clock biases (27.5%–29.1% and 15.1% slope attenuation; 61.3%–66.4% and 50.4%–58.4% hypoxia blindness) outside U.S. jurisdiction.

## 1. Introduction

Dissolved oxygen ($DO$) governs the metabolic habitability, biogeochemical cycling, and biodiversity of flowing freshwaters (Odum, 1956; Bernhardt et al., 2018; Battin et al., 2023). Because oxygen solubility decreases nonlinearly with water temperature (Benson and Krause, 1984) while ectotherm oxygen demand rises exponentially with warming (Pörtner, 2010), climate-driven river deoxygenation threatens freshwater ecosystems worldwide (van Vliet et al., 2023; Blaszczak et al., 2023), mirroring widespread oxygen loss in oceans (Breitburg et al., 2018) and temperate lakes (Jane et al., 2021).

Two landmark continental-to-global paradigms currently anchor scientific understanding of warming-driven river deoxygenation. First, Zhi et al. (2023, *Nature Climate Change*) synthesized nearly 800 U.S. and European rivers from routine grab-sample archives (Water Quality Portal and GEMStat), finding widespread warming-driven oxygen decline and attributing deoxygenation to solubility reductions alongside watershed productivity and urbanization. Second, Guan et al. (2026, *Science Advances*) leveraged sun-synchronous Landsat satellite observations and stacked machine learning across 21,439 global river reaches (1985–2023), reporting sustained deoxygenation in 78.8% of global rivers ($-0.045\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ decade}^{-1}$) and attributing 62.7% of long-term decline to physical solubility, 22.7% to heatwaves, and only ~12% to ecosystem metabolism.

Both paradigms share an underexamined observational constraint: **they sample rivers almost exclusively during daylight hours**. Field monitoring crews populating grab-sample repositories overwhelmingly sample during working hours—concentrated in late morning and afternoon (13:00–16:00 local clock time)—while sun-synchronous Landsat 5–9 sensors cross mid-latitude rivers within a narrow mid-morning window (~10:00–11:00 local mean solar time). Yet flowing waters exhibit pronounced 24-hour metabolic oxygen cycles driven by daytime gross primary production ($GPP$) and continuous 24-hour ecosystem respiration ($ER$) (Odum, 1956; Hall and Hotchkiss, 2017; Appling et al., 2018). Crucially, Metabolic Theory of Ecology predicts an asymmetric thermal response across this diurnal cycle: heterotrophic respiration exhibits substantially higher thermal activation energy ($E_R \approx 0.65\text{ eV}$) than photosynthesis ($E_P \approx 0.32\text{ eV}$; Brown et al., 2004; Allen et al., 2005; Demars et al., 2011; Yvon-Durocher et al., 2012). Under climate warming, daytime photosynthesis can partially buffer—or even offset—Henry's Law solubility losses during bright afternoon hours, whereas unopposed nocturnal respiration accelerates oxygen drawdown toward lethal pre-dawn minima ($DO_{\min}$).

If warming asymmetrically widens the diurnal oxygen cycle, daytime monitoring archives suffer a systematic **24-hour observational clock-bias** with two distinct components: (i) **warming-sensitivity attenuation**, whereby afternoon photosynthetic compensation masks true nocturnal thermal sensitivity ($dDO_{\min}/dT$), and (ii) **hypoxia-threshold blindness**, whereby single daytime measurements miss acute pre-dawn excursions below regulatory chronic ($5.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$), acute stress ($3.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$), and severe hypoxic ($2.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) thresholds. Moreover, because reservoirs, dam tailraces, water-control structures, tidal estuaries, and karst springheads possess non-fluvial stratification, hypolimnetic releases, or saline mixing physics, any rigorous continental test requires systematically purifying continuous sensor networks to genuinely free-flowing freshwater rivers and auditing proximity to national dam inventories.

Here, we execute a comprehensive, multi-continental empirical audit built on three pillars:
1. **A strictly purified, dam-audited continental census of 397 free-flowing U.S. rivers** across 38 states and 17 of 18 contiguous U.S. HUC-2 watershed basins (1,446,764 continuous daily observations, 2005–2025), linked geospatially to all 62,226 dams in the USACE National Inventory of Dams (NID), isolating within-river thermal decoupling under Year $\times$ Month fixed effects, nonlinear discharge controls ($\log Q, (\log Q)^2$), exact diurnal $T_{\min}/T_{\max}$ barometric- and water-vapor-adjusted Benson–Krause solubility, water-quality covariates, compound heatwave $\times$ drought interactions, uniform discharge-controlled split-half out-of-sample productivity tests, and 20-year secular trend audits.
2. **A multi-year 15-minute subdaily 24-hour clock-bias census** across 139 clean U.S. rivers spanning 25 states and all 17 populated HUC-2 basins (92,800 warm-season river-days, 2021–2024), mapping warming-sensitivity attenuation and hypoxia-threshold blindness across every hour of local solar and civil clock time, directly contrasting afternoon grab-sample windows against Landsat 10:00–11:00 overpasses, and validating a well-conditioned out-of-sample transfer function against a 21-feature nonlinear piecewise-spline benchmark via 17-fold Leave-One-HUC2-Basin-Out cross-validation.
3. **An independent, spatially clustered trans-Atlantic replication** across 65 purified, 2-km-deduplicated free-flowing English rivers from the UK Environment Agency Hydrology API (33,019 warm-season river-days, 19 1° hydrometric grid clusters, 54 distinct river systems), verifying diurnal thermal decoupling, both solar and civil BST afternoon clock biases, and Landsat hypoxia blindness outside U.S. Clean Water Act jurisdiction.

## 2. Data Architecture, Purification Audit, and Methods

### 2.1 Continental USGS Continuous Census, Hydrologic Purification, and USACE NID Linkage

We queried the official U.S. Geological Survey National Water Information System (`waterservices.usgs.gov`) across all 48 contiguous U.S. states plus the District of Columbia (49 jurisdictions) for every station reporting daily continuous dissolved oxygen (`parameterCd=00300`), retrieving both expanded site metadata (`siteOutput=expanded`: `site_tp_cd`, coordinates, altitude `alt_va`, 8- to 12-digit hydrologic unit code `huc_cd`, drainage area `drain_area_va`, and timezone `tz_cd`) and complete series catalogs (`seriesCatalogOutput=true`).

Out of 1,577 discovered daily-value DO gauges nationwide, 565 stations met our pre-locked metadata eligibility criteria—applied strictly before inspecting any DO observations—requiring complete daily maximum (`00001`), minimum (`00002`), and mean (`00003`) dissolved oxygen plus daily mean water temperature (`00010`), with $\ge 1,000$ valid catalog days overlapping 2005–2025. To eliminate physical confounding from impounded, regulated tailrace, water-control, lentic, artificial conveyance, wetland-swamp, or coastal-tidal regimes, we enforced a transparent multi-stage exclusion audit (`data/processed/excluded_sites_audit.csv`), removing **148 stations**:
- **Dam, tailrace, lock-and-dam, powerhouse, water-control structure, and treatment-plant gauges ($n = 60$):** including lock channels (e.g., Des Plaines River Lock Channel, IL), numbered lock pools (Monongahela L&D2 Lower Pool, PA), Corps of Engineers structures (Caloosahatchee River at S-79 Franklin Lock & Dam and Reedy Creek at S-40, FL), John H. Kerr Dam / Buggs Island tailrace (`02079500`, VA), abbreviated treatment-plant reaches (`05406479` Cross Plains, WI; `03431091` Omohundro Water Plant, TN), and all four forensic dam/outflow stations flagged in prior pilot screening (`0212880025`, `03220510`, `421401121480900`, `03036000`).
- **Non-stream USGS site types ($n = 46$):** strictly enforcing `site_tp_cd == "ST"`, thereby removing 35 tidal streams (`ST-TS`), 6 canals (`ST-CA`), 3 estuaries (`ES`), and 2 lakes (`LK`).
- **Lakes, reservoirs, impoundments, and springheads ($n = 35$):** e.g., upstream/downstream pools, backwaters, and karst spring vents.
- **Klamath / Link River / Keno lake-outflow system ($n = 16$):** hyper-eutrophic Upper Klamath Lake outflow gauges with lentic bloom advection.
- **Estuarine, coastal, marina, freshwater-tidal, and swamp reaches ($n = 15$)**—including Delaware River at Chester, PA (`01477050`, River Mile 82 tidal freshwater estuary), Waccamaw River at Conway Marina (`02110704`, SC), and coastal swamps—plus **canals/ditches ($n = 13$)** and 2 invalid HUC-2 entries (several excluded stations triggered multiple rules).

Fetching full 2005–2025 daily continuous DO (max/min/mean), water temperature (max/min/mean), discharge (`00060`), specific conductance (`00095`), and turbidity (`63680`) for all 417 eligible stations yielded 403 stations passing physical quality control ($0 \le DO_{\min} \le DO_{\text{mean}} \le DO_{\max} \le 25\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ within $0.15\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ rounding tolerance, diurnal swing $\le 22\text{ mg L}^{-1}$, $-2 \le T \le 42^\circ\text{C}$, $\ge 1,000$ paired days, and $\ge 400$ May–October warm-season days across $\ge 3$ years).

A final **water-chemistry salinity screen** (`median SpCond > 5,000 uS/cm`, approximately $>2.7\text{ PSU}$) caught **6 unflagged marine/estuarine stations** classified by USGS as inland `ST` without tidal naming—including three South Carolina gauges at literal seawater salinity (`02176640` Beaufort River at Parris Island, $48{,}500\text{ }\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}$; `02176603` Beaufort River at Beaufort, $47{,}800\text{ }\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}$; `02176585` Brickyard Creek, $47{,}100\text{ }\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}$), plus Arroyo Colorado, TX ($22{,}200\text{ }\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}$), New River at Jacksonville, NC ($17{,}300\text{ }\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}$), and Broadkill River, DE ($6{,}590\text{ }\mu\text{S cm}^{-1}$). Removing these 6 brackish gauges yields our final purified census of **397 free-flowing freshwater CONUS rivers** across **38 states/jurisdictions** and **17 of 18 HUC-2 basins** (only HUC-08 Lower Mississippi lacked a qualifying inland station after filtering), totaling **1,446,764 station-days** (771,486 warm-season days). Of these, **324 rivers** (36 states, 17 HUC-2 basins) provide $\ge 400$ warm-season days of co-located continuous discharge $Q$ passing full-rank checks, **323** have exact daily $T_{\min}$ and $T_{\max}$, **315** have continuous specific conductance, **133** have turbidity, **311** pass a secondary ultra-strict zero-infrastructure flag, and **194** provide long-term $\ge 10$-warm-season secular records.

Because station names alone can omit an upstream dam located several kilometers away, we geospatially linked every gauge to the complete official **U.S. Army Corps of Engineers National Inventory of Dams (NID; 62,226 CONUS dams)** using exact spherical great-circle distances (`cKDTree` in 3D ECEF coordinates). Among the 324 primary Q-controlled rivers, **242** lie $>5\text{ km}$ from any NID dam $\ge 500\text{ acre-ft}$, **247** lie $>10\text{ km}$ from any major reservoir $\ge 5{,}000\text{ acre-ft}$, and **141** lie $>5\text{ km}$ from *any* NID structure of any size (including small farm ponds). We also evaluate a strictly **2-km reach-deduplicated** subsample ($N = 318$).

### 2.2 Exact Altitude-Adjusted and Diurnal Benson–Krause Thermodynamics

Following USGS TWRI Book 9 and Benson and Krause (1984), sea-level freshwater oxygen saturation at temperature $T_K = T_C + 273.15$ is:

$$DO_{\text{sat,1atm}}(T_C) = \exp\left(-139.34411 + \frac{1.575701 \times 10^5}{T_K} - \frac{6.642308 \times 10^7}{T_K^2} + \frac{1.243800 \times 10^{10}}{T_K^3} - \frac{8.621949 \times 10^{11}}{T_K^4}\right)$$

To account for lower atmospheric pressure in mountain watersheds (e.g., HUC-14 Upper Colorado and HUC-10 Missouri gauges at 1,500–2,500 m elevation), we compute exact barometric- and water-vapor-adjusted saturation at station elevation $z$ (meters, converted from USGS `alt_va`, clamped at $0\text{ m}$ when sea-level or missing):

$$DO_{\text{sat,alt}}(T_C, z) = DO_{\text{sat,1atm}}(T_C) \cdot \frac{P_{\text{atm}}(z) - P_{wv}(T_K)}{1 - P_{wv}(T_K)}, \quad P_{\text{atm}}(z) = (1 - 2.25577 \times 10^{-5} z)^{5.25588}$$

where $P_{wv}(T_K) = \exp(11.8571 - 3840.70/T_K - 216961/T_K^2)$ in atm. Because water temperature also oscillates diurnally, we additionally compute exact pre-dawn solubility $DO_{\text{sat,alt}}(T_{\min}, z)$ and afternoon peak solubility $DO_{\text{sat,alt}}(T_{\max}, z)$ across all 323 rivers with daily temperature extrema to verify that diurnal temperature swings do not artifactually generate biological excess slopes.

### 2.3 Full-Rank Econometric Identification and Multi-Level Spatial Cluster Inference

Warm-season (May–October) within-river thermal sensitivity is identified by absorbing every river-specific Year $\times$ Month cell ($\alpha_{i,ym}$, dropping cells with $<5$ days), completely controlling for inter-annual climate shifts, secular nutrient changes, sensor recalibrations, and seasonal photoperiod/phenology. Our primary hydrologic specification (Spec 2) adds flexible nonlinear discharge controls ($\log Q_{it}$ and $(\log Q_{it})^2$) to absorb hydrodynamic reaeration, residence time, dilution, and scouring effects:

$$Y_{it} = \alpha_{i,ym} + \beta_i T_{it} + \gamma_{1i} \log Q_{it} + \gamma_{2i} (\log Q_{it})^2 + \varepsilon_{it}$$

where $Y_{it} \in \{DO_{\text{sat,alt}}, DO_{\max}, DO_{\text{mean}}, DO_{\min}, DO_{\min} - DO_{\max}, DO_{\min} - DO_{\text{sat,alt}}, DO_{\max} - DO_{\text{sat,alt}}, DO_{\text{mean}} - DO_{\min}\}$. Every within-river Frisch–Waugh–Lovell (FWL) demeaned design matrix is explicitly verified for full rank and well-conditioned singular values ($\sigma_{\min} > 10^{-8}\sigma_{\max}$), and verified numerically against explicit dummy-variable OLS to $<10^{-10}$.

Because proximate rivers share synoptic heatwaves, geology, and regional land use, standard river-level $t$-tests overstate precision. We therefore conduct primary inference using CR1 cluster-robust standard errors with finite-sample $t(G - 1)$ critical values (Cameron and Miller, 2015) clustered separately at two spatial hierarchies: **HUC-2 watershed basins** ($G = 17$) and **U.S. states** ($G = 36$), complemented by 5,000-replication pairs cluster bootstraps and conservative equal-cluster-mean $t$ and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests. We further confirm full equivalence with a single double-precision (`float64`) pooled 607,735-river-day panel regression (Spec 6) absorbing all 21,155 River $\times$ Year $\times$ Month fixed effects simultaneously with exact $(G/(G-1)) \cdot (N-1)/(N - K_{\text{FE}} - k)$ CR1 degrees-of-freedom scaling.

### 2.4 Multi-Year 15-Minute IV 24-Hour Clock Census, Transfer Function, and UK EA Replication

To resolve the continuous 24-hour observational clock-bias, we deterministically selected 140 active 2021–2024 clean rivers balanced round-robin across all 17 populated HUC-2 basins and 25 states via SHA-256 hash ordering of `site_no` (zero outcome peeking). Fetching four full warm seasons (May–October 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024) of 15-minute `00300` DO and `00010` temperature yielded **139 valid clean rivers** (99.3% yield, **92,800 complete warm-season river-days**, each requiring $\ge 20$ distinct observed hours of the 24-hour cycle so partial-day sensor dropouts never bias daily extrema). Using exact USGS timezone codes (`tz_cd`: EDT/EST, CDT/CST, MDT/MST, PDT/PST) and station longitude, every 15-minute timestamp is converted to both **local civil clock time** (governing afternoon grab-sampling crews, 13:00–16:00 and 13:30–15:30) and **local mean solar time (LMST)**, $\text{LST} = (\text{UTC} + \text{lon}/15^\circ) \bmod 24$ (governing sun-synchronous Landsat 10:00–11:00 overpasses and solar irradiance). We compute both raw hourly slopes $dDO(h)/dT$ and exact within-river hourly biological excess slopes $d(DO(h) - DO_{\text{sat,alt}})/dT$ for all 24 solar hours.

For bias correction, we formulate a parsimonious, orthogonalized nocturnal-drawdown transfer function predicting $\Delta_{\text{noc}} = DO_{\text{obs}}(h) - DO_{\min} \ge 0$ strictly from observable daytime grab/satellite variables using the exact altitude- and water-vapor-adjusted saturation $DO_{\text{sat,alt}}(T_{\text{obs}}, z)$: signed saturation excess $E = DO_{\text{obs}} - DO_{\text{sat,alt}}$, the nonlinear **daytime supersaturation hinge** $S_+ = \max(0, E)$, centered water temperature $T_{20} = T_{\text{obs}} - 20^\circ\text{C}$, $T_{20}^2$, the thermal supersaturation interaction $S_+ \cdot T_{20}$, seasonal harmonics ($\sin/\cos(2\pi \cdot \text{DOY}/365.25)$), latitude, longitude, and elevation. Dropping raw $DO_{\text{sat,alt}}$ alongside $(T_{20}, T_{20}^2, z)$ eliminates severe thermodynamic multicollinearity (reducing maximum variance inflation factor from $>1,100$ to **3.09–4.00**, standardized condition number **4.04–4.30**) and centers the supersaturation slope directly at realistic warm-season river temperature ($20^\circ\text{C}$). We evaluate transfer performance strictly out-of-sample under **17-fold Leave-One-HUC2-Basin-Out spatial cross-validation** and **2021–2023 train $\to$ 2024 temporal holdout**, benchmarking our 10-feature physical model against an expanded **21-feature nonlinear piecewise-spline + interaction model** on the identical cross-validation folds.

Finally, to test international external validity outside U.S. Clean Water Act waters, we queried the public UK Environment Agency Hydrology API (`environment.data.gov.uk/hydrology/id/stations`), discovering 1,912 continuous DO stations across England. Applying an identical metadata filter—requiring multi-year paired subdaily DO ($\text{mg L}^{-1}$) and water temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$), deduplicating probes within **2.0 km** so adjacent reach sensors are never double-counted, and systematically excluding **67** tidal Thames/Tyne/Witham estuary gauges, Windermere/Esthwaite/Ennerdale lake outflows, coastal beach outlets, weirs, dams, locks, reservoirs, sewage treatment/CSO outfalls, mine adits, wetland dykes, and groundwater boreholes (`GW_`)—across all 81 clean candidates yielded **65 independent free-flowing English rivers** spanning **33,019 complete warm-season river-days** ($\ge 20$ observed hours/day across 2–6 warm seasons). Unlike naive river-level testing, we compute spatially clustered UK inference via CR1 $t(G-1)$ standard errors and 5,000-rep pairs bootstraps across **19 1° $\times$ 1° hydrometric grid cells** (and verify across **54 distinct river-name clusters**), while evaluating both solar afternoon (13:00–16:00 LST) and UK civil summer afternoon (13:00–16:00 BST = 12:00–15:00 UTC) windows.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Continental Diurnal Thermal Decoupling and Hypoxia Blindness

Across all 397 purified free-flowing CONUS rivers (1,446,764 station-days), nocturnal hypoxia is widespread yet overwhelmingly invisible to daily-mean or daytime-maximum metrics. Rivers experienced **103,272 nocturnal hypoxic days below the EPA chronic criterion ($DO_{\min} < 5.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$)** across 318 rivers (80.1% of all clean CONUS rivers), of which **55.2%** were missed by daily-mean $DO_{\text{mean}}$ and **75.5%** were missed by daytime $DO_{\max}$ (a $4.07\times$ ratio; 78.1% missed during warm months May–October). Severe pre-dawn thresholds exhibit identical blindness: **54.7%–55.0%** of $<3.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ (26,917 days across 221 rivers) and $<2.0\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ (12,570 days across 160 rivers) hypoxic events are missed by $DO_{\text{mean}}$, and **72.1%–72.9%** are missed by $DO_{\max}$.

Crucially, climate warming does not shift the diurnal oxygen cycle uniformly downward—it **pulls daytime maxima and nocturnal minima apart** (Fig. 1, Table 1). In our primary discharge-controlled specification (Spec 2: Year $\times$ Month FE $+ \log Q + (\log Q)^2$, $N = 324$ rivers, 36 states, 17 HUC-2 basins), altitude- and vapor-adjusted Benson–Krause physical solubility declines at $-0.1855\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$ (100.0% negative). Nocturnal $DO_{\min}$ declines at **$-0.2319\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$** (99.4% negative; HUC-2 $p = 1.49 \times 10^{-15}$, State $p = 8.05 \times 10^{-28}$), exceeding Henry's Law by **$-0.0465\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$** (+25.1% respiratory acceleration; 89.5% of rivers negative; HUC-2 $t = -9.41, p = 6.34 \times 10^{-8}$; State $t = -8.76, p = 2.40 \times 10^{-10}$; cluster bootstrap $p = 0.0002$). Conversely, daytime $DO_{\max}$ declines at **$-0.1566\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$**, running **15.6% slower** than physical solubility ($DO_{\max} - DO_{\text{sat}} = +0.0289\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$; HUC-2 $t = +3.09, p = 0.0070$; State $p = 0.0093$; bootstrap $p = 0.0002$). Together, this biological bifurcation opens a continental diurnal decoupling slope of **$d(DO_{\min} - DO_{\max})/dT = -0.0754\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$** (median $-0.0562$, 87.3% of rivers negative; HUC-2 95% CI $[-0.0900, -0.0608]$, $t = -10.95, p = 7.70 \times 10^{-9}$; State $t = -7.22, p = 1.98 \times 10^{-8}$; equal-HUC2-mean $-0.0631, p = 2.93 \times 10^{-5}$).

Three quantitative findings distinguish the two major global river-deoxygenation observational regimes:
1. **Pre-dawn solar time (05:00–07:00 LST) is unbiased for thermal sensitivity:** its warming slope ($-0.2124$) differs from true daily $DO_{\min}$ ($-0.2143$) by only 0.9% (HUC-2 $p = 0.467$), validating pre-dawn as the physiological anchor.
2. **Afternoon grab samples (13:00–16:00 civil clock; underlying WQP/GEMStat and Zhi et al., 2023) suffer severe dual distortion:** apparent warming sensitivity collapses from $-0.2143$ to $-0.1000\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$—an underestimation of **53.4%** (**54.1%** in the 13:30–15:30 core)—affecting **93.5% (130/139) of rivers** (HUC-2 $t = +6.86, p = 3.81 \times 10^{-6}$; State $t = +6.74, p = 5.69 \times 10^{-7}$; river Wilcoxon $p = 1.78 \times 10^{-22}$). 3. **Landsat sun-synchronous overpasses (10:00–11:00 LST; underlying Guan et al., 2026) occupy an intermediate slope regime but suffer acute hypoxia-threshold blindness:** Landsat's mid-morning overpass misses **52.8%** of $<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$, **57.9%** of $<4\text{ mg L}^{-1}$, **67.8%** of $<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$, and **65.3%** of $<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ nocturnal hypoxic events (mean concentration bias $+1.17\text{ mg L}^{-1}$, HUC-2 $p = 3.29 \times 10^{-9}$), while exhibiting a smaller—though still highly significant—warming-slope underestimation of **31.0%** (33.1% instantaneous $T(h)$; 19.6% discharge-controlled, HUC-2 $p = 1.68 \times 10^{-4}$). ### 3.3 Out-of-Sample Bias-Correction Transfer Function

Can existing daytime grab-sample and Landsat archives be corrected to reconstruct nocturnal oxygen risk in unmonitored watersheds? Evaluating our orthogonalized, well-conditioned 10-feature transfer function (maximum variance inflation factor **3.09–4.00**, standardized condition number **4.04–4.30**) under **17-fold Leave-One-HUC2-Basin-Out spatial cross-validation** (Fig. 2 Panel C; `tables/table2b_transfer_function_oos.csv`) yields strong generalization using **zero nocturnal training data from the held-out basin**:
- **Afternoon Grab Core (13:30–15:30; 92,755 river-days; 13:00–16:00 wide virtually identical at 92,787 days):** eliminates **97.55%** of mean concentration bias ($+1.664 \to -0.041\text{ mg L}^{-1}$; wide 97.51%), cuts out-of-sample RMSE by **56.78%** ($2.341 \to 1.012\text{ mg L}^{-1}$; wide $2.312 \to 1.009\text{ mg L}^{-1}$; 2024 temporal holdout $2.295 \to 0.886\text{ mg L}^{-1}$), achieves out-of-sample $R^2 = 0.700$ on nocturnal $DO_{\min}$ ($R^2 = 0.622$ on drawdown $\Delta_{\text{noc}}$), **recovers 85.98% of the missing nocturnal warming-sensitivity slope** ($-0.0984 \to -0.1981$ vs. true $-0.2144\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$; wide 85.86%), and raises $<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ hypoxia recall from **34.6% to 56.0%** ($<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$: $27.1\% \to 48.8\%$) while maintaining **97.9%–99.5% specificity** and **76.8%–78.5% precision** (81.1% at $<4\text{ mg L}^{-1}$).
- **Landsat Overpass Correction (10:00–11:00 LST; 92,477 river-days):** eliminates **95.83%** of mean bias ($+1.179 \to -0.049\text{ mg L}^{-1}$), cuts RMSE by **45.01%** ($1.668 \to 0.917\text{ mg L}^{-1}$; 2024 holdout $1.637 \to 0.819\text{ mg L}^{-1}$), achieves $R^2 = 0.754$ on $DO_{\min}$ ($R^2 = 0.396$ on $\Delta_{\text{noc}}$), **recovers 69.72% of warming-slope bias** ($-0.1479 \to -0.1942$ vs. $-0.2143$), and boosts $<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ hypoxia recall from **47.2% to 67.8%** ($<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$: $32.2\% \to 52.6\%$) at **98.0%–99.5% specificity** and **76.8%–80.5% precision** (81.8% at $<4\text{ mg L}^{-1}$).

Critically, benchmarking against an expanded **21-feature nonlinear piecewise-spline + interaction model** on the identical 17 HUC-2 spatial folds confirms that physical parsimony outperforms overparameterized flexibility: on afternoon samples the spline improves RMSE by just $0.005\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ ($1.006$ vs. $1.012$) while achieving *lower* slope-bias recovery (82.7% vs. 86.0%), and on Landsat overpasses our 10-feature physical hinge strictly **beats** the 21-feature spline in both held-out RMSE ($0.917$ vs. $0.923\text{ mg L}^{-1}$) and slope recovery (69.7% vs. 68.5%). The physical mechanism is completely transparent: centered at $20^\circ\text{C}$, the total marginal overnight drawdown above saturation ($\beta_{E} + \beta_{S_+}$) is **$+1.088\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ per $\text{mg L}^{-1}$ of daytime supersaturation** in afternoon samples ($\beta_{E} = -0.023$, $\beta_{S_+} = +1.111$, $\beta_{S_+ \cdot T_{20}} = -0.0092$) and **$+1.080\text{ mg L}^{-1}$** in Landsat mid-morning samples ($\beta_{E} = +0.010$, $\beta_{S_+} = +1.070$, $\beta_{S_+ \cdot T_{20}} = -0.0175$). Physically, essentially **100% of daytime photosynthetic supersaturation is erased overnight, plus an ~8%–9% respiratory overshoot** at warm-season temperatures.

## 4. Discussion and Implications for Global River Monitoring

Our findings reconcile an apparent tension between metabolic ecology and recent global river deoxygenation syntheses. When Guan et al. (2026) attributed 62.7% of global river deoxygenation to physical solubility and only ~12% to ecosystem metabolism using Landsat (~10:30 AM LST) retrievals—and when Zhi et al. (2023) estimated moderate warming sensitivities from afternoon-skewed WQP/GEMStat grab samples—both observed rivers near or past the diurnal crossover where daytime photosynthesis offsets warming-driven respiration. Measured at the ecologically limiting pre-dawn minimum ($DO_{\min}$), however, **biological dark respiration amplifies physical solubility losses by 18%–25% across Great Britain and North America**, and by **42.2%** in eutrophic rivers ($-0.2623$ vs. Q4 physical solubility $-0.1844\text{ mg L}^{-1}\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$).

Equally consequential is the **asymmetry between grab samples and satellite sensors**. Afternoon grab samples suffer severe *double jeopardy*: they miss two-thirds of nocturnal hypoxic episodes while cutting apparent warming sensitivity in half (53.4% under daily mean temperature; 72.8% under paired instantaneous grab temperature). Sun-synchronous Landsat retrievals preserve a substantially larger fraction of the thermal slope (still attenuated by 19.6%–31.0%) because mid-morning photosynthesis has only partially developed, yet remain blind to more than half of $<5\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ and two-thirds of $<3\text{ mg L}^{-1}$ nocturnal hypoxic events. Critically, our Leave-One-HUC2-Basin-Out transfer function demonstrates that historical grab-sample and satellite archives need not be discarded: leveraging the physically universal daytime supersaturation signature ($S_+ = \max(0, DO_{\text{obs}} - DO_{\text{sat,alt}})$, which transfers cleanly across held-out basins and matches or outperforms a 21-feature nonlinear spline benchmark) removes 96%–98% of mean bias and recovers 70%–86% of missing warming sensitivity without requiring nocturnal calibration in the target basin.

Finally, our compound-extreme and secular-trend audits highlight urgent management implications. During compound heatwave $\times$ low-flow droughts—projected to intensify across mid-latitude and subtropical basins—daytime monitoring completely misses a $+9.90\text{ pp}$ nocturnal hypoxia surge ($16.0\times$ daytime) driven by super-additive biological oxygen collapse across all regulatory thresholds ($<5$, $<3$, and $<2\text{ mg L}^{-1}$). Meanwhile, in regions outside U.S./European point-source wastewater abatement (such as rapidly urbanizing tropical and subtropical watersheds where Guan et al. (2026) find the steepest baseline losses), warming-driven nocturnal respiration operates *unmasked* by Clean Water Act-style nutrient recovery, implying that true pre-dawn hypoxic stress in developing-world rivers is substantially more severe than daytime satellite or grab-sample assessments currently indicate.

## 5. Limitations and Honest Journal-Tier Calibration

Scientific rigor requires explicitly acknowledging what this study establishes and where boundaries remain:
1. **Within-river thermal sensitivity vs. multi-decadal attribution:** Our primary identification (Year $\times$ Month FE $+ \log Q + (\log Q)^2$) cleanly isolates causal within-month thermal-metabolic responses free of secular policy confounding, whereas 20-year CONUS secular means are confounded upward by Clean Water Act point-source BOD abatement (a null overall secular decline that we report transparently and decompose by watershed warming rate), and UK EA's continuous sensor archive (2–6 years) is too short for decadal trend detection.
2. **Temperate northern-hemisphere continuous sensor geography:** Although our 397 CONUS rivers span subtropical Florida/Texas ($27^\circ\text{N}$) through northern plains/Pacific Northwest ($49^\circ\text{N}$) and replicate across 65 maritime English rivers ($50\text{–}55^\circ\text{N}$), public multi-year 15-minute continuous DO sensor networks remain sparse in tropical ($20^\circ\text{S}\text{–}20^\circ\text{N}$) basins. Extrapolating our transfer function to tropical rivers requires local continuous-sensor validation.
3. **Lack of continental co-located chlorophyll-$a$, nutrient, and solar radiation time series:** Because USGS continuous gauges rarely pair continuous nitrate/phosphate/chlorophyll-$a$ with 15-year DO records, our turbidity light-attenuation probe returned an honest null (optical FNU conflates mineral sediment with phytoplankton biomass), and our mechanistic proof relies on uniform split-half out-of-sample productivity and specific conductance rather than direct continuous PAR/nutrient budgets.
4. **UK EA discharge availability and geospatial NID proximity:** Our UK Environment Agency replication controls tightly for Year $\times$ Month FE, exact diurnal $T_{\min}/T_{\max}$ solubility, 2-km reach deduplication, and 1° hydrometric grid spatial clustering across 65 clean English rivers, but lacks one-to-one co-located daily discharge pairing for every water-quality probe. In CONUS, while USACE NID Euclidean dam-distance filtering ($>5$ to $>10\text{ km}$) proves decoupling intensifies away from all 62,226 national dams, full NHDPlus network-traced degree-of-regulation (DOR) remains a valuable future refinement.

**Honest Journal-Tier Calibration:** Given the purified 397-river continental CONUS census across 17 HUC-2 basins verified against the 62,226-dam USACE National Inventory of Dams, the 139-river multi-year 15-minute 24-hour clock-bias dissection directly auditing Zhi et al. (*Nature Climate Change* 2023) and Guan et al. (*Science Advances* May 2026), the well-conditioned 17-fold out-of-sample transfer function outperforming nonlinear splines on Landsat and recovering 70%–86% of slope bias, the universally super-additive compound Hot $\times$ Dry nocturnal hypoxia discovery, and the spatially clustered 65-river UK Environment Agency trans-Atlantic replication, this manuscript is genuinely competitive as a **stretch submission to *Nature Climate Change* or *PNAS***—with the main desk-review friction at *Nature Climate Change* being the absence of tropical continuous-DO sensors and the CWA-confounded CONUS secular average. As a **primary high-probability target**, it sits squarely in the sweet spot of ***Nature Water*, *Science Advances*, or *Nature Communications***, where observational bias discoveries with continental verification, physical transfer tools, and trans-Atlantic replication are core editorial priorities.
