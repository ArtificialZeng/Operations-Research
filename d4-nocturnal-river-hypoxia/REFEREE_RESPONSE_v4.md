## Response to P0 #1 — Eliminate Shared Reference-Outcome Circularity

> **Referee Critique:** Remove the shared-reading circularity causing zero pre-dawn false alerts by definition; adopt Leave-One-Reading-Out (`LORO`) as the primary analysis (noting your independent check showing `37/8,499` U.S. and `112/2,472` English reference positives dropped and gains of `49.47` and `35.13 pp`), add threshold-tolerance and persistence checks, validate on held-out sites/regions or independent co-located probes, and frame continuous outcomes explicitly as within-probe recorded event detection rather than regulatory compliance.

**Actions Taken (`scripts/11_loro_and_sensitivity_analysis.py`, Main Text Section 2.2, Fig. 2, Tables 1–2):**
1. **Primary LORO Estimator Reproduces Independent Verification Exactly:** On the exact common-support raw UTC records (`87,785` U.S. site-nights across `139` USGS STAC stations; `27,635` English site-nights across `65` EA stations), dropping the candidate pre-dawn reading from the nocturnal reference reduces `<5 mg/L` positives by exactly **`37 / 8,499` (to `8,462`)** in the U.S. and **`112 / 2,472` (to `2,360`)** in England. Primary LORO pre-dawn gains over `14:30` are **`+49.47 pp` (95% CI `[+36.87, +62.07]`)** in the U.S. and **`+35.13 pp` (`[+22.69, +47.57]`)** in England—matching the referee's independent recalculation to the second decimal place. All four thresholds (`<5`, `<4`, `<3`, `<2 mg/L`) are reported in **Table 1**.
2. **Transparent Non-Zero LORO Reference-Negative Alarm Rates:** Removed all "zero false-positive by definition" claims. Pre-dawn LORO reference-negative alarm rates are **`0.047%`** (`37 / 79,323` nights) in the U.S. and **`0.443%`** (`112 / 25,275` nights) in England—still lower than `14:30` (`0.198%` / `0.514%`) and `10:30` (`0.271%` / `1.207%`). Excluding an entire `±30 min` buffer around pre-dawn preserves gains of **`+49.63 pp`** (U.S.) and **`+34.77 pp`** (England) with alarm rates of `0.277%` and `0.533%`.
3. **Sensor-Tolerance & Persistence Battery (Table 2):**
   - **Persistence (`≥2` consecutive non-candidate readings & `≥2 h` continuous actionable episodes):** Gains rise to **`+51.54`–`+53.58 pp`** (U.S.) and **`+34.98`–`+38.09 pp`** (England), ruling out single-point probe spikes.
   - **Threshold buffers (`±0.1`, `±0.2 mg/L`) & independent probe-noise Monte Carlo (`σ = 0.10, 0.20 mg/L`, 200 reps simulating two independent co-located probes per USGS Wagner et al. 2006):** Gains remain **`+43.05`–`+51.94 pp`** (U.S.; MC `σ = 0.20`: `+46.55 pp`) and **`+33.29`–`+34.55 pp`** (England).
4. **Independent Co-Located Field-Meter Validation (`scripts/10_fetch_wqp_grab_logs.py`, Fig. 1b):** Retrieved `10,019` real timestamped warm-season U.S. Water Quality Portal (WQP) discrete DO field visits (`205` rivers, 2015–2024; `1,391` visits across `76` of our `139` subdaily stations, 2021–2024). Across **`1,287` paired independent field-meter vs. continuous sonde readings (`|Δt| ≤ 30 min`, median `0.0 min`)**, continuous probes match independent field meters with **Pearson `r = 0.961`**, bias `-0.027 mg/L`, and median absolute error **`0.20 mg/L`** (exactly meeting the USGS `±0.20 mg/L` tolerance). On `1,112` matched agency visit dates, real field visits missed **`61.0%`** (`50/82`) of LORO `<5 mg/L` nights and **`57.7%`** (`41/71`) of `≥2 h` actionable episodes (vs. `93.0%` detected pre-dawn on those same visit dates).
5. **Out-of-Sample Region, Site, and Cross-Atlantic Holdout Validation (Fig. 2b, Table 2):** Optimizing single-visit solar timing via LORO Youden's $J$ on training folds independently selects **geometric sunrise (`SR + 0 min`)** in `16/16` U.S. Leave-One-HUC2-Basin-Out folds, `5/5` U.S. site folds, `11/15` English 1°-grid folds (4 selecting `SR − 30 min`), and both directions of zero-shot cross-Atlantic transfer (`+50.56 pp` U.S., `+36.03 pp` England)—uncovering a near-peak, daylight-safe **first-light plateau (`SR + 30 min`: `83.85%` U.S., `78.80%` England)**!
6. **Scoped Outcome Framing:** Continuous-sensor metrics are consistently framed throughout Abstract, Introduction, Results, and Discussion as **within-probe recorded nocturnal event detection** on screened monitoring networks, not regulatory compliance rates, noting dual co-located continuous probe trials as priority future work.

## Response to P0 #2 — Equal-Budget Operational Monitoring Protocol Comparison

> **Referee Critique:** Move beyond idealized `14:30` vs. pre-dawn recall to real monitoring decisions using timestamped sampling logs, comparing current schedules, rotation, pre-dawn, and risk-stratified continuous sondes under fixed annual visit/dollar budgets with explicit nocturnal labor, travel, and equipment costs, pre-defined actionable episodes, new detections per 100 visits, lead time, and reference-negative alarms. Clearly state where low-cost rotation or hybrid deployment outperforms pure pre-dawn grabs and distinguish novelty from Elfferich et al. (2024).

**Actions Taken (`scripts/12_operational_protocol_decision_analysis.py`, Main Text Sections 2.1, 2.3–2.4, Figs. 1, 3–4, Table 3, Table S4):**
1. **Real Timestamped Agency Sampling Clock (Fig. 1a):** Actual WQP field visits (`10,019` visits, `205` rivers) peak at **11:00 local civil time (`09:50` solar; IQR `09:30–12:40` civil)** with `92.9%` in `08:00–17:00` and only `0.51%` in the 2 h before sunrise. We therefore evaluate **both** the real-world Empirical WQP Agency Clock and canonical `14:30` afternoon sampling.
2. **Pre-Defined Actionable Episodes & Zero Double-Counting:** Pre-defined primary actionable episodes as LORO-verified continuous `<5 mg/L` runs lasting **`≥2.0 h`** (with `<4 mg/L`, `<3 mg/L`, and `≥4.0 h` sensitivities). Crucially, we group consecutive actionable site-nights at the same station into **distinct non-overlapping episodes** (`1,066` U.S., `348` England post-Year-1) so multi-day chronic episodes cannot artificially inflate continuous-sonde counts!
3. **Explicit Cost Decomposition & 1,000-Calendar Equal-Budget Simulation (Table 3, Table S4):** Fixed annual visit budgets (`12`, `24`, `52` visits/site-yr) and equal total dollar budgets decomposing travel/access (`40%`), daytime labor (`60%`), nocturnal two-person safety/overtime multipliers (`m_night ∈ {1.0×, 1.35×, 1.75×, 2.5×}`), early-shift first-light (`1.0 + 0.2(m_night − 1)` = `1.15×` central), and amortized continuous sonde costs (`K_sonde ∈ {20, 30, 40, 60, 80, 100}` daytime-visit-equivalents/yr; benchmark `\$200`/daytime visit).
4. **Honest Decision Findings (Fig. 4, Table 3):**
   - **Pure dark pre-dawn grabs (`m_night = 1.75×`) LOSE to the existing empirical WQP agency clock on cost-effectiveness!** Analytical breakeven multipliers are only **`m* = 1.53×` (U.S.)** and **`1.38×` (England)**; at `1.75×`, dark pre-dawn yields `1.85` vs. `1.91` (U.S.) and `1.51` vs. `1.51` (England) distinct episodes per 100 budget-equivalents.
   - **First-light civil daylight grabs (`SR + 30 min`, `1.15×`) dominate all grab-only protocols** (`2.56` U.S. / `2.06` England distinct ep/100 eq; `\$7,813` / `\$9,729` per episode; `55.2%` / `54.2%` impaired site-years flagged) without pitch-dark safety surcharges.
   - **Risk-stratified continuous sondes (top-20% sites selected strictly out-of-sample from Year-1 daytime grabs) + first-light grabs elsewhere dominate the entire decision frontier under the identical budget:** **`6.71` (U.S.) and `7.11` (England) distinct episodes/100 eq** (`3.5×` and `4.7×` WQP grabs; **`72%–79%` unit cost reduction** to `\$2,980` / `\$2,812` per episode), flagging **`63.8%` / `60.6%`** of impaired site-years — holding even with zero-DO-history static basin covariates (`5.07` / `4.49` ep/100 eq) and across `K_sonde = 20–100`.
5. **Episodic Lead-Time Realism (Fig. 3c):** Separating episodic (`2–24 h`) from chronic (`≥24 h`) hypoxia shows that while pre-dawn/first-light grabs catch `77%–87%` while active (`+3.9` to `+5.0 h` before recovery), they still arrive right at/after the oxygen nadir (`-1.18` to `+0.50 h`). **Only telemetered continuous sondes deliver genuine pre-nadir early warning** (`+4.5 h` U.S. and `+3.5 h` England before nadir; `94.2%–95.8%` caught pre-nadir).

## Response to P1 #3 — Sample Coverage, Native Frequency Downsampling & Censoring Sensitivity

> **Referee Critique:** Explain the `~94%` U.S. and `~82%` England common-support entry rates by site, native interval, and season; downsample high-frequency stations to `15/30/60 min`; report equal-site weighting and chronic-site exclusion on held-out regions; explicitly address censoring (`1,246/8,499` closed runs) before describing risk duration distributions; cite Blaszczak et al. (2023).

**Actions Taken (`scripts/11_loro_and_sensitivity_analysis.py`, Fig. 1c, Fig. 3a–b, Table 2, Supplementary Tables S1–S3):**
1. **Explicit Coverage & Attrition Tables (Fig. 1c, Tables S1–S2):** Complete station-level (`table_s1_station_coverage_attrition.csv`, `204` stations) and cadence/month attrition summaries (`table_s2_coverage_by_cadence_month.csv`) show entry rates are season-invariant (`<2.3 pp` spread May–Oct) and driven by native cadence—specifically England's `28` 30-min stations (`67.0%` entry, `4,537` of `5,824` English nocturnal gap failures) vs. U.S. 15-min stations (`115` sites, `94.6%` entry). Partial-data excluded nights show no uniform directional omission bias (`11.89%` vs. `9.68%` `<5 mg/L` U.S.; `3.69%` vs. `8.95%` England).
2. **Native Frequency Downsampling (`15 / 30 / 60 min`, Table 2):** Downsampling all `119` U.S. `≤15-min` stations onto exact `15`, `30`, and `60 min` grids keeps LORO gain rock-solid (`+50.88 → +51.97 → +51.80 pp`) while proving that England's higher LORO alarm rate (`0.443%`) is largely a cadence effect (`0.023% → 0.123% → 0.368%` in U.S.; corroborated on England's `35`-site `≤30-min` cohort: `+30.54` / `+29.54 pp` at 30/60 min).
3. **Equal-Site Weighting & Chronic-Site Exclusion (Table 2):** Equal-site LORO gains (`≥5` hypoxic nights) are `+46.26 pp` (U.S., `79` sites; `+48.38 pp` on held-out HUC-2 regions) and `+30.47 pp` (England, `30` sites; `+30.48 pp` held-out). Dropping chronically hypoxic sites (`>50%`, `>30%`, `>20%` of nights `<5`, or `>25%` of `14:30` readings `<5`) *increases* U.S. gain to `+50.29`–`+63.73 pp` and English gain to `+36.12`–`+40.18 pp`.
4. **Censoring Audit & Kaplan–Meier Survival Analysis (Fig. 3a–b, Table S3):** Explicitly discloses that only `1,246 / 8,499` (`14.7%`) U.S. and `378 / 2,472` (`15.3%`) English `<5 mg/L` nights close at both ends inside darkness, while `80.1%` (U.S.) and `76.7%` (England) are right- or doubly-censored at sunrise. Applying Kaplan–Meier right-censored survival estimation and tracing unbroken multi-day continuous runs across sunrise/sunset corrects the ~8-fold downward bias of naive closed-in-night medians (`1.0–2.0 h`) to true continuous medians of **`13.5 h` (IQR `6.5–40.0 h`, U.S.)** and **`13.0 h` (IQR `6.0–67.0 h`, England)**.
