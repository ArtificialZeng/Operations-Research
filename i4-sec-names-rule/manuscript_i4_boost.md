# Bifurcated Regulatory Name Arbitrage: Evidence from the SEC's 2023 "80% Names Rule" Overhaul

**Draft Date:** November 26, 2026  
**Target Journals:** *Journal of Finance* / *Review of Financial Studies* / *Journal of Financial Economics* / *Management Science*  
**Data Universe:** Official SEC Form N-PORT & Form N-CEN Bulk Microdata (`2019Q4–2026Q3`), Live SEC EDGAR `497`/`485BPOS` Prospectus Filings (`2024–2026`), and 4-Tier Look-Through Security Holdings (`2023Q2 → 2026Q2/Q3`)

## Abstract

When securities regulators tighten mandatory disclosure-backed product labels, do asset managers align portfolios with labels, or do they arbitrage the statutory vocabulary? We exploit the Securities and Exchange Commission's (SEC) September 2023 overhaul of Rule 35d-1 (the "80% Names Rule," Release No. 33-11238 / IC-35000)—which extended the 80% asset-basket mandate to investment-characteristic terms such as *Growth*, *Value*, and *ESG*, with substantive compliance phased in on each open-end fund's first on-cycle annual prospectus update on or after June 11, 2026 for large complexes ($\ge \$1$ billion; Release No. 33-11368 / IC-35500)—across the complete universe of U.S. open-end mutual funds and ETFs (17,151 funds, 307,298 `REPORT_DATE`-aligned fund-quarters, 935,950 fund-months, \$46.20 trillion in AUM). To separate pure regulatory arbitrage from U.S. anti-ESG political backlash, we identify *non-political* multi-asset *Growth*/*Value*/*Income* (GVI) funds whose blended stock-bond mandates face acute legal and monitoring friction under an 80% characteristic-basket regime. Across `2025Q1–2026Q3`, 161 unique funds managing \$299.03 billion across 65 unified complexes completely stripped newly regulated terms—including 50 multi-asset funds managing \$117.63 billion across 19 complexes. We document an asymmetric **Bifurcated Compliance Equilibrium**: **opaque SEC-exempt near-synonym substitution** (*Aggressive*, *Moderately Aggressive*, *Moderate*, *Opportunities*, *Multi-Asset*) is broad-based across 16 complexes, accounting for **84.0% of renamed multi-asset funds** (42/50, \$49.79 billion, 52.4% in captive variable-annuity/insurance trusts, 0.0% index/ETF), whereas **transparency-enhancing quantitative allocation ratios** (*80/20*, *60/40*, *40/60*, *20/80*, *70%*) account for 14.0% of funds (7 funds across Vanguard, BlackRock/iShares, and Fidelity) and 57.7% (\$67.82 billion) of renamed multi-asset AUM overall, but drop sharply to **4.9% of renamed AUM (\$2.56 billion)** when excluding a single index giant—Vanguard LifeStrategy (\$65.26 billion, or **96.2%** of numeric-ratio AUM; excluding Vanguard, opaque synonyms constitute **91.3% of funds and 95.1% of AUM**). Difference-in-differences estimates show a +1.71 percentage-point per-quarter surge in regulated name drops (CR1 $t = +3.23$, exact LSDV-equivalent wild cluster bootstrap $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$; joint 13-quarter pre-trend $F = 1.06, p = 0.392$), driven 91% by broad-based opaque synonyms (+1.55 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$; ex-Vanguard opaque +1.58 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$). Estimates survive strictly within `COMPLEX_ID × REP_YQ` fixed effects (+1.75 pp/qtr on identifying complexes only, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.003$), an **SEC 2025–2026 Names Rule FAQs strict non-exempt `Growth`/`Value` subsample** excluding all *Income* and *Growth and Income* hybrids (35/50 droppers; +2.78 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$; within-complex FE +2.50 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.003$), a zero-keyword 4-tier look-through single-asset-class concentration proxy (`max(%Eq, %Bond) < 80%`: +1.27 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.003$), complete-quarter (`2020Q1–2026Q2`) truncation, and leave-one-complex-out exclusion. A 100% symmetric live EDGAR `497`/`485BPOS` audit across all 161 droppers and 50 matched keeper controls confirms higher trust-level Rule 35d-1 boilerplate among keepers (66.0% vs. 42.0%, Fisher $p = 0.0268$), sharp name-change filing concentration among droppers (80.0% vs. 26.0%, $p < 0.001$), and effective-date lock-in across April–May 2025, September 2025, and May 2026 (28/36 parsed dates, 77.8%). Corrected 4-tier look-through holdings with cross-quarter CUSIP consensus resolution (`100/100` top-100 accuracy) and non-overlapping monthly drift benchmarks establish strong **differential stock-bond allocation inertia**—not a claim of statutory noncompliance, since Rule 35d-1 allows prospectus-defined multi-asset 80% baskets—with `<80%` single-class droppers actively maintaining static targets (-2.82 pp vs. +4.10 pp among keepers; diff -6.92 pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$; strict non-FAQ `GV` diff -5.47 pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.050$) alongside +13.26 pp *higher* `IS_FOF`-controlled CUSIP overlap ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.033$). Finally, after fixing an 18.6% negative-redemption sign bug in raw Form N-PORT data (lifting TNA-implied flow correlation from $r = 0.088$ to $r = 0.955$), monthly event studies with flat pre-trends reveal the demand microfoundation: redemptions never spike, captive variable-annuity flows stay flat (+0.16%/mo, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.62$), retail opaque renames face a new-sales drag (-0.57%/mo, $t = -2.28$), and numeric-ratio funds earn positive net flows (+0.92%/mo, quarterly +4.40%/qtr).

## 1. Introduction and Institutional Setting

Product labels are the primary low-cost heuristic retail investors use to screen mutual funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) in delegated asset management (Sirri and Tufano, 1998; Cooper, Gulen, and Rau, 2005; Barber, Huang, and Odean, 2016). Because fund names convey salient style and risk signals without requiring investors to parse hundreds of pages of prospectuses or security-level holdings, regulators have long treated misleading fund names as an investor-protection failure. In 2001, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) adopted Rule 35d-1 under the Investment Company Act of 1940 (Release No. IC-24828), mandating that any registered fund whose name suggests a focus on a particular asset class, industry, or geographic region invest at least 80% of its assets accordingly. Yet for more than two decades, Rule 35d-1 exempted terms denoting *investment strategies or characteristics*—such as *Growth*, *Value*, *Income*, *Balanced*, or *ESG*—creating a widely documented gap between marketing labels and underlying portfolios (Sensoy, 2009; Espenlaub, ul Haq, and Khurshed, 2017; Fisch and Robertson, 2023).

On September 20, 2023, the SEC enacted the most sweeping overhaul of mutual fund naming regulation in twenty-two years (Release No. 33-11238 / IC-35000). Amended Rule 35d-1 expanded the mandatory 80% investment basket to any fund name containing terms suggesting an investment focus on "particular characteristics," explicitly encompassing style terms (*Growth*, *Value*) as well as sustainability and thematic labels (*ESG*, *Sustainable*, *Climate*, *Impact*). Two subsequent regulatory clarifications are critical for empirical design. First, on January 8, 2025, the SEC Division of Investment Management issued updated *2025–26 Names Rule FAQs* clarifying staff views that standalone *"income"* (when not referring to fixed-income securities) and certain *"growth and income"* hybrids generally indicate portfolio-wide objectives rather than requiring an 80% investment policy, whereas *"growth"* and *"value"* generally trigger an 80% requirement unless modified in context. Second, the SEC established a staggered statutory compliance schedule split sharply at **\$1 billion in net assets** across a "group of related investment companies" (Rule 0-10(a)). Originally slated for December 11, 2025 (large groups, $\ge \$1$ billion) and June 11, 2026 (small groups, $< \$1$ billion), the SEC on March 14, 2025 (Release No. 33-11368 / IC-35500) both extended the start dates by six months—to **June 11, 2026** (large groups) and **December 11, 2026** (small groups)—and tied ongoing open-end funds' actual compliance timing to each fund's **first "on-cycle" annual prospectus update filed on or after June 11, 2026 or December 11, 2026**, rather than a single calendar-quarter cliff. Subsequently, on February 18, 2026, the SEC issued Release No. **IC-35963** extending the *Form N-PORT Names-Rule reporting fields* (which carry a separate \$10 billion reporting cutoff, postponed to November 17, 2027 and May 18, 2028) while simultaneously issuing **SEC Press Release 2026-19** proposing amendments to Form N-PORT to reduce reporting burdens—including proposing to eliminate Form N-PORT Names-Rule reporting items altogether—while leaving the substantive Rule 35d-1 mandate in full force. Direct live schema comparison of official SEC bulk files (`2020q1_nport.zip` vs. `2026q3_nport.zip`) confirms zero new N-PORT columns (`FUND_REPORTED_INFO.tsv` retains exactly 47 columns), underscoring why prospectus-grounded (`497`/`485BPOS`) and security-level look-through evaluation is indispensable even for future oversight.

This institutional shock poses a fundamental economic question in financial regulation: when a regulator expands a bright-line holdings mandate to cover ubiquitous style and ESG terms while leaving adjacent marketing vocabulary legally exempt, **how do asset managers respond in equilibrium?** A textbook compliance view predicts real portfolio reallocation toward the 80% threshold. A regulatory-arbitrage view predicts purely cosmetic renaming—stripping regulated terms to escape the 80% basket while leaving portfolios untouched. Yet a deeper institutional tension arises *within* the set of renaming managers: if a fund drops a regulated label such as *Growth* or *Income*, what replaces it? Replacing *Growth* with a vague exempt near-synonym (*Aggressive*, *Moderately Aggressive*, *Opportunities*) preserves full managerial discretion and conceals exact portfolio weights, whereas replacing *Growth* with an explicit quantitative ratio (*80/20*, *60/40*, *40/60*) actually *increases* portfolio transparency relative to the pre-rule status quo.

Studying this question solely through ESG funds—the focus of recent European SFDR evaluations (Allcott, Egan, Smeets, and Yang, 2026) and U.S. ESG commitment studies (Hartzmark and Sussman, 2019; Gibson Brandon et al., 2022; Kim and Yoon, 2023)—faces a severe identification confound in the United States. Between 2023 and 2026, U.S. ESG funds experienced intense political scrutiny, state-level divestment mandates, and retail outflows completely independent of Rule 35d-1. Indeed, we verify empirically that ESG name drops rose early in 2023–2024 and occurred at *higher* rates among exempt small complexes ($< \$1$ billion: 3.23%/qtr) than among binding large complexes ($\ge \$100$ billion: 0.80%/qtr; $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.922$).

We overcome this confound by exploiting a **non-political, structurally exposed segment** inside the exact same rule: **multi-asset allocation and fund-of-funds portfolios carrying *Growth*, *Value*, or *Income* in their names** (e.g., *Vanguard LifeStrategy Moderate Growth Fund*, *AST Prudential Growth Allocation Portfolio*, *iShares Core Growth Allocation ETF*, *American Funds Growth Allocation Portfolio*). There is zero political backlash against the words *Growth* or *Value*. While amended Rule 35d-1 permits a multi-asset fund to define a prospectus-level multi-asset 80% basket—and the SEC's *2025–26 Names Rule FAQs* carve out standalone non-fixed-income *Income* and certain *Growth and Income* hybrids—maintaining a regulated style label on a blended 60/40 or 40/60 stock-bond portfolio creates substantial legal-drafting, quarterly basket-monitoring, and 90-day cure friction relative to pure single-class equity funds. Crucially, we verify that all headline naming and portfolio-inertia results hold identically when strictly excluding every *Income* and *Growth and Income* fund (`35/50` multi-asset droppers).

Using the complete population of U.S. open-end funds and ETFs across 28 quarters of SEC Form N-PORT and 27 quarters of Form N-CEN (`2019Q4–2026Q3`), supplemented by a 100% symmetric live SEC EDGAR `497`/`485BPOS` prospectus audit and 4-tier security-level look-through holdings across `2023Q2` and `2026Q2/Q3`, we establish five principal findings:

1. **Broad-Based Opaque Lexical Substitution vs. Concentrated Numeric-Ratio Labeling:** Between `2025Q1` and `2026Q3` (indexed strictly by true `REPORT_DATE` calendar quarter `REP_YQ`), 161 unique U.S. funds holding \$299.03 billion across 65 unified complexes completely stripped newly regulated Rule 35d-1 terms—comprising 50 multi-asset *Growth*/*Value*/*Income* (GVI) funds holding \$117.63 billion across 19 complexes, 56 ESG funds holding \$22.94 billion, and 56 pure-equity/bond GVI funds holding \$158.46 billion (55 exclusive of 1 sequential ETF that dropped *ESG* in `2025Q1` and *Value* in `2026Q1`; led by Capital Group's \$124.35 billion *EuroPacific Growth Fund* $\to$ *EUPAC Fund*). Inside multi-asset GVI droppers, compliance splits into an asymmetric **Bifurcated Equilibrium**: **84.0% of funds** (42/50 across 16 complexes, \$49.79 billion, 42.3% of AUM) adopt **opaque SEC-exempt near-synonyms** (*Aggressive*, *Moderately Aggressive*, *Moderate*, *Opportunities*, *Multi-Asset*), concentrated in active and captive variable-annuity/insurance trusts (52.4% VA/insurance, 0.0% index/ETF). By contrast, **14.0% of funds** (7/50)—Vanguard LifeStrategy (\$65.26 billion), iShares Core Allocation ETFs (\$2.28 billion), and Fidelity VIP Asset Manager 70% (\$0.28 billion)—adopt **transparency-enhancing numeric ratios** (*80/20*, *60/40*, *40/60*, *20/80*, *70%*), accounting for **57.7% of renamed multi-asset AUM** (\$67.82 billion) overall. Crucially, explicit Ex-Vanguard sensitivity analysis reveals that Vanguard alone accounts for **96.2%** of numeric-ratio AUM; excluding Vanguard, numeric ratios fall from 57.7% to **4.9% of renamed AUM** (\$2.56 billion, 3/46 funds), while opaque-synonym evasion rises to **91.3% of funds and 95.1% of AUM** across 18 non-Vanguard complexes.
2. **Sharp Causal Identification Surviving Within-Complex FE, Ex-Vanguard Exclusion, SEC-FAQ Filtering, and Look-Through Holdings:** Relative to pure-equity/bond GVI funds, multi-asset GVI funds exhibit a +1.71 pp/qtr compliance-window surge ($t = +3.23$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$; `2020Q1–2026Q2` complete-quarter panel: +1.62 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$) with flat 13-quarter pre-trends ($F(13, 543) = 1.06, p = 0.392$). Decomposing by substitution type confirms that broad-based opaque synonyms drive +1.55 pp/qtr ($t = +3.24$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$; ex-Vanguard opaque +1.58 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$). The surge holds identically inside `COMPLEX_ID × REP_YQ` fixed effects (+1.75 pp/qtr comparing multi-asset vs. pure-equity GVI funds inside the *exact same fund complex in the exact same quarter*, $N = 50,660, G = 105, p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.003$), inside the **SEC-FAQ strict non-exempt `Growth`/`Value` subsample** excluding all *Income* and *Growth and Income* funds (+2.78 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$; within-complex FE +2.50 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.003$), under strictly objective N-CEN structural FOF/target-date flags (+1.90 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.003$), under a zero-keyword 4-tier look-through single-asset-class concentration proxy (`max(%Eq, %Bond) < 80%` in `2023Q2`: +1.27 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.003$; with `COMPLEX_ID × REP_YQ` FE: +1.24 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.010$), and across every leave-one-complex-out exclusion (worst case dropping Fidelity: +1.43 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$).
3. **Symmetric Live SEC EDGAR `497`/`485BPOS` Legal Attribution and Prospectus Lock-In Waves:** Auditing all 161 dropping funds plus 50 randomly matched non-dropping multi-asset GVI keepers on live SEC EDGAR under a 100% symmetric search protocol (untruncated up to 6 MB per filing, identical date windows, form priorities, and detectors) uncovers both the legal disclosure divergence and the timing mechanism. Trust-level Rule 35d-1 compliance disclosures appear significantly more often among Keepers (66.0% vs. 42.0% for multi-asset droppers, Fisher $p = 0.0268$; fund-level 34.0% vs. 20.0%, and 0.0% among numeric-ratio droppers). Conversely, 80.0% of multi-asset droppers (100.0% of numeric droppers, vs. 26.0% of keepers undergoing non-GVI brand rebrands; Fisher $p < 0.001$) executed renames via `497`/`497K` filings—frequently certifying verbatim that *"each Fund's investment objective, strategies, and policies will remain unchanged"* (57.1% of numeric droppers; 27.5% of all located name-change filings). Parsed legal effective dates (36 multi-asset GVI funds) cluster sharply across three regulatory windows (28/36 = 77.8%): April–May 2025 (12 funds, with 7/7 May-2025 dates on exact **May 1, 2025**—consistent with Release `33-11368`'s note that December-31 fiscal-year-end funds file on-cycle annual updates by March 2 for a May 1 effective date, locked in before the March 14, 2025 extension), September 2025 (6 funds, 60-day Rule 35d-1 notice lead), and April–May 2026 (10 funds directly ahead of the June 11, 2026 large-group compliance start date).
4. **Differential Stock-Bond Allocation Inertia (Cross-Quarter CUSIP-Consensus 4-Tier Look-Through Proof):** Resolving both Form N-PORT's `ASSET_CAT = EC` FOF reporting defect and 2026 custodian title truncation via a 5,597-CUSIP cross-quarter consensus registry yields `100/100` verified accuracy on the top-100 multi-asset underlying positions (`0` fallback rows in the top 100) and matches Vanguard LifeStrategy `20/80`, `40/60`, `60/40`, and `80/20` within $\pm 1.5$ pp of stated targets in both `2023Q2` and `2026Q3`. Because Rule 35d-1 permits prospectus-defined multi-asset 80% baskets, our `max(%Eq, %Bond) < 80%` look-through indicator serves strictly as a **single-asset-class concentration and allocation-inertia proxy** rather than a test of literal statutory noncompliance. Against exact non-overlapping monthly benchmark returns (pure-equity +55.6% vs. pure-bond +14.2% from `2023-06` to `2026-06`), `<80%` single-class droppers actively trimmed equity drift (**-2.82 pp** relative to passive drift) to hold fixed blended targets while `<80%` keepers shifted **+4.10 pp** toward higher single-class concentration—a **-6.92 pp allocation-inertia separation ($t = -4.00, p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$)** that persists cleanly inside the strict SEC-FAQ non-exempt `Growth`/`Value` subsample (**-5.47 pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.050$**). Across all paired multi-asset GVI funds, `IS_FOF`-controlled 3-year CUSIP overlap is *+13.26 pp higher* among droppers (73.9% vs. 59.3%, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.033$).
5. **N-PORT Redemption-Sign Correction and Investor-Demand Microfoundations:** We uncover a pervasive data pitfall in SEC Form N-PORT bulk files: 18.61% of all monthly redemption records (22.01% of nonzero records) report redemptions as *negative* dollar figures rather than positive magnitudes, causing naive `Sales + Reinvest - Redemptions` net flows to correlate at only $r = 0.0882$ with TNA-implied net flows across $N = 299,416$ fund-quarters. Applying `abs(REDEMPTION)` raises the correlation to $r = 0.9546$ (Spearman $\rho = 0.9021$). Using corrected monthly flows ($m = -12 \dots +12$) aligned to validated EDGAR effective months—with flat linear pre-trends across both Multi-Asset GVI ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.70$) and ESG ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.52$)—we find that, reversing Cooper, Gulen, and Rau (2005), compliance-induced name drops trigger **zero redemption run** across every arm. Inflow/outflow decomposition exposes the demand wedge driving bifurcation: captive variable-annuity flows are dead flat (+0.16%/mo, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.62$), retail opaque renames face a new-sales slowdown (-0.57%/mo, $t = -2.28$), and transparent numeric-ratio funds earn positive net flows (+0.92%/mo, $t = +2.00$; quarterly Fixed +4.40%/qtr vs. TNA-implied +4.52%/qtr).

Our paper contributes to three literatures. First, we advance the mutual fund naming and disclosure-regulation literature (Cooper, Gulen, and Rau, 2005; Sensoy, 2009; Espenlaub, ul Haq, and Khurshed, 2017; Fisch and Robertson, 2023; Allcott, Egan, Smeets, and Yang, 2026) by providing the first causal, universe-wide evaluation of the SEC's 2023–2026 Rule 35d-1 compliance cliff, demonstrating that bright-line lexical rules without underlying mandate verification generate a *bifurcated* response—opaque evasion by active and captive-annuity managers alongside transparency-enhancing quantitative labeling by low-fee retail beta leaders. Second, we contribute to delegated asset management and investor flow sensitivity (Chevalier and Ellison, 1997; Sirri and Tufano, 1998; Berk and Green, 2004; Kacperczyk, Sialm, and Zheng, 2008; Barber, Huang, and Odean, 2016; Pástor, Stambaugh, and Taylor, 2021) by documenting how distribution-channel captivity and active fee rents jointly determine equilibrium product transparency. Third, we provide two methodological public goods for empirical asset-pricing researchers using SEC Form N-PORT: documenting and correcting the 18.6% negative-redemption sign inconsistency and resolving both the `ASSET_CAT = EC` FOF look-through defect and custodian title truncation.

## 2. Conceptual Framework: A Simple Model of Bifurcated Regulatory Name Arbitrage

To discipline our empirical predictions on *who* renames, *how* they rename (opaque near-synonym vs. explicit numeric ratio), and *why* portfolios remain invariant, consider a single-period fund complex managing a multi-asset fund $i$ with beginning assets $A_i > 0$, management fee $f_i \ge f_{\text{beta}} > 0$ (where $f_{\text{beta}}$ is the competitive passive/beta fee and $f_i - f_{\text{beta}} \ge 0$ is the active fee premium), true target equity share $w_i^* \in (0, 0.80)$ (so single-asset-class concentration $m_i^* \equiv \max(w_i^*, 1 - w_i^*) < 0.80$), and a legacy regulated name (e.g., *Moderate Growth*). Under amended Rule 35d-1 (Release No. 33-11238 / IC-35000 and Release No. 33-11368 / IC-35500), complexes at or above the statutory \$1 billion threshold face substantive compliance on their first on-cycle annual prospectus update on or after June 11, 2026 and choose among three actions $a \in \{R, O, Q\}$:

1. **Retain Regulated Name and Comply ($a = R$):** Keep the regulated name and satisfy Rule 35d-1 either by shifting toward a higher single-class concentration ($m_i \ge 0.80$) or by defining and defending a prospectus-level multi-asset 80% basket. Retaining the regulated label incurs proportional mandate-constraint/drift-distortion loss $\frac{1}{2}\gamma(0.80 - m_i^*)^2 f_i A_i$ (with $\gamma > 0$, capturing tightening equity/style tilts or lost tactical flexibility) plus ongoing fixed legal, quarterly basket-testing, and 90-day cure compliance cost $c_{80}$.
2. **Opaque Exempt-Synonym Substitution ($a = O$):** Strip the regulated term and adopt a legally exempt vague descriptor (*Aggressive*, *Moderate*, *Opportunities*) while preserving $w_i = w_i^*$ and full tactical discretion (retaining full management fee $f_i$). The manager incurs fixed prospectus-sticker cost $c_s \ll c_{80}$ and suffers a retail shopper ambiguity/search flow drag $\kappa_i \lambda_O A_i$, where $\kappa_i \in [0, 1]$ measures distribution-channel flow elasticity ($\kappa_i \approx 0$ in captive variable-annuity insurance wrappers; $\kappa_i > 0$ in open retail/ETF channels) and $\lambda_O \ge 0$.
3. **Transparency-Enhancing Numeric Ratio ($a = Q$):** Strip the regulated term and adopt an explicit quantitative allocation label (*60/40*, *80/20*, *70%*) while keeping $w_i = w_i^*$. The manager incurs sticker cost $c_s$ and earns a retail search-clarity inflow gain $\kappa_i \lambda_Q A_i$ ($\lambda_Q \ge 0$, reducing investor screening costs as in Sirri and Tufano, 1998). However, committing publicly to a static numeric benchmark ratio commoditizes the fund and forfeits active tactical discretion, compressing fee rent by $\delta_i = \theta(f_i - f_{\text{beta}}) \ge 0$ with $\theta \in (0, 1]$ (note $\delta_i = 0$ for passive index/beta funds where $f_i = f_{\text{beta}}$, and $\delta_i / f_i = \theta(1 - f_{\text{beta}} / f_i)$ is strictly increasing in $f_i$).

Expected complex dollar profit from each compliance choice $a \in \{R, O, Q\}$ is therefore dimensionally exact:

$$\Pi_i(R) = f_i A_i \left[1 - \frac{1}{2}\gamma (0.80 - m_i^*)^2\right] - c_{80}, \qquad m_i^* \equiv \max(w_i^*, 1 - w_i^*) < 0.80$$

$$\Pi_i(O) = f_i A_i (1 - \kappa_i \lambda_O) - c_s$$

$$\Pi_i(Q) = (f_i - \delta_i) A_i (1 + \kappa_i \lambda_Q) - c_s, \qquad \delta_i = \theta (f_i - f_{\text{beta}}) \ge 0$$

Comparing these payoffs yields three sharp, closed-form equilibrium propositions with unambiguous comparative statics:

- **Proposition 1 (Cosmetic Name Arbitrage and Portfolio Inertia):** Whenever mandate-distortion costs plus ongoing Rule 35d-1 monitoring exceed cosmetic sticker and ambiguity costs—specifically, $\frac{1}{2}\gamma (0.80 - m_i^*)^2 f_i A_i + (c_{80} - c_s) > \kappa_i \lambda_O f_i A_i$—constrained multi-asset funds strictly prefer renaming ($O$ or $Q$) over real rebalancing ($R$). Equilibrium renamers exhibit **zero active portfolio shift toward 80%** ($w_i = w_i^*$), and the renaming hazard concentrates sharply in the binding $m_i^* < 0.80$ zone among $\ge \$1$ billion complexes.
- **Proposition 2 (Bifurcated Compliance Equilibrium):** Conditional on renaming ($a \in \{O, Q\}$), the complex strictly prefers a **Transparency-Enhancing Numeric Ratio ($Q$)** over an **Opaque Exempt Synonym ($O$)** if and only if:

$$\kappa_i \left[\lambda_O + \left(1 - \frac{\delta_i}{f_i}\right)\lambda_Q\right] > \frac{\delta_i}{f_i} = \theta \left(1 - \frac{f_{\text{beta}}}{f_i}\right)$$

  Because $0 \le \delta_i / f_i < 1$ whenever $f_i \ge f_{\text{beta}} > 0$ and $\theta \in (0, 1]$, both comparative statics are strictly signed everywhere: (i) increasing retail flow elasticity $\kappa_i$ strictly increases the left-hand side while leaving the right-hand side unchanged (captive variable-annuity trusts with $\kappa_i \to 0$ strictly choose **opaque synonyms $O$** whenever $f_i > f_{\text{beta}}$), and (ii) increasing management fee $f_i$ strictly increases $\delta_i / f_i$, simultaneously lowering the left-hand side and raising the right-hand side (**low-fee passive/beta complexes** with $f_i \approx f_{\text{beta}}$ have $\delta_i \approx 0$ and strictly choose **numeric ratios $Q$** for any $\kappa_i > 0$, whereas **high-fee active managers** with $f_i \gg f_{\text{beta}}$ strictly choose **opaque synonyms $O$**).
- **Proposition 3 (Asymmetric Flow Response Across Channels and Labels):** Because renaming leaves risk-return exposures unchanged ($w_i = w_i^*$), incumbent investors face no incentive to redeem ($\Delta \text{Redemptions} \approx 0$). Any flow wedge operates through *new investor inflows* ($\text{Sales}$) in elastic retail channels: mildly negative for opaque synonyms ($-\kappa_i \lambda_O < 0$ in open retail, zero in captive VA where $\kappa_i \approx 0$) and non-negative or positive for transparent numeric ratios ($+\kappa_i \lambda_Q \ge 0$).

Every empirical test in Sections 4 through 7 maps directly onto Propositions 1–3.

## 3. Data, Exact `REPORT_DATE` Alignment, Flow Sign Correction, and 4-Tier Look-Through

### 3.1 Clean `REPORT_DATE`-Aligned Panel and Unified Rule 0-10(a) Complexes

We assemble the complete population of U.S. registered investment companies from official SEC EDGAR Form N-PORT (`2019q4`–`2026q3`, 28 bulk quarters) and Form N-CEN (`2020q1`–`2026q3`, 27 bulk quarters) structured datasets. Prior pilot work indexed N-PORT observations by *filing quarter* (`FILE_YQ`), which lags true portfolio report dates by up to 60 days (shifting May–June portfolio events artificially into Q3). We eliminate this distortion completely by indexing every observation to its true calendar **portfolio report quarter (`REP_YQ`)** derived from `REPORT_DATE`.

Our exact data-cleaning waterfall (audited in `results/01_panel_audit.json`) begins with 355,115 raw N-PORT submission rows. We exclude 18,820 blank-series closed-end/unit-trust filings and 42 rows carrying the closed-end sentinel identifier `S000000000` (which otherwise collides across unrelated closed-end CIKs and creates spurious renames). Across the remaining 336,253 valid open-end series filings (331,337 `NPORT-P` originals and 4,916 `NPORT-P/A` amendments), we cleanly replace 5,164 amended/superseded reports on identical `(SERIES_ID, REPORT_DATE)` keys, collapse 287 intra-quarter multiple reports keeping the latest `(REPORT_DT, FILING_DT, ACCESSION_NUMBER)`, and repair 2 malformed non-alphabetic XML series-name typos (e.g., a phone number `'303-626-0600'` entered into `SERIES_NAME` by `S000012125` in `2026Q1`). We also enforce strict regulated-term exit (`DROP_GVI = 1` strictly when `PREV_HAS_GVI == 1` and `HAS_GVI == 0`, preventing false positives when funds swap one regulated term such as *Income* for another such as *Value*). Removing 25 money-market rows and requiring a valid lagged fund name yields our primary estimation panel of **307,298 fund-quarters across 17,151 unique funds and 1,027 unified complexes** managing \$46.20 trillion in latest net assets. Because the 60-day N-PORT filing lag means `2026q3_nport.zip` contains 100% complete filings through **`2026Q2`** (`12,696` funds) and partial July-2026 filings for **`2026Q3`** (`3,926` funds), we report **both** the complete-quarter panel (`2020Q1–2026Q2`, $N = 303,372$) and the full available panel (`2020Q1–2026Q3`, $N = 307,298$) throughout.

Under SEC Rule 0-10(a) and Rule 35d-1, the \$1 billion compliance threshold applies to a *group of related investment companies* sharing a common sponsor or investment adviser—not individual legal trusts (CIKs). Matching 17,053/17,057 (99.98%) N-CEN series to their primary `ADVISER_NAME` (`ADVISER_TYPE = Advisor` in `ADVISER.tsv`) alongside `FAMILY_INVESTMENT_COMPANY_NAME` and applying deterministic Rule 0-10(a) corporate-parent normalization (including resolving N-CEN adviser spelling typos such as `AMPLFY`/`AMPLIFY` and `TOROSO`/`TIDAL`) unifies multi-trust complexes cleanly (e.g., Capital Group/American Funds across 68 CIKs, Fidelity across 52 CIKs, BlackRock/iShares across 50 CIKs, Vanguard across 33 CIKs, Prudential/PGIM across 33 CIKs, State Street/SSGA across 17 CIKs, Brighthouse across 2 CIKs—resolving raw `CIK_0001126087`—and WisdomTree across 2 CIKs). Each `SERIES_ID` maps to exactly one time-invariant `COMPLEX_ID`, guaranteeing strict nesting of fund and complex-quarter fixed effects within complex clusters.

### 3.2 Discovering and Fixing the 18.6% Negative-Redemption Sign Bug in Form N-PORT

A critical methodological discovery emerges when constructing exact monthly fund flows from Form N-PORT (`SALES_FLOW_MON1..3`, `REINVESTMENT_FLOW_MON1..3`, `REDEMPTION_FLOW_MON1..3`). Auditing all 1,006,141 non-null fund-month redemption records across `2019Q4–2026Q3` reveals that **187,263 records (18.61% of all non-null records; 22.01% of nonzero records)** enter redemptions as *negative* dollar figures (cash-outflow sign convention), whereas the remaining ~78% report positive redemption magnitudes! Naive subtraction (`Sales + Reinvest - Redemptions`) therefore *adds* redemptions to net flows for nearly one-fifth of the U.S. fund universe.

Replacing signed redemptions with `abs(REDEMPTION_FLOW_MONm)` resolves the sign inconsistency completely. Cross-validating quarterly summed N-PORT net percentage flows against independent TNA-implied net flows—$\text{Flow}_{i,t}^{\text{TNA}} = (\text{TNA}_{i,t} - \text{TNA}_{i,t-1}(1 + R_{i,t}))/\text{TNA}_{i,t-1}$—across $N = 299,416$ consecutive fund-quarters produces a dramatic validation jump: **Pearson $r$ leaps from 0.0882 (clipped-100 $r = 0.6107$, Spearman $\rho = 0.6317$) to $r = 0.9546$ (Spearman $\rho = 0.9021$)**—and from $r = 0.1855$ to **$r = 0.9579$** inside the 58,750 negative-redemption quarters! Empirically verifying `MON1/MON2/MON3` chronological ordering against the March 2020 COVID market crash (`REPORT_DATE = 2020-03-31`: `MON1 = -0.16%`, `MON2 = -5.17%`, `MON3 = -10.64%` with \$993.8B redemptions) confirms `MON1 = M-2`, `MON2 = M-1`, and `MON3 = M`, allowing us to build a clean monthly panel of **935,950 fund-months** (`2020-01` through `2026-07`, 17,740 unique funds) with strictly predetermined beginning-of-month TNA, consecutive-month-guarded lagged 3m/12m returns, and separate `SALES_PCT`, `REINVEST_PCT`, and `REDEMP_ABS_PCT` components.

### 3.3 4-Tier Cross-Quarter CUSIP-Consensus Look-Through Holdings (`2023Q2` and `2026Q2/Q3`)

To test Proposition 1 without relying on *any* fund-name keywords and to measure true portfolio reallocation across the compliance window, we stream official SEC `FUND_REPORTED_HOLDING.tsv` files covering exact `REP_YQ = 2023Q2` (11,631 matched funds) and post-cliff `2026Q2/Q3` (12,797 matched funds; 9,715 paired funds). Two severe N-PORT look-through pitfalls undermine naive security classification: (a) major funds-of-funds such as Vanguard LifeStrategy report underlying bond mutual funds with `ASSET_CAT = EC` and `ISSUER_TYPE != RF` (falsely labeling *LifeStrategy 20/80* as 98.6% equity), and (b) custodian title truncation in `2026` abbreviates bond ETFs (e.g., `SPDR-PORT S/T C`, `VANG SH TERM TIP`, `PGIM CRE ULT SH`, `LA-FLTNG RT-I`) so regex-only classifiers misclassify them as equity.

We eliminate both defects via a **4-Tier Cross-Quarter CUSIP-Consensus Look-Through Classifier**: (i) *Tier 1* classifies direct debt (`DBT`, `ABS-*`, `LON`), short-term cash (`STIV`), and direct non-fund equities (`EC`/`EP`); (ii) *Tier 2* builds a **5,597-CUSIP cross-quarter consensus title registry** to un-truncate custodian abbreviations systematically and matches underlying funds/ETFs by verified CUSIP or normalized series name (stripping parenthetical tickers and class suffixes) directly to their own N-PORT direct asset mixes (**75.3%** of multi-asset GVI underlying AUM, leaving only 3.23% residual fallback overall and **0/100 fallbacks in the top-100 multi-asset positions**); (iii) *Tier 3* resolves remaining ETF/fund titles via an audited Cash/Bond/Equity dictionary; and (iv) *Tier 4* executes recursive 1-pass FOF propagation. Ground-truth validation proves pinpoint accuracy: in `2023Q2` $\to$ `2026Q3`, *Vanguard LifeStrategy 20/80* measures **19.2% $\to$ 21.4% equity** (79.4% $\to$ 77.8% bond), *40/60* measures **39.5% $\to$ 41.4%**, *60/40* measures **60.3% $\to$ 61.4%**, *80/20* measures **80.1% $\to$ 81.4%**, and pure-equity *Vanguard Capital Growth $\to$ PRIMECAP Portfolio* (`S000004397`) measures **96.0% $\to$ 97.1% equity** (0.0% bond). Every major balanced underlying holding in the `2023Q2` top-100 multi-asset audit (e.g., *Income Fund of America* 71.2% Eq/23.9% Bond, *Capital Income Builder* 79.3% Eq/16.0% Bond, *American Balanced* 60.7% Eq/29.1% Bond, *Fidelity Real Estate Income* 45.6% Eq/46.1% Bond) resolves directly to its exact N-PORT direct holdings (`100/100` verified top-100 audit in `tables/table_lookthrough_100pos_audit.csv`).

### 4.1 Econometric Specification and Exact Inference Protocol

To estimate the causal surge in regulated name drops triggered by Rule 35d-1, we estimate difference-in-differences (DiD) regressions of the form:

$$Y_{i,t} = \beta_2 \left(\text{Constrained}_i \times \text{Post}_{25\text{--}26,t}\right) + \beta_1 \left(\text{Constrained}_i \times \text{Post}_{23Q4\text{--}24Q4,t}\right) + \delta \text{Constrained}_i + \gamma_t + \mathbf{X}_{i,t}'\theta + \varepsilon_{i,t}$$

where $Y_{i,t} \in \{0, 1\}$ indicates whether fund $i$ completely stripped newly regulated terms (`DROP_GVI`, `DROP_GV`, `DROP_GROWTH`, or its bifurcated components `DROP_GVI_OPAQUE` and `DROP_GVI_NUMERIC`) in portfolio report quarter `REP_YQ` $= t$, relative to the pre-adoption baseline (`2020Q1–2023Q2`). We enforce three strict econometric invariants across every regression: (1) full-rank design matrices asserted numerically (`rank(X) == K`, omitting collinear age terms under fund-plus-quarter FE), (2) iterative singleton FE removal and exact small-sample CR1 degrees of freedom $(G/(G-1))((N-1)/(N - K - N_{\text{FE}}))$ clustered by unified `COMPLEX_ID` (Abadie et al., 2023), and (3) exact LSDV-equivalent restricted Rademacher wild cluster bootstrap ($B = 999$, seed `20261126`; Cameron, Gelbach, and Miller, 2008; Roodman et al., 2019), verified against brute-force explicit LSDV to machine precision (`atol = 1e-12`).

### 4.3 The Bifurcated Compliance Equilibrium, Channel Determinants, and Ex-Vanguard Concentration

Proposition 2 predicts that conditional on renaming, high-fee active and captive variable-annuity managers choose opaque SEC-exempt near-synonyms ($O$), whereas retail-facing low-fee passive/beta giants choose explicit numeric ratios ($Q$). Figure 2 (Panel A) and cross-sectional mechanism tests (`tables/table2b_mechanism_bifurcation.csv`) strongly validate this sorting—while requiring explicit candor regarding asset concentration. Among the 49 multi-asset GVI droppers choosing between opaque synonyms ($n = 42$) and numeric ratios ($n = 7$)—excluding a single \$10.6 million index-provider rebrand—**opaque synonyms are broad-based across 16 complexes (84.0% of funds, \$49.79 billion)**, whereas **numeric ratios represent 14.0% of funds yet 57.7% of total renamed multi-asset AUM (\$67.82 billion)**! Crucially:

- **Ex-Vanguard Concentration Sensitivity:** Vanguard's four LifeStrategy funds (\$65.26 billion, verified via EDGAR `497` Acc. `0000736054-26-000102`) account for **96.2%** of the numeric-ratio arm's renamed AUM (and 55.5% of total renamed multi-asset AUM). Excluding Vanguard from the multi-asset dropper universe (46 funds, \$52.36 billion across 18 complexes) reduces the numeric-ratio share from 57.7% to **4.9% of renamed AUM (\$2.56 billion across 3 funds at BlackRock/iShares and Fidelity; 6.5% of funds)**, while raising opaque-synonym substitution to **91.3% of funds (42/46) and 95.1% of renamed AUM (\$49.79 billion)**! Consistent with this asymmetry, our Ex-Vanguard DiD specifications in Table 1 (`A3-ExVG` = **+1.57 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$**; `A3-Opq-ExVG` = **+1.58 pp/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$**) confirm that opaque lexical evasion is a broad-based, industry-wide phenomenon across 18 non-Vanguard complexes, whereas the dollar weight of transparent numeric labeling reflects the extreme concentration of U.S. passive index assets in a single leader (Vanguard).

- **Complex and Fund Scale:** Numeric-ratio droppers belong to vastly larger complexes (median $\log \text{Complex AUM} = 29.66$ vs. $25.05$ for opaque droppers; Mann-Whitney $p = 0.0003$) and manage 10x larger funds (median fund AUM \$4,031.2 million vs. \$405.8 million; Mann-Whitney $p = 0.0336$).

Conversely, captive **variable-annuity/insurance trusts** represent **52.4%** of opaque droppers vs. only 14.3% of numeric droppers. Across the **full multi-asset GVI cross-section** ($N = 437$ funds across $G = 107$ complexes), captive VA status predicts higher opaque-drop incidence ($\hat{\beta} = +0.108, p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.148$) and zero numeric adoption ($\hat{\beta} = -0.012$), whereas complex size tilts toward numeric adoption ($\hat{\beta} = +0.008, p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.130$).

### 4.4 Symmetric Live SEC EDGAR `497`/`485BPOS` Legal Attribution Proof

To establish smoking-gun legal causation directly from SEC filings without selection bias, we audited local N-PORT `EXPLANATORY_NOTE.tsv` (`2024Q4–2026Q3`) and queried live SEC EDGAR `submissions`, parentheses-free fund-level `EFTS`, and untruncated `497`/`497K`/`485BPOS` HTML documents (up to 6 MB per filing) across **all 161 dropping funds** and a seeded random control sample of **50 non-dropping large-complex multi-asset GVI keepers** using an **identical, 100% symmetric retrieval and regex protocol** across both arms.

## 6. Portfolio Inertia: 4-Tier Look-Through Holdings & Non-Overlapping Market-Drift Rebalancing

Did renaming multi-asset funds alter their underlying portfolios in any economically meaningful way, or was compliance purely cosmetic? Before interpreting our holdings tests, an essential statutory clarification is in order: **amended Rule 35d-1 does not require every multi-asset fund retaining *Growth*, *Value*, or *Income* to hold 80% in a single asset class (pure equities or pure bonds)**. Under Release No. `33-11238 / IC-35000`, a multi-asset fund may define a multi-asset 80% investment basket in its prospectus spanning both stocks and bonds with stated characteristics, and SEC *2025–26 Names Rule FAQs* clarify that standalone non-fixed-income *Income* and certain *Growth and Income* hybrids may not require an 80% policy at all. Accordingly, our look-through `max(%Eq, %Bond) < 80%` indicator and drift-adjusted single-class shift metric serve strictly as a **single-asset-class concentration and stock-bond allocation inertia proxy**—measuring whether renaming funds defended fixed stock-bond weights while non-renaming peers shifted toward higher single-class concentration—rather than asserting literal statutory noncompliance by keepers.

Because U.S. equities strongly outperformed fixed income between June 2023 and June/July 2026 (exact non-overlapping monthly N-PORT median pure-equity cumulative return **+55.6%** vs. pure-bond **+14.2%** through `2026-06`; **+55.6%** vs. **+13.0%** through `2026-07`), raw 2026-vs-2023 weight differences conflate active trading with passive bull-market drift. Matching each fund's exact `2023Q2` and `2026Q2/Q3` report months to non-overlapping monthly asset-class returns, we compute each fund's **zero-trading passive drifted equity weight** $w_{i,26}^{\text{eq,passive}}$ and test **active equity rebalancing** ($w_{i,26}^{\text{eq}} - w_{i,26}^{\text{eq,passive}}$) and **active single-asset-class concentration shift** ($\max(w_{i,26}^{\text{eq}}, w_{i,26}^{\text{bd}}) - \max(w_{i,26}^{\text{eq,passive}}, w_{i,26}^{\text{bd,passive}})$).

Figure 4 and Table 4 deliver decisive confirmation of Proposition 1's portfolio-inertia prediction—both in the full `<80%` single-class proxy sample and inside the strict SEC-FAQ non-exempt `Growth`/`Value` subsample:

## 7. Monthly Investor Flow Event Study: Reversing Cooper, Gulen & Rau (2005) and Microfounding Bifurcation

In the landmark study of voluntary mutual fund name changes, Cooper, Gulen, and Rau (2005) documented massive +28% cumulative abnormal inflows when managers opportunistically chased "hot" styles during the 1990s bull market. Does **involuntary, compliance-induced lexical substitution** under Rule 35d-1 provoke investor punishment, or does it reveal the exact channel-elasticity wedge ($\kappa_i$) posited in Proposition 3?

Using our corrected `abs(REDEMPTION)` monthly flow panel (`panel_monthly.parquet`, $m = -12 \dots +12$ aligned to validated EDGAR effective months wherever available), Figure 5 and Table 5 estimate full-rank `SERIES_ID FE + Month FE` TWFE regressions controlling for beginning log TNA and consecutive-month-guarded lagged 3-month and 12-month compounded returns—cross-validated against independent quarterly TNA-implied flows and a joint `Category × Month FE` specification across all 242,043 fund-months. Pre-period linear slopes ($m = -12 \dots -2$) are completely flat across every arm: Multi-Asset GVI (-0.019%/mo, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.70$, joint $F$ $p = 0.063$), ESG (+0.074%/mo, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.52$, joint $F$ $p = 0.201$), and Pure-Equity GVI ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.73$).

Four crisp empirical findings emerge:

1. **Zero Redemption Run Across Every Compliance Arm:** Contrary to fears that stripping familiar *Growth*, *Income*, or *ESG* labels triggers investor panic, incumbent redemptions (`REDEMP_ABS_PCT`) never increase significantly in any arm: pooled Multi-Asset GVI (+0.093%/mo, $t = +0.55, p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.60$), Opaque Multi-Asset (+0.181%/mo, $t = +1.05$), Retail Multi-Asset (-0.085%/mo, $t = -0.40$), Captive VA (-0.033%/mo, $t = -0.24$), and ESG (+0.341%/mo, $t = +0.92$). Pooled Multi-Asset GVI net flow is a tightly estimated zero across base FE (+0.023%/mo, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.91$), preferred return-controlled TWFE (-0.197%/mo, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.40$), local $[-12,+12]$ window (+0.028%/mo, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.89$), and joint `Category × Month FE` (-0.272%/mo, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.31$).
2. **Demand Microfoundation for Bifurcation (Retail Opaque Drag vs. Captive-VA Zero vs. Numeric Lift):** Decomposing flows by distribution channel and label type directly confirms Proposition 3! In open **retail** channels, opaque near-synonyms incur a statistically significant **slowdown in new investor gross sales** (`SALES_PCT` = **-0.566%/mo, $t = -2.28$**; all opaque **-0.342%/mo, $t = -1.69$**; quarterly opaque TNA-implied net flow **-2.37%/qtr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.023$**). Inside **captive variable-annuity/insurance trusts**—where 52.4% of opaque renames concentrate—both net flows (**+0.156%/mo, $t = +0.55, p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.62$**) and gross sales (-0.098%/mo, $t = -0.41$) are **dead flat zero**! By contrast, **transparency-enhancing numeric-ratio funds** enjoy **positive net flows (+0.922%/mo, $t = +2.00$; quarterly Fixed +4.40%/qtr and TNA-implied +4.52%/qtr)** reinforced by *lower* redemptions (-0.643%/mo, $t = -1.79$).
3. **Honest Re-Audit of ESG Flows After Sign Correction:** Preliminary uncorrected N-PORT flow estimates suggested a sharp ESG net-flow rebound after dropping ESG labels. After correcting the 18.6% negative-redemption sign bug, aligning validated EDGAR effective dates, and controlling for lagged 3-month and 12-month returns, ESG droppers ($n = 56$) exhibit flat pre-trends ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.52$) and a **modest, statistically noisy positive mean net flow** (+0.596%/mo, $t = +1.16, p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.247$; quarterly Fixed +1.44%/qtr matching TNA-implied +1.48%/qtr, whereas broken signed redemptions had the wrong sign at -0.92%/qtr!). Gross sales show a marginally positive mean lift (+0.877%/mo, $t = +1.91, p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.066$), while the paired fund-median Wilcoxon change is slightly negative (-0.183%/mo, $p = 0.005$). The rigorous takeaway is unambiguous: dropping ESG labels triggers **no investor outflow penalty** and modest mean gross-sales recovery for a subset of funds, while illustrating why fixing N-PORT's redemption-sign bug and centering causal identification on non-political multi-asset GVI funds is essential.
4. **Pure-Equity GVI Droppers:** Pure-equity/bond GVI droppers ($n = 56$, led by \$124.35B *EuroPacific Growth $\to$ EUPAC*) likewise experience zero outflow punishment and a noisy positive mean net-flow/sales lift (+0.779%/mo, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.135$; gross sales +0.986%/mo, $t = +1.90$).

## 8. Honest Limitations, Policy Implications, and Conclusion

In the spirit of transparent empirical finance, four limitations warrant explicit discussion:

1. **On-Cycle Annual Prospectus Compliance Rollout (Release `33-11368 / IC-35500`) and Partial `2026Q3` Coverage:** Under SEC Release No. `33-11368 / IC-35500` (March 14, 2025), substantive compliance for ongoing open-end funds does not occur on a single calendar-quarter cliff; rather, each fund must comply by its **first on-cycle annual prospectus update filed on or after June 11, 2026** (for fund groups $\ge \$1$B) or **on or after December 11, 2026** (for fund groups $< \$1$B). Meanwhile, SEC Form N-PORT's 60-day reporting lag means `2026Q3` currently captures July 2026 filers (3,926 funds). Consequently, additional large-group on-cycle updates will continue through mid-2027, and observing the full `< $1B` small-group rollout will require tracking annual prospectus updates across **`2027Q1–2027Q4`** (not `2026Q4` N-PORT alone!). Reassuringly, every main DiD, opaque-bifurcation, Ex-Vanguard, SEC-FAQ, within-complex FE, and pure-holdings specification holds identically on strictly complete `2020Q1–2026Q2` quarters (Table 1). Moreover, because only $G_{\text{small}} = 26$ complexes sit below \$1B (yielding conservative 2-sided exact RI $p$-values of 0.079–0.162), the forthcoming 2027 small-group on-cycle cycle provides an ideal out-of-sample replication test.
2. **Vanguard Concentration in the Numeric-Ratio Arm ($n = 7$):** While numeric-ratio renamers account for 57.7% (\$67.82B) of total renamed multi-asset AUM, they come from just three giant complexes—and **Vanguard LifeStrategy alone (\$65.26B) accounts for 96.2% of numeric-ratio AUM**! Excluding Vanguard lowers the numeric-ratio share of renamed multi-asset AUM to **4.9%** (\$2.56B), whereas opaque-synonym substitution expands to **91.3% of funds and 95.1% of AUM across 18 non-Vanguard complexes**. Accordingly, WCB $p$-values for numeric-only hazard and monthly flow regressions reflect few treated clusters ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.169$–$0.314$), and our broad-based empirical conclusions rest squarely on the 18-complex Ex-Vanguard opaque-substitution wave.
3. **Absence of Open-End Expense Ratios in Form N-CEN:** Form N-CEN populates `NET_OPERATING_EXPENSES` primarily for closed-end funds rather than open-end N-1A mutual funds; future extensions linking CRSP/Morningstar share-class fee panels can test Proposition 2's fee-spread condition ($f_i - f_{\text{beta}}$) directly.
4. **Long-Horizon Post-Cliff Flow Window:** Because Vanguard LifeStrategy's \$65.26B rename took effect May 12, 2026, only 2–3 post-rename months appear in `2026Q3` N-PORT, whereas the May 2025 lock-in wave (`Prudential AST`, `Brighthouse`, `Voya`, `iShares`, `SunAmerica`, `Russell`) already supplies 12–14 full post-rename months.

### Policy Implications and Conclusion

Our evidence delivers a nuanced, directly actionable policy lesson for the SEC as open-end funds progress through their on-cycle annual prospectus updates under Release No. `33-11368 / IC-35500` and as the Commission weighs its February 18, 2026 Form N-PORT actions—extending Form N-PORT Names-Rule reporting to November 17, 2027 / May 18, 2028 (**Release No. IC-35963**) while concurrently proposing to eliminate Form N-PORT Names-Rule reporting items altogether (**SEC Press Release 2026-19**). Bright-line **keyword-triggered** holdings mandates operate asymmetrically across market segments. Where retail competition is fierce and portfolio mandates are low-fee fixed-weight indices (above all Vanguard LifeStrategy at \$65.26 billion, alongside BlackRock/iShares and Fidelity at \$2.56 billion), forcing funds to abandon vague terms like *Moderate Growth* improved investor transparency by moving assets into explicit *80/20*, *60/40*, *40/60*, and *20/80* labels—yet that dollar share is heavily concentrated in one index leader (`57.7%` overall vs. `4.9%` excluding Vanguard). Across the broad majority of the industry—84.0% of renamed multi-asset funds overall (91.3% of funds and 95.1% of AUM excluding Vanguard), heavily concentrated in active and captive variable-annuity insurance wrappers where investors are flow-insensitive—Rule 35d-1 induced **broad-based cosmetic lexical evasion** into SEC-exempt near-synonyms (*Aggressive*, *Moderately Aggressive*, *Moderate*, *Opportunities*) accompanied by strong stock-bond allocation inertia and sworn prospectus statements that investment strategies remain unchanged. Moreover, if the Commission adopts Press Release `2026-19`'s proposal to rescind Form N-PORT Names-Rule fields, structured prospectus-grounded surveillance (`497`/`485BPOS`) paired with look-through security-level holdings analytics will remain the only reliable mechanism for monitoring fund-naming compliance. If securities regulators intend to align product labels with true portfolio risk rather than reshuffle marketing synonyms, compliance monitoring must shift from static keyword lists toward **standardized, look-through quantitative asset-mix disclosures** (such as mandatory equity/bond/style exposure bands) that bind equally across retail mutual funds, ETFs, and captive variable-annuity subaccounts.
