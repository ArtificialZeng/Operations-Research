## Abstract

When securities regulation expands the reach of fund-name mandates, asset managers face an interconnected choice: retain a fund's existing name and document or adjust its investment basket, replace a potentially covered term with a broad non-numeric descriptor, adopt an explicit numeric allocation label, or alter the underlying portfolio as well. We examine this choice across 17,151 U.S. registered open-end funds and exchange-traded funds (307,298 `REPORT_DATE`-aligned fund-quarters across 1,027 fund complexes through July 2026) during the implementation rollout of the 2023 amendments to SEC Rule 35d-1 (the Names Rule). Focusing on 50 multi-asset funds ($117.63 billion in prior-report assets) that dropped *Growth*, *Value*, or *Income* from their names during 2025Q1–2026Q3, we conduct a per-fund statutory audit linking EDGAR `497`/`485BPOS` filings, registrant CIKs, series identifiers, and fiscal-year-end compliance schedules under SEC Release `33-11368`. Non-numeric descriptors account for 84.0% of funds (42/50, $49.79 billion), whereas numeric allocation ratios account for 14.0% of funds (7/50, $67.82 billion, or 57.7% of assets)—a dollar share heavily concentrated in four Vanguard LifeStrategy funds (96.2% of numeric-ratio assets; excluding Vanguard, numeric ratios represent 4.9% of renamed assets and non-numeric descriptors represent 91.3% of funds and 95.1% of assets). Critically, 49/50 verified effective dates preceded the June 11, 2026 large-group on-cycle start, 36/50 funds do not reach their first legally binding on-cycle annual update until 2027, and filings representing $66.67 billion (56.7% of renamed assets, led by Vanguard) explicitly confirm that investment objectives, strategies, and policies remained unchanged. Against audited EDGAR disclosures, the 35/50 *Growth*/*Value*-without-*Income* filter operates strictly as a lexical screen (8.6% precision for fund-scoped Rule 35d-1 citations) rather than confirmed statutory exposure. While all 15 historical (2021–2022) and unregulated-word renaming placebos are null, joint pre-trends reject in the *Growth*/*Value* lexical screen ($p = 0.043$ and $p = 0.003$), Rambachan and Roth (2023) sensitivity breakdown values are narrow ($\bar{M}^* \in [0.02, 0.18]$), and early-due versus not-yet-due fiscal-year-end cohorts do not yet separate in 2026Q2–2026Q3—requiring a disciplined descriptive and institutional interpretation. Repairing look-through holdings with same-period historical amended N-PORT filings (resolving Capital Income Builder and Income Fund of America and eliminating the default-equity fallback) reduces unmapped multi-asset holdings from 5.25% to 1.53% (0/100 in the top 100 positions) and yields a drift-adjusted largest-class difference of -7.19 percentage points ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$; sharp partial-identification set $[-11.33, -3.24]$ pp across all 2,502 paired funds), with -4.56 pp already emerging by 2024Q2. Exploratory investor-flow regressions across the 7 numeric-ratio funds (3 complexes) show noisy, statistically insignificant differences ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.170$), precluding welfare conclusions.

**Keywords:** Mutual fund names; SEC Rule 35d-1; Multi-asset allocation funds; Form N-PORT look-through; Partial identification; Parallel-trends sensitivity

## 1. Introduction and Institutional Setting

Fund names serve as the primary search anchor in U.S. retail and retirement asset management, shaping investor attention and product categorization (Sirri and Tufano, 1998; Cooper, Gulen, and Rau, 2005; Barber, Huang, and Odean, 2016). When disclosure rules constrain how labels relate to portfolios, managers confront an integrated product-design choice: keep the legacy name and satisfy or document a compliant investment basket, replace a potentially covered term with a broad non-numeric descriptor, adopt a quantitative allocation ratio, or adjust portfolio holdings alongside the name. Prior scholarship shows that mutual fund renamings after the original 2001 Names Rule (`IC-24828`) frequently left underlying holdings unchanged (Espenlaub, ul Haq, and Khurshed, 2017; SEC, 2001), while recent work examines thematic alignment among exchange-traded funds (ETFs) under the 2023 rule expansion (He, Wang, and Ward, 2025) and sustainability-label compliance in ESG funds (Fisch and Robertson, 2023; Gibson Brandon et al., 2022; Kim and Yoon, 2023).

This study extends that literature to **multi-asset allocation funds**—a large, understudied segment encompassing balanced mutual funds, target-risk portfolios, allocation ETFs, and insurance-dedicated variable annuity trusts where stock-and-bond mixing interacts directly with statutory 80% policy design. On September 20, 2023, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission adopted comprehensive amendments to Rule 35d-1 (Release No. `33-11238 / IC-35000`; SEC, 2023), expanding the 80% investment policy mandate beyond asset-class and geographic names to encompass terms suggesting particular investment characteristics such as *growth*, *value*, and ESG themes. Crucially, however, statutory applicability for multi-asset funds is highly contextual. Under the amended rule and the Division of Investment Management's *2025–26 Names Rule FAQs* (SEC, 2025a), a multi-asset fund may define a multi-asset 80% investment basket encompassing multiple asset classes (and, where appropriate, the full value of acquired funds), while standalone non-fixed-income *income*, certain *growth and income* pairings, and modified uses of *growth* or *value* can denote portfolio-wide objectives rather than triggering an 80% policy. A keyword token alone therefore cannot establish legal exposure.

Compliance timing is equally staggered. On March 14, 2025, the SEC issued Release No. `33-11368 / IC-35500` (SEC, 2025b), extending the compliance dates by six months and tying compliance for existing continuously offered open-end funds to each fund's **first "on-cycle" annual prospectus update** filed on or after **June 11, 2026** for larger fund groups (net assets of `$1 billion` or more at the end of the most recent fiscal year) and **December 11, 2026** for smaller fund groups (below `$1 billion`), with substantive compliance required upon effectiveness of that update. Separately, SEC Release `IC-35963` (February 18, 2026; SEC, 2026a) extended Form N-PORT Names Rule reporting dates, and SEC Press Release `2026-19` (SEC, 2026b) proposed eliminating Form N-PORT Names Rule reporting fields entirely—making prospectus-grounded (`497`/`485BPOS`) and look-through holdings audits indispensable for independent monitoring.

Because our Form N-PORT panel runs through July 2026 (complete-quarter endpoint `2026Q2`), it observes only the anticipation window, early annual-cycle adjustments, and the earliest weeks of large-group on-cycle compliance. Accordingly, this paper makes four disciplined, verifiable contributions tailored to institutional finance research:

1. **Per-Fund Statutory EDGAR Verification:** Rather than inferring legal exposure from name tokens, we link all 50 primary multi-asset *Growth*/*Value*/*Income* (`GVI`) renamers and 50 stratified retained-name controls across registrant CIKs, series IDs, fiscal-year-end complex assets, and pre-/post-rename EDGAR `497`/`485BPOS` filings. We document exact effective dates against each fund's first eligible on-cycle date under Release `33-11368`, quantify precision and recall of the 35/50 *Growth*/*Value* lexical screen against filing disclosures, and construct a cleaned same-date control group of 164 substantive unregulated-word multi-asset renames ($287.16 billion).
2. **Counterfactual Pre-Trend Transparency and Sensitivity Bounds:** We document both what passes and what fails standard difference-in-differences diagnostics. All 15 historical same-quarter (2021–2022) and unregulated-word renaming placebos are cleanly null, ruling out generic multi-asset rebranding or calendar drift. Simultaneously, we foreground joint pre-trend rejections in the *Growth*/*Value* lexical screen ($p = 0.043$ and $p = 0.003$), report Rambachan and Roth (2023) $\Delta^{\text{SD}}$ and $\Delta^{\text{RM}}$ trend-deviation breakdown values, decompose announcement versus early-response versus on-cycle phases, and estimate Callaway and Sant'Anna (2021) fiscal-year-end cohort contrasts—explicitly withdrawing causal "regulatory-induced" claims wherever identification assumptions fail.
3. **Repaired Zero-Leakage Look-Through Holdings and Partial-Identification Bounds:** We repair baseline look-through misclassification by streaming amended `REP_YQ = 2023Q2` filings (resolving Capital Income Builder and Income Fund of America directly from same-period historical N-PORT direct holdings) and eliminating the silent 100%-equity fallback completely. Across the full 2,502-fund paired GVI universe, we compute sharp Manski-style partial-identification intervals and outer 95% confidence intervals (Imbens and Manski, 2004) over all remaining unmapped holdings—allowing both deletion and entry of boundary crossers—and reconstruct multi-quarter trajectories (`2023Q2 → 2024Q2 → 2025Q2 → 2026Q2/Q3`) alongside fund-level passive return benchmarks.
4. **Strictly Exploratory Investor-Flow Audit:** We report monthly investor-flow contrasts transparently as exploratory descriptive evidence, emphasizing small treated-cluster counts (7 numeric funds across 3 complexes; $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.170$) and avoiding welfare claims.

## 2. Data, Sample Construction, and Per-Fund Statutory EDGAR Verification

### 2.1. Form N-PORT and Form N-CEN Panel Architecture

Our census links SEC Form N-PORT quarterly portfolio reports (`2019Q4–2026Q3`) with Form N-CEN structural metadata (`2020Q1–2026Q3`), aligned strictly to portfolio `REPORT_DATE` calendar quarters (`REP_YQ`) rather than filing dates. After deduplicating `NPORT-P/A` amendments and excluding closed-end sentinel records and money market funds, the panel contains 307,298 fund-quarter observations (303,372 across complete quarters `2020Q1–2026Q2`) and 935,950 fund-months covering 17,151 unique series across 1,027 unified Rule 0-10(a) fund complexes. In the latest complete quarter (`2026Q2`, covering 12,696 series), the gross row-level sum of net assets is `$44.31` trillion (including fund-of-funds positions and differing fiscal reporting days within the quarter); summing each series' latest observation across all dates instead produces `$46.20` trillion. Full reconciliation of these totals and our Form N-PORT negative-redemption sign harmonization (18.61% negative raw redemption entries, lifting Pearson concordance with asset-and-return-implied flows from $r = 0.088$ to $r = 0.955$ across 299,416 fund-quarters) are documented in the separate **Data and Measurement Appendix** (Supplementary Information, Appendix A).

We identify fund-name exits by comparing standardized `SERIES_NAME` strings across consecutive `REPORT_DATE` observations for each `SERIES_ID`. A `GVI` exit occurs strictly when a series containing *Growth*, *Value*, or *Income* in quarter $t-1$ no longer contains any of those terms in quarter $t$. Multi-asset allocation funds are flagged using pre-period name descriptors combined with N-CEN fund-of-funds and target-date structural fields, plus a separate purely holdings-based baseline concentration proxy (`max(%Equity, %Bond) < 80%` in `2023Q2`) that uses zero name keywords. To weight renames without post-event feedback, we use prior-report net assets. Among all 161 funds ($299.03 billion across 65 complexes) exiting `GVI` or `ESG` terms during `2025Q1–2026Q3`, exactly **50 multi-asset `GVI` funds** ($117.63 billion across 19 complexes) form our primary focus group.

### 2.2. Per-Fund Statutory 80% Policy, FYE Compliance Schedule, and Two-Pass EDGAR Audit

To overcome the limitations of automated token screening, we execute a per-fund statutory verification across all **50 primary multi-asset renamers** and a **stratified control sample of 50 retained-name multi-asset `GVI` funds** (stratified by `< $1B` vs. `≥ $1B` complex tier, captive variable-annuity channel, and *Growth*/*Value* vs. *Income* wording). Every fund is linked via registrant `CIK10`, `SERIES_ID`, and N-PORT `FYE_MONTH`, cross-checked against EDGAR registrant submissions metadata (`submissions/CIK*.json`) and historical `485BPOS` annual-update cycles. Under SEC Release `33-11368` (SEC, 2025b), we measure each complex's net assets as of the most recent fiscal year end prior to June 11, 2026: 49/50 renamers belong to large fund groups (`≥ $1B`, eligible on-cycle start June 11, 2026) and 1/50 (*Donoghue Forlines Risk Managed Income Fund*, complex FYE assets `$0.405B`) belongs to a small fund group (`< $1B`, eligible on-cycle start December 11, 2026).

Mapping each fund's fiscal year end to its standard 120-day post-FYE Form N-1A annual update reveals a striking institutional fact. While Pass 1 (automated `497`/`485BPOS` parsing) recovered 36 effective dates (35/36 before June 11, 2026), Pass 2 (fund-scoped pre-/post-rename document retrieval across cached and live EDGAR filings) verified effective dates for **all 50/50 renamers**. Exactly **49/50 (`98.0%`)** took effect *before* June 11, 2026, and **49/50** took effect *before* their own fund's first statutory on-cycle annual prospectus update. Indeed, 40/50 funds do not reach their first eligible on-cycle update until after our July 31, 2026 sample end, and **36/50 (`72.0%`)**—including 23 December-31 FYE funds (annual update ~May 1, 2027) and 10 October-31 FYE funds such as Vanguard LifeStrategy (annual update ~February 28, 2027)—are not legally required to complete on-cycle compliance until **2027**. Only one fund (*Aristotle Portfolio Optimization Aggressive Growth Fund*, `S000080071`, effective `2026-07-29`, March 31 FYE) renamed on its exact first eligible on-cycle date, and even that fund renamed alongside unregulated multi-asset peers in the same complex-quarter.

### 2.3. Validation of the 35/50 Growth/Value Lexical Screen and Unregulated Control Group

Because the SEC's *2025–26 Names Rule FAQs* (SEC, 2025a) distinguish standalone *income* and certain *growth and income* funds from single-focus *growth* or *value* names, `35/50` primary renamers (`$104.55B`) pass a narrow *Growth*/*Value*-without-*Income* lexical filter. Testing this filter against our audited EDGAR disclosures demonstrates why it **must be labeled strictly as a lexical screen, not confirmed statutory exposure**: its precision against Pass-2 fund-scoped Rule 35d-1 mentions is `8.6%` (recall `100.0%`, false-positive rate `68.1%`), against Pass-1 document-level Rule 35d-1 mentions is `25.7%` (recall `90.0%`), and against Pass-2 fund-scoped 80% policy disclosures is `22.9%` (recall `80.0%`). Moreover, `7` of the 35 screened funds are Vanguard unchanged-policy or iShares index-paired renames. Because independent legal double-coding of full historical N-1A registration statements by securities counsel lies beyond automated filing audits, the remainder of this paper maintains a **disciplined descriptive and institutional framework** whenever individual statutory applicability cannot be confirmed from public filings.

To benchmark these renames against routine industry rebranding, we extract a **same-date unregulated-word renaming control group** from `panel_quarterly.parquet`: all multi-asset funds undergoing a substantive word-level name change during `2025Q1–2026Q3` without dropping any `GVI`, `ESG`, or legacy 2001 regulated term (after stripping `9` cosmetic trademark `®`/punctuation edits). This yields **164 unregulated-word multi-asset renames** across 46 complexes (`$287.16` billion)—confirming that multi-asset rebranding is an active ongoing phenomenon that must be directly tested via counterfactual placebos.

## 3. Naming Responses, Pre-Trend Transparency, and Counterfactual Placebo Audits

### 3.1. Category Concentration and Ex-Vanguard Sensitivity

Across the 50 multi-asset `GVI` renames (`$117.63B`), manager label choices diverge sharply between fund counts and asset weights (Figure 1, Panel A). Exactly **42 funds (`84.0%`, `$49.79B`, `42.3%` of assets across 16 complexes)** adopted broad non-numeric risk or allocation descriptors (such as *Aggressive*, *Moderate*, *Dynamic*, or *Retirement*), including `22/42` (`52.4%`) captive variable-annuity insurance portfolios and `0.0%` index funds or ETFs. Only **7 funds (`14.0%`, across 3 complexes: Vanguard, BlackRock/iShares, and Fidelity VIP)** adopted explicit numeric ratios (`80/20`, `60/40`, `40/60`, `20/80`, `70%`), alongside 1 fund (`Global X Thematic Growth → Dorsey Wright Thematic`, `$0.01B`) reflecting an index/brand transition.

Although numeric ratios represent `57.7%` (`$67.82B`) of renamed multi-asset assets overall, Vanguard's four LifeStrategy funds alone account for **`96.2%` (`$65.26B`)** of numeric-ratio assets. **Excluding Vanguard** (`46` funds, `$52.36B` across 18 complexes), numeric ratios fall to **`4.9%` of renamed assets (`$2.56B`, `3/46` funds)**, whereas non-numeric descriptors comprise **`91.3%` of funds (`42/46`) and `95.1%` of renamed assets (`$49.79B`)**. Thus, broad non-numeric relabeling is widespread across complexes, whereas quantitative ratio labeling is heavily concentrated in one index leader.

Inspection of quarter-level event-study coefficients traces these rejections to negative pre-rule contrasts in `2020Q1` (`-0.49` pp, $p = 0.012$), `2020Q4` (`-0.36` pp), `2021Q2` (`-0.35` pp), and `2021Q4` (`-0.39` pp). Larger point estimates in `FAQ2` (`+2.78` pp) and `FAQ4` (`+1.98` pp; within-complex FE `+2.50` pp) therefore cannot be treated as clean causal policy timing.
3. **Narrow Rambachan–Roth (2023) Trend-Deviation Breakdown Thresholds:** Because the `2025Q1–2026Q3` target window lies 7 to 13 quarters beyond the `2023Q2` reference quarter, even modest nonlinear trend deviations accumulate rapidly across quarters. Under Rambachan and Roth's (2023) smoothness restriction $\Delta^{\text{SD}}(M)$, the 95% fixed-length confidence interval includes zero once per-quarter curvature exceeds $M^* = 0.004$ pp/qtr² in `GVI All`, `0.009` in `GVI Opaque`, and `0.002` in `FAQ4`. Under relative magnitudes $\Delta^{\text{RM}}(\bar{M})$, robust 95% intervals include zero at $\bar{M}^* = 0.18$ for `GVI Opaque`, `0.07` for `GVI All`, and just `0.02` for `FAQ4` (Figure 2, Panel C). Long-horizon parallel-trends extrapolation is therefore fragile to small departures from exact parallel trends.

### 3.3. Historical Same-Quarter Placebos, Unregulated-Word Placebos, and Callaway–Sant'Anna Cohort Audit

## 4. Repaired Look-Through Holdings, Multi-Quarter Portfolio Paths, and Partial-Identification Bounds

### 4.1. Repairing Historical Look-Through Misclassification and Eliminating Default-Equity Fallbacks

Evaluating whether renaming managers simultaneously altered underlying portfolios requires accurate look-through stock-and-bond weights for fund-of-funds structures using **strictly historical pre-rule data** (never leaking 2026 holdings or titles into the `2023Q2` baseline). In earlier iterations, 5.25% of multi-asset `GVI` underlying-fund assets fell into a default 100%-equity fallback (`TIER3_FALLBACK_EQ`), including `$5.44` billion across four positions in *Capital Income Builder* and *Income Fund of America* whose `REP_YQ = 2023Q2` N-PORT reports were amended in `FILE_QTR = 2023Q4`, plus truncated bond-fund titles (such as GuideStone Medium-Duration Bond and SunAmerica Fixed Income portfolios) and Dimensional's short-term cash collateral vehicle (`23320U405`).

We repair this pipeline completely in `scripts/03_holdings_lookthrough_rebalancing.py`:

1. **Same-Period Historical Amended N-PORT Recovery:** We stream the `109` amended `REP_YQ = 2023Q2` filings filed in `2023Q4` (`70`), `2024Q1` (`29`), and `2024Q2` (`10`)—preserving 100% strict pre-rule portfolio timestamps (`April–June 2023`). This resolves *Capital Income Builder* (`S000009000`: **74.9% equity / 20.4% bond / 8.0% cash**; AFIS `S000045163`: 75.4% / 20.2%), *Income Fund of America* (`S000008814`: **68.2% equity / 24.3% bond / 8.9% cash**), *GuideStone Medium-Duration Bond Fund* (`S000001155`: 100.9% bond), and *DFA Short-Term Extended Quality* (`S000024202`: 95.9% bond) directly from their historical `2023Q2` direct holdings, while verifying Vanguard LifeStrategy (`20/80`, `40/60`, `60/40`, `80/20`) within `±1.5` pp of stated targets.
2. **Elimination of Silent 100%-Equity Defaults:** All remaining unrecognized positive underlying-fund holdings are strictly tagged **`TIER_UNMAPPED`** with zero assumed equity or bond weight and tracked explicitly per fund in both `2023Q2` and `2026Q2/Q3`. Across multi-asset `GVI` funds in `2023Q2`, unmapped assets fall from **5.25% to `1.53%`**, with **`0/100` unmapped positions in the top-100 holdings audit** (`tables/table_lookthrough_100pos_audit.csv`).
3. **Boundary-Crosser Resolution Across the Full Paired Universe:** Under the prior fallback, 41 of 2,482 paired `GVI` funds (including 5 renamers) could cross the 80% single-class threshold. After repairing amended historical filings and truncated bond titles, three misclassified renamers (*GuideStone Growth Allocation*, *SA Allocation Growth*, and *SA Allocation Moderate Growth*) enter the `<80%` sample cleanly, and remaining boundary crossers across the entire **2,502-fund paired `GVI` universe** drop to **21 funds (`0` renamers, `21` retained funds)**.

We define the empirical baseline concentration indicator `max(%Equity, %Bond) < 80%` directly from the two look-through weights. In a simplified two-asset portfolio whose weights sum to one, `max(w, 1 − w) < 0.80` holds exactly when `0.20 < w < 0.80`; in the data, cash and other holdings can make stock and bond weights sum to less than one, so the empirical proxy is computed directly from the two weights. We emphasize that this single-class concentration indicator **does not measure literal statutory noncompliance**, because amended Rule 35d-1 permits prospectus-defined multi-asset 80% baskets.

### 4.2. Multi-Quarter Trajectories, Fund-Level Drift, and Full Partial-Identification Bounds

To distinguish active target-weight maintenance from passive bull-market equity drift (`+55.6%` pure equity vs. `+14.2%` pure bond from `2023-06` to `2026-06`), we compute three complementary passive counterfactuals matched to each fund's exact report months: (i) broad non-overlapping monthly equity/bond medians, (ii) style-stratified domestic/international equity and core/high-yield bond medians, and (iii) **fund-level realized-return-calibrated passive drift** using each fund's own monthly compounded total return from `panel_monthly.parquet`. We also stream intermediate `REP_YQ = 2024Q2` (`11,768` funds) and `REP_YQ = 2025Q2` (`12,045` funds) snapshots to construct full **four-point portfolio trajectories (`2023Q2 → 2024Q2 → 2025Q2 → 2026Q2/Q3`)**.

Finally, for every fund in the paired universe with positive `TIER_UNMAPPED` holdings in either `2023Q2` or `2026Q2/Q3`, we allow unmapped holdings to take *any* admissible split across equity, bond, and other assets in the simplex $\Delta^2$, optimizing jointly over both sample-membership boundary crossers (testing deletion *and* maximal entry) and worst-case follow-up allocations to obtain exact Manski-style **sharp partial-identification sets** and Imbens and Manski (2004) outer 95% confidence bounds. Three substantive findings stand out:

1. **Robust Concentration Divergence Across Benchmarks and Partial-Identification Sets:** In the repaired `<80%` `GVI` sample (`39` vs. `350` funds), renamed funds actively trimmed passive drift by `-3.62` pp while retained funds moved `+3.57` pp toward `≥80%` single-class concentration—a difference of **`-7.19` pp** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$). Results remain nearly identical under style-stratified benchmarks (`-7.12` pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), fund-level realized-return passive drift (`-6.59` pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.005$), and deletion of all `21` remaining crossers (`-7.54` pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$). 2. **Multi-Quarter Paths Reveal Pre-Existing Target-Weight Rebalancing Discipline:** Tracing the four historical snapshots in Figure 3 (Panel A) uncovers a vital economic distinction. Over half of the 3-year divergence (`-4.56` pp of `-7.19` pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$) had **already materialized between `2023Q2` and `2024Q2`**—before the 2025–2026 renaming wave occurred—expanding modestly to `-5.47` pp by `2025Q2` ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$) and `-7.19` pp by `2026Q2/Q3` ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$). Renamed multi-asset funds maintained stable mean largest-class weights (`67.3% → 68.3% → 67.7% → 66.3%`) alongside significantly higher `IS_FOF`-controlled 3-year top-level CUSIP overlap (`69.7%` vs. `49.7%`, diff `+11.45` pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.023$), whereas retained funds had already concentrated upward from `57.8%` to `63.2%` by `2024Q2`.
3. **Manager-Choice Interpretation:** Rather than reflecting sudden post-rename liquidation or rebalancing shocks, the joint naming-and-holdings evidence points to **manager selection along pre-existing portfolio architecture**: rigid fixed-ratio target-risk allocators (which systematically rebalance back toward interior stock-bond weights like 60/40 or 40/60) disproportionately exited *Growth*/*Value*/*Income* labels while preserving their underlying asset mix, whereas more flexible or higher-concentration funds retained their names.

We emphasize strict interpretive discipline on these flow patterns:

1. **No Evidence of Redemption Runs or Statistically Robust Numeric-Label Premia:** Across all 50 multi-asset renamers pooled, monthly net flows are statistically indistinguishable from zero (`-0.20` pp/mo, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.404$). 3. **Institutional and Regulatory Monitoring Implications:** Our findings carry direct relevance for ongoing rulemaking. Because SEC Press Release `2026-19` (SEC, 2026b) proposes eliminating Form N-PORT Names Rule reporting fields altogether, public evaluation of Rule 35d-1 will continue to depend on linking EDGAR prospectus supplements (`497`/`485BPOS`) with look-through N-PORT holdings. Moreover, because `36/50` large-complex renamers and virtually all `< $1B` small complexes reach their first legally binding on-cycle updates between late 2026 and late 2027, formal staggered causal identification of binding compliance—comparing funds with verified pre- and post-update 80% baskets against matched not-yet-due peers—must await full `2026Q3–2027Q4` prospectus and N-PORT releases.

## 6. Conclusion

This paper documents how U.S. multi-asset fund managers navigated name selection and portfolio allocation during the early rollout of the 2023 SEC Names Rule amendments through July 2026. Linking Form N-PORT holdings, Form N-CEN fund structures, and per-fund EDGAR `497`/`485BPOS` filings across 50 renaming multi-asset funds and stratified retained controls reveals three core institutional facts. First, label choices split sharply between broad non-numeric descriptors (84.0% of funds; 91.3% of funds and 95.1% of assets outside Vanguard) and quantitative ratios (57.7% of assets overall, but 4.9% excluding Vanguard's four LifeStrategy funds, whose `497` filing explicitly confirmed unchanged policies). Second, 49/50 renames preceded the June 11, 2026 large-group on-cycle start and 36/50 precede 2027 statutory on-cycle updates; while all 15 historical and unregulated-word placebos are null, pre-trend rejections in the *Growth*/*Value* lexical screen ($p = 0.043$, $p = 0.003$), narrow Rambachan–Roth breakdown values, and null early-versus-late fiscal-year-end cohort contrasts underscore why observational early-rollout comparisons support a descriptive manager-choice framework rather than causal enforcement claims. Third, repairing look-through holdings with same-period historical amended N-PORT reports cuts unmapped assets to 1.53% and confirms a robust `-7.19` pp largest-class concentration separation (sharp partial-identification set `[-11.33, -3.24]` pp), over half of which already emerged by `2024Q2`—consistent with fixed-ratio managers preserving static interior allocations while shedding ambiguous legacy labels.
