## Abstract

The transition toward software-defined vehicles (SDVs) has introduced over-the-air (OTA) wireless software updates as an alternative to physical dealership visits in automotive safety recalls. Yet OTA remediation introduces a fundamental operational tension—the **Software-Defined Vehicle Recall Paradox**: software recalls remediated via OTA involve far larger campaign counts than in-shop dealer software fixes, whereas wireless deployment promises higher remediation compliance, especially among older secondary-market vehicles. Using the complete U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) vehicle recall census (2015–2026), linked to official U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) recall completion rates (`6axg-epim`), a 1,452-campaign live audit of NHTSA's public Campaign API, and 437,886 unique consumer safety complaints (636,875 component-level records), we examine this trade-off across four empirical pillars with OEM-clustered CR1 $t(G - 1)$ and $B = 999$ Rademacher Wild Cluster Bootstrap ($p_{\text{WCB}}$) inference.

Our two sharpest, most robust empirical findings govern **lifecycle compliance dynamics** and **federal public-API measurement integrity**. First, across mature ($\ge 18$-month post-`RCDATE`) campaigns, **OTA remediation substantially flattens the vehicle-age compliance-decay cliff**: in-shop dealer software completion falls by **$-3.57$ percentage points per year of vehicle age** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), mirroring physical hardware decay ($-3.41$ pp/yr), whereas **Pure-OTA offsets 86.3%** ($+3.08$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.039$), **Hybrid-OTA offsets 63.0%** ($+2.25$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.044$), and **Official API OTA offsets 77.0%** ($+2.74$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$). Crucially, this age-decay mitigation survives complete exclusion of Tesla for both pooled audited OTA ($+2.37$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.030$) and Official API OTA ($+1.96$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.023$), and holds strictly within light-duty passenger OEMs (Pure-OTA $+3.22$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.032$; Hybrid-OTA $+2.36$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.007$). Second, our live audit documents a sharp **public NHTSA Campaign API endpoint metadata anomaly**: NHTSA's live `overTheAirUpdate` flag achieved **100.0% exact agreement (54/54)** with audited remedy text during 2019–2023 and **93.9% (31/33)** in 2024 (with **0/250 = 0.0%** physical false negatives), drifted to **4/7 (57.1%)** during January 1–May 8, 2025 (final `True`: Volvo `25V282000`, 36/36 records `True`), and returned **0/68 (0.0%) `True` after May 8, 2025**—beginning May 9 with Ford `25V315000` (44/44 records `False` despite explicit OTA remedy text). Contrasting this live public API drop (`4/38` in 2025) against NHTSA's separate *2025 Annual Recalls Report* (March 2026), which internally tallied **33 OTA recalls in 2025**, demonstrates a public-endpoint metadata propagation discrepancy rather than an internal agency tracking halt.

Third, separating **Pure-OTA** ($N = 61$, EV-native dominant) from **Hybrid-OTA** ("over-the-air or by a dealer", $N = 101$, 100% of legacy-OEM OTA campaigns) illuminates two distinct campaign-size estimands: conditional on remedy modality within OEMs, **Hybrid-OTA** campaigns are **$7.01\times$ larger** ($+1.947$ log points, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.006$; Official API OTA 2018–2024 $+1.887$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.039$) than dealer software recalls whereas **Pure-OTA** shows no significant within-OEM premium ($+1.084$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.233$); unconditionally across all 2015–2026 software recalls around an OEM's first OTA recall, staggered adoption intent-to-treat (ITT) estimates are statistically null under both TWFE ($+0.176$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.579$) and saturated **Sun–Abraham (2021) interaction-weighted (IW)** estimation ($-0.532$, $p_t = 0.470$; IW pre-trend $p = 0.775$). While qualitatively consistent with legacy OEMs selectively routing larger campaigns into hybrid delivery rather than uniform fleet-wide blast-radius inflation, these distinct estimands do not identify a point percentage decomposition. Fourth, evaluating a **recall-level unremedied vehicle-instance proxy**—recognizing that summed affected counts (228.43M vehicle-instances) double-count VINs across campaigns and that DOT completion denominators follow **49 CFR § 573.7** unreachable-vehicle exclusions and remedy-available quarterly timing—reveals sharp sample concentration and modality divergence: Tesla accounts for **98.7%** of mature Pure-OTA affected vehicle-instances ($27/46$ campaigns; Non-Tesla $N = 19$ averages 99.11% completion across 1.3% of instances). Holding campaign size fixed, both Pure-OTA ($-2.062$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.035$) and Hybrid-OTA ($-0.666$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.012$) compress unremedied residuals. Unconditionally under conservative wild cluster bootstrap inference, estimates relative to physical hardware are marginally significant at 10% for Pure-OTA ($-1.918$, $-85.3\%$ relative level transform, $p_t < 0.0001$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.065$**) and Hybrid-OTA ($+1.415$, $p_t = 0.018$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.055$**), with a statistically significant Hybrid-minus-Pure contrast ($+3.334$, $p_t < 0.00001$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.007$**); relative to OTA-capable OEMs' in-shop dealer software, Pure-OTA is statistically null ($-0.599$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.401$**), Hybrid-OTA is marginally positive ($+1.130$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.097$**), and their contrast is $+1.729$ (**$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.079$**). Finally, we transparently report four empirical nulls—time-to-recall ("sell first, fix later"), 365- and 730-day repeat cascades, acute hazard advisories, and 24-month ODI consumer complaint-decay DiD ($p_{\text{WCB}} \ge 0.27$).

## 1. Introduction

Over the past decade, motor vehicles have evolved from largely mechanical assemblies into networked cyber-physical platforms governed by tens of millions of lines of embedded code. This architectural transition to software-defined vehicles (SDVs) has altered how automakers manage post-sale product quality and regulatory safety recalls. Under traditional physical recall operations—studied extensively across automotive, medical-device, and consumer-goods settings in operations management, economics, and marketing (Jarrell and Peltzman 1985; Barber and Darrough 1996; Rupp and Taylor 2002; Haunschild and Rhee 2004; Bae and Benítez-Silva 2011; Hora et al. 2011; Thirumalai and Sinha 2011; Kalaignanam et al. 2013; Eilert et al. 2017; Shah et al. 2017; Ball et al. 2018)—remediating a safety defect required mailing physical owner notification letters, provisioning parts or service bays, and persuading owners to schedule a dealership visit. That physical compliance friction erodes sharply as vehicles age into secondary used-car markets, leaving roughly one-quarter to one-third of recalled vehicle-instances unremedied even after statutory quarterly reporting windows close.

Over-the-air (OTA) wireless software remediation promises to reduce dealership friction by pushing safety patches directly to vehicles over cellular or Wi-Fi connections. Yet the rise of SDV recalls has sparked intense debate among operations scholars, safety regulators, and automotive executives because wireless patching introduces an operational tension—the **Software-Defined Vehicle Recall Paradox**. On one hand, centralized software platforms shared across vehicle lines and model years—the digital analogue of physical component sharing examined by Ramdas and Randall (2008) and complex code coupling studied by MacCormack et al. (2006)—could concentrate defects across large fleets, while classic analytical work in *Management Science* on software patching warns that low post-release patching costs can alter release-quality incentives ("sell first, fix later"; Arora et al. 2006) or shift compliance burdens across imperfectly responsive users (August and Tunca 2006; Cavusoglu et al. 2008). On the other hand, if OTA wireless delivery sustains high completion rates even among older, secondary-market vehicles whose owners rarely visit franchised dealers, frictionless remediation could mitigate lifecycle compliance attrition and reduce unremedied defect residuals.

Evaluating this trade-off empirically requires confronting four distinct measurement and econometric challenges:

1. **Public Federal API Metadata Integrity:** Empirical researchers and safety monitors routinely rely on NHTSA's public Campaign API boolean `overTheAirUpdate` field to identify OTA recalls. Whether that live public endpoint stays synchronized with NHTSA's underlying corrective-action texts and internal annual reporting across recent years has not been systematically audited.
2. **Lifecycle Vehicle-Age Attrition vs. Campaign Maturation:** Under **49 CFR § 573.7(a)**, manufacturers file six consecutive quarterly completion reports beginning with the quarter in which purchasers are notified of remedy availability, while **49 CFR § 573.7(b)(5)** tracks vehicles excluded as scrapped, stolen, exported, or unlocated. Moreover, pooling EV-native **Pure-OTA** campaigns with legacy-OEM **Hybrid-OTA** campaigns ("updated over-the-air or by a dealer") conflates distinct connectivity architectures and severe OEM weight concentration.
3. **Conditional Campaign Selection vs. Unconditional OEM Adoption:** Legacy automakers do not assign OTA versus dealer-bay software remedies randomly (analogous to endogenous recall strategy in Hora et al. 2011 and Ball et al. 2018). Because OTA packaging entails positive fixed engineering overhead ($F_{\text{OTA}} > 0$) with low per-vehicle marginal cost ($c_{\text{OTA}} \approx 0$), whereas dealer reprogramming entails low setup cost ($F_{\text{dealer}} \approx 0$) but high per-vehicle warranty labor cost ($c_{\text{dealer}} \gg 0$), conditional comparisons between OTA and dealer software campaigns measure a different estimand from unconditional OEM-level shifts upon adopting OTA capability.
4. **Proxy Measurement and Finite-Cluster Inference on Unremedied Exposure:** Summing recall-level affected counts across campaigns counts **vehicle-instances** (not unique fleet VINs, since one VIN can enter multiple recalls), and DOT completion denominators reflect 49 CFR § 573.7 exclusions. Furthermore, because `Tesla, Inc.` represents a concentrated share of mature Pure-OTA campaigns and affected vehicle-instances, asymptotic cluster-robust $t$-statistics can substantially over-reject compared with finite-sample wild cluster bootstrap inference (Cameron et al. 2008; Cameron and Miller 2015; MacKinnon and Webb 2017).

In this study, we assemble and reconcile four official U.S. federal datasets spanning 2015–2026: (i) NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation (ODI) complete vehicle recall flat file ($N = 10{,}559$ vehicle campaigns; $7{,}967$ in 2018–2026); (ii) U.S. DOT's official recall completion-rate registry (`6axg-epim`; $7{,}385$ rated 2018–2026 vehicle campaigns, including $6{,}412$ mature $\ge 18$-month post-`RCDATE` campaigns spanning 228.43 million summed recall-level affected vehicle-instances); (iii) a live 1,452-campaign audit of NHTSA's public Campaign API covering 100% of software and OTA-candidate recalls plus 250 stratified non-software physical recalls; and (iv) 437,886 unique NHTSA ODI consumer safety complaints (`ODINO`; 636,875 component-level records, 2020–2026) strictly deduplicated and linked into a balanced 114,000-observation $[-12, +12]$ relative-month event-study panel.

Our investigation establishes five empirical contributions, anchored by two headline findings that hold cleanly across every cluster-robust and subsample stress test:

- **Primary Contribution 1 — OTA Flattening of the Vehicle-Age Compliance-Decay Cliff:** On mature ($\ge 18$-month post-`RCDATE`) campaigns within OTA-capable OEMs, in-shop **Dealer-Software completion erodes by $-3.57$ percentage points per year of vehicle age** ($t = -11.96$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), dropping from 90.26% on 0–1 year-old vehicles to 63.63% on 6+ year-old vehicles—virtually identical to Physical Hardware's $-3.41$ pp/yr decline. Wireless patching sharply flattens this secondary-market attrition cliff: **`Pure-OTA × Age` $= +3.08$ pp/yr** ($t = 8.52$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.039$, offsetting 86.3% of decay), **`Hybrid-OTA × Age` $= +2.25$ pp/yr** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.044$, offsetting 63.0%), **`Any OTA × Age` $= +2.81$ pp/yr** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.009$), and **`Official API OTA × Age` $= +2.74$ pp/yr** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$, offsetting 77.0%). Crucially, this age-decay mitigation holds even when **completely excluding Tesla** (`Any OTA × Age` $= +2.37$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.030$; `Official API OTA × Age` $= +1.96$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.023$) and strengthens strictly within light-duty passenger-vehicle OEMs (`Pure-OTA × Age` $= +3.22$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.032$; `Hybrid-OTA × Age` $= +2.36$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.007$). In completion levels, mature `Hybrid-OTA` adds **$+6.25$ pp** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$) over dealer software within the same OEM, and `Official API OTA` adds **$+3.73$ pp** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.006$; $+10.43$ pp vs. physical hardware, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$).
- **Primary Contribution 2 — Forensic Verification of a May 8, 2025 NHTSA Public Campaign API Metadata Anomaly:** Live querying of 1,452 campaigns (zero HTTP errors; 0/250 physical false negatives) reveals that NHTSA's public `api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber` `overTheAirUpdate` boolean matched audited corrective-action texts on **54/54 (100.0%)** campaigns in 2019–2023 and **31/33 (93.9%)** in 2024, degraded to **4/7 (57.1%)** during January 1–May 8, 2025—ending abruptly with Volvo `25V282000` (May 8, 2025; 36/36 model-year records `True`)—and returned **`False` on 68/68 (100.0%)** subsequent 2025–2026 OTA campaigns, beginning the very next day with Ford `25V315000` (May 9, 2025; 44/44 records `False` despite explicit OTA remedy text). Reconciling this public API snapshot against NHTSA's separate ***2025 Annual Recalls Report*** (NHTSA 2026), which internally tallied **33 OTA recalls in 2025** (versus 38 in our broader audited text classification and only 4 flagged `True` on the live public API), isolates a **public Campaign API endpoint metadata discrepancy** that silently biases downstream analyses relying on live API queries after May 8, 2025.
- **Contribution 3 — Separating Conditional Campaign-Size Differentials from Unconditional OEM-Level First-OTA ITT:** Auditing passive-voice clauses cleanly separates **61 Pure-OTA** (EV-native dominant) from **101 Hybrid-OTA** (100% of legacy-OEM OTA campaigns) and **984 Dealer-Software** recalls. Within OTA-capable OEMs, conditional on remedy modality, **Hybrid-OTA** campaigns are **$7.01\times$ larger** ($+1.947$ log points, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.006$; Official API OTA 2018–2024 $+1.887$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.039$) than dealer software—robust to OEM $\times$ Year FE, Make $\times$ Model FE, excluding Tesla and Ford, light-duty passenger restriction, iterative singleton dropping, and breadth controls—whereas **Pure-OTA** exhibits no statistically significant within-OEM size premium ($+1.084$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.233$). By contrast, unconditional OEM-level staggered first-OTA adoption event studies pooling all 2015–2026 software recalls yield clean parallel pre-trends and statistically null post shifts under both TWFE ($+0.176$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.579$; pre-trend $p = 0.424$) and fully saturated **Sun–Abraham (2021) IW** estimation ($-0.532$, $p_t = 0.470$; IW pre-trend $p = 0.775$), while unconditional Software $\times$ SDV Cohort ($\text{MY} \ge 2021$) DiD yields $+0.262$ ($p_t = 0.064$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.094$). We report these two estimands strictly for what each identifies: the null unconditional ITT is qualitatively consistent with legacy OEMs selectively routing larger campaigns into hybrid delivery rather than an immediate fleet-wide blast-radius surge, without claiming an unidentified percentage share across distinct estimands.
- **Contribution 4 — Transparent Recall-Level Unremedied Vehicle-Instance Proxy & Composition Disclosure:** Evaluating `log(1 + unremedied vehicle-instance proxy)` with full disclosure of **49 CFR § 573.7** definitions, OEM composition weights, and exact $p_{\text{WCB}}$ values clarifies both the promise and statistical limits of aggregate net-exposure inference. Among the 46 mature Pure-OTA campaigns, **Tesla accounts for 58.7% of campaigns (27/46) and 98.7% of affected vehicle-instances (9.02M of 9.14M)**, while the 19 Non-Tesla Pure-OTA campaigns span 10 OEMs (1.3% of instances; 99.11% mean / 100.00% median completion). Holding campaign size fixed, both Pure-OTA ($-2.062$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.035$) and Hybrid-OTA ($-0.666$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.012$) significantly compress unremedied residuals. Unconditionally, descriptive break-even accounting places Pure-OTA above its sample-matched frontier ($99.18\%$ vs. $97.34\%$, $+1.84$ pp) and Legacy Hybrid-OTA below ($95.66\%$ vs. $98.19\%$, $-2.53$ pp; pure SW-choice Hybrid-OTA $-2.76$ pp), and the all-vehicle **Hybrid-minus-Pure contrast ($+3.334$ log points)** is strongly significant under exact WCB (**$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.007$**; Non-Tesla Pure-OTA cross-OEM tier $-3.359$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.007$, light-duty $-3.395$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.029$). However, individual unconditional within-OEM coefficients carry wider wild-cluster-bootstrap uncertainty: relative to physical hardware, Pure-OTA ($-1.918$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.065$**) and Hybrid-OTA ($+1.415$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.055$**) are marginally significant at 10%, while relative to OTA-capable OEMs' dealer software, Pure-OTA is statistically null ($-0.599$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.401$**), Hybrid-OTA is marginally positive ($+1.130$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.097$**), and the software-only Hybrid-minus-Pure contrast ($+1.729$) has **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.079$**.
- **Contribution 5 — Regulatory Composition, Pre-Recall Complaint Buildup, and Four Transparent Empirical Nulls:** Within OEMs, Pure-OTA recalls are **$+27.77$ pp more likely** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.005$; API OTA $+15.70$ pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.042$) to involve FMVSS technical noncompliances and Official API OTA recalls are **$-5.63$ pp less likely** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.019$) to stem from formal ODI investigations. Linking 437,886 deduplicated ODI complaints reveals steeper pre-recall complaint acceleration ($2.43\times$ for Pure-OTA, pre-trend Wald $p = 0.006$) alongside **four honest empirical nulls**: lifecycle-lag ("sell first, fix later"), 365- and 730-day repeat cascades, acute hazard advisories, and post-recall consumer complaint-decay DiD ($p_{\text{WCB}} \ge 0.27$).

## 2. Institutional Context, Conceptual Framework, and Proxy Accounting Mechanics

### 2.1 U.S. Automotive Recall Governance and 49 CFR § 573.7 Completion Methodology

Under the National Traffic and Motor Vehicle Safety Act (49 U.S.C. Chapter 301) and implementing regulations in 49 CFR Part 573, motor vehicle manufacturers must file a Defect and Noncompliance Information Report (Part 573 Report) with NHTSA within five business days of determining that a safety-related defect or Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) noncompliance exists. Each campaign receives a unique NHTSA Campaign Number (e.g., `25V282000`) detailing affected makes, models, model years, production date bounds, potentially affected recall-level vehicle counts, defect and consequence narratives, and corrective-action remedy plans.

Three statutory features of **49 CFR § 573.7** (Office of the Federal Register 2026) directly govern how U.S. DOT recall completion rates (`6axg-epim`) must be interpreted in empirical work:

1. **Remedy-Available Trigger for Six Quarterly Reports (§ 573.7(a)):** Manufacturers must submit quarterly completion reports *"for each of six consecutive quarters beginning with the quarter in which the campaign was initiated (i.e., the date the manufacturer notifies its purchasers of the availability of a remedy) or corrective action has been completed on all defective or noncomplying vehicles ... whichever occurs first."* Because phased recalls can mail interim risk notices months before final remedies become available (e.g., Ford `25V315000`, filed May 9, 2025, with interim letters June 26, 2025 and final remedy letters October 8, 2025), our primary cutoff of **$\ge 540$ days ($\ge 18$ months) post-`RCDATE`** serves as an operational maturity proxy rather than an exact guarantee that every phased campaign has filed its sixth quarterly report. We therefore complement our $\ge 18$-month primary cutoff with full-sample specifications controlling flexibly for a cubic polynomial in campaign age plus filing-quarter fixed effects.
2. **Unreachable-Vehicle Denominator Exclusions (§ 573.7(b)(5)):** Each quarterly report separately tallies vehicles *"determined to be unreachable for inspection due to export, theft, scrapping, failure to receive notification, or other reasons,"* which U.S. DOT excludes from or adjusts within completion accounting. Reported `completion_rate` therefore reflects completion among reachable, active vehicles rather than raw unadjusted production totals.
3. **Recall-Level Vehicle-Instances vs. Unique Fleet VINs:** Because Part 573 filings report potentially affected vehicle counts *per campaign*, summing `PotAff` across multiple campaigns yields **summed recall-level affected vehicle-instances** (campaign–VIN pairs), not unique vehicles on U.S. roads: a single vehicle VIN subject to three separate software or hardware recalls is counted three times across those campaigns.

### 2.2 Endogenous Fixed-Cost Remedy Selection

Consider an automaker facing a safety-related software defect affecting $N$ recall-level vehicle-instances. The automaker chooses between traditional dealer-bay reprogramming ($D$) and over-the-air remediation ($O$), which for legacy manufacturers typically operates as a hybrid channel ("over-the-air or by a dealer"). Dealer reprogramming incurs minimal fixed software-packaging overhead ($F_D \approx 0$) because technicians flash modules via existing OBD-II diagnostic bays, but imposes a positive variable warranty labor reimbursement cost $c_D > 0$ per vehicle (typically \$75–\$250 plus bay congestion). Conversely, legacy OEMs operating multi-supplier electronic control unit (ECU) networks face a strictly positive fixed engineering, cybersecurity signing, telematics integration, and validation cost $F_O > 0$ to prepare an OTA campaign, paired with near-zero marginal distribution cost ($c_O \approx 0$) for connected vehicles. Total direct compliance costs are:

$$C_D(N) = F_D + c_D N \approx c_D N, \qquad C_O(N) = F_O + c_O N + \theta c_D N$$

where $\theta \in [0, 1)$ is the residual share of unconnected or opt-out vehicles falling back to dealership service under Hybrid-OTA. Cost-minimizing legacy OEMs deploy OTA if and only if $N$ clears an endogenous threshold:

$$N^* = \frac{F_O - F_D}{(1 - \theta) c_D - c_O} > 0$$

This operational cost structure yields three qualitative predictions for distinct empirical estimands:

1. **Conditional Legacy Hybrid-OTA Size Sorting:** Within legacy OEMs employing both dealer software and Hybrid-OTA remedies, conditional on observing a software recall, Hybrid-OTA campaigns should concentrate above $N^*$, yielding $E[\log N \mid \text{Hybrid-OTA}] \gg E[\log N \mid \text{Dealer-SW}]$.
2. **Absence of Within-OEM Size Sorting in EV-Native Pure-OTA:** EV-native manufacturers built around centralized vehicle operating systems amortize $F_O$ across routine software releases ($F_O^{\text{native}} \approx 0$), deploying Pure-OTA across small and large recalls alike without a large within-OEM size differential.
3. **Distinct Unconditional OEM-Level Adoption Estimand:** Pooling *all* software recalls unconditionally (OTA + Dealer-Software) around an OEM's first OTA recall estimates an OEM-level intent-to-treat (ITT) effect of OTA capability adoption on average software recall size. If legacy automakers mainly sort large software recalls into hybrid delivery rather than experiencing an immediate fleet-wide blast-radius explosion upon first adopting OTA, the unconditional OEM-level ITT will remain near zero even while the conditional Hybrid-OTA differential is large. Crucially, because the conditional campaign-level contrast and the unconditional OEM-level ITT are **distinct estimands**, observing a null ITT alongside a large conditional gap supports selection qualitatively but cannot identify an exact percentage share of selection versus architecture.

### 2.3 Recall-Level Unremedied Vehicle-Instance Proxy and Descriptive Break-Even Frontier

For an individual campaign $i$ affecting $N_i$ vehicle-instances with official DOT completion percentage $r_i \in [0, 100]$, we define the **recall-level unremedied vehicle-instance proxy**:

$$U_i = N_i \cdot \left(1 - \frac{r_i}{100}\right)$$

Consistent with Section 2.1, $U_i$ is a **campaign-level proxy** for unremedied vehicle-instances rather than a deduplicated VIN-level count of unique hazardous vehicles on public roads (Bae and Benítez-Silva 2011), because overlapping campaigns on the same model share VINs and DOT completion denominators incorporate 49 CFR § 573.7(b)(5) unreachable-vehicle adjustments.

As a descriptive diagnostic benchmark, suppose a baseline dealer-software campaign affects $N_0$ vehicle-instances with mature completion rate $r_0 \in (0, 1)$, and an OTA modality exhibits a recall-size multiplier $M = N_{\text{OTA}} / N_0$ and completion rate $r_{\text{OTA}}$. Under this campaign-level proxy arithmetic, $U_{\text{OTA}} < U_0$ if and only if:

$$M \cdot (1 - r_{\text{OTA}}) < (1 - r_0) \iff r_{\text{OTA}} > r^*(M; r_0) \equiv 1 - \frac{1 - r_0}{M}$$

Because $r^*(M; r_0)$ is steeply concave in $M$, every doubling of recall size requires halving the unremedied residual fraction $(1 - r)$. Anchored at OTA-capable OEMs' mature Dealer-Software baseline ($r_0 = 85.71\%$), a $7.89\times$ multiplier requires **$r^* = 98.19\%$** merely to match the baseline recall-level unremedied instance count. We use $r^*(M; r_0)$ purely as an illustrative operational benchmark while relying on full fixed-effects regressions with exact wild cluster bootstrap inference ($p_{\text{WCB}}$) for statistical testing.

## 3. Data, Public API Metadata Audit, and Econometric Methodology

### 3.1 Four Linked Official Public Federal Datasets

All data are drawn exclusively from authorized, publicly accessible U.S. federal endpoints downloaded with cryptographic SHA-256 receipts on November 25, 2026:

1. **NHTSA ODI Complete Recalls Flat File (`FLAT_RCL_POST_2010.zip`):** Official tab-delimited flat file (248,685 make-model-year rows across all recall types; 197,488 Vehicle rows spanning 2015–2026 through November 20, 2026, representing **$10{,}559$ unique Vehicle recall campaigns**, of which **$7{,}967$** were filed in 2018–2026). Provides exact filing dates (`RCDATE`), manufacturing date bounds (`BGMAN`, `ENDMAN`), owner notification dates (`ODATE`), affected makes, models, model years, component hierarchies (`COMPNAME`), recall initiation source (`INFLUENCED_BY`), FMVSS codes, defect/consequence/remedy narratives, and *Do Not Drive* / *Park Outside* indicators.
2. **U.S. DOT Official Completion-Rate Registry (`6axg-epim`):** Complete Socrata census of 30,484 recalls (26,561 Vehicle recalls) maintained by U.S. DOT / NHTSA and updated November 25, 2026. Exact 1-to-1 campaign linkage matches **100.0% ($7{,}967 / 7{,}967$)** of 2018–2026 vehicle campaigns, yielding **$7{,}385$** campaigns with non-null official `completion_rate` and **$6{,}412$** mature ($\ge 540$-day post-`RCDATE`) rated campaigns totaling **228,425,666 summed recall-level affected vehicle-instances** (noting that multi-recall VINs are counted once per campaign). Log-size correlation between `FLAT_RCL` and `6axg-epim` is $r = 1.00000$. Inspection identified exactly **three administrative outlier campaigns** in 2018–2026 with `completion_rate` above 100% (`20V664000` Proterra, 5 vehicles, 3,380%; `20V731000` Bugatti, 3 vehicles, 133.3%; `19V871000` Proterra, 140 vehicles, 108.5%), arising from post-filing denominator amendments. Primary specifications clip `completion_rate` to $[0, 100]$ and verify exact invariance on `completion_rate_strict` excluding those three campaigns ($N = 6{,}409$).
3. **Live NHTSA Public Campaign API (`api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber`):** We queried **100% of software and OTA-candidate Vehicle campaigns** filed during 2018–2026 ($N = 1{,}202$) plus a **stratified random audit sample of 250 non-software physical campaigns** across all nine years (total $N = 1{,}452$, 100.0% HTTP 200 success, zero errors). - **Physical-Hardware ($N = 6{,}821$; 5,557 mature $\ge 18$m):** Non-software mechanical, structural, and hardware recalls.

### 3.3 Econometric Specifications and Cluster-Robust Inference

Our primary within-OEM campaign-level specification estimates:

$$Y_i = \beta_{\text{pure}} \mathbf{1}[\text{Pure-OTA}_i] + \beta_{\text{hyb}} \mathbf{1}[\text{Hybrid-OTA}_i] + \gamma_{\text{oem}(i)} + \delta_{\text{time}(i)} + \phi_{\text{my}(i)} + \psi_{\text{comp}(i)} + \varepsilon_i$$

where $Y_i$ denotes log campaign size $\log(1 + \text{PotAff}_i)$, official mature completion rate $r_i \in [0, 100]$, log recall-level unremedied vehicle-instance proxy $\log(1 + U_i)$, or safety/oversight outcomes; $\gamma_{\text{oem}}$ absorbs canonical OEM fixed effects; $\delta_{\text{time}}$ absorbs Recall-Year FE (Pillar 1 and Pillar 4) or Recall-Quarter FE (Pillar 2 and Pillar 3 maturity controls); $\phi_{\text{my}}$ absorbs exact Min-Model-Year FE (eliminating vehicle-vintage truncation bias); and $\psi_{\text{comp}}$ absorbs top-14 NHTSA component categories plus `OTHER`. Primary software specifications compare Pure-OTA and Hybrid-OTA against **Dealer-Software** strictly within the 32 OTA-capable OEMs; secondary universe benchmarks compare all three software modalities against **Physical-Hardware** across all 532 OEMs.

Every regression is programmatically verified to have **strictly full column rank** via pivoted QR orthogonalization before inversion, and high-dimensional FE models are additionally verified after **iterative singleton-FE removal**. Standard errors employ CR1 cluster-robust variance estimators clustered by canonical OEM with **finite-sample $t(G - 1)$ inference** (Cameron and Miller 2015), paired prominently throughout text, tables, and figures with **exact null-imposed Cameron–Gelbach–Miller (2008) Rademacher Wild Cluster Bootstrap $p$-values ($B = 999$ draws, $p_{\text{WCB}}$)**. Whenever `Tesla, Inc.` represents a concentrated share of a single binary indicator (58.7% of mature Pure-OTA campaigns and 98.7% of mature Pure-OTA affected vehicle-instances), we never rely solely on asymptotic $p_t$: we report exact $p_{\text{WCB}}$ upfront, document the exact Tesla vs. Non-Tesla sample composition, and test Non-Tesla sensitivity explicitly (MacKinnon and Webb 2017).

To examine whether adopting OTA capability shifts unconditional software recall size at the OEM level, we estimate an **unconditional Intent-to-Treat (ITT) staggered OEM first-OTA event study** across all 2015–2026 software recalls ($N = 1{,}239$, $G = 68$ OEMs with $\ge 3$ software recalls, using never-OTA OEMs as controls):

$$\log(1 + \text{PotAff}_i) = \sum_{k \in \{-3,-2,0,1,2,3\}} \tau_k \, \mathbf{1}[t_i - E_{\text{oem}(i)} = k] + \gamma_{\text{oem}(i)} + \delta_{\text{yr}(i)} + \psi_{\text{comp}(i)} + \varepsilon_i$$

with endpoints binned at $\le -3$ and $\ge +3$ and reference period $k = -1$. To avoid staggered-TWFE negative-weighting contamination (Callaway and Sant'Anna 2021; Roth et al. 2023), we additionally estimate the **exact Sun and Abraham (2021) saturated cohort $\times$ relative-time interaction-weighted (IW)** estimator across all seven adoption cohorts with complete pre-period reference observations (2019–2025; 39 saturated cohort-period cells; excluding partial-year 2026 so zero post-adoption observations leak into controls), computing cluster-robust delta-method standard errors for every dynamic lead/lag and the pooled post-adoption IW average (25 post cells). Finally, for our consumer complaint event study ($114{,}000$ campaign-month observations), we absorb all campaign fixed effects ($\alpha_i$) via exact Frisch–Waugh–Lovell demeaning alongside relative-month ($\delta_m$) and calendar year-month ($\lambda_{ym}$) fixed effects, clustering both by OEM and by campaign.

### 4.2 Pillar 2 (Primary Empirical Finding): Remediation Velocity and Eradication of the Vehicle-Age Compliance-Decay Cliff

We next evaluate how wireless delivery affects official U.S. DOT recall completion rates (`6axg-epim`)—our **strongest and most broadly robust empirical contribution**. Panel D of Table 3 and Figure 3B highlight our centerpiece empirical contribution: **OTA flattens the vehicle-age compliance-decay cliff**. Traditional automotive recalls suffer steep attrition as vehicles age and enter secondary used-car markets where owner mailers miss current drivers and franchised dealership service visits drop (Rupp and Taylor 2002; Bae and Benítez-Silva 2011). In our mature census, **Dealer-Software completion plunges by $-3.57$ percentage points per year of vehicle age** ($t = -11.96$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$**), falling from **90.26%** on 0–1 year-old vehicles to **81.80%** (2–3 yr), **73.20%** (4–5 yr), and **63.63%** (6+ yr)—essentially identical to Physical Hardware's $-3.41$ pp/yr slope (80.88% down to 53.38%; interaction `Dealer-SW × Age` $= +0.20$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.478$). Wireless patching breaks this physical-ownership attrition channel: **`Pure-OTA × Vehicle Age` $= +3.08$ pp/yr** ($t = 8.52$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.039$**), offsetting **86.3%** of compliance decay and sustaining **97.41% mean (99.06% median)** completion even on vehicles aged 6+ years; **`Hybrid-OTA × Vehicle Age` $= +2.25$ pp/yr** (**$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.044$**) offsets **63.0%** across 0–5 year connected fleets; and **`Official API OTA × Age` $= +2.74$ pp/yr** ($t = 5.50$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$**) confirms a **77.0%** pooled offset.

Crucially, Panel D's new **non-Tesla and light-duty sensitivity tests** establish that OTA compliance-decay flattening is **not merely a Tesla artifact**. Completely excluding Tesla ($N = 503$, $G = 27$ OEMs) leaves the dealer-software age cliff unchanged ($-3.60$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$) and preserves statistically significant 5% WCB mitigation under both pooled audited OTA (**`Any OTA × Age` $= +2.37$ pp/yr**, $p_t = 0.048$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.030$**) and NHTSA's official flag (**`API OTA × Age` $= +1.96$ pp/yr**, $p_t = 0.043$, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.023$**). In the non-Tesla 4-way split, `Hybrid-OTA × Age` stays at $+2.23$ pp/yr ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.057$) while `Pure-OTA × Age` remains large and positive ($+3.44$ pp/yr) but imprecise ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.168$) strictly because non-Tesla EV startups (Lucid, Rivian, Fisker, Polestar) have younger fleets with **zero mature campaigns aged 6+ years** (max age 5.0 yr, versus 6 campaigns up to 12 years old for Tesla; Table 4B). Restricting to the 20 light-duty passenger-vehicle OEMs ($N = 488$) strengthens both interactions (`Pure-OTA × Age` $+3.22$ pp/yr, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.032$**; `Hybrid-OTA × Age` $+2.36$ pp/yr, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.007$**; `API OTA × Age` $+2.84$ pp/yr, **$p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.005$**).

## 5. Managerial, Architectural, and Regulatory Policy Implications

Our findings carry actionable prescriptions for automotive operations leaders and federal safety regulators:

1. **Capturing the Secondary-Market Lifecycle Dividend of OTA (Primary Operational Lever):** Traditional dealership software recalls hemorrhage compliance at **$-3.57$ pp per year of vehicle age** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), dropping from 90.3% on new vehicles to 63.6% on 6+ year-old vehicles—identical to physical hardware attrition ($-3.41$ pp/yr). Investing in persistent OTA safety-update architecture—decoupling emergency safety cellular connectivity from paid consumer infotainment subscriptions and defaulting critical safety patches to automatic scheduled installation—flattens 63–86% of this age-decay cliff (and survives full Tesla exclusion at $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.023$–$0.030$). OTA is therefore not merely a warranty-cost reduction lever during years 1–3; it is the primary operational mechanism for preserving lifecycle recall compliance after vehicles enter secondary used-car markets.
2. **Closing the Final 3–4 Percentage-Point Legacy Hybrid-OTA Tail:** Legacy automakers route much larger campaigns ($7.01\times$ within-OEM) into Hybrid-OTA, lifting completion by $+6.25$ pp ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$) over dealer software to 95.66% (95.41% on pure software-choice hybrids). Yet because residual unremedied instances scale linearly with $(1 - r)$, a 4.3–4.6% residual tail on multi-hundred-thousand-vehicle campaigns still leaves large campaign-level unremedied counts (`Hybrid − Pure` $+3.334$ log points across all mature vehicles, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.007$). Narrowing that residual requires migrating from fragmented opt-in telematics gateways toward non-expiring, unified safety-OTA delivery.
3. **Restoring Public NHTSA Campaign API Metadata Synchronization & Granular 49 CFR § 573.7 Reporting (For U.S. DOT / NHTSA):**
   - *Public API Endpoint Synchronization:* While NHTSA's *2025 Annual Recalls Report* (NHTSA 2026) confirms that the agency continued tracking OTA recalls internally (tallying 33 in 2025), the live public `api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber` endpoint stopped returning `overTheAirUpdate = True` after May 8, 2025 (`25V282000`), mislabeling 68 of 68 subsequent 2025–2026 OTA recalls (`0.0%`) including next-day Ford `25V315000`. NHTSA should audit and restore the backend serialization pipeline linking internal recall flags to the public Campaign API.
   - *Channel-Disaggregated Quarterly Reporting under 49 CFR § 573.7:* Amending quarterly reports to disaggregate **VINs remedied wirelessly vs. VINs remedied at dealerships**—and publishing the remedy-available notification dates initiating § 573.7(a)'s six-quarter clock alongside § 573.7(b)(5) unreachable-vehicle counts—would enable direct VIN-level evaluation of hybrid compliance tails.

## 6. Limitations and Threats to Validity

Rigorous empirical interpretation requires candid acknowledgment of five methodological boundaries:

1. **Recall-Level Vehicle-Instance Proxy vs. Unique Fleet Exposure & 49 CFR § 573.7 Timing:** As formalized in Section 2.1, summed affected counts (228.43M) and unremedied totals (64.00M) represent **recall-level vehicle-instances**, not deduplicated unique vehicles, because one VIN can appear in multiple campaigns. Furthermore, DOT completion denominators incorporate **49 CFR § 573.7(b)(5)** exclusions (scrapped, stolen, exported, or unlocated vehicles), and statutory six-quarter reporting starts from the **remedy-available notification date** (§ 573.7(a)) rather than Part 573 filing date (`RCDATE`), meaning phased remedies with delayed availability may still be maturing beyond 540 days post-`RCDATE`. Accordingly, Pillar 3 outcomes measure a **recall-level unremedied vehicle-instance proxy**, not unique on-road vehicle-level safety exposure.
2. **Mature Pure-OTA Sample Concentration (98.7% Tesla by Vehicle-Instances) and Finite-Cluster WCB Uncertainty:** Among the 46 mature Pure-OTA campaigns, Tesla accounts for **58.7% of campaigns (27/46)**, **98.7% of affected vehicle-instances**, and **all six campaigns aged 6+ years** (Table 4B). Consequently, unconditional within-OEM Pillar 3 estimates exhibit wider wild-cluster-bootstrap $p$-values than asymptotic $p_t$ (`Pure-OTA` vs. physical $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.065$, vs. dealer software $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.401$; `Hybrid-OTA` vs. physical $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.055$, vs. dealer software $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.097$). While our **core vehicle-age compliance-decay finding survives full Tesla exclusion** (`Any OTA × Age` $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.030$; `API OTA × Age` $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.023$) and Non-Tesla Pure-OTA ($N = 19$) corroborates 99.11% mean completion and lower cross-OEM unremedied proxies ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.007$; light-duty $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.029$), non-Tesla Pure-OTA fleets remain too young (max age 5 years) to separately identify 6+-year Pure-OTA compliance retention without Tesla.
3. **Distinct Conditional vs. Unconditional Campaign-Size Estimands:** Legacy OEMs choose Hybrid-OTA versus Dealer-Software endogenously on specific recalls. While our null unconditional OEM-level first-OTA ITT (TWFE $+0.176, p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.579$; Sun–Abraham IW $-0.532, p_t = 0.470$) is qualitatively consistent with conditional size sorting rather than uniform fleet-wide blast-radius inflation, contrasting distinct conditional and unconditional estimands cannot identify an exact selection percentage share; likewise, within-OEM completion gains ($+6.25$ pp) could still reflect unobserved connectivity or defect differences correlated with OTA eligibility.
4. **Cross-Sectional Maturation vs. Longitudinal Quarterly Trajectories:** Because NHTSA's `quarterlyReports` API endpoint requires restricted authentication (HTTP 403 publicly), Figure 3A relies on cross-sectional campaign-age strata rather than within-campaign longitudinal quarterly panels. Primary completion and proxy analyses therefore restrict to $\ge 18$-month post-`RCDATE` campaigns and verify stability with cubic campaign-age splines plus Recall-Quarter FE.
5. **Sparsity, Fleet Ramp-Up, and Self-Reporting Noise in ODI Consumer Complaints:** NHTSA's ODI complaint registry cannot identify whether an individual complaining VIN has completed an OTA or dealer remedy, exhibits steeper pre-recall exposure ramp-up on newer SDV fleets (pre-trend Wald $p = 0.006$), and contains unvalidated protest entries (`99`-fatality filings). Our null complaint-decay DiD ($p_{\text{WCB}} \ge 0.27$) shows that aggregate voluntary ODI complaints lack statistical power to distinguish incremental incident reductions between 83–90% and 96–99% completion; VIN-level linkage to police crash registries (e.g., FARS/CRSS) remains an important avenue for future work.

## 7. Conclusion

Using the complete 2015–2026 U.S. automotive recall census, official DOT completion rates, a 1,452-campaign live public NHTSA API audit, and 437,886 deduplicated consumer safety complaints, we provide a disciplined empirical resolution of the Software-Defined Vehicle Recall Paradox. Two primary findings stand on rock-solid econometric and forensic footing. First, **OTA remediation substantially flattens the vehicle-age compliance-decay cliff**: whereas in-shop dealer software completion falls by $-3.57$ pp/yr ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$) as vehicles age into secondary markets, Pure-OTA offsets 86.3% ($+3.08$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.039$), Hybrid-OTA offsets 63.0% ($+2.25$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.044$), and Official API OTA offsets 77.0% ($+2.74$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$)—surviving complete exclusion of Tesla ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.023$–$0.030$) and strengthening strictly among light-duty passenger OEMs. Second, our live audit documents an independently verifiable **May 8, 2025 NHTSA public Campaign API endpoint metadata anomaly**—dropping from 100.0% agreement in 2019–2023 and 36/36 `True` records on Volvo `25V282000` (May 8, 2025) to 0/68 `True` records thereafter (starting May 9 with Ford `25V315000`, 44/44 `False`)—even as NHTSA's separate *2025 Annual Recalls Report* internally tallied 33 OTA recalls in 2025. Alongside these core findings, separating conditional campaign-size differentials (`Hybrid-OTA` $+1.947$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.006$) from null unconditional OEM-level first-OTA ITTs (`Sun–Abraham IW` $-0.532$, $p_t = 0.470$) and reporting exact wild-cluster-bootstrap inference alongside Tesla's 98.7% mature Pure-OTA vehicle-instance share clarify both where OTA already delivers measurable lifecycle compliance gains and where legacy hybrid execution and public federal data infrastructure still require operational reform.
