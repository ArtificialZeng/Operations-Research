## Abstract

The transition toward software-defined vehicles (SDVs) has introduced over-the-air (OTA) wireless software updates as a transformative alternative to physical dealership visits in automotive safety recalls. Yet OTA remediation creates a fundamental operational tension—the **Software-Defined Vehicle Recall Paradox**: centralized codebases and low marginal patching costs are associated with dramatically larger recalled fleets, whereas frictionless wireless deployment promises near-universal compliance. Which force dominates net public safety? Using the complete U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) vehicle recall census (2015–2026), linked to official U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) recall completion rates, a 1,452-campaign live ground-truth audit of NHTSA's Campaign API, and 437,886 unique consumer safety complaints (636,875 component-level records), we resolve this paradox across four empirical pillars.

First, our measurement-integrity audit uncovers a critical federal data-governance failure: NHTSA's official API `overTheAirUpdate` flag achieved **100.0% exact agreement (54/54)** with audited remedy text during 2019–2023 and **93.9% (31/33)** in 2024, began degrading in early 2025 (**4/7 = 57.1%** during January 1–May 8, 2025), and **completely ceased populating `True` after May 8, 2025 (0/68 = 0.0% through November 2026)**, while 250 randomly audited physical recalls show **0.0% false negatives**. Auditing passive-voice clauses separates **Pure-OTA** ($N = 61$, EV-native dominant) from **Hybrid-OTA** ("over-the-air or by a dealer", $N = 101$, 100% of legacy-OEM OTA campaigns), while strictly remedy-based classification removes 30 pure hardware replacements from dealer software ($N = 984$). Second, within-OEM fixed-effects regressions with finite-sample $t(G - 1)$ and $B = 999$ Rademacher Wild Cluster Bootstrap (WCB) inference reveal that **Hybrid-OTA** campaigns are **$7.01\times$ larger** ($+1.947$ log points, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.006$; Official API OTA 2018–2024 $+1.887$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.039$) than dealer-remedied software recalls, whereas **Pure-OTA** exhibits no significant within-OEM size premium ($+1.084$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.233$). Unconditional intent-to-treat (ITT) staggered OEM adoption event studies ($+0.176$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.579$), a fully saturated **Sun–Abraham (2021) cohort $\times$ relative-time interaction-weighted (IW)** estimator ($-0.532$, $p = 0.470$; IW pre-trend $p = 0.775$), and unconditional Software $\times$ SDV cohort ($\text{MY} \ge 2021$) difference-in-differences ($+0.262$, $p_t = 0.064$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.094$) prove that **approximately 85–90% of the conditional blast-radius gap reflects endogenous fixed-cost remedy selection** by legacy automakers rather than an exogenous architectural shock. Third, across mature ($\ge 18$-month) campaigns, OTA elevates remediation completion and **flattens the vehicle-age compliance-decay cliff**: dealer software completion collapses by **$-3.57$ percentage points per year of vehicle age** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), whereas **Pure-OTA offsets 86.3%** ($+3.08$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.039$), **Hybrid-OTA offsets 63.0%** ($+2.25$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.044$), and **Official API OTA offsets 77.0%** ($+2.74$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$). Fourth, evaluating exact net unremedied exposure against a sample-matched **Break-Even Completion-Rate Frontier** (anchored at OTA-capable OEMs' 85.71% mature dealer-software baseline) reveals a stark divergence: **Pure-OTA** achieves **99.18% mean (99.99% median)** completion—clearing its 97.34% frontier by $+1.84$ pp and slashing net unremedied vehicles by **85.3%** ($-1.918$ log points vs. physical recalls, $p_t < 0.0001$; median 3.9 unremedied vehicles; 0.14% of U.S. unremedied vehicles despite 4.00% of affected vehicles)—whereas **Legacy Hybrid-OTA** reaches **95.66%** (pure software-choice Hybrid-OTA **95.41%**), falling **$-2.53$ pp ($-2.76$ pp)** below its 98.19% break-even frontier and leaving **364.0 (442.6 SW-choice) median unremedied vehicles** ($+3.334$ log points above Pure-OTA across all vehicles, Wald $p < 0.00001$): the **Hybrid-OTA Safety Trap**. Finally, we transparently report four empirical nulls—time-to-recall ("sell first, fix later"), 365- and 730-day repeat cascades, acute hazard advisories, and 24-month consumer complaint-decay DiD ($p_{\text{WCB}} \ge 0.27$)—while documenting steeper pre-recall complaint ramp-up ($2.43\times$ for Pure-OTA, pre-trend Wald $p = 0.006$) and data-hygiene anomalies in voluntary ODI complaint filings.

**Keywords:** *software-defined vehicles; over-the-air recalls; product recall operations; compliance decay; endogenous remedy selection; wild cluster bootstrap; NHTSA*

## 1. Introduction

Over the past decade, motor vehicles have evolved from largely mechanical assemblies into networked cyber-physical platforms governed by tens of millions of lines of embedded code. This architectural transition to software-defined vehicles (SDVs) has fundamentally altered how automakers manage post-sale product quality and regulatory safety recalls. Under traditional physical product recall operations—studied extensively across automotive, medical-device, and consumer-goods settings in operations management, economics, and marketing (Jarrell and Peltzman 1985; Barber and Darrough 1996; Rupp and Taylor 2002; Haunschild and Rhee 2004; Bae and Benítez-Silva 2011; Hora et al. 2011; Thirumalai and Sinha 2011; Kalaignanam et al. 2013; Eilert et al. 2017; Shah et al. 2017; Ball et al. 2018)—remediating a safety defect required mailing physical owner notification letters, manufacturing replacement parts, and persuading vehicle owners to schedule and complete a dealership service visit. That physical compliance friction leaves roughly one-third of recalled hardware vehicles unremedied on public roads even years after recall initiation.

Over-the-air (OTA) wireless software remediation promises to abolish dealership friction by pushing safety patches directly to vehicles via cellular or Wi-Fi connectivity. Yet the rise of SDV recalls has sparked intense debate among operations scholars, safety regulators, and automotive executives because wireless patching introduces a striking operational ambiguity—the **Software-Defined Vehicle Recall Paradox**. On one hand, centralized software platforms shared across vehicle lines and model years—the digital analogue of physical component sharing examined by Ramdas and Randall (2008) and complex code coupling studied by MacCormack et al. (2006)—could magnify recall *blast radius*, exposing hundreds of thousands or millions of vehicles to a single software fault. Furthermore, classic analytical work in *Management Science* on software patching warns that low post-release patching costs can induce "sell first, fix later" quality trade-offs (Arora et al. 2006) or shift compliance burdens across imperfectly responsive users (August and Tunca 2006; Cavusoglu et al. 2008). On the other hand, if OTA wireless pushing drives recall completion rates toward 99–100%—especially among older, secondary-market vehicles whose owners rarely visit franchised dealers—frictionless remediation velocity could decisively offset blast-radius concentration and dramatically reduce the stock of unremedied hazardous vehicles circulating on U.S. highways.

Resolving whether OTA remediation increases or reduces net public-safety exposure is impossible using raw recall headlines or small single-OEM case studies. Answering it rigorously requires confronting four severe econometric and measurement challenges:

1. **Federal Data-Integrity Risk:** Researchers routinely rely on NHTSA's public API boolean `overTheAirUpdate` field or coarse keyword filters to identify OTA recalls. Neither has been systematically cross-audited across the complete multi-year vehicle recall census.
2. **Endogenous Fixed-Cost Remedy Selection:** Legacy automakers do not assign OTA versus dealer-bay software remedies randomly, just as recall initiation and remediation strategy depend on behavioral and economic thresholds (Hora et al. 2011; Ball et al. 2018). Packaging, validating, and certifying an OTA deployment entails substantial fixed engineering and telematics overhead ($F_{\text{OTA}} > 0$) with near-zero per-vehicle marginal cost ($c_{\text{OTA}} \approx 0$), whereas dealer reprogramming entails low fixed setup cost ($F_{\text{dealer}} \approx 0$) but high per-vehicle warranty labor cost ($c_{\text{dealer}} \gg 0$). Comparing OTA and dealer-remedied software recalls *conditionally* therefore conflates underlying SDV architectural blast radius with endogenous cost-minimizing threshold selection by OEMs.
3. **Campaign Maturation and Modality Heterogeneity:** Recall completion rates rise mechanically across six mandatory quarterly reporting cycles under 49 CFR 573.7. Moreover, pooling pure-play EV-native **Pure-OTA** recalls (where every affected vehicle receives an automated wireless update) with legacy-OEM **Hybrid-OTA** recalls ("updated over-the-air or by a dealer") masks fundamentally different fleet architectures and compliance tails.
4. **Net Safety Translation:** Higher percentage completion rates do not automatically imply fewer unremedied vehicles if the underlying recalled fleet is an order of magnitude larger. Nor do administrative completion filings automatically prove superior real-world incident suppression.

In this study, we assemble and reconcile four complete, official U.S. federal datasets spanning 2015–2026: (i) NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation (ODI) complete vehicle recall flat file ($N = 10{,}559$ vehicle campaigns; $7{,}967$ in 2018–2026); (ii) U.S. DOT's official recall completion-rate registry (`6axg-epim`; $7{,}385$ rated 2018–2026 vehicle campaigns, including $6{,}412$ mature $\ge 18$-month campaigns covering 228.4 million affected vehicles); (iii) a live 1,452-campaign ground-truth audit of NHTSA's Campaign API covering 100% of software and OTA-candidate recalls plus 250 stratified non-software physical recalls; and (iv) 437,886 unique NHTSA ODI consumer safety complaints (`ODINO`; 636,875 component-level records, 2020–2026) deduplicated and linked at the exact make-model-year-component level into a balanced 114,000-observation $[-12, +12]$ relative-month event-study panel.

Our investigation yields five principal contributions to operations management, information systems, and automotive safety policy:

- **Discovery 1 — Forensic Audit of NHTSA's Silent May 2025 API Cutoff:** Querying 1,452 official campaign records with zero HTTP errors reveals that NHTSA's `overTheAirUpdate` API flag agreed with our audited corrective-action classifier on **54 of 54 campaigns (100.0%)** during 2019–2023 and **31 of 33 (93.9%)** in 2024 (the only two 2024 exceptions required physical hardware replacement alongside an OTA patch), with **0 false negatives across 250 physical recalls (0.0%)**. However, reporting began degrading in early 2025 (**4/7 = 57.1%** between January 1 and May 8, 2025) and **ceased completely after May 8, 2025 (`25V282000`)**, recording **0/68 (0.0%)** across all subsequent 2025–2026 OTA recalls (`4/38 = 10.5%` in 2025; `0/37 = 0.0%` in 2026). Auditing passive-voice remedy clauses ("updated by a dealer or through an OTA update") further separates **61 Pure-OTA** recalls (EV-native dominant) from **101 Hybrid-OTA** recalls (100% of legacy-OEM OTA campaigns at Ford 24/24, GM 19/19, Mercedes-Benz 5/5, Stellantis 4/4, Kia 4/4, Nissan 4/4, BMW 3/3, and Honda 2/2).
- **Discovery 2 — Decomposing Blast Radius into Endogenous Selection vs. Architecture:** Within OTA-capable OEMs (controlling for OEM, Recall-Year, Min-Model-Year, and Component fixed effects with finite-sample $t(G - 1)$ and $B = 999$ Rademacher Wild Cluster Bootstrap inference), **Hybrid-OTA** recalls affect **$7.01\times$ more vehicles** ($+1.947$ log points, $t = 5.32$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.006$; Official API OTA 2018–2024 $+1.887$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.039$) than dealer-remedied software recalls—robust to OEM $\times$ Year FE ($+2.059$), Make $\times$ Model FE ($+2.213$), excluding Tesla and Ford ($+1.525$), restricting to light-duty passenger-vehicle OEMs ($+1.930$), iterative singleton-FE dropping ($+1.947$), and breadth controls ($+0.968$). By contrast, **Pure-OTA** exhibits **no statistically significant within-OEM fleet-size premium** ($+1.084$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.233$). Crucially, unconditional intent-to-treat (ITT) staggered OEM OTA-adoption event studies (pooling all 2015–2026 software recalls with no conditioning on remedy modality) pass pre-trend tests ($F = 0.87$, $p = 0.424$) and yield a small, insignificant post-adoption shift ($+0.176$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.579$). A fully saturated **Sun–Abraham (2021) cohort $\times$ relative-time interaction-weighted (IW)** estimator across all seven 2019–2025 cohorts (39 cohort-period cells; zero control leakage) confirms clean pre-trends ($F(2, 66) = 0.26$, $p = 0.775$) and an insignificant pooled post effect ($-0.532$, $p = 0.470$), while an unconditional Software $\times$ Modern SDV Cohort ($\text{MY} \ge 2021$) DiD yields $+0.262$ ($p_t = 0.064$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.094$). Roughly **85–90% of the conditional blast-radius premium therefore reflects endogenous fixed-cost remedy selection by legacy OEMs**, rather than an exogenous architectural shock upon adopting OTA capability.
- **Discovery 3 — Eradication of the Vehicle-Age Compliance-Decay Cliff:** On mature ($\ge 18$-month) campaigns, **Hybrid-OTA** lifts completion by **$+6.25$ percentage points** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$) over dealer software within the same OEM, and **Official API OTA** lifts completion by **$+3.73$ pp** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.006$). Across all 6,412 mature vehicle recalls relative to physical hardware, completion rises by **$+13.74$ pp** for Pure-OTA ($p_t < 0.0001$), **$+9.06$ pp** for Hybrid-OTA ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), **$+10.43$ pp** for Official API OTA ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), and **$+2.65$ pp** for dealer software ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$). Most importantly, we uncover a striking dynamic mechanism: traditional dealer software compliance erodes by **$-3.57$ percentage points per year of vehicle age** ($t = -11.96$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), falling from 90.3% on 0–1 year-old vehicles to 63.6% on 6+ year-old vehicles. **Pure-OTA offsets 86.3% of this decay** ($+3.08$ pp/yr, $t = 8.52$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.039$), sustaining 97.4% completion even on vehicles aged 6+ years; **Hybrid-OTA offsets 63.0%** ($+2.25$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.044$); and **Official API OTA offsets 77.0%** ($+2.74$ pp/yr, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$).
- **Discovery 4 — The Sample-Matched Break-Even Frontier and the Hybrid-OTA Safety Trap:** Evaluating exact net unremedied exposure $U = N(1 - r)$ against a **sample-matched within-OTA-OEM Break-Even Frontier** (anchored at OTA-capable OEMs' 85.71% mature Dealer-Software baseline) resolves the SDV Recall Paradox with a decisive architectural split. Holding fleet size constant, both Pure-OTA ($-2.062$ log points, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.035$) and Hybrid-OTA ($-0.666$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.012$) compress unremedied residuals. Unconditionally, however, **Pure-OTA** achieves **99.18% mean (99.99% median)** mature completion—clearing its 97.34% frontier by **$+1.84$ pp** (and clearing its raw median multiplier frontier by $+7.35$ pp)—which produces a dramatic **public-safety inversion**: Pure-OTA represents **4.00%** of recalled U.S. vehicles (9.14 million) but only **0.14%** of unremedied vehicles (91,604; median **3.9** unremedied vehicles per campaign; $-1.918$ log points vs. physical recalls, $p_t < 0.0001$; Light-Duty Non-Tesla Pure-OTA $-3.395$ vs. dealer software, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.029$). Conversely, **Legacy Hybrid-OTA** reaches **95.66%** mean completion—falling **$-2.53$ pp below** the 98.19% break-even threshold required to offset its $7.89\times$ mature within-OEM blast-radius multiplier (and pure software-choice **Hybrid-SW-Choice** reaches **95.41%**, falling **$-2.76$ pp below** its 98.17% frontier and leaving **442.6 median unremedied vehicles**). Hybrid-OTA leaves **$+1.415$ log points more unremedied vehicles** than physical recalls ($p_t = 0.018$; Hybrid-SW-Choice $+1.456$, $p_t = 0.030$; formal Hybrid minus Pure Wald gap $= +3.334$ log points across all vehicles, $p < 0.00001$, and $+1.729$ log points within software recalls, $p = 0.018$).
- **Discovery 5 — Regulatory Composition, Pre-Recall Complaint Buildup, and Four Transparent Empirical Nulls:** Within OEMs, **Pure-OTA** recalls are **$+27.8$ pp more likely** ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.005$; Official API OTA $+15.7$ pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.042$) to involve Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) compliance standards and significantly *less* likely to stem from formal NHTSA ODI investigations (`Official API OTA` $-5.63$ pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.019$). Linking 437,886 deduplicated ODI complaints documents steeper **pre-recall complaint acceleration** ($[-3,-1]$ vs. $[-12,-4]$ buildup ratio $2.43\times$ for Pure-OTA and $2.06\times$ for Hybrid-OTA vs. $1.63\times$ for Dealer-Software; dynamic pre-trend Wald $F(11, 114) = 2.56, p = 0.006$), reflecting rapid fleet growth on newer SDVs prior to recall initiation. Rigorous fixed-effects audits transparently establish **four empirical nulls**: no causal "sell first, fix later" lifecycle-lag distortion once model-year truncation is absorbed ($p_{\text{WCB}} \ge 0.30$); no excess 365- or 730-day repeat-defect cascades within OEM and component ($p_{\text{WCB}} \ge 0.32$); no elevation in acute *Do Not Drive* or *Park Outside* severity; and no statistically detectable differential post-recall suppression in self-reported ODI complaints under either full $[-12, +12]$ or short $[-3, +12]$ windows ($p_{\text{WCB}} \ge 0.27$).

## 2. Institutional Context, Conceptual Framework, and Break-Even Mechanics

### 2.1 U.S. Automotive Recall Governance and Statutory Completion Reporting

Under the National Traffic and Motor Vehicle Safety Act (49 U.S.C. Chapter 301) and implementing regulations in 49 CFR Part 573, motor vehicle manufacturers must file a Defect and Noncompliance Information Report (Part 573 Report) with NHTSA within five business days of determining that a safety-related defect or FMVSS noncompliance exists. Each campaign receives a unique NHTSA Campaign Number (e.g., `23V838000`) recording affected makes, models, model years, production date bounds, potentially affected fleet size, defect and consequence narratives, and corrective-action remedy plans. Crucially, under **49 CFR 573.7**, manufacturers must submit **six consecutive quarterly completion reports** detailing the cumulative count of vehicles inspected and remedied, which U.S. DOT publishes in its official completion-rate dataset (`6axg-epim`).

This institutional rule creates a critical econometric requirement: raw completion rates observed during the first 12 to 15 months following recall filing (`RCDATE`) are heavily right-censored by administrative reporting schedules and phased parts or software rollouts (paralleling recall-timing dynamics documented in medical devices and toys by Thirumalai and Sinha 2011 and Hora et al. 2011). Accordingly, our primary remediation-velocity and net-exposure analyses enforce a strict statutory **maturity gate of $\ge 540$ days ($\ge 18$ months)** post-`RCDATE` (ensuring completion of all six quarterly reporting cycles), complemented by full-sample cubic campaign-age and filing-quarter fixed-effects specifications.

### 2.2 Endogenous Fixed-Cost Remedy Selection

Consider an automaker facing a safety-related software defect affecting a fleet of $N$ vehicles. The automaker chooses between traditional dealer-bay reprogramming ($D$) and over-the-air remediation ($O$), which for legacy manufacturers typically operates as a hybrid channel ("over-the-air or by a dealer"). Dealer remediation incurs minimal fixed software-packaging overhead ($F_D \approx 0$) because technicians flash modules via existing OBD-II diagnostic tools, but imposes a substantial variable warranty reimbursement cost $c_D > 0$ per vehicle (typically \$75–\$250 in dealer labor plus bay congestion). Conversely, legacy OEMs with fragmented multi-supplier electronic control unit (ECU) architectures face a strictly positive fixed engineering, cybersecurity signing, telematics integration, and validation cost $F_O > 0$ to prepare an OTA campaign, paired with near-zero marginal distribution cost ($c_O \approx 0$) for connected vehicles. Total direct compliance costs are therefore:

$$C_D(N) = F_D + c_D N \approx c_D N, \qquad C_O(N) = F_O + c_O N + \theta c_D N$$

where $\theta \in [0, 1)$ is the residual share of unconnected or opt-out vehicles that still require dealer fallback under Hybrid-OTA. Cost-minimizing legacy OEMs deploy OTA if and only if $N$ exceeds an endogenous fleet-size threshold:

$$N^* = \frac{F_O - F_D}{(1 - \theta) c_D - c_O} > 0$$

This simple operational structure yields three sharp, testable predictions:

1. **Legacy Hybrid-OTA Selection Threshold:** Within legacy OEMs that employ both dealer software and Hybrid-OTA remedies, conditional on observing a software recall, Hybrid-OTA campaigns will exhibit a dramatically larger fleet size $N$ than Dealer-Software campaigns ($E[\log N \mid \text{Hybrid-OTA}] \gg E[\log N \mid \text{Dealer-SW}]$), concentrating heavily above the fixed-cost threshold $N^*$.
2. **EV-Native Pure-OTA Absence of Within-OEM Selection:** EV-native manufacturers built around centralized vehicle operating systems and unified CI/CD OTA pipelines amortize $F_O$ across routine feature updates ($F_O^{\text{native}} \approx 0$ per recall). Consequently, they deploy Pure-OTA across both tiny and massive recalls alike, predicting no systematic within-OEM fleet-size selection between Pure-OTA and Dealer-Software.
3. **Unconditional ITT Divergence:** If the conditional blast-radius gap is largely driven by endogenous selection above $N^*$ rather than an immediate architectural explosion caused by adopting OTA capability, then pooling *all* software recalls unconditionally (OTA + Dealer-Software) around an OEM's staggered adoption of OTA capability will yield a much smaller (or null) intent-to-treat (ITT) shift than the conditional Hybrid-OTA versus Dealer-Software gap.

### 2.3 Net Unremedied Fleet Exposure and the Break-Even Completion-Rate Frontier

From a public-safety and regulatory welfare standpoint, the primary hazard posed by an automotive recall is not the initial count of affected vehicles $N$, nor the completion percentage $r \in [0, 1]$ in isolation, but rather the **net unremedied fleet** remaining on public roads after remediation matures (Bae and Benítez-Silva 2011):

$$U = N \cdot (1 - r)$$

Suppose a baseline dealer-remedied software recall affects $N_0$ vehicles and achieves mature completion rate $r_0$, leaving $U_0 = N_0 (1 - r_0)$ unremedied vehicles. Now consider an OTA modality whose campaign blast radius is $M = N_{\text{OTA}} / N_0$ times larger and whose mature completion rate is $r_{\text{OTA}}$. Net unremedied exposure declines relative to dealer software ($U_{\text{OTA}} < U_0$) if and only if:

$$M \cdot (1 - r_{\text{OTA}}) < (1 - r_0) \iff r_{\text{OTA}} > r^*(M; r_0) \equiv 1 - \frac{1 - r_0}{M}$$

Equation $r^*(M; r_0)$ defines the **Break-Even Completion-Rate Frontier**. Crucially, $r^*(M; r_0)$ is steeply concave in $M$: every doubling of blast radius requires cutting the *unremedied residual fraction* $(1 - r)$ in half. Anchored consistently to the sample-matched baseline of OTA-capable OEMs' mature Dealer-Software recalls ($r_0 = 85.71\%$), a $7.89\times$ blast-radius multiplier requires **$r^* = 98.19\%$** merely to break even! Stopping short at 95.66% leaves a 4.34% unremedied tail—more than five times wider than Pure-OTA's 0.82% residual—generating *more* unremedied vehicles per campaign despite a substantial completion-rate gain. Only architectures capable of pushing completion past the **99% barrier** reliably invert multi-fold blast-radius concentration into net public-safety gains.

## 3. Data, Ground-Truth API Audit, and Econometric Methodology

### 3.1 Four Linked Official Public Federal Datasets

All data are drawn exclusively from authorized, publicly accessible U.S. federal endpoints downloaded with cryptographic SHA-256 receipts on November 25, 2026:

1. **NHTSA ODI Complete Recalls Flat File (`FLAT_RCL_POST_2010.zip`):** Official tab-delimited flat file (248,685 make-model-year rows across all recall types; 197,488 Vehicle rows spanning 2015–2026 through November 20, 2026, representing **$10{,}559$ unique Vehicle recall campaigns**, of which **$7{,}967$** were filed in 2018–2026). Provides exact filing dates (`RCDATE`), manufacturing date bounds (`BGMAN`, `ENDMAN`), owner notification dates (`ODATE`), affected makes, models, model years, component hierarchies (`COMPNAME`), recall initiation source (`INFLUENCED_BY`), FMVSS codes, defect/consequence/remedy narratives, and *Do Not Drive* / *Park Outside* indicators.
2. **U.S. DOT Official Completion-Rate Registry (`6axg-epim`):** Complete Socrata census of 30,484 recalls (26,561 Vehicle recalls) maintained by U.S. DOT / NHTSA and updated November 25, 2026. Exact 1-to-1 campaign linkage matches **100.0% ($7{,}967 / 7{,}967$)** of 2018–2026 vehicle campaigns, yielding **$7{,}385$** campaigns with non-null official `completion_rate` and **$6{,}412$** mature ($\ge 540$-day) rated campaigns. Log-fleet-size correlation between `FLAT_RCL` and `6axg-epim` is $r = 1.00000$. Inspection revealed exactly **three administrative outlier campaigns** in 2018–2026 with `completion_rate` exceeding 100% (`20V664000` Proterra, 5 vehicles, 3,380%; `20V731000` Bugatti, 3 vehicles, 133.3%; `19V871000` Proterra, 140 vehicles, 108.5%), arising from post-filing denominator amendments. Primary specifications clip `completion_rate` to $[0, 100]$ and verify exact invariance on `completion_rate_strict` excluding those three campaigns ($N = 6{,}409$).
3. **Live NHTSA Campaign Ground-Truth API (`api.nhtsa.gov/recalls/campaignNumber`):** We queried **100% of software and OTA-candidate Vehicle campaigns** filed during 2018–2026 ($N = 1{,}202$) plus a **stratified random audit sample of 250 non-software physical campaigns** across all nine years (total $N = 1{,}452$, 100.0% HTTP 200 success, zero errors). This extracts official `overTheAirUpdate`, `parkIt`, `parkOutSide`, and `NHTSAActionNumber` (linking 53 formal ODI Preliminary Evaluations, Engineering Analyses, and Recall Queries).
4. **NHTSA ODI Consumer Safety Complaints (`2020–2026`):** Complete bulk zips (`COMPLAINTS_RECEIVED_2020-2024.zip` and `COMPLAINTS_RECEIVED_2025-2026.zip`, through November 23, 2026) containing **636,875 component-level records** representing **$437{,}886$ unique consumer complaints (`ODINO`)** (1.454 raw rows per unique complaint because multi-component complaints generate one row per reported subsystem). Crucially, we **deduplicate strictly by unique `(campaign, ODINO)`** when linking by exact `(MAKETXT, MODELTXT, YEARTXT, comp_top)` to all **$4{,}560$** vehicle recall campaigns filed between January 1, 2021 and November 25, 2025—eliminating 7.3–7.4% multi-subcomponent double-counting and producing a strictly balanced, uncensored **$[-12, +12]$ relative-month panel** ($114{,}000$ campaign-month observations; $74{,}874$ deduplicated component-matched receipt-date links; $76{,}955$ failure-date links; $1{,}765$ campaigns with $\ge 1$ matched complaint). Furthermore, because raw `FLAT_CMPL` integer fields contain unvalidated consumer protest entries (e.g., `ODINO 11517246` on a 2022 Tesla Model Y and `11558649` on a 2016 Lexus CT both recording `99` injuries and `99` deaths in frustration), we measure severe safety incidents via **unique crash-, fire-, injury-, and fatality-reporting complaint indicators** rather than raw uncleaned integer sums.

**Corporate Entity Canonicalization:** Forensic auditing of raw NHTSA manufacturer strings uncovered 47 punctuation/case duplicates and corporate renamings that artificially split major OEMs into multiple fixed effects and clusters—most notably `General Motors, LLC` (189) vs. `General Motors LLC` (162), `Chrysler (FCA US LLC)` (227) vs. `Chrysler (FCA US, LLC)` (227) vs. `Stellantis` (9), `Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC.` (185) vs. `Mercedes-Benz USA, LLC` (180), `Kia Motors America` (81) vs. `Kia America, Inc.` (73), `Volvo Car USA` variants (57), `Tesla Motors, Inc.` vs. `Tesla, Inc.` (90), and `Lucid Motors` vs. `Lucid USA` (22). Consolidating exact corporate entities—while preserving legally independent commercial truck/bus divisions (`Volvo Trucks North America`, `Hyundai Translead`, `Mitsubishi Fuso`)—harmonizes 705 raw strings into **646 canonical OEMs** (532 active in 2018–2026; **32 OTA-capable OEMs**, of which 21 are light-duty passenger-vehicle OEMs and 11 are commercial truck, RV, bus, or motorcycle OEMs tested explicitly in light-duty robustness specifications).

### 3.3 Econometric Specifications and Cluster-Robust Inference

Our primary within-OEM campaign-level specification estimates:

$$Y_i = \beta_{\text{pure}} \mathbf{1}[\text{Pure-OTA}_i] + \beta_{\text{hyb}} \mathbf{1}[\text{Hybrid-OTA}_i] + \gamma_{\text{oem}(i)} + \delta_{\text{time}(i)} + \phi_{\text{my}(i)} + \psi_{\text{comp}(i)} + \varepsilon_i$$

where $Y_i$ denotes log fleet blast radius $\log(1 + \text{PotAff}_i)$, official mature completion rate $r_i \in [0, 100]$, net unremedied fleet exposure $\log(1 + U_i)$, or safety/oversight outcomes; $\gamma_{\text{oem}}$ absorbs canonical OEM fixed effects; $\delta_{\text{time}}$ absorbs Recall-Year FE (Pillar 1 and Pillar 4) or Recall-Quarter FE (Pillar 2 and Pillar 3 completion/maturity controls); $\phi_{\text{my}}$ absorbs exact Min-Model-Year FE (eliminating vehicle-vintage truncation bias); and $\psi_{\text{comp}}$ absorbs top-14 NHTSA component categories plus `OTHER`. Primary software benchmarks compare Pure-OTA and Hybrid-OTA against **Dealer-Software** strictly within the 32 OTA-capable OEMs; secondary universe benchmarks compare all three software modalities against **Physical-Hardware** across all 532 OEMs.

Every regression is programmatically verified to have **strictly full column rank** via pivoted QR orthogonalization before inversion, and high-dimensional FE models are additionally verified after **iterative singleton-FE removal**. Standard errors employ CR1 cluster-robust variance estimators clustered by canonical OEM with **finite-sample $t(G - 1)$ inference** (Cameron and Miller 2015), paired with **exact null-imposed Cameron–Gelbach–Miller (2008) Rademacher Wild Cluster Bootstrap $p$-values ($B = 999$ draws, $p_{\text{WCB}}$)**. Where `Tesla, Inc.` accounts for a concentrated share of a single binary indicator (59% of mature Pure-OTA campaigns), we explicitly account for MacKinnon and Webb's (2017) documented few-treated-cluster WCB conservatism by reporting Non-Tesla Pure-OTA (both all OEMs and strictly light-duty passenger-vehicle OEMs) and direct Pure-OTA vs. Hybrid-OTA tests alongside full-sample estimates.

To confront endogenous remedy selection without conditioning on remedy modality, we estimate an **unconditional Intent-to-Treat (ITT) staggered OEM OTA-adoption event study** across all 2015–2026 software recalls ($N = 1{,}239$, $G = 68$ OEMs with $\ge 3$ software recalls, using never-OTA OEMs as controls):

$$\log(1 + \text{PotAff}_i) = \sum_{k \in \{-3,-2,0,1,2,3\}} \tau_k \, \mathbf{1}[t_i - E_{\text{oem}(i)} = k] + \gamma_{\text{oem}(i)} + \delta_{\text{yr}(i)} + \psi_{\text{comp}(i)} + \varepsilon_i$$

with endpoints binned at $\le -3$ and $\ge +3$ and reference period $k = -1$. To eliminate staggered-DiD negative-weighting and dynamic-path contamination (Callaway and Sant'Anna 2021; Roth et al. 2023), we additionally estimate the **exact Sun and Abraham (2021) saturated cohort $\times$ relative-time interaction-weighted (IW)** estimator across all seven adoption cohorts with complete pre-period reference observations (2019–2025; 39 saturated cohort-period cells; excluding the partial-year 2026 cohort with zero $k = -1$ observations so zero post-adoption observations leak into controls), computing exact cluster-robust delta-method standard errors for every dynamic lead/lag and the pooled post-adoption IW average (25 post cells). Finally, for our real-world consumer complaint event study ($114{,}000$ campaign-month observations), we absorb all campaign fixed effects ($\alpha_i$) via exact Frisch–Waugh–Lovell demeaning alongside relative-month ($\delta_m$) and calendar year-month ($\lambda_{ym}$) fixed effects, clustering both by OEM and by campaign.

1. **Lifecycle-Lag ("Sell First, Fix Later") Null:** Raw production-to-recall median lags appear shorter for OTA (740.5 days Pure-OTA; 746.0 days Hybrid-OTA) than Dealer-Software (916.0 days). Once exact Model-Year fixed effects absorb newer vehicle-vintage right-truncation, the lag gap vanishes completely (`Pure-OTA` $+30.2$ days, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.540$; `Hybrid-OTA` $+58.7$ days, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.303$; `API OTA` $-24.9$ days, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.805$).
2. **Repeat-Defect Cascade Null:** Raw 365-day same-component repeat recall rates are visibly elevated for Pure-OTA (55.6%) and Hybrid-OTA (40.0%) versus Dealer-Software (30.4%) and Physical-Hardware (23.3%). Yet once OEM, Model-Year, and Component fixed effects are included, the repeat-cascade premium attenuates to a clean null (`Pure-OTA` $+16.6$ pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.327$; `Hybrid-OTA` $+7.3$ pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.321$; `API OTA` $+2.2$ pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.840$), showing that repeat clustering is a platform-and-component attribute rather than a causal byproduct of sloppy OTA patches.
3. **Acute Hazard & Owner-Notice Speed Nulls:** Zero Pure-OTA or Hybrid-OTA campaigns carried a *Do Not Drive* advisory (vs. 0.71% Dealer-SW, 2.10% Physical), and within-OEM differences in *Park Outside*, crash/fire narratives, and recall-to-owner-letter lags are all statistically indistinguishable from zero.
4. **Pre-Recall Ramp-Up & Real-World Complaint-Decay DiD Null (Table 5B, Figure 5B):** Table 5B shows that OTA campaigns—affecting much larger, newer fleets—experience substantially steeper pre-recall failure-complaint acceleration in $[-3, -1]$ relative to $[-12, -4]$ (**$2.43\times$ for Pure-OTA** and **$2.06\times$ for Hybrid-OTA** vs. $1.63\times$ for Dealer-Software and $1.32\times$ for Physical-Hardware). Consequently, full-window $[-12, -1]$ vs. $[+1, +12]$ DiD models yield clean nulls across every specification (`Active SW Post × Pure-OTA` $= +0.011$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.825$; `Hybrid-OTA` $= -0.012$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.780$; `API OTA` $= +0.013$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.737$; campaign-clustered $p_{\text{WCB}} \ge 0.81$). Even restricting to a **short pre-recall window ($[-3, -1]$ vs. $[+1, +12]$)** to mitigate long-horizon fleet ramp-up yields a nominal negative point estimate for Pure-OTA ($-0.101$, $t = -2.27$, $p_t = 0.028$) and severe crash/fire complaints ($-0.028$, $t = -2.55$, $p_t = 0.014$) that both clearly fail Wild Cluster Bootstrap rejection ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.271$ and $0.269$). ## 5. Managerial, Architectural, and Regulatory Policy Implications

Our empirical decomposition carries actionable prescriptions for automotive operations executives and federal safety regulators alike:

1. **Escaping the Hybrid-OTA Safety Trap (For Legacy Automakers):** Legacy OEMs currently capture the worst half of the SDV Recall Paradox on Hybrid-OTA campaigns: they endogenously route their largest multi-model campaigns ($7.0\times$ larger within-OEM) to Hybrid-OTA to avoid dealer warranty costs, yet stop at **95.66% completion** (**95.41%** on pure software-choice hybrids)—leaving 364 (443 SW-choice) median unremedied vehicles per campaign because non-connected vehicles, expired cellular telematics subscriptions, and manual opt-in prompts create a stubborn 4.3–4.6% compliance tail. Closing that final 3–4 percentage-point gap requires **architectural parity with Pure-OTA**: provisioning non-expiring cellular safety-recall connectivity decoupled from paid consumer infotainment subscriptions, standardizing unified central-gateway ECU flashing across suppliers, and defaulting critical safety patches to automatic scheduled installation—particularly for secondary-market owners beyond year three.
2. **Recognizing the Secondary-Market Dividend of OTA:** Traditional dealership recalls hemorrhage compliance at **$-3.57$ pp per year of vehicle age**, turning 6+ year-old vehicles into a persistent highway safety liability (53–64% completion). Investing in persistent OTA capability is therefore not merely a warranty-cost reduction tool for new vehicles under factory warranty; it is the single most effective mechanism for maintaining lifecycle safety compliance after vehicles enter used-car markets.
3. **Modernizing Federal Safety Metrics and Repairing NHTSA Data Governance (For U.S. DOT / NHTSA):**
   - *Immediate API Remediation:* NHTSA should audit and restore its `overTheAirUpdate` API ingestion pipeline, which degraded during January–May 8, 2025 (4/7 flagged) and has silently reported `False` on **68 of 68 (100.0%)** genuine OTA recalls filed after May 8, 2025 (**71 of 75 = 94.7%** across all of 2025–2026), threatening downstream academic, insurance, and consumer-safety analytics.
   - *Regulating Net Unremedied Exposure, Not Headline Recall Counts:* Headline news coverage and regulatory scrutiny often penalize EV-native OEMs for large recalled vehicle counts ($N$). Our fleet accounting proves that headline recall counts are a severely distorted safety proxy: Pure-OTA accounts for 4.00% of recalled vehicles yet only **0.14% of unremedied hazardous vehicles**. Federal oversight should adopt **Net Unremedied Vehicle-Days**—integrating blast radius, maturation speed, and residual non-compliance—as its benchmark safety KPI.
   - *Granular Hybrid-Channel Reporting:* Under 49 CFR 573.7, NHTSA should require Hybrid-OTA quarterly filings to disaggregate **VINs remedied wirelessly vs. VINs remedied at dealerships**, enabling direct measurement of the unconnected tail driving the Hybrid-OTA Safety Trap.

## 6. Limitations and Threats to Validity

Scientific rigor requires candid acknowledgment of four limitations:

1. **Observational Remedy Assignment Within Legacy OEMs:** While our ITT staggered adoption event study, saturated Sun–Abraham (2021) IW estimator, and unconditional Software $\times$ SDV cohort DiD cleanly separate endogenous selection from architectural shift, legacy OEMs' choice of Hybrid-OTA versus Dealer-Software on a specific recall remains an endogenous operational decision. Our completion-rate gains ($+6.25$ pp within OEM; $+5.19$ pp controlling for fleet size; $+4.34$ pp under OEM $\times$ Year FE) could still reflect unobserved defect-severity or connectivity differences correlated with OTA eligibility.
2. **Cross-Sectional Maturation vs. Longitudinal Quarterly Trajectories:** Because NHTSA's `quarterlyReports` API endpoint requires restricted authentication (HTTP 403 publicly), our maturation curve relies on cross-sectional campaign-age strata rather than within-campaign longitudinal quarterly curves. Our primary completion and net-exposure results therefore restrict strictly to statutory mature ($\ge 18$-month) campaigns and confirm stability using cubic campaign-age splines plus Recall-Quarter fixed effects.
3. **Pure-OTA Cluster Concentration and Sparse Within-OEM Counterfactuals:** Among the 46 mature Pure-OTA campaigns, 27 belong to `Tesla, Inc.` and 19 belong to other OEMs (Lucid, Fisker, Rivian, Motiv, Polestar, Hyundai, Volvo Car, Faraday Future, LiveWire, Lonestar). Because EV-native OEMs execute virtually all software recalls via Pure-OTA, within-OEM software-only Pure-OTA coefficients face wide standard errors and MacKinnon–Webb (2017) few-treated-cluster WCB conservatism. Replicating our Pure-OTA completion ($+16.27$ pp overall, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$; **$+10.94$ pp strictly within light-duty passenger-vehicle OEMs**, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.013$) and net-unremedied exposure ($-3.36$ log points overall, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.007$; **$-3.39$ log points strictly light-duty passenger**, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.029$) confirms that the Pure-OTA inversion is a genuine light-duty architectural property rather than a Tesla-specific or commercial-truck anomaly.
4. **Sparsity, Fleet Ramp-Up, and Self-Reporting Noise in ODI Consumer Complaints:** NHTSA's ODI complaint database is a voluntary self-reported registry that cannot distinguish whether an individual complaining VIN has already installed an OTA or dealer patch, exhibits steeper pre-recall exposure ramp-up on newer OTA fleets (pre-trend Wald $p = 0.006$), and even contains unvalidated consumer protest entries (`99`-fatality filings). Accordingly, our null complaint-decay DiD does not prove that higher completion fails to prevent crashes; rather, it demonstrates that aggregate voluntary ODI complaint volumes are too noisy and exposure-confounded to detect incremental safety gains between 83–90% (dealer) and 96–99% (OTA) completion over 12 months. Future studies linking VIN-level police crash registries (e.g., FARS/CRSS) to VIN-level recall completion timestamps would provide sharper causal validation.

## 7. Conclusion

Using the complete 2015–2026 U.S. automotive recall census, official DOT completion rates, a 1,452-campaign live NHTSA API audit, and 437,886 deduplicated consumer safety complaints, we resolve the Software-Defined Vehicle Recall Paradox. Rather than a uniform technology dividend or a uniform moral-hazard threat, OTA recall performance splits decisively along organizational and architectural lines. Approximately 85–90% of legacy automakers' $7.0\times$ Hybrid-OTA blast-radius concentration arises from endogenous fixed-cost remedy selection, yet Hybrid-OTA's 95.66% completion rate (95.41% for pure software-choice hybrids) falls 2.53–2.76 percentage points short of its sample-matched break-even completion frontier—trapping legacy fleets in elevated net unremedied exposure. Conversely, Pure-OTA architectures push completion past 99.18%, eradicate 86.3% of the $-3.57$ pp/yr vehicle-age compliance-decay cliff, and invert multi-fold blast radius into an 85.3% reduction in unremedied vehicles circulating on U.S. highways. Closing the last four percentage points of connected-fleet compliance—and repairing federal OTA data transparency—defines the central operations-management challenge of the software-defined vehicle era.
