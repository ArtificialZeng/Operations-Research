## Abstract

When the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency tightened the primary annual PM$_{2.5}$ National Ambient Air Quality Standard from $12.0$ to $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ in February 2024 (89 FR 16202), compliance collided with rapid adoption of optical broadband-spectrometer monitors (Teledyne API T640/T640x) and intensifying North American wildfire smoke. Combining stitched Appendix N histories (410 repaired split-year T640 monitors), 63,951 same-rooftop collocated T640–FRM daily pairs (69,224 through May 2026), EPA speciation and meteorology, Census demographics, and eight full years of satellite NOAA Hazard Mapping System (HMS) smoke polygons, we establish four findings centered on a causal same-rooftop physical core. First, EPA's May 2024 T640 software alignment alone shifted 47 U.S. counties (18.39M residents; 49 / 19.87M incl. SPMs) across the 2021–2023 monitor Design-Value threshold ($\ge 9.1 \to \le 9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$) without removing ambient pollution—representing **monitor Design-Value threshold reclassifications**, not formal Clean Air Act § 107(d) legal nonattainment designations (which also weigh EPA's 5-factor area analysis of emissions, meteorology, topography, and jurisdiction). Reclassifications cluster outside the West (46/47 counties; 17.0% pop-weighted Black residents vs. 12.9% cleanly $\le 9.0$, $p = 0.016$), while exogenous gravimetric FRM PM$_{2.5}$ (+0.007 $\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.951$), FEM-to-FRM switching (-0.030, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.287$), and monitor survival (+0.026, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.358$) show clean nulls. Second, forming the study's causal physical core, post-alignment `T640_FW` (`636/638`) retains a $+0.468\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ same-rooftop Design Value bias ($t = +9.17$, $N = 178$), creating a 9.5:1 threshold classification lottery (19 vs. 2, exact binomial $p = 0.0002$; strict 4q $\times$ 3y 18 vs. 3, $p = 0.001$). Via EIV-robust independent instruments, `T640_FW` bias amplifies during smoke by $+3.97\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($+10.0\%$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$) on other-site $\ge 25\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ days and $+3.02\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($+13.5\%$, $t = +12.88$) under NOAA HMS Heavy plumes, whereas pure mass-based `BAM` (`170/209`) stays null ($+0.51\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.694$). Observational EPA speciation ($N = 14,992$) matches Mie optical theory and Long, Urbanski, & Lincoln (2023)—positive organic carbon ($+0.657$, $t = +6.40$; $+0.287$ extra under HMS Heavy) and RH ($+0.066$ per $+10\%$, $t = +3.13$), negative elemental carbon ($-0.936$, $t = -3.93$), absent in BAM—though controlled multi-site calibration experiments remain necessary for causal correction formulas. Third, under identical NOAA HMS Heavy smoke two meters apart, `T640_FW` doubles 24-hour NAAQS level-equivalent daily exceedances ($>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$: 52 vs. 26, 26 vs. 0 discordant, McNemar $p = 2.98 × 10^{-8}$; BAM $p = 0.688$) and increases exceedances of EPA's Wildfire Tier-1 5-year historical (2018–2022) FRM 98th-percentile threshold ($\min(p_{98,18\text{–}22}, 35)$) by 1.33$\times$ (168 vs. 126, 42 vs. 0, $p = 4.55 × 10^{-13}$; 1.66$\times$ all days, $p = 7.24 × 10^{-161}$; BAM null, Heavy $p = 0.774$, all $p = 0.195$). Fourth, in the newly binding exogenous 2018–2020 $[8.5, 11.5]$ band, 40 CFR § 50.14 flagging rose $+8.17$ pp (State $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.004$, RI $p = 0.0010$)—driven primarily by anticipatory/designation-window retroactive flagging in 2022–2023 *before* the Feb 2024 final rule (67.5% unconcurred; null 2024–2025 EPA-concurred flow). Cross-monitor `U2` shows positive observational T640 associations with heavy-smoke flagging ($+15.2$ pp, State $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.016$) and exclusions ($+0.250\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, State $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), **but the within-county-year instrument comparison for flagging is null ($+0.81$ pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.931$)**; thus we do not claim that optical bias causally induces strategic Exceptional Event filing.

## 1. Introduction

Ambient fine particulate matter (PM$_{2.5}$) remains the largest environmental mortality risk factor in the United States, with substantial health benefits documented even below historic federal thresholds (Di et al., 2017; Chay & Greenstone, 2005; Greenstone, 2002). On February 7, 2024, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) finalized a landmark tightening of the primary annual PM$_{2.5}$ National Ambient Air Quality Standard (NAAQS) from $12.0$ to $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, effective May 6, 2024 (U.S. EPA, 2024a, 89 FR 16202). Under 40 CFR Part 50, Appendix N, a county whose three-year regulatory monitor Design Value exceeds $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ (rounded to one decimal place, i.e., $\ge 9.1\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$) breaches the primary annual threshold—initiating Clean Air Act § 107(d) area designation proceedings, wherein EPA evaluates a statutory five-factor area analysis (air quality data, emissions, meteorology, geography/topography, and jurisdictional boundaries) before issuing formal legal nonattainment designations that trigger State Implementation Plans (SIPs), industrial emission offsets, and transportation conformity requirements (Auffhammer, Bento, & Lowe, 2009; Lu, 2026).

Yet threshold compliance under a tight $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ standard depends as critically on *how* air quality is measured as on physical emissions themselves (Zou, 2021; Grainger & Schreiber, 2019; Fowlie, Rubin, & Walker, 2019). Between 2018 and 2025, state and local air agencies rapidly replaced intermittent filter-based gravimetric Federal Reference Method (FRM) samplers with continuous optical broadband-spectrometer Federal Equivalent Method (FEM) monitors—predominantly the Teledyne API Model T640 and T640x—whose network footprint expanded more than fourfold to over 550 active sites. Field and chamber evaluations by Long, Urbanski, & Lincoln (2023) documented substantial positive bias in unaligned T640 units relative to collocated gravimetric FRMs, prompting EPA in May 2024 to issue an unprecedented network-wide software alignment introducing firmware-aligned method codes (`636/638`) and retroactively adjusted historical series (`736/738`) (U.S. EPA, 2024b). Simultaneously, climate-driven North American wildfire smoke reversed decades of U.S. PM$_{2.5}$ progress (Burke et al., 2023; Childs et al., 2022), placing unprecedented pressure on EPA's Exceptional Events Rule (40 CFR § 50.14), which allows agencies to exclude smoke-influenced monitoring days only when those exclusions carry "regulatory significance" for attainment determinations (Jaffe et al., 2026; Krupnick et al., 2025).

This paper provides a unified physical-to-regulatory forensic evaluation connecting optical aerosol metrology, EPA's 2024 T640 alignment, observational chemical speciation, and 40 CFR § 50.14 Exceptional Event triage across the United States through May 2026. We deliberately separate **causal physical identification**—established by Pillar 2 and Unification Test `U1` comparing collocated optical and gravimetric instruments two meters apart on the exact same air parcel—from **administrative Design-Value accounting** (Pillar 1) and **anticipatory/observational regulatory triage** (Pillar 3 and `U2`/`U3`).

Our analysis makes four distinct contributions to environmental economics, atmospheric chemistry, and regulatory design:

1. **Exact Stitched Appendix N Design-Value Threshold Accounting, EPA Reconciliation, and Environmental Justice Audit (Pillar 1):** Previous administrative audits suffer from mid-2023 T640 split-year truncation—where retroactive `736/738` data sit on separate Parameter Occurrence Codes (`POC + 20`) from post-upgrade `636/638` firmware records—and from non-regulatory Special Purpose or Industrial monitor contamination. Stitching 410 split-year 2023 T640 monitors and implementing 40 CFR Part 50 Appendix N § 3.0(d) Combined-Site daily records brings our monitor-threshold counts into close agreement with all five official EPA Final PM$_{2.5}$ Design Value workbooks (2019–2021 through 2023–2025), including 98/99 (99.0% precision) exact overlap with EPA's published 2021–2023 county roster ($\ge 9.1\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$). EPA's May 2024 T640 alignment alone shifted 47 regulatory counties home to 18.39 million Americans across the 2021–2023 Design-Value threshold ($\ge 9.1 \to \le 9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$; 49 counties / 19.87M including long-term NAAQS-comparable SPMs)—which we carefully distinguish as monitor Design-Value threshold reclassifications rather than formal Clean Air Act § 107(d) 5-factor legal designations. Of these 47 reclassified counties, 46/47 lie outside the West and carry significantly higher Black resident shares than cleanly $\le 9.0$ counties ($p = 0.016$). Using strictly exogenous 2018–2020 Design Values, we confirm dead-zero early-compliance effects on true gravimetric FRM PM$_{2.5}$ ($+0.007\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.951$), longitudinal FEM-to-FRM switching, and monitor pruning—showing that naive comparisons using overlapping 2021–2023 Design Values suffer from mechanical regression-to-the-mean.
2. **Causal Physical Core: Same-Rooftop Optical Lottery, EIV-Robust Smoke Amplification, and Historical-Baseline Tier-1 Screening Divergence (Pillar 2A & `U1`):** Using 63,951 same-rooftop daily collocated `T640_FW`–`FRM` pairs across 234 sites in 2023–2025 (69,224 pairs through May 2026), we show EPA's firmware alignment leaves a highly significant $+0.468\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ 3-year Design Value bias ($t = +9.17$, $p = 1.20 × 10^{-16}$) that creates a 9.5-to-1 Design-Value threshold classification lottery (19 rooftops where `T640_FW` $\ge 9.1$ while collocated FRM $\le 9.0$ vs. only 2 in reverse, exact binomial $p = 0.0002$). Eliminating direct own-FRM mathematical coupling (errors-in-variables from conditioning on FRM itself) via EIV-robust independent instruments—leave-one-site-out county PM$_{2.5}$ and external satellite NOAA HMS smoke polygons, corroborated by Bland & Altman (1986) diagnostics—we demonstrate that `T640_FW` bias amplifies nonlinearly by $+3.97\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($+10.0\%$) on independent other-site $\ge 25\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ days and $+3.02\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($+13.5\%$) under satellite Heavy smoke, while pure mass-based BAM monitors remain statistically flat ($+0.51\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.694$). Directly evaluating EPA's Wildfire Tier-1 screening benchmark using each site's strictly pre-period 5-year historical (2018–2022) FRM 98th percentile ($\min(p_{98,18\text{–}22}, 35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3)$), `T640_FW` generates 1.33$\times$ more Tier-1 exceedances under NOAA HMS Heavy smoke (42 vs. 0 discordant days, McNemar $p = 4.55 × 10^{-13}$; 1.66$\times$ across all days, $p = 7.24 × 10^{-161}$) and doubles 24-hour NAAQS level-equivalent daily exceedances ($>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$: 2.00$\times$, 26 vs. 0, $p = 2.98 × 10^{-8}$), while collocated BAM shows complete nulls ($p = 0.774$ and $p = 0.688$).
3. **Observational Chemical Speciation and Hygroscopicity Associations Consistent with Mie Theory (Pillar 2B):** Merging EPA daily Chemical Speciation Network (CSN/IMPROVE) measurements ($\text{NO}_3^-$, $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$, OC, EC) and ambient meteorology (RH, temperature) onto 14,992 `T640_FW`–`FRM` pairs and 7,333 `BAM`–`FRM` pairs yields observational associations tightly consistent with Mie optical scattering theory and Long, Urbanski, & Lincoln (2023)'s controlled evaluations: `T640_FW` bias rises by $+0.657\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ per $\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ of organic carbon ($t = +6.40$, escalating by $+0.287$ under HMS Heavy smoke) and $+0.066\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ per $+10\%$ relative humidity ($t = +3.13$), while *declining* with light-absorbing elemental carbon ($-0.936$, $t = -3.93$). Heated-inlet BAM displays null associations with OC ($t = +1.34$) and EC ($t = -0.97$) and opposite-sign slopes on RH ($-0.106$) and semi-volatile nitrate ($-0.358$). Because these ambient regressions are observational, controlled multi-site physical calibration and chamber experiments are required to establish causal firmware correction formulas.
4. **Pre-Trend-Clean Anticipatory 40 CFR § 50.14 Triage and Candid Observational `U2` Calibration (Pillar 3 & `U2`/`U3`):** Correcting a 49.9% false-positive EPA-concurrence coding artifact in AQS annual summaries and standardizing all difference-in-differences tests onto pre-trend-clean exogenous 2018–2020 Design Values ($[8.5, 11.5]\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$), we document a $+8.17$ pp monitor-year surge ($+9.96$ pp county-year) in state Exceptional Event flagging (State-clustered $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.004$, within-state randomization inference $p = 0.0010$). Crucially, the main surge occurred during the **2022–2023 anticipatory/designation window prior to the February 2024 final rule** (67.5% unconcurred precautionary state flags, with null 2024–2025 EPA-concurred flow). Moreover, while cross-monitor `U2` regressions controlling for `State × Year FE` and satellite HMS smoke find positive observational associations between T640 deployment and heavy-smoke flagging ($+15.2$ pp, State $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.016$) and claimed annual-mean exclusions ($+0.250\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, State $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), **the within-county-year instrument comparison for flagging is cleanly null ($+0.81$ pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.931$)**; we therefore explicitly avoid claiming that optical bias causally drives strategic EE filing.

## 2. Institutional Setting and Formal Regulatory Metrology Model

### 2.1 Regulatory Architecture: Appendix N, T640 Alignment, and 40 CFR § 50.14

Under 40 CFR Part 50, Appendix N, annual PM$_{2.5}$ threshold compliance is governed by each regulatory monitoring site's three-year Design Value—the unweighted mean of three consecutive annual means, where each annual mean averages four quarterly means requiring at least 75% quarterly scheduled sample completeness (with site-level daily combining across primary and collocated regulatory samplers under § 3.0(d) and limited substitution under § 4.1(c)). Design Values are rounded to one decimal place; under EPA's February 2024 Final Rule (89 FR 16202), any regulatory monitor with $\text{DV} \ge 9.1\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ exceeds the $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ annual standard. Crucially, monitor Design-Value threshold exceedance is the quantitative trigger for—not legally identical to—formal Clean Air Act § 107(d) nonattainment area designations, which incorporate EPA's five-factor area analysis (air quality data, emissions, meteorology, geography/topography, and jurisdictional boundaries) and can draw partial-county boundaries or include contributing neighboring counties. Separately, the 24-hour PM$_{2.5}$ NAAQS ($35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$) is evaluated on a three-year average of annual 98th percentiles rather than a single daily exceedance; for wildfire Exceptional Events screening, EPA's Wildfire Tiering Tool compares event-day readings against each site's **5-year historical 98th percentile** (and $\min(p_{98,5\text{-yr}}, 35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3)$ for Tier-1/Tier-2 historical-fluctuation screening). Because state designation recommendations rely on the most recent three complete calendar years (2021–2023 upon promulgation, updated through 2022–2024 and 2023–2025 prior to final designations), the 2022–2023 wildfire seasons sat squarely inside the anticipatory designation window following EPA's January 2023 proposed rule.

Two institutional mechanisms interacted sharply with this $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ threshold. First, following EPA's May 2024 T640 alignment decision (U.S. EPA, 2024b), Teledyne deployed a firmware revision converting optical broadband-scattering outputs using an ambient-temperature linear adjustment (`method_code = 636/638`), while EPA retroactively generated aligned historical daily data (`method_code = 736/738`) on separate AQS Parameter Occurrence Codes (`POC + 20`) to supersede raw pre-alignment series (`236/238`). Second, under the 2016 Exceptional Events Rule (40 CFR § 50.14), state agencies may flag wildfire-influenced monitoring days in AQS and submit formal technical demonstrations requesting EPA concurrence for exclusion—explicitly conditioned on **regulatory significance**, meaning EPA prioritizes and concurs with demonstrations capable of altering a NAAQS designation or attainment determination (Jaffe et al., 2026; Krupnick et al., 2025).

### 2.2 Formal Equilibrium Model of Optical Bias and Threshold Triage

To generate sharp testable hypotheses across all three empirical pillars, consider monitor $i$ in county $c$ observed on day $t \in \{1,\dots,T\}$. True ambient gravimetric fine particulate mass is $M_{it} = M^0_{it} + S_{it} \ge 0$ ($\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$), where $M^0_{it}$ is background PM$_{2.5}$ with ammonium-nitrate mass fraction $\nu_{it} \in [0,1]$ and ambient relative humidity $h_{it} \in [0,1]$, and $S_{it} \ge 0$ is wildfire-smoke organic-carbon aerosol mass. A gravimetric FRM observes $\hat{M}^{\text{FRM}}_{it} = M_{it} + \varepsilon^{\text{FRM}}_{it}$ with $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon^{\text{FRM}}_{it}] = 0$. A post-alignment optical T640 spectrometer converts scattered broadband light via a fixed refractive-index and size-distribution model, yielding conditional daily measurement bias:

$$b^{\text{T640}}_{it} \equiv \mathbb{E}\Big[\hat{M}^{\text{T640}}_{it} - \hat{M}^{\text{FRM}}_{it} \mid \mathbf{X}_{it}\Big] = \beta_0 + \beta_N \nu_{it} M^0_{it} f(h_{it}) + \beta_S S_{it} + \gamma_S S_{it}^2$$

where $\beta_N > 0$ captures hygroscopic aerosol swelling ($f'(h) > 0$) unmodeled by EPA's temperature-only linear alignment, and $\beta_S > 0, \gamma_S \ge 0$ capture superlinear fine-mode organic-carbon Mie-scattering over-response on wildfire smoke days (whereas mass-based beta attenuation satisfies $\beta_S^{\text{BAM}} = \gamma_S^{\text{BAM}} = 0$).

Let $\bar{M}_i^{\text{FRM}}$ denote the monitor's three-year true Appendix N Design Value, $\bar{b}_i \equiv \mathbb{E}[b^{\text{T640}}_{it}]$ its mean residual optical bias, and $\bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}} = \bar{S}_i + \bar{b}_i^S$ the annual-mean smoke mass recorded by the instrument (including optical smoke amplification $\bar{b}_i^S \equiv \mathbb{E}[\beta_S S_{it} + \gamma_S S_{it}^2]$). Facing statutory cutoff $\tau \in \{12.0, 9.0\}$, nonattainment compliance cost $L > 0$, convex 40 CFR § 50.14 demonstration cost $\frac{c}{2} e^2$ ($c > 0$) for flagging intensity $e \in [0,1]$, and effective regulatory option value $\tilde{\rho} = \rho + (1-\rho)\omega \in (0,1]$ combining EPA concurrence probability $\rho$ and precautionary option value $\omega$, the state air agency chooses $e \in [0,1]$ to maximize:

$$U_i(e) = L \tilde{\rho}\; \Phi\!\left(\frac{\tau - (\bar{M}_i^{\text{FRM}} + \bar{b}_i - e \bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}})}{\sigma}\right) - \frac{c}{2} e^2$$

where $\Phi(\cdot)$ and $\phi(\cdot)$ are the standard normal CDF and PDF with attainment-noise scale $\sigma > 0$. Any interior optimum $e^*_i$ satisfies the first-order condition:

$$c\, e^*_i = \frac{L \tilde{\rho}\, \bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}}}{\sigma}\; \phi\!\left(\frac{\tau - \text{DV}_i(e^*_i)}{\sigma}\right), \qquad \text{DV}_i(e) = \bar{M}_i^{\text{FRM}} + \bar{b}_i - e \bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}}.$$

Numerical verification across calibrated EPA PM$_{2.5}$ parameters confirms exact dimensionally consistent concavity ($\partial^2 U_i / \partial e^2 = -(L \tilde{\rho} (\bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}})^2 / \sigma^2)\, z \phi(z) - c < 0$ everywhere, matching numerical central finite differences to $< 2 \times 10^{-5}$) and yields three closed-form empirical predictions:

- **Proposition 1 (Optical DV-Threshold Lottery & Hygroscopic Regional Skew):** Under cutoff $\tau$, any rooftop with $\bar{M}_i^{\text{FRM}} \in (\tau - \bar{b}_i, \tau]$ crosses the Design-Value threshold on T640 while remaining below $\tau$ on collocated FRM. Because $\partial \bar{b}_i / \partial(\nu_i f(h_i)) = \beta_N M^0_i > 0$, post-alignment discordance odds skew sharply positive (calibrated Midwest/Winter odds ratio $97.1\times$, regional bias ratio $2.82\times$) and concentrate in high-humidity, high-nitrate Eastern/Midwestern regimes relative to arid Western sites.
- **Proposition 2 (Inverted-U 40 CFR § 50.14 Triage & Alignment Bailout Dampening):** Lowering $\tau$ from $12.0$ to $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ shifts the density peak $\phi((\tau - \text{DV}_i)/\sigma)$ into the newly binding band. Consequently, the filing surge $\Delta e^*_i = e^*_i(9.0) - e^*_i(12.0)$ follows an **inverted-U** in pre-rule $\bar{M}_i^{\text{FRM}}$—near zero for cleanly attaining counties ($\bar{M}_i < 8.0$), peaking inside $[8.5, 11.5]$ (calibrated peak minus clean $+0.317$), and declining for deeply nonattainment counties ($> 12.0$)—while administrative T640 alignment bailouts reduce $\bar{b}_i$ and dampen post-alignment triage incentives.
- **Proposition 3 (Optical-to-Screening Divergence & Theoretical Filing-Return Channel):** On wildfire smoke days ($S_{it} > 0$), nonlinear T640 smoke over-response ($\beta_S > 0, \gamma_S \ge 0$) generates two testable hypotheses: (i) a direct *same-rooftop screening divergence* pushing marginal smoke plumes above both 24-hour level-equivalent ($>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$) and EPA 5-year historical 98th-percentile Tier-1 screening thresholds (calibrated $+40.4$ pp excess $>35$ exceedance at $S=25$), absent in BAM and causally testable on collocated rooftops (`U1`); and (ii) a theoretical *filing-return comparative static* raising $\bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}}$ inside the binding band ($\partial e^*_i / \partial \bar{b}_i^S > 0$, calibrated $+0.131$), which we evaluate candidly in observational cross-monitor and within-county-year tests (`U2`), explicitly noting below where within-county-year empirical flagging rejects a causal behavioral interpretation.

## 3. Data Architecture, Forensic Repairs, and Exact Econometric Methods

### 3.1 Integrated Multi-Source Microdata

We assemble a national regulatory and atmospheric-chemistry microdata warehouse spanning 2018 through May 2026 across five integrated federal data streams:

1. **EPA Air Quality System (AQS) PM$_{2.5}$ Daily & Annual Files (`88101`, 2018–May 2026):** All 24-hour and 24-hour block-average records across all 50 states and the District of Columbia, linked to `aqs_monitors.zip` and `aqs_sites.zip` metadata (`Monitor Type`, `Monitoring Objective`, `NAAQS Primary Monitor`, `First Year of Data`, and coordinates).
2. **Official EPA Final PM$_{2.5}$ Design Value Workbooks (2019–2021 through 2023–2025):** All five published EPA `Table 4a` county-level Annual Design Value workbooks from `epa.gov/air-trends/air-quality-design-values`, including the June 12, 2026 final release for 2023–2025.
3. **EPA AQS Chemical Speciation (`daily_SPEC`, 2023–2025) & Meteorology (`daily_TEMP`, `daily_RH_DP`, 2023–2025):** Daily total nitrate (`88306`, $\text{NO}_3^-$), sulfate (`88403`, $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$), thermal-optical reflectance organic carbon (`88320`/`88370`, OC) and elemental carbon (`88321`/`88380`, EC), crustal soil (`88348`), outdoor temperature (`62101`, converted to $^\circ\text{C}$), and relative humidity (`62201`, %). Each collocated pair is matched first to the exact same rooftop ($0\;\text{km}$) and otherwise to the nearest active speciation or meteorological site within $25\;\text{km}$ in the same county-day (with exact-$0\;\text{km}$ sensitivity verified separately).
4. **External Satellite NOAA HMS Wildfire Smoke Plume Polygons (2018–2025):** Daily analyst-verified satellite smoke polygons (`Light`, `Medium`, `Heavy`) downloaded across **100.0% of calendar days** (`2,922/2,922` days, zero HTTP 404s, zero errors) and spatially intersected via `shapely` `STRtree` against all 2,272 unique EPA PM$_{2.5}$ monitoring sites, yielding 1,471,932 smoke site-days (92,299 Heavy smoke site-days).
5. **Official U.S. Census Bureau County Demographics (3,144 Counties, 340.11M Residents):** Official Census Bureau 2023 Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates (SAIPE, median household income and all-ages poverty rate) linked with 2024 Census Population Estimates Program county race/ethnicity characteristics (PEP ASRH, non-Hispanic Black, Hispanic, and non-White shares; used cleanly in place of the API-key-gated `api.census.gov` endpoint) plus `co-est2024-alldata.csv` 2024 county populations.

### 3.2 Four Critical Forensic Repairs

Preliminary audits uncovered four severe data and econometric traps in raw EPA AQS files that would bias naive analyses if left uncorrected:

1. **Mid-2023 T640 Split-Year Stitching, Non-Diluted EE Aggregation, & Instrument Purity:** In 2023, EPA posted retroactively aligned `736/738` data on `POC + 20` through mid-year while firmware `636/638` resumed on the original POC post-upgrade. We deterministically stitch 451/452 `POC - 20` retroactive monitors plus non-$+20$ transitions, repairing 410 split-year 2023 T640 monitors into continuous physical histories while avoiding observation-count dilution when aggregating Exceptional Event reductions across overlapping `POC` and `POC + 20` annual rows. We also filter strictly to regulatory Appendix N monitor types (`SLAMS`, `TRIBAL`, `EPA`, `NON-EPA FEDERAL`, excluding `SPM`, `INDUSTRIAL`, `OTHER`, `QA COLLOCATED`, and `QUALITY ASSURANCE` objectives—removing 281 non-regulatory monitors), while presenting long-term `SPM` sensitivity transparently, and restrict our mass-based `BAM` benchmark strictly to pure Met One BAM-1020/1022 (`170/209`) so optical-nephelometer hybrid Thermo 5030 SHARP (`184`) and Thermo 5014i (`183`) units cannot contaminate the non-optical control group.
2. **Elimination of the 49.9% EPA-Concurrence False-Positive Artifact:** EPA's `annual_conc_by_monitor` files populate a `"Concurred Events Excluded"` row whenever *any* exceptional event is flagged—even when EPA concurred with zero events, leaving `Observation Count` and `Arithmetic Mean` identical to `"Events Included"`. Across 2018–2025, only 597 of 1192 concurrence rows (50.1%) reflect actual exclusions. We code `true_ee_conc = 1` strictly iff `obs_conc < obs_incl` or $|\text{mean}_{\text{incl}} - \text{mean}_{\text{conc}}| > 10^{-6}$, cleanly decomposing all state flags (`true_ee_flag`, $N = 1257$) into unconcurred precautionary state flags (`unconc_flag`, $N = 660$) and true EPA-concurred exclusions (`true_ee_conc`, $N = 597$).
3. **EIV-Robust Independent-Instrument Identification in Smoke Regressions:** Conditioning daily bias $(\text{FEM} - \text{FRM})$ on $\mathbb{I}(\text{FRM} \ge 25)$ mechanically induces negative errors-in-variables (EIV) coupling $\text{Cov}(-\varepsilon^{\text{FRM}}, \mathbb{I}(\text{FRM} \ge 25)) < 0$, attenuating T640 smoke spikes and creating spurious negative BAM bias. We abandon own-FRM conditioning in favor of two strictly independent external/other-site instruments—leave-one-site-out county PM$_{2.5}$ (including a pure non-T640 FRM/BAM-only subset) and external satellite NOAA HMS plumes—which eliminate direct own-FRM mechanical coupling, supplemented by Bland-Altman pair means $(\text{FEM} + \text{FRM})/2$ as a complementary diagnostic (noting explicitly that Bland-Altman eliminates EIV exactly only when FEM and FRM share equal error variances).
4. **Exact Nested-FE Degrees of Freedom & LSDV-Equivalent Wild Cluster Bootstrap:** In two-way panels with nested fixed effects such as `[Monitor/County, State × Year]`, naive dummy counting under-subtracts redundant state components ($S$ connected components rather than 1). Our econometric engine (`scripts/econ_utils.py`) computes exact bipartite connected-component rank via Union-Find, absorbs high-dimensional FEs via Frisch-Waugh-Lovell alternating projections to tolerance $10^{-13}$, and executes Roodman et al. (2019) restricted Wild Cluster Bootstrap (Rademacher weights, $B = 999$, re-demeaning at $10^{-12}$)—verified in automated unit tests to match explicit brute-force LSDV WCB to $\text{atol} = 10^{-12}$ (Cameron, Gelbach, & Miller, 2008; Abadie et al., 2023).

### 6.2 Same-Rooftop Screening Divergence (`U1`) and Observational Triage Linkages (`U2`, `U3`)

To test how Pillar 2's nonlinear optical smoke bias interacts with 40 CFR § 50.14 Exceptional Event screening and administrative flagging, we conduct three tests summarized in Table 5 and Figure 5—maintaining a sharp epistemic boundary between **causal same-rooftop physical screening divergence (`U1`)** and **observational cross-monitor administrative associations (`U2`, `U3`)**:

- **Unification Test U1 (Causal Physical Core — Same-Rooftop EPA Wildfire Tier-1 Historical Screening & 24-Hr Level-Equivalent Exceedances):** Two regulatory distinctions are essential here: first, a single daily reading $>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ is *not* a statutory 24-hour PM$_{2.5}$ NAAQS violation (which requires a three-year average of annual 98th percentiles); second, EPA's Wildfire Tiering Tool screens Exceptional Events using each monitor's **5-year historical 98th percentile** (specifically $\min(p_{98,5\text{-yr}}, 35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3)$ for Tier-1/Tier-2 historical-fluctuation screening). We therefore test *both* benchmarks directly on collocated rooftops in 2023–2025:
  1. **Official EPA Wildfire Tier-1 5-Year Historical (`2018–2022`) FRM 98th-Percentile Screening:** Computing each collocated site's strictly pre-period 5-year historical (`2018–2022`) gravimetric FRM 98th percentile (matched across 220/234 `T640_FW` rooftops, median historical $p_{98} = 18.51\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, 0.0% $> 35$, and 99/107 `BAM` rooftops, median $20.69\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, 21.2% $> 35$), `T640_FW` exceeds EPA's historical Tier-1 criterion $\min(p_{98,18\text{–}22}, 35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3)$ **1.66$\times$ more frequently** than the collocated FRM across all 60,929 paired days (2,224 vs. 1,338, **1,043 vs. 157 discordant days**, exact McNemar $p = 7.24 × 10^{-161}$, Site-CR1 $t = +7.83$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$). Under **satellite NOAA HMS Heavy smoke**, `T640_FW` generates **1.33$\times$ more historical Tier-1 exceedances** (168 vs. 126, 36.44% vs. 27.33%, **42 vs. 0 discordant days—zero in the reverse direction—exact McNemar $p = 4.55 × 10^{-13}$**, paired diff $+9.11$ pp, Site-CR1 $t = +7.46$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$; strict $\ge 3$ historical-year sensitivity: 154 vs. 117, 37 vs. 0, $p = 1.46 × 10^{-11}$), and on independent other-site $\ge 25\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ days 261 vs. 240 (29 vs. 8, $p = 0.0008$). In stark contrast, pure Met One `BAM` (`170/209`) displays **clean nulls across every historical Tier-1 stratum**: all paired days (498 vs. 517, McNemar $p = 0.195$), NOAA HMS Heavy smoke (75 vs. 77, 5 vs. 7, $p = 0.774$, $t = -0.71$; uncapped $> p_{98,18\text{–}22}$: 74 vs. 77, $p = 0.549$), and other-site $\ge 25$ (141 vs. 142, $p = 1.000$).
  2. **24-Hour NAAQS Level-Equivalent Daily Exceedances ($>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$):** Across all collocated days, `T640_FW` crosses $35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ **1.86$\times$ more often** than the collocated FRM two meters away (210 vs. 113 days; 115 vs. 18 discordant days, exact McNemar $p = 1.72 × 10^{-18}$). Under **satellite NOAA HMS Heavy smoke**, `T640_FW` **exactly doubles 24-hour NAAQS level-equivalent daily exceedances** (52 vs. 26, 11.02% vs. 5.51%, **26 vs. 0 discordant days—zero in the reverse direction—exact McNemar $p = 2.98 × 10^{-8}$**, paired diff $+5.51$ pp, $t = +5.12$), and on independent other-site $\ge 25\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ days reaches **1.94$\times$ (51 vs. 2 discordant days, $p = 3.18 × 10^{-13}$)**. Pure Met One `BAM` (`170/209`) again shows **zero excess exceedances** (all days 198 vs. 217, $t = -1.67$; HMS Heavy $p = 0.688$; other-site $\ge 25$ $p = 0.648$).
- **Unification Test U2 (Cross-Monitor Observational Association & Within-County-Year Null — No Causal Filing Claim):** Restricting inside borderline newly binding counties ($\text{DV}_{18\text{–}20} \in [8.5, 11.5]$, 2022–2025) and comparing T640 against pure gravimetric FRM and pure mass-based BAM (`170/209`) while controlling for `State × Year FE`, satellite NOAA HMS smoke-day counts, and $\log(\text{valid days})$ (omitting ground PM$_{2.5}$ as an optical mediator), we observe two cross-monitor patterns: (i) T640 monitors record **$+0.250\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ larger annual-mean claimed EE reductions** (County $t = +5.22$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$; State $t = +3.91$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$); and (ii) the interaction `is_T640 × log(1 + HMS Heavy Days)` covaries positively with state EE flagging at **$+15.19$ pp per log Heavy smoke day** (County $t = +4.11$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$; State $t = +3.61$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.016$), offsetting a marginal negative zero-smoke intercept ($-11.93$ pp, $p = 0.057$) with an insignificant un-interacted T640 main effect ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.453$). **Crucially, however, within-county-year fixed-effects comparisons ($N = 981$ monitor-years across $G = 60$ counties with both T640 and non-T640 monitors) yield a clean null on EE flagging ($+0.81$ pp, $t = +0.10$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.931$)**, while claimed annual-mean reduction remains $+0.208\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ larger on T640 ($t = +3.12$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$)—a pattern consistent with agencies flagging county-wide events symmetrically across local monitors while mechanically excluding larger smoke-day concentrations on optically inflated T640 units. Because cross-monitor deployment is non-random, within-county-year flagging is null ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.931$), and the primary 2022–2023 flagging surge preceded the February 2024 final rule, **we explicitly do not claim that optical bias causally drives strategic EE filing**, treating `U2` strictly as a cross-monitor observational association and anchoring our causal claims on the same-rooftop physical metrology in Pillar 2 and `U1`.
- **Unification Test U3 (Satellite HMS Backing of Administrative Triage):** Satellite validation across all 748 EE-flagged regulatory monitor-years in 2022–2025 confirms that state filings overwhelmingly track real wildfire smoke—**99.7%** experienced NOAA HMS smoke overhead and **97.7%** experienced Medium or Heavy smoke—ruling out fabricated non-smoke gaming. Estimating a fully saturated stratified DiD (including the pre-period interaction `Borderline × Heavy HMS`, which absorbs pre-rule Western clean-county fire episodes at $-5.73$ pp, $t = -2.72$) reveals a clear regulatory divide: **85.8%** of EPA-concurred exclusions had satellite Heavy smoke backing, and true EPA-concurred DiD surges **strictly in Heavy-smoke years ($+3.39$ pp, $t = +2.37$, $p = 0.018$)** while remaining statistically null in Non-Heavy years ($+1.94$ pp, $p = 0.349$). By contrast, only **66.1%** of unconcurred state flags had Heavy smoke backing, and unconcurred state flagging rises across both Heavy ($+4.36$ pp, $t = +2.48$, $p = 0.014$) and marginally Non-Heavy years ($+3.50$ pp, $t = +1.73$, $p = 0.084$), driving total `true_ee_flag` to $+7.75$ pp ($t = +3.81$) in Heavy smoke years vs. $+5.44$ pp ($p = 0.066$) in Non-Heavy years. Thus Pillar 3 reflects **anticipatory 2022–2023 statutory triage on intense smoke episodes combined with precautionary state flagging**, without requiring a causal optical-to-strategic-filing claim.

## 7. Policy Implications, Limitations, and Conclusion

Our findings reveal an overlooked structural vulnerability in modern environmental regulation: as statutory air quality thresholds tighten into the $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ regime, **sub-microgram physical metrology errors become first-order determinants of monitor Design-Value threshold compliance and wildfire Exceptional Event screening**. The **causal physical core** of this study (Pillar 2 and Unification Test `U1`, comparing collocated instruments two meters apart on the exact same air parcel) proves that even after EPA's May 2024 temperature-only software alignment—which alone produced 47 U.S. county 2021–2023 monitor Design-Value threshold reclassifications (18.39M residents, distinct from formal Clean Air Act § 107(d) 5-factor legal designations)—post-alignment `T640_FW` (`636/638`) monitors retain a $+0.468\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ residual Design Value bias, a 9.5-to-1 same-rooftop threshold classification lottery, EIV-robust nonlinear wildfire smoke amplification of $+3.97\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($+10.0\%$) on independent other-site $\ge 25$ days and $+3.02\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($+13.5\%$) under satellite NOAA HMS Heavy plumes, a $2.00\times$ doubling of 24-hour NAAQS level-equivalent daily exceedances ($>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$; 26 vs. 0 discordant days), and a $1.33\times$ surge in EPA Wildfire Tier-1 5-year historical (`2018–2022`) 98th-percentile screening exceedances (42 vs. 0 discordant days, McNemar $p = 4.55 × 10^{-13}$; $1.66\times$ across all paired days)—patterns completely absent in collocated pure mass-based Met One `BAM (170/209)`. Observational EPA chemical speciation regressions ($N = 14,992$) align closely with Mie optical scattering theory and Long, Urbanski, & Lincoln (2023), showing strong positive covariance with organic carbon ($+0.66$, rising to $+0.86$ under HMS Heavy plumes) and relative humidity ($+0.066$ per $+10\%$ RH) and opposite-sign negative covariance with light-absorbing elemental carbon ($-0.94$). Alongside this physical core, Pillar 3 documents a pre-trend-clean $+8.17$ pp anticipatory/designation-window (2022–2023) surge in state 40 CFR § 50.14 flagging (67.5% unconcurred precautionary flags), while `U2` documents positive cross-monitor T640 associations alongside a clean within-county-year flagging null ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.931$), confirming that our evidence does **not** establish that optical bias causally drives strategic EE filing.

Three concrete policy reforms follow immediately:

1. **Controlled Multi-Site Calibration Toward Humidity- and Chemistry-Aware Optical FEM Standards:** Rather than relying solely on ambient temperature, EPA should sponsor controlled multi-site physical calibration and chamber experiments (building on Long, Urbanski, & Lincoln, 2023) to derive and validate causal humidity- and organic-carbon/wildfire-regime correction formulas for optical broadband-spectrometer FEMs—particularly across the Midwest and South where post-alignment winter bias reaches $+0.67\text{–}0.76\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$.
2. **Instrument-Parity Screening Under 40 CFR § 50.14:** Because post-alignment T640 monitors double 24-hour NAAQS level-equivalent daily exceedances ($>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$) and generate 42 vs. 0 discordant EPA Wildfire Tier-1 5-year historical 98th-percentile exceedances relative to gravimetric FRMs under identical Heavy wildfire smoke, EPA's Wildfire Tiering Tool should incorporate instrument-parity adjustments or symmetric weighting of collocated gravimetric/BAM readings so screening eligibility does not hinge on rooftop hardware choice.
3. **Transparent AQS Reporting of Unconcurred vs. Concurred Exclusions:** EPA should revise AQS annual summary extracts so `"Concurred Events Excluded"` rows are generated *only* when concurrence actually modifies observations or annual means, eliminating the 49.9% false-positive reporting artifact and allowing transparent tracking of state precautionary flagging versus binding EPA concurrences.

Five limitations warrant candid emphasis. First, **epistemic scope of `U2` and Pillar 3:** whereas same-rooftop collocated comparisons (Pillar 2 and Unification Test `U1`) identify causal physical instrument bias and screening divergence exactly—comparing two samplers two meters apart on the identical air parcel—cross-monitor deployment in `U2` is purely observational, within-county-year instrument comparisons for EE flagging are null ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.931$), and the primary 2022–2023 EE surge preceded the February 2024 final rule; we therefore explicitly refrain from claiming that optical bias causally induces strategic EE filing. Second, **Design-Value reclassifications vs. § 107(d) designations:** our Pillar 1 counts (47 counties, 18.39M residents) capture 2021–2023 monitor Design-Value threshold reclassifications ($\ge 9.1 \to \le 9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$), not final EPA legal designations, which additionally weigh emissions, meteorology, topography, and jurisdictional boundaries under Clean Air Act § 107(d). Third, **observational chemical speciation:** our CSN/IMPROVE regressions (matched within $25\;\text{km}$, corroborated on exact-$0\;\text{km}$ rooftops, $N = 9,978$) provide observational associations consistent with Mie optical theory rather than causal calibration formulas, which require controlled multi-site chamber and physical-sizing experiments. Fourth, **concurrence right-censoring:** 2024–2025 EPA concurrence counts remain right-censored by EPA's 1–2 year formal Region review queue, which is why we report 2024–2025 nulls transparently rather than extrapolating premature declines. Fifth, **county-level Census demographics:** our Environmental Justice audit links official Census Bureau 2023 SAIPE income/poverty and 2024 PEP race/ethnicity files because `api.census.gov` enforces an API-key gate on keyless requests; future tract-level ACS work can evaluate within-county disparities around DV-reclassified monitors. Even with these caveats, the core message from the causal same-rooftop evidence is unmistakable: under a $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ particulate standard in an era of continental wildfire smoke, atmospheric metrology is regulatory policy.
