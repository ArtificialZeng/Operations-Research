## Abstract

When the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency tightened the primary annual fine particulate matter (PM$_{2.5}$) National Ambient Air Quality Standard from $12.0$ to $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ in February 2024 (89 FR 16202), regulatory compliance collided with a nationwide shift toward optical broadband-spectrometer monitors (Teledyne API T640/T640x) and intensifying North American wildfire smoke. Combining stitched 40 CFR Part 50 Appendix N monitor histories (repairing 410 mid-2023 split-year T640 records), 63,951 same-rooftop collocated T640–FRM daily pairs (69,224 through May 2026), EPA aerosol speciation and meteorology, Census demographics, and eight full calendar years of satellite NOAA Hazard Mapping System (HMS) smoke polygons, we establish four linked findings. First, EPA's May 2024 software alignment of T640/T640x monitors alone shifted 47 U.S. counties (18.39 million residents; 49 counties / 19.87M including long-term SPMs) from nonattainment to attainment in 2021–2023 without removing a single microgram of ambient pollution—concentrated overwhelmingly in the Eastern and Midwestern United States (46/47 counties, including 17.0% population-weighted Black residents vs. 12.9% in cleanly attaining counties, $p = 0.016$)—while exogenous gravimetric FRM concentrations (+0.007 $\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.951$), longitudinal FEM-to-FRM switching (-0.030, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.287$), and monitor survival (+0.026, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.358$) exhibit clean nulls. Second, post-alignment firmware T640_FW (636/638) monitors retain a persistent $+0.468\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ same-rooftop Design Value bias ($t = +9.17$, $N = 178$ rooftops), creating a 9.5-to-1 statutory nonattainment lottery (19 vs. 2 rooftops, exact binomial $p = 0.0002$; strict 4-quarter $\times$ 3-year stitched 18 vs. 3, $p = 0.001$). Third, using three strictly errors-in-variables-free instruments, post-alignment T640_FW bias amplifies nonlinearly during wildfire smoke by $+3.97\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($+10.0\%$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$) on independent other-site $\ge 25\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ days and $+3.02\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($+13.5\%$, $t = +12.88$) under satellite NOAA HMS Heavy plumes, whereas mass-based Beta Attenuation Monitors (BAM 170/209) show zero other-site smoke bias ($+0.51\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.694$). Direct EPA chemical speciation ($N = 14,992$ paired days) identifies the exact Mie-scattering mechanism: T640_FW over-responds to light-scattering organic carbon ($+0.657\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ per $\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ OC, $t = +6.40$; escalating by an additional $+0.287$ under HMS Heavy smoke, $t = +2.38$) and relative humidity ($+0.066$ per $+10\%$ RH, $t = +3.13$), while under-responding to light-absorbing elemental carbon ($-0.936$, $t = -3.93$)—a textbook optical fingerprint completely absent in BAM. Fourth, this optical amplification directly triggers regulatory triage under 40 CFR § 50.14: sitting two meters from collocated FRMs under identical NOAA HMS Heavy smoke, T640_FW monitors double federal $>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ daily exceedance triggers (52 vs. 26, 26 vs. 0 discordant days, McNemar $p = 2.98 × 10^{-8}$; BAM $p = 0.688$), and inside the newly binding exogenous 2018–2020 $[8.5, 11.5]\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ band, state Exceptional Event flagging surges $+8.17$ pp (State-clustered $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.004$, within-state randomization inference $p = 0.0010$)—67.5% of which consists of unconcurred precautionary state flags—with T640 $\times \log(\text{HMS Heavy})$ driving $+15.2$ pp higher flagging ($t = +4.11$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$) and $+0.250\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ larger claimed exclusions ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$).

## 1. Introduction

Ambient fine particulate matter (PM$_{2.5}$) remains the largest environmental mortality risk factor in the United States, with substantial health benefits documented even below historic federal thresholds (Di et al., 2017; Chay & Greenstone, 2005; Greenstone, 2002). On February 7, 2024, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) finalized a landmark tightening of the primary annual PM$_{2.5}$ National Ambient Air Quality Standard (NAAQS) from $12.0$ to $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, effective May 6, 2024 (U.S. EPA, 2024a, 89 FR 16202). Under the Clean Air Act, counties whose three-year Appendix N Design Value exceeds $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ (rounded to one decimal place, i.e., $\ge 9.1\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$) face mandatory nonattainment designation, triggering State Implementation Plans (SIPs), strict industrial emission offsets, and transportation conformity sanctions (40 CFR Part 50, Appendix N; Auffhammer, Bento, & Lowe, 2009; Lu, 2026).

Yet regulatory compliance under a tight $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ standard depends as critically on *how* air quality is measured as on physical emissions themselves (Zou, 2021; Grainger & Schreiber, 2019; Fowlie, Rubin, & Walker, 2019). Between 2018 and 2025, state and local air agencies rapidly replaced intermittent filter-based gravimetric Federal Reference Method (FRM) samplers with continuous optical broadband-spectrometer Federal Equivalent Method (FEM) monitors—predominantly the Teledyne API Model T640 and T640x—whose network footprint expanded more than fourfold to over 550 active sites. Field comparisons documented substantial positive bias in unaligned T640 units relative to collocated gravimetric FRMs (Long, Urbanski, & Lincoln, 2023), prompting EPA in May 2024 to issue an unprecedented network-wide software alignment introducing firmware-aligned method codes (`636/638`) and retroactively adjusted historical series (`736/738`) (U.S. EPA, 2024b). Simultaneously, climate-driven North American wildfire smoke reversed decades of U.S. PM$_{2.5}$ progress (Burke et al., 2023; Childs et al., 2022), placing unprecedented pressure on EPA's Exceptional Events Rule (40 CFR § 50.14), which allows agencies to exclude smoke-influenced monitoring days only when those exclusions carry "regulatory significance" for attainment status (Jaffe et al., 2026; Krupnick et al., 2025).

This paper provides the first unified physical-to-regulatory forensic evaluation connecting optical aerosol metrology, EPA's 2024 T640 alignment, chemical speciation, and 40 CFR § 50.14 Exceptional Event triage across the entire United States through May 2026. Rather than treating measurement error and administrative data exclusions as separate phenomena, we show that both arise from a single coupled mechanism: broadband optical light-scattering instruments systematically over-estimate fine-mode organic carbon and hygroscopic aerosols relative to gravimetric mass, distorting statutory attainment boundaries on both clean-air annual averages and extreme wildfire smoke days.

Our analysis makes four distinct contributions to environmental economics, atmospheric chemistry, and regulatory design:

1. **Exact Stitched Appendix N Accounting, Official EPA Reconciliation, and Environmental Justice Audit (Pillar 1):** Previous administrative audits suffer from mid-2023 T640 split-year truncation—where retroactive `736/738` data sit on separate Parameter Occurrence Codes (`POC + 20`) from post-upgrade `636/638` firmware records—and from non-regulatory Special Purpose or Industrial monitor contamination. Stitching 410 split-year 2023 T640 monitors and implementing 40 CFR Part 50 Appendix N § 3.0(d) Combined-Site daily records brings our regulatory counts into close agreement with all five official EPA Final PM$_{2.5}$ Design Value workbooks (2019–2021 through 2023–2025), including 98/99 (99.0% precision) overlap with EPA's published 2021–2023 nonattainment roster. EPA's May 2024 T640 alignment alone flipped 47 regulatory counties home to 18.39 million Americans to attainment in 2021–2023 (49 counties / 19.87M including long-term NAAQS-comparable SPMs), with 46/47 flipped counties located outside the West and carrying significantly higher Black resident shares than cleanly attaining counties ($p = 0.016$). Crucially, using strictly exogenous 2018–2020 Design Values, we confirm dead-zero early-compliance effects on true gravimetric FRM PM$_{2.5}$ ($+0.007\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.951$), longitudinal FEM-to-FRM switching, and monitor pruning—demonstrating that naive comparisons using overlapping 2021–2023 Design Values suffer from mechanical regression-to-the-mean.
2. **Same-Rooftop Optical Lottery and Zero-EIV Wildfire Smoke Amplification (Pillar 2A):** Using 63,951 same-rooftop daily collocated T640_FW–FRM pairs across 234 sites in 2023–2025 (69,224 pairs through May 2026), we show EPA's firmware alignment leaves a highly significant $+0.468\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ 3-year Design Value bias ($t = +9.17$, $p = 1.20 × 10^{-16}$) that creates a 9.5-to-1 statutory nonattainment lottery (19 rooftops where T640_FW $\ge 9.1$ while collocated FRM $\le 9.0$ vs. only 2 in reverse, exact binomial $p = 0.0002$). Eliminating mathematical coupling (errors-in-variables from conditioning on FRM itself) via three strictly independent instruments—other-site county PM$_{2.5}$, Bland & Altman (1986) pair means, and external satellite NOAA HMS smoke polygons—we prove that T640_FW bias explodes nonlinearly by $+3.97\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($+10.0\%$) on high-smoke days and $+3.02\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($+13.5\%$) under satellite Heavy smoke, while mass-based BAM monitors remain statistically flat on other-site smoke days ($+0.51\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.694$).
3. **Direct Chemical Speciation and Hygroscopicity Mechanism (Pillar 2B):** Merging EPA daily Chemical Speciation Network (CSN/IMPROVE) measurements ($\text{NO}_3^-$, $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$, OC, EC) and ambient meteorology (RH, temperature) onto 14,992 T640_FW–FRM pairs and 7,333 BAM–FRM pairs, we uncover the exact physical signature of Mie light scattering: T640_FW over-reads by $+0.657\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ per $\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ of organic carbon ($t = +6.40$, rising to $+0.864$ under satellite HMS Heavy smoke) and $+0.066\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ per $+10\%$ relative humidity ($t = +3.13$), while *under-reading* light-absorbing elemental carbon by $-0.936\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($t = -3.93$). Heated-inlet BAM monitors display null sensitivity to OC ($t = +1.34$) and EC ($t = -0.97$) and opposite-sign responses to RH ($-0.106$, $t = -2.76$) and semi-volatile nitrate ($-0.358$, $t = -7.40$).
4. **Pre-Trend-Clean 40 CFR § 50.14 Triage and the Optical-to-Flagging Multiplier (Pillar 3 & Unification):** Correcting a 49.9% false-positive EPA-concurrence coding artifact in AQS annual summaries and standardizing all difference-in-differences tests onto pre-trend-clean exogenous 2018–2020 Design Values ($[8.5, 11.5]\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$), we document a $+8.17$ pp monitor-year surge ($+9.96$ pp county-year) in state Exceptional Event flagging (State-clustered $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.004$, within-state randomization inference $p = 0.0010$), concentrated in the 2022–2023 designation window with transparent 2024–2025 EPA-concurred nulls reflecting lower eastern smoke and administrative concurrence lags. Unifying Pillars 2 and 3 directly, we show T640_FW doubles same-rooftop $>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ exceedance triggers under NOAA HMS Heavy smoke (2.00$\times$, 26 vs. 0 discordant days, McNemar $p = 2.98 × 10^{-8}$) and drives $+15.2$ pp higher flagging per log Heavy smoke day ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$) and $+0.250\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ larger claimed exclusions ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$) inside the binding 40 CFR § 50.14 band.

## 2. Institutional Setting and Formal Regulatory Metrology Model

### 2.1 Regulatory Architecture: Appendix N, T640 Alignment, and 40 CFR § 50.14

Under 40 CFR Part 50, Appendix N, annual PM$_{2.5}$ compliance is governed by each regulatory monitoring site's three-year Design Value—the unweighted mean of three consecutive annual means, where each annual mean averages four quarterly means requiring at least 75% quarterly scheduled sample completeness (with site-level daily combining across primary and collocated regulatory samplers under § 3.0(d) and limited substitution under § 4.1(c)). Design Values are rounded to one decimal place; under EPA's February 2024 Final Rule (89 FR 16202), any county containing a valid regulatory site with $\text{DV} \ge 9.1\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ violates the $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ standard. Because state designation recommendations rely on the most recent three complete calendar years (2021–2023 upon promulgation, updated through 2022–2024 and 2023–2025 prior to final designations), the 2022–2023 wildfire seasons sat squarely inside the statutory designation window.

Two institutional mechanisms interacted sharply with this $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ threshold. First, following EPA's May 2024 T640 alignment decision (U.S. EPA, 2024b), Teledyne deployed a firmware revision converting optical broadband-scattering outputs using an ambient-temperature linear adjustment (`method_code = 636/638`), while EPA retroactively generated aligned historical daily data (`method_code = 736/738`) on separate AQS Parameter Occurrence Codes (`POC + 20`) to supersede raw pre-alignment series (`236/238`). Second, under the 2016 Exceptional Events Rule (40 CFR § 50.14), state agencies may flag wildfire-influenced monitoring days in AQS and submit formal technical demonstrations requesting EPA concurrence for exclusion—explicitly conditioned on **regulatory significance**, meaning EPA prioritizes and concurs with demonstrations capable of altering a NAAQS designation or attainment determination (Jaffe et al., 2026; Krupnick et al., 2025).

### 2.2 Formal Equilibrium Model of Optical Bias and Threshold Triage

To generate sharp testable hypotheses across all three empirical pillars, consider monitor $i$ in county $c$ observed on day $t \in \{1,\dots,T\}$. True ambient gravimetric fine particulate mass is $M_{it} = M^0_{it} + S_{it} \ge 0$ ($\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$), where $M^0_{it}$ is background PM$_{2.5}$ with ammonium-nitrate mass fraction $\nu_{it} \in [0,1]$ and ambient relative humidity $h_{it} \in [0,1]$, and $S_{it} \ge 0$ is wildfire-smoke organic-carbon aerosol mass. A gravimetric FRM observes $\hat{M}^{\text{FRM}}_{it} = M_{it} + \varepsilon^{\text{FRM}}_{it}$ with $\mathbb{E}[\varepsilon^{\text{FRM}}_{it}] = 0$. A post-alignment optical T640 spectrometer converts scattered broadband light via a fixed refractive-index and size-distribution model, yielding conditional daily measurement bias:

$$b^{\text{T640}}_{it} \equiv \mathbb{E}\Big[\hat{M}^{\text{T640}}_{it} - \hat{M}^{\text{FRM}}_{it} \mid \mathbf{X}_{it}\Big] = \beta_0 + \beta_N \nu_{it} M^0_{it} f(h_{it}) + \beta_S S_{it} + \gamma_S S_{it}^2$$

where $\beta_N > 0$ captures hygroscopic aerosol swelling ($f'(h) > 0$) unmodeled by EPA's temperature-only linear alignment, and $\beta_S > 0, \gamma_S \ge 0$ capture superlinear fine-mode organic-carbon Mie-scattering over-response on wildfire smoke days (whereas mass-based beta attenuation satisfies $\beta_S^{\text{BAM}} = \gamma_S^{\text{BAM}} = 0$).

Let $\bar{M}_i^{\text{FRM}}$ denote the monitor's three-year true Appendix N Design Value, $\bar{b}_i \equiv \mathbb{E}[b^{\text{T640}}_{it}]$ its mean residual optical bias, and $\bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}} = \bar{S}_i + \bar{b}_i^S$ the annual-mean smoke mass recorded by the instrument (including optical smoke amplification $\bar{b}_i^S \equiv \mathbb{E}[\beta_S S_{it} + \gamma_S S_{it}^2]$). Facing statutory cutoff $\tau \in \{12.0, 9.0\}$, nonattainment compliance cost $L > 0$, convex 40 CFR § 50.14 demonstration cost $\frac{c}{2} e^2$ ($c > 0$) for flagging intensity $e \in [0,1]$, and effective regulatory option value $\tilde{\rho} = \rho + (1-\rho)\omega \in (0,1]$ combining EPA concurrence probability $\rho$ and precautionary option value $\omega$, the state air agency chooses $e \in [0,1]$ to maximize:

$$U_i(e) = L \tilde{\rho}\; \Phi\!\left(\frac{\tau - (\bar{M}_i^{\text{FRM}} + \bar{b}_i - e \bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}})}{\sigma}\right) - \frac{c}{2} e^2$$

where $\Phi(\cdot)$ and $\phi(\cdot)$ are the standard normal CDF and PDF with attainment-noise scale $\sigma > 0$. Any interior optimum $e^*_i$ satisfies the first-order condition:

$$c\, e^*_i = \frac{L \tilde{\rho}\, \bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}}}{\sigma}\; \phi\!\left(\frac{\tau - \text{DV}_i(e^*_i)}{\sigma}\right), \qquad \text{DV}_i(e) = \bar{M}_i^{\text{FRM}} + \bar{b}_i - e \bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}}.$$

Numerical verification across calibrated EPA PM$_{2.5}$ parameters confirms exact dimensionally consistent concavity ($\partial^2 U_i / \partial e^2 = -(L \tilde{\rho} (\bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}})^2 / \sigma^2)\, z \phi(z) - c < 0$ everywhere, matching numerical central finite differences to $< 2 \times 10^{-5}$) and yields three closed-form empirical predictions:

- **Proposition 1 (Optical Nonattainment Lottery & Hygroscopic Regional Skew):** Under cutoff $\tau$, any rooftop with $\bar{M}_i^{\text{FRM}} \in (\tau - \bar{b}_i, \tau]$ is classified nonattainment by T640 and attainment by collocated FRM. Because $\partial \bar{b}_i / \partial(\nu_i f(h_i)) = \beta_N M^0_i > 0$, post-alignment discordance odds skew sharply positive (calibrated Midwest/Winter odds ratio $97.1\times$, regional bias ratio $2.82\times$) and concentrate in high-humidity, high-nitrate Eastern/Midwestern regimes relative to arid Western sites.
- **Proposition 2 (Inverted-U 40 CFR § 50.14 Triage & Alignment Bailout Dampening):** Lowering $\tau$ from $12.0$ to $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ shifts the density peak $\phi((\tau - \text{DV}_i)/\sigma)$ into the newly binding band. Consequently, the filing surge $\Delta e^*_i = e^*_i(9.0) - e^*_i(12.0)$ follows an **inverted-U** in pre-rule $\bar{M}_i^{\text{FRM}}$—near zero for cleanly attaining counties ($\bar{M}_i < 8.0$), peaking inside $[8.5, 11.5]$ (calibrated peak minus clean $+0.317$), and declining for deeply nonattainment counties ($> 12.0$)—while administrative T640 alignment bailouts reduce $\bar{b}_i$ and dampen triage incentives.
- **Proposition 3 (Optical-to-Regulatory Smoke Amplification Multiplier):** On wildfire smoke days ($S_{it} > 0$), nonlinear T640 smoke over-response ($\beta_S > 0, \gamma_S \ge 0$) acts through two reinforcing channels: (i) an *exceedance-trigger multiplier* pushing marginal smoke plumes above federal daily screening thresholds ($>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ and site 98th percentiles; calibrated $+40.4$ pp excess trigger at $S=25$), absent in BAM; and (ii) a *filing-return multiplier* raising both $\bar{S}_i^{\text{meas}}$ and statutory cutoff pressure inside the newly binding band ($\partial e^*_i / \partial \bar{b}_i^S > 0$, calibrated effort gain $+0.131$).

## 3. Data Architecture, Forensic Repairs, and Exact Econometric Methods

### 3.1 Integrated Multi-Source Microdata

We assemble a national regulatory and atmospheric-chemistry microdata warehouse spanning 2018 through May 2026 across five integrated federal data streams:

1. **EPA Air Quality System (AQS) PM$_{2.5}$ Daily & Annual Files (`88101`, 2018–May 2026):** All 24-hour and 24-hour block-average records across all 50 states and the District of Columbia, linked to `aqs_monitors.zip` and `aqs_sites.zip` metadata (`Monitor Type`, `Monitoring Objective`, `NAAQS Primary Monitor`, `First Year of Data`, and coordinates).
2. **Official EPA Final PM$_{2.5}$ Design Value Workbooks (2019–2021 through 2023–2025):** All five published EPA `Table 4a` county-level Annual Design Value workbooks from `epa.gov/air-trends/air-quality-design-values`, including the June 12, 2026 final release for 2023–2025.
3. **EPA AQS Chemical Speciation (`daily_SPEC`, 2023–2025) & Meteorology (`daily_TEMP`, `daily_RH_DP`, 2023–2025):** Daily total nitrate (`88306`, $\text{NO}_3^-$), sulfate (`88403`, $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$), thermal-optical reflectance organic carbon (`88320`/`88370`, OC) and elemental carbon (`88321`/`88380`, EC), crustal soil (`88348`), outdoor temperature (`62101`, converted to $^\circ\text{C}$), and relative humidity (`62201`, %). Each collocated pair is matched first to the exact same rooftop ($0\;\text{km}$) and otherwise to the nearest active speciation or meteorological site within $25\;\text{km}$ in the same county-day (with exact-$0\;\text{km}$ sensitivity verified separately).
4. **External Satellite NOAA HMS Wildfire Smoke Plume Polygons (2018–2025):** Daily analyst-verified satellite smoke polygons (`Light`, `Medium`, `Heavy`) downloaded across **100.0% of calendar days** (`2,922/2,922` days, zero HTTP 404s, zero errors) and spatially intersected via `shapely` `STRtree` against all 2,272 unique EPA PM$_{2.5}$ monitoring sites, yielding 1,471,932 smoke site-days (92,299 Heavy smoke site-days).
5. **Official U.S. Census Bureau County Demographics (3,144 Counties, 340.11M Residents):** Official Census Bureau 2023 Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates (SAIPE, median household income and all-ages poverty rate) linked with 2024 Census Population Estimates Program county race/ethnicity characteristics (PEP ASRH, non-Hispanic Black, Hispanic, and non-White shares; used cleanly in place of the API-key-gated `api.census.gov` endpoint) plus `co-est2024-alldata.csv` 2024 county populations.

### 3.2 Four Critical Forensic Repairs

Preliminary audits uncovered four severe data and econometric traps in raw EPA AQS files that would bias naive analyses if left uncorrected:

1. **Mid-2023 T640 Split-Year Stitching, Non-Diluted EE Aggregation, & Instrument Purity:** In 2023, EPA posted retroactively aligned `736/738` data on `POC + 20` through mid-year while firmware `636/638` resumed on the original POC post-upgrade. We deterministically stitch 451/452 `POC - 20` retroactive monitors plus non-$+20$ transitions, repairing 410 split-year 2023 T640 monitors into continuous physical histories while avoiding observation-count dilution when aggregating Exceptional Event reductions across overlapping `POC` and `POC + 20` annual rows. We also filter strictly to regulatory Appendix N monitor types (`SLAMS`, `TRIBAL`, `EPA`, `NON-EPA FEDERAL`, excluding `SPM`, `INDUSTRIAL`, `OTHER`, `QA COLLOCATED`, and `QUALITY ASSURANCE` objectives—removing 281 non-regulatory monitors), while presenting long-term `SPM` sensitivity transparently, and restrict our mass-based `BAM` benchmark strictly to pure Met One BAM-1020/1022 (`170/209`) so optical-nephelometer hybrid Thermo 5030 SHARP (`184`) and Thermo 5014i (`183`) units cannot contaminate the non-optical control group.
2. **Elimination of the 49.9% EPA-Concurrence False-Positive Artifact:** EPA's `annual_conc_by_monitor` files populate a `"Concurred Events Excluded"` row whenever *any* exceptional event is flagged—even when EPA concurred with zero events, leaving `Observation Count` and `Arithmetic Mean` identical to `"Events Included"`. Across 2018–2025, only 597 of 1192 concurrence rows (50.1%) reflect actual exclusions. We code `true_ee_conc = 1` strictly iff `obs_conc < obs_incl` or $|\text{mean}_{\text{incl}} - \text{mean}_{\text{conc}}| > 10^{-6}$, cleanly decomposing all state flags (`true_ee_flag`, $N = 1257$) into unconcurred precautionary state flags (`unconc_flag`, $N = 660$) and true EPA-concurred exclusions (`true_ee_conc`, $N = 597$).
3. **Permanent Elimination of Errors-in-Variables (EIV) Coupling in Smoke Regressions:** Conditioning daily bias $(\text{FEM} - \text{FRM})$ on $\mathbb{I}(\text{FRM} \ge 25)$ mechanically induces negative coupling $\text{Cov}(-\varepsilon^{\text{FRM}}, \mathbb{I}(\text{FRM} \ge 25)) < 0$, attenuating T640 smoke spikes and creating spurious negative BAM bias. We permanently abandon FRM conditioning in favor of three EIV-free instruments: leave-one-site-out other-site county PM$_{2.5}$, Bland-Altman pair means, and external satellite NOAA HMS plumes.
4. **Exact Nested-FE Degrees of Freedom & LSDV-Equivalent Wild Cluster Bootstrap:** In two-way panels with nested fixed effects such as `[Monitor/County, State × Year]`, naive dummy counting under-subtracts redundant state components ($S$ connected components rather than 1). Our econometric engine (`scripts/econ_utils.py`) computes exact bipartite connected-component rank via Union-Find, absorbs high-dimensional FEs via Frisch-Waugh-Lovell alternating projections to tolerance $10^{-13}$, and executes Roodman et al. (2019) restricted Wild Cluster Bootstrap (Rademacher weights, $B = 999$, re-demeaning at $10^{-12}$)—verified in automated unit tests to match explicit brute-force LSDV WCB to $\text{atol} = 10^{-12}$ (Cameron, Gelbach, & Miller, 2008; Abadie et al., 2023).

### 6.2 The Killer Unification: Linking Optical Smoke Amplification Directly to 40 CFR § 50.14 Triage (`U1`, `U2`, `U3`)

Does Pillar 2's nonlinear optical smoke bias actually propagate into Pillar 3's regulatory Exceptional Event triage? Three direct unification tests—summarized in Table 5 and Figure 5—prove **Proposition 3** definitively:

- **Unification Test U1 (Same-Rooftop Daily $>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ Exceedance Trigger Multiplier):** Across collocated rooftops on identical days, `T640_FW` crosses the federal $35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ 24-hour NAAQS and Tier-1 Exceptional Event visibility threshold **1.86$\times$ more often** than the collocated FRM sitting two meters away (210 vs. 113 days; 115 vs. 18 discordant days, exact McNemar $p = 1.72 × 10^{-18}$). On **satellite NOAA HMS Heavy smoke days**, `T640_FW` **exactly doubles $>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ exceedance triggers** (52 vs. 26, 11.02% vs. 5.51%, **26 vs. 0 discordant days—zero in the reverse direction—exact McNemar $p = 2.98 × 10^{-8}$**, paired diff $+5.51$ pp, $t = +5.12$), and likewise jumps **1.50$\times$** on site 98th-percentile Tier-1/Tier-2 screening exceedances (56 vs. 1, McNemar $p = 8.05 × 10^{-16}$). On independent other-site $\ge 25\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ days, the trigger ratio is **1.94$\times$ (51 vs. 2 discordant days, $p = 3.18 × 10^{-13}$)**. Across every single stratum, **collocated pure Met One BAM (`170/209`) monitors show zero excess exceedance triggers** (in fact slightly *fewer* exceedances than FRM across all days, 198 vs. 217, Site-clustered $t = -1.67$, and null under HMS Heavy $p = 0.688$ and other-site $\ge 25$ $p = 0.648$).
- **Unification Test U2 (T640 Instrument Deployment Drives Excess EE Exclusions Under Identical Satellite Smoke):** Restricting strictly inside borderline newly binding counties ($\text{DV}_{18\text{–}20} \in [8.5, 11.5]$, 2022–2025) and controlling for `State × Year FE`, exact satellite NOAA HMS smoke-day counts (`Light/Medium/Heavy` + quadratic), and $\log(\text{valid days})$—crucially omitting ground PM$_{2.5}$ because ground PM$_{2.5}$ is the optical mediator itself, and comparing T640 against pure gravimetric FRM and pure mass-based Met One BAM (`170/209`)—**T640 monitors claim $+0.250\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ larger annual-mean EE exclusions than FRM/BAM monitors facing identical satellite smoke plumes (County $t = +5.22$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$; State $t = +3.91$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$)**. Moreover, interacting instrument type with heavy satellite smoke isolates the exact nonlinear smoke channel: **`is_T640 × log(1 + HMS Heavy Days)` increases state EE flagging probability by $+15.19$ pp per log Heavy smoke day (County $t = +4.11$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.002$; State $t = +3.61$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.016$)**, offsetting a negative zero-smoke intercept ($-11.93$ pp, $p = 0.057$) so that excess T640 flagging emerges specifically on multi-day Heavy smoke episodes (whereas un-interacted baseline T640 main effects are null, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.453$, and within-county-year FE is null, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.931$, reflecting coordinated county-wide agency flagging across collocated sites).
- **Unification Test U3 (Statutory Triage vs. Precautionary Flagging via Satellite NOAA HMS):** Satellite validation across all 748 EE-flagged regulatory monitor-years in 2022–2025 confirms that state filings overwhelmingly track real wildfire smoke—**99.7%** experienced NOAA HMS smoke overhead and **97.7%** experienced Medium or Heavy smoke—ruling out fabricated non-smoke gaming. Estimating a fully saturated stratified DiD (explicitly including the pre-period interaction `Borderline × Heavy HMS`, which absorbs pre-rule Western clean-county fire episodes at $-5.73$ pp, $t = -2.72$) reveals a clear regulatory divide: **85.8%** of EPA-concurred exclusions had satellite Heavy smoke backing, and true EPA-concurred DiD surges **strictly in Heavy-smoke years ($+3.39$ pp, $t = +2.37$, $p = 0.018$)** while remaining statistically null in Non-Heavy years ($+1.94$ pp, $p = 0.349$). By contrast, only **66.1%** of unconcurred state flags had Heavy smoke backing, and unconcurred state flagging rises across both Heavy ($+4.36$ pp, $t = +2.48$, $p = 0.014$) and marginally Non-Heavy years ($+3.50$ pp, $t = +1.73$, $p = 0.084$), driving total `true_ee_flag` to $+7.75$ pp ($t = +3.81$) in Heavy smoke years vs. $+5.44$ pp ($p = 0.066$) in Non-Heavy years. Thus Pillar 3 reflects **lawful 40 CFR § 50.14 statutory triage on intense smoke episodes combined with precautionary state over-flagging and amplified by T640 optical inflation**.

## 7. Policy Implications, Limitations, and Conclusion

Our findings reveal an overlooked structural vulnerability in modern environmental regulation: as statutory air quality thresholds tighten into the $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ regime, **sub-microgram physical metrology errors cease to be technical footnotes and become first-order determinants of federal nonattainment designations, industrial permitting burdens, and state administrative triage**. EPA's May 2024 T640 alignment eliminated the primary pre-alignment raw offset—single-handedly shifting 47 U.S. counties (18.39M residents) into attainment—yet our same-rooftop speciation and satellite-smoke forensics demonstrate that EPA's temperature-only linear adjustment leaves behind a $+0.468\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ residual bias, a 9.5-to-1 statutory nonattainment lottery, a $+3.97\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ ($+10.0\%$) nonlinear wildfire smoke over-response driven by organic carbon Mie scattering ($+0.66$ per $\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ OC, rising to $+0.86$ under Heavy HMS plumes) and humidity swelling ($+0.066$ per $+10\%$ RH), and a $2.00\times$ doubling of daily $>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ regulatory triggers that propagates directly into 40 CFR § 50.14 Exceptional Event flagging.

Three concrete policy reforms follow immediately:

1. **Humidity- and Chemistry-Aware Optical FEM Calibration:** Rather than relying solely on ambient temperature, EPA's FEM equivalency testing and T640 alignment should incorporate explicit relative-humidity hygroscopicity corrections and multi-wavelength or organic-carbon wildfire-regime adjustments—particularly across the Midwest and South where residual winter bias reaches $+0.67\text{–}0.76\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$.
2. **Instrument-Parity Screening Under 40 CFR § 50.14:** Because post-alignment T640 monitors double daily $>35\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ exceedances relative to gravimetric FRMs under identical Heavy wildfire smoke, EPA's Tier-1/Tier-2 Exceptional Event screening tools should adjust optical monitor exceedances or weight collocated gravimetric/BAM readings symmetrically so statutory nonattainment does not depend on rooftop hardware choice.
3. **Transparent AQS Reporting of Unconcurred vs. Concurred Exclusions:** EPA should revise AQS annual summary extracts so `"Concurred Events Excluded"` rows are generated *only* when concurrence actually modifies observations or annual means, eliminating the 49.9% false-positive reporting artifact and allowing transparent tracking of state precautionary flagging versus binding EPA concurrences.

Four limitations warrant candid emphasis. First, while same-rooftop collocated comparisons (Pillar 2 and Unification Test U1) identify causal physical instrument bias exactly—comparing two samplers two meters apart on the exact same air parcel—cross-monitor deployment in Unification Test U2 is observational; although controlling for `State × Year FE` and satellite NOAA HMS plumes rules out regional smoke confounding, within-county-year comparisons lack power because state agencies typically flag entire counties simultaneously. Second, 2024–2025 EPA concurrence counts remain right-censored by EPA's 1–2 year formal review queue, which is why we report 2024–2025 nulls transparently rather than extrapolating premature declines. Third, chemical speciation matching uses nearest within-$25\;\text{km}$ county CSN/IMPROVE sites where rooftop speciation is absent, though our strict exact-$0\;\text{km}$ rooftop subsample ($N = 9,978$) reproduces the organic-carbon ($+0.747$, $t = +6.00$), elemental-carbon ($-1.204$), and relative-humidity ($+0.068$) mechanisms cleanly. Fourth, our Environmental Justice audit links official Census Bureau 2023 SAIPE income/poverty and 2024 PEP race/ethnicity files because `api.census.gov` enforces an API-key gate on keyless requests; future tract-level ACS work can evaluate within-county disparities around T640-flipped monitors. Even with these caveats, the core message is unmistakable: under a $9.0\;\mu\text{g}/\text{m}^3$ particulate standard in an era of continental wildfire smoke, atmospheric metrology is regulatory policy.
