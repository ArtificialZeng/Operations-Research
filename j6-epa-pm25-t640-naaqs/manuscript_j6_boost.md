## Abstract

In 2024, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) lowered the primary annual PM₂.₅ National Ambient Air Quality Standard (NAAQS) from 12.0 to 9.0 µg/m³, issued firmware-alignment revisions for Teledyne API Model T640/T640x optical monitors (AQS method codes 636/638), and published its April 2024 *PM₂.₅ Wildland Fire Exceptional Events Tiering Document* (EPA-457/R-24-001). Here we evaluate how residual optical–gravimetric divergence in post-alignment field networks alters site-month Tier-1 screening classifications when identical regulatory rules are applied to collocated instruments measuring the same air parcel. Across 63,951 same-site, same-day aligned T640_FW–Federal Reference Method (FRM) pairs (234 monitoring sites, 2023–2025) and 22,927 background-reference beta attenuation monitor (BAM 170/209)–FRM pairs (107 sites), a formal common-support audit reveals that the two networks share only 4 same-site, same-day triple observations (27 overlapping dates) under NOAA Hazard Mapping System (HMS) Heavy smoke and diverge sharply in EPA regional coverage (23.9% of BAM Heavy pairs occur in Regions 9–10, where T640 has zero Heavy pairs). We therefore treat each instrument's within-site pairing against its own collocated FRM as primary and retain BAM strictly as a background reference benchmark. Replaying EPA's official published 2021–2025 site-month Tier-1 thresholds on 468 threshold-matched T640_FW–FRM Heavy-smoke pairs (165 sites), T640_FW triggers 123 Tier-1 crossings versus 89 on collocated FRM (36 T640-only vs. 2 FRM-only; paired difference +7.26 percentage points [pp], 95% CI [+4.15, +10.38] across 27 national smoke-date blocks; [+3.43, +11.10], $p = 0.0004$ under two-way Site × Region-Smoke-Episode clustering across 55 geographically distinct regional episodes), with consistent yearly point estimates (+6.77, +6.41, and +8.08 pp in 2023–2025) and positive leave-one-EPA-region-out stability ([+6.33, +9.15] pp). Crucially, strictly out-of-time prospective rolling 5-year reconstructions following EPA's exact Appendix N formula and May 1 certification calendar (2018–2022 → 2023, 2019–2023 → 2024, 2020–2024 → 2025) confirm unidirectional excess Tier-1 triggering across all four flag-exclusion specifications (+5.57 to +7.07 pp; 26–33 T640-only vs. 0 FRM-only; all two-way $p \le 0.002$), whereas background BAM–FRM remains near zero (+0.00 pp official; +0.00 to +0.52 pp out-of-time) even after restricting to overlapping dates, regions, and concentration bins (overlap-weighted differential +10.42 pp, bootstrap 95% CI [+1.43, +18.80]). Concentration-matched non-smoke controls (HMS = 0) and collocated chemical speciation reveal two distinct physical layers: residual baseline optical bias creates modest two-sided threshold sensitivity on elevated non-smoke days (+3.09 to +9.02 pp at 15–25 µg/m³), but confirmed ground-level wildfire smoke (surface PM₂.₅ anomaly > site-month p90 and Organic Carbon ≥ 5 µg/m³) amplifies T640_FW bias three- to fivefold (+5.18 to +7.98 µg/m³ vs. -2.81 to +1.66 µg/m³ non-smoke, all $p < 10^{-4}$) and drives steeply one-sided Tier-1 discordance near the decision boundary. These field findings quantify instrument-induced sensitivity at the initial Tier-1 screening stage; they do not evaluate subsequent agency filings, EPA concurrences, or Clean Air Act area designations.

**Synopsis:** Post-alignment optical PM₂.₅ monitors exhibit residual smoke-amplified positive bias relative to collocated gravimetric reference samplers, increasing EPA Tier-1 wildland fire screening crossings by +5.6 to +7.3 percentage points across retrospective and strictly out-of-time 5-year thresholds.

## 1. Introduction

Wildland fire smoke has become a dominant driver of extreme fine particulate matter (PM₂.₅) episodes across North America, eroding long-term clean-air gains and elevating acute mortality risk even below historical regulatory thresholds (Di et al., 2017; Childs et al., 2022; Burke et al., 2023; Jaffe et al., 2026). In February 2024, the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) strengthened the primary annual PM₂.₅ National Ambient Air Quality Standard (NAAQS) from 12.0 to 9.0 µg/m³ (U.S. EPA, 2024a; 89 FR 16202). Because marginal annual averages near 9.0 µg/m³ can be pushed above compliance by a small number of severe smoke days, the Exceptional Events Rule (40 CFR § 50.14) plays an increasingly consequential role in regulatory air-quality accounting (Krupnick et al., 2025). To streamline clear-causal-relationship demonstrations for wildland fires, EPA issued its April 2024 *PM₂.₅ Wildland Fire Exceptional Events Tiering Document* (EPA-457/R-24-001; U.S. EPA, 2024b), which assigns candidate event days to Tier 1 (streamlined evidence), Tier 2 (intermediate evidence), or Tier 3 (full weight of evidence) based on site- and month-specific historical 98th-percentile thresholds computed from combined-site records under 40 CFR Part 50, Appendix N.

At the same time, U.S. state and local regulatory networks rely heavily on continuous Federal Equivalent Method (FEM) monitors rather than intermittent 24-hour gravimetric Federal Reference Method (FRM) filter samplers (Zou, 2021; Fowlie, Rubin, & Walker, 2019; Grainger & Schreiber, 2019). Among optical FEMs, the Teledyne API Model T640 and T640x broadband spectrometer instruments proliferated rapidly before field audits uncovered a systematic positive mass bias relative to FRMs. In May 2024, EPA approved a firmware network alignment (method codes 636/638 for aligned real-time collection and 736/738 for retroactively aligned historical records; U.S. EPA, 2024c). Controlled chamber experiments by Long et al. (2023) established that T640 light-scattering retrievals remain sensitive to aerosol composition, particle size distribution, refractive index, and biomass-burning smoke properties even after standard calibration adjustments. Separately, McClure et al. (2026) developed an automated nationwide screening pipeline to identify probable wildland fire exceptional events across the contiguous United States, and Krupnick et al. (2025) quantified how exceptional-event exclusions create growing divergence between ambient PM₂.₅ exposure and regulatory attainment metrics.

What remains untested at national field scale is whether *post-alignment* optical monitors operating in routine regulatory networks still diverge enough from collocated gravimetric FRMs during wildfire smoke to alter classification under EPA's exact April 2024 Tiering thresholds—and whether any observed threshold sensitivity is specific to biomass smoke or reflects general high-concentration optical bias. Answering this metrological question requires solving four methodological challenges simultaneously: (i) pairing post-alignment T640_FW (636/638) and FRM samplers on the exact same rooftop and calendar day to eliminate site-location and episode-timing confounders; (ii) auditing common geographic, temporal, and concentration support against beta attenuation monitors (BAM 170/209) so disjoint monitoring networks are not mistakenly treated as head-to-head equivalents; (iii) testing both EPA's official published 2021–2025 site-month thresholds (U.S. EPA, 2026) and strictly *out-of-time* prospective rolling 5-year thresholds reconstructed from historical certified AQS archives under EPA's May 1 annual certification schedule; and (iv) separating smoke-specific optical amplification from general non-smoke threshold sensitivity using concentration-matched non-smoke controls, independent surface chemical speciation, and multi-way event-level clustered inference (Cameron, Gelbach, & Miller, 2011).

This study provides that field-scale metrological evaluation across the 2023–2025 U.S. regulatory archive. We focus squarely on collocated measurement agreement and screening-threshold sensitivity under EPA-457/R-24-001; auxiliary screening-level 3-year annual Design Value sensitivity analyses (including the 2021–2023 county-level monitor Design Value threshold waterfall and observational Exceptional Event archival context) are documented separately in the Supporting Information to preserve strict separation between measurement screening and administrative determinations.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Collocated Pairing Construction, Method Codes, and Case-Flow Design

We constructed same-site, same-day paired daily 24-hour PM₂.₅ observations (parameter code 88101; sample durations `24 HOUR` and `24-HR BLK AVG`, 50 states plus DC) from EPA AirData bulk daily files (`daily_88101_2023.zip` through `daily_88101_2025.zip`). To isolate post-alignment optical performance without retroactive-alignment stitching artifacts, the optical cohort is restricted strictly to real-time firmware-aligned Teledyne T640 and T640x records (AQS method codes `636` and `638`, denoted `T640_FW`), paired against collocated gravimetric FRM samplers (11 gravimetric method codes) operating at the identical `site_id` on the identical `Date Local`. For a mass-based continuous reference comparator, we constructed an analogous `BAM–FRM` dataset restricted strictly to pure beta attenuation monitors (Met One BAM-1020/1022, AQS method codes `170` and `209`), excluding hybrid optical-nephelometer beta analyzers (such as Thermo 5030 SHARP, code `184`). Where multiple FRM or FEM Parameter Occurrence Codes (POCs) operated on the same rooftop-day, arithmetic means within instrument class yielded one unique `(site_id, Date Local)` pair.

Overhead wildfire smoke plumes were linked at exact monitor coordinates (`Latitude`, `Longitude`) across 100% of calendar days (2018–2025; 2,922/2,922 days) from NOAA Hazard Mapping System (HMS) daily smoke polygons, coding the highest intersecting density category per site-day (`0` = None, `1` = Light, `2` = Medium, `3` = Heavy). Because satellite HMS polygons detect column-integrated smoke aloft rather than guaranteed surface contact, we augmented the paired records with three independent surface indicators: (1) leave-one-site-out same-county-day PM₂.₅ from non-T640 (`FRM`/`BAM`) monitors (`other_site_pm_non_t640`), (2) site-month non-smoke 90th-percentile FRM anomalies, and (3) EPA Chemical Speciation Network (CSN) and IMPROVE 24-hour chemical speciation (`OC`, `EC`, `NO₃⁻`, `SO₄²⁻`) and AQS meteorology (`RH`, `Temp`), matched at exact 0 km rooftop coordinates first and nearest `< 25 km` same-county-day monitor second. Full data provenance, file sizes, and SHA-256 digests are exported in `tables/aqs_and_threshold_version_manifest.csv` and summarized in Supporting Information Section S1.

### 2.2 Common-Support Audit Between T640–FRM and BAM–FRM Queues

A critical methodological hazard when comparing collocated FEM–FRM cohorts across national monitoring networks is unequal geographic, seasonal, and concentration support. Before comparing `T640_FW–FRM` and `BAM–FRM` screening outcomes, we performed an explicit common-support audit quantifying: (a) exact same-site, same-day triple observations where `T640_FW`, `BAM`, and `FRM` operated simultaneously on the same rooftop (both across all 2023–2025 days and under HMS Heavy smoke); (b) overlap in calendar smoke dates, monitoring sites, and U.S. EPA Regions (1–10); and (c) FRM concentration quantile distributions.

To test whether cross-network differences persist when support is aligned, we estimated differential Tier-1 exceedance gaps, $\Delta_{\text{diff}} = \bar{\delta}_{\text{T640}} - \bar{\delta}_{\text{BAM}}$, across five progressively stricter overlap filters (Full Matched Heavy, Overlapping EPA Regions, Overlapping Smoke Dates, Overlapping Dates & Regions, and Overlapping Dates, Regions & FRM $\le 50\;\mu\text{g/m}^3$) using pooled regressions clustered on common national smoke-date blocks, plus an exact overlap-weighted (harmonic/ATO-weighted) estimator across shared `EPA Region × FRM-concentration-bin` strata (`<10`, `10–15`, `15–25`, `25–40`, `≥40 µg/m³`) with 1,000 smoke-block bootstrap replications. Because exact Heavy-smoke triple support is sparse ($n = 4$ site-days), our inferential design retains each instrument's within-site comparison against its own collocated gravimetric FRM as the primary estimand and explicitly demotes `BAM–FRM` to a background reference benchmark rather than claiming balanced head-to-head network equivalence.

### 2.3 Official EPA April 2024 Tiering Algorithm and Out-of-Time Prospective Reconstruction

Under EPA-457/R-24-001 (U.S. EPA, 2024b), the baseline tiering threshold (Tier 2 lower bound) for site $s$ and calendar month $m$ is defined from the most recent five certified calendar years of combined-site daily 24-hour PM₂.₅ data (constructed per 40 CFR Part 50, Appendix N § 3.0(d)) after excluding site-days carrying any Request Exclusion (`R`) or fire-related Informational Only (`I`) qualifier at any monitor on site:

$$T_{2,sm} = \min\!\left(P_{98,sm}^{(5\text{-yr})},\; \min_{y \in \mathcal{Y}_5} P_{98,sy}\right), \qquad T_{1,sm} = 1.5 \times T_{2,sm}$$

where $P_{98,sm}^{(5\text{-yr})}$ is the 5-year month-specific 98th percentile for calendar month $m$, $P_{98,sy}$ is the annual 98th percentile for year $y$ computed per 40 CFR Part 50, Appendix N § 4.5(a) Table 1 order statistics, and a candidate day meets Tier 1 iff its 24-hour concentration is $\ge T_{1,sm}$ (and Tier 2 iff $T_{2,sm} \le C < T_{1,sm}$). Annual data certifications are due May 1 of the following year, shifting $\mathcal{Y}_5$ forward by one year each May (U.S. EPA, 2024b, p. 10).

We evaluate paired `T640_FW–FRM` and `BAM–FRM` crossings using two complementary threshold architectures:
1. **Retrospective Official EPA Published Table (`2021–2025`):** We pinned EPA's live public PM₂.₅ Tiering Tool table (`for_posting.csv`, AQS query date `2026-09-14`, `13,138` site-month rows, `R` and fire-related `I` flags excluded; U.S. EPA, 2026) and verified exact algebraic identity ($T_1 = 1.5 \times T_2$ and $T_2 = \min(\text{monthly}_{98}, \text{annual}_{98,\min})$) across every row. Because the `2021–2025` baseline overlaps the `2023–2025` evaluation window, this replay is explicitly labeled **retrospective**. Both collocated readings on a site-day receive the identical published site-month cutoff.
2. **Strictly Out-of-Time Prospective Rolling 5-Year Reconstructions (`2018–2022 → 2023`, `2019–2023 → 2024`, `2020–2024 → 2025`):** To eliminate all in-sample baseline leakage and mirror contemporaneous operational screening under EPA's May 1 certification calendar (note: `0` of `472` T640 Heavy-smoke days occurred before May 1, so `100%` of T640 Heavy days use certified years $Y-5$ through $Y-1$; the `5` January BAM Heavy days use $Y-6$ through $Y-2$), we reconstructed site-level combined daily records from our 2018–2025 AQS daily archive (`daily_88101_2018..2025.zip`) and `aqs_monitors.zip` (prioritizing each site's `NAAQS Primary Monitor` POC per Appendix N § 3.0(d), excluding pre-alignment raw T640 `236/238`, and applying exact Appendix N § 4.5(a) Table 1 98th percentiles). Because public AirData bulk daily CSVs expose AQS `Excluded` daily records and monitor-year `Events Excluded` 98th percentiles (`annual_conc_by_monitor_YYYY.zip`, which strip `R`-flagged days) but do not distribute individual-day `I`-qualifier codes (`IF`/`IT`), we evaluate four transparent out-of-time specifications:
   - **`OOT-A` (Conservative `R`-Excluded AQS / AMP480 Bound):** Combined-site daily series dropping `Excluded` site-days, paired with AQS `annual_conc_by_monitor` `Events Excluded` annual 98th percentiles. Per EPA-457/R-24-001 (p. 16), retaining `I`-flagged days yields a conservative upper bound on thresholds (analogous to AQS report AMP480).
   - **`OOT-B` (Fire-Screen Proxy — Excluding HMS Heavy):** `OOT-A` additionally removing NOAA HMS Heavy (`level = 3`) fire-smoke site-days from the historical 5-year window as a transparent proxy for unexposed fire-`I` qualifiers.
   - **`OOT-B2` (Expanded Fire-Screen Proxy — Excluding HMS Medium+Heavy):** `OOT-A` removing HMS Medium and Heavy (`level ≥ 2`) days from the historical 5-year baseline.
   - **`OOT-C` (Pure Gravimetric FRM-Only Rolling Baseline):** Computing rolling 5-year thresholds exclusively from collocated gravimetric FRM measurements so no optical instrument enters the baseline itself.

We validated our Appendix N reconstruction code directly against EPA's published `2021–2025` table before running prospective windows: across zero-event clean sites (1,148 site-months), median absolute Tier-1 error is exactly **0.000 µg/m³** ($r = 0.876$; `OOT-B` clean-site $r = 0.895$, median $|e| = 0.000$ µg/m³; full benchmark in SI Table S2 and Figure S2A). Across both retrospective and out-of-time thresholds, we report full **bidirectional discordance** (`FEM ≥ T1 & FRM < T1` vs. `FRM ≥ T1 & FEM < T1`), exact paired McNemar binomial $p$-values, and **near-threshold band sensitivity** within `±10%` and `±20%` of Tier-1 defined both by FRM and by the symmetric pair midpoint $(\text{FEM} + \text{FRM})/2$.

### 2.4 Multi-Dimensional Inference and Ground-Level Smoke Specificity Controls

Each paired site-day $i$ contributes a binary threshold-crossing difference $\delta_i = \mathbf{1}(\text{FEM}_i \ge T_i) - \mathbf{1}(\text{FRM}_i \ge T_i) \in \{-1, 0, +1\}$. Because wildfire plumes span multiple days and monitoring sites simultaneously, naive site-day or one-way site standard errors understate uncertainty (Abadie et al., 2023). Alongside one-way site ($G = 165$), calendar-date ($G = 45$), and national consecutive smoke-date block ($G = 27$) finite-sample $t$ intervals, we construct geographically separated **Region-Smoke-Episodes** grouping Heavy-smoke dates within the same U.S. EPA Region separated by $\le 2$ calendar days ($G = 55$ regional episodes for T640) and compute **Cameron, Gelbach, & Miller (2011) two-way cluster-robust intervals** simultaneously clustering on `(Site × National Smoke Block)` and `(Site × Region-Smoke-Episode)` with $\min(G_1, G_2) - 1$ degrees of freedom. We further verify stability via year-by-year estimation (`2023`, `2024`, `2025`), leave-one-national-block-out, leave-one-regional-episode-out, and leave-one-EPA-region-out validation.

To distinguish **smoke-specific optical amplification** from **general high-PM₂.₅ threshold sensitivity**, we compare `T640_FW − FRM` concentration bias and official Tier-1 exceedance differences on non-smoke days (`HMS = 0`) versus Heavy smoke days (`HMS = 3`) within matched elevated concentration bands (`≥15`, `15–25`, `≥20`, and `≥25 µg/m³`). Because conditioning solely on noisy FRM can induce errors-in-variables (EIV) attenuation at high FRM values (Bland & Altman, 1986), we evaluate all three concentration definitions side-by-side: (i) collocated FRM, (ii) strictly independent other-site `FRM/BAM`-only PM₂.₅ in the same county-day (`other_site_pm_non_t640`), and (iii) Bland–Altman pair midpoint $(\text{FEM} + \text{FRM})/2$ (exact under equal error variances), testing `Heavy − Non-Smoke` differentials via Site-clustered CR1 regressions (Cameron, Gelbach, & Miller, 2008; Roodman et al., 2019). Finally, we stratify HMS Heavy days by independent ground-level smoke confirmation—surface PM₂.₅ anomaly above site-month non-smoke `p90` versus aloft plumes ($\le$ `p90`), and collocated CSN/IMPROVE Organic Carbon (`OC ≥ 5` vs. `< 5 µg/m³`) and biomass-burning ratio (`OC/EC ≥ 5`)—and pair these with Site + Month fixed-effects speciation regressions (`N = 14,992` T640 pairs; `9,978` exact 0 km rooftops).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Collocated Pairing Quality, Baseline Agreement, and Common-Support Audit

Across 2023–2025, the post-alignment optical archive comprises 63,951 same-site, same-day `T640_FW–FRM` pairs across 234 monitoring sites (`2023-06-14` through `2025-12-31`), retaining a modest positive daily baseline bias of +0.56 µg/m³ (median +0.45 µg/m³) that peaks in the Midwest (+0.67 µg/m³) and winter DJF (+0.76 µg/m³) while nearing zero in the arid West (+0.12 µg/m³, $p = 0.514$). The background `BAM–FRM` archive comprises 22,927 pairs across 107 sites (mean daily bias +0.00 µg/m³, median +0.00 µg/m³). Filtering to NOAA HMS Heavy smoke yields 472 `T640_FW–FRM` pairs (167 sites, 45 dates), of which 468 (165 sites; `4` unmatched new sites) match EPA's official site-month threshold table, and 201 `BAM–FRM` pairs (54 sites, 65 dates), of which 197 (53 sites; `4` unmatched) match EPA thresholds.

Our common-support audit reveals pronounced geographic and concentration disjointness between the two networks (**Figure 1A**, **Table 1**). First, across all 2023–2025 days, only 401 same-site, same-day triple observations (`T640_FW`, `BAM`, and `FRM` co-operating on the same rooftop) occur across 5 stations; under HMS Heavy smoke, exact triple overlap collapses to just **4 site-days across 4 sites** (with 6 overlapping sites and **27 overlapping Heavy-smoke dates**). Notably, even in those 401 same-rooftop triples (**Figure 1B** inset), mean `T640_FW − BAM` rises monotonically with smoke intensity from +0.41 µg/m³ (`HMS = 0`, $n = 248$) and +0.29 µg/m³ (`Light`, $n = 124$) to +0.71 µg/m³ (`Medium`, $n = 25$) and +2.88 µg/m³ across the 4 Heavy-smoke triples (`T640_FW − FRM` = +2.43 vs. `BAM − FRM` = -0.45 µg/m³; individual triple records listed in SI Table S1). Second, `T640_FW` Heavy-smoke pairs lie exclusively in EPA Regions 1–5, 7, and 8 (zero pairs in Regions 6, 9, and 10; median FRM `12.25` µg/m³, 90th percentile `30.13` µg/m³), whereas `47/197` (`23.9%`) of `BAM` Heavy-smoke pairs sit in Pacific Regions 9–10 with a much higher reference concentration distribution (median FRM `16.70`, 90th percentile `61.18` µg/m³).

### 3.2 Official EPA Tier-1 Screening Replay and Strictly Out-of-Time Prospective Validation

Under EPA's official published 2021–2025 site-month thresholds (**Table 2**, **Figure 2A**), aligned `T640_FW` registers **123** Tier-1 crossings compared with **89** on collocated gravimetric FRM across 468 matched Heavy-smoke site-days (165 sites), with steeply asymmetric bidirectional discordance of **36 T640-only** versus **2 FRM-only** crossings (exact paired McNemar $p = 5.40 \times 10^{-9}$). The paired exceedance difference is **+7.26 pp**, excluding zero across every clustering structure: 95% CI [+4.15, +10.38] across 27 national smoke-date blocks, [+4.26, +10.27] under two-way `Site × National Block` clustering, and **[+3.43, +11.10] ($p = 0.0004$)** under our primary two-way `Site × Region-Smoke-Episode` clustering across 55 geographically distinct regional episodes. At EPA's Tier-2 lower threshold (which includes Tier-1 days), `T640_FW` registers 232 versus 193 crossings (43 vs. 4 discordant; +8.33 pp, two-way 95% CI [+3.31, +13.36]). At the fixed daily `>35 µg/m³` level-equivalent benchmark (distinct from the 3-year 98th-percentile 24-hour NAAQS), `T640_FW` records 51 versus 26 crossings in the threshold-matched sample (25 vs. 0; +5.34 pp; 52 vs. 26 across all 472 Heavy pairs), but its national-block 95% CI spans zero ([-0.63, +11.31]) due to episodic concentration of extreme-smoke exceedances, confirming that the site-month EPA Tier-1 threshold provides the more stable screening metric. In the background `BAM–FRM` comparator, both instruments record exactly 84 Tier-1 crossings (5 BAM-only vs. 5 FRM-only; +0.00 pp, two-way 95% CI [-2.92, +2.92]).

### 3.3 Ground-Level Smoke Specificity, Equal-Concentration Non-Smoke Controls, Chemical Speciation, and Event-Level Robustness

A central scientific question is whether T640's Tier-1 excess reflects **smoke-specific optical amplification** or **general high-PM₂.₅ threshold sensitivity** present whenever ambient concentrations approach a cutoff. Comparing `T640_FW − FRM` bias and Tier-1 crossings on non-smoke days (`HMS = 0`) against HMS Heavy smoke days (`HMS = 3`) within matched elevated concentration bands yields a nuanced, two-layer physical answer (**Table 3**, **Figure 3A**):

1. **General Non-Smoke Baseline Sensitivity at Moderate Concentrations (`15–25 µg/m³`):** Across all non-smoke pairs (`HMS = 0`, $n = 40,811$), mean T640 bias is +0.49 µg/m³ and Tier-1 excess is minimal (+0.44 pp). However, restricting non-smoke days to moderately elevated concentrations (`15–25 µg/m³`) reveals non-trivial positive Tier-1 discordance even without overhead smoke: +3.09 pp when conditioned on independent non-T640 other-site monitors (`bias +0.88 µg/m³`, $n = 550$), +7.15 pp by FRM (`bias +0.88 µg/m³`), and +9.02 pp by pair midpoint (`bias +1.64 µg/m³`). Seasonal decomposition (**Figure 3B**) shows this non-smoke sensitivity occurs predominantly during humid winter (`DJF`) inorganic pollution episodes (`FRM ≥ 20`: bias +0.73 µg/m³, Tier-1 +4.06 pp, two-sided `22` vs. `11`), whereas fire-season (`May–Sep`) non-smoke elevated days (`FRM ≥ 20`) exhibit *negative* bias (-6.15 µg/m³, Tier-1 -20.93 pp, likely reflecting coarse dust or fireworks aerosols).
2. **Pronounced Smoke-Specific Optical Amplification:** Conditioning on identical concentration bands across *every* EIV-robust and reference metric, HMS Heavy smoke multiplies T640_FW's positive optical bias three- to fivefold relative to non-smoke days (**Table 3**, **Figure 3A**). At `Other-Site (FRM/BAM) ≥ 15 µg/m³`, optical bias jumps from +0.64 µg/m³ on non-smoke days to +5.18 µg/m³ under Heavy smoke ($\Delta = +4.54$ µg/m³, Site-clustered $p = 3.44 \times 10^{-13}$), driving Tier-1 excess from +3.43 pp to +16.46 pp ($\Delta = +13.02$ pp, $p = 0.004$). At high concentrations (`≥25 µg/m³`), non-smoke bias turns flat or negative (-1.47 µg/m³ by Other-Site; -2.81 by FRM; +1.66 by Midpoint), whereas Heavy-smoke bias surges to **+7.81–+7.98 µg/m³** ($\Delta = +6.26$ to +10.79 µg/m³, all $p \le 0.0002$), with zero FRM-only Tier-1 reversals (`5–9` T640-only vs. `0` FRM-only). Across all four HMS smoke levels, T640_FW bias increases monotonically (`HMS 0`: +0.49 → `Light`: +0.57 → `Medium`: +1.26 → `Heavy`: +3.06 µg/m³).

## 4. Discussion and Environmental Implications

Our collocated field audit shows that even after EPA's May 2024 T640/T640x network alignment (U.S. EPA, 2024c), residual optical–gravimetric divergence during wildland fire smoke routinely alters initial screening classification under EPA's April 2024 Tiering framework (U.S. EPA, 2024b). By combining same-site, same-day optical–FRM pairing, strict out-of-time 5-year threshold reconstruction, equal-concentration non-smoke controls, and surface speciation verification, this work bridges controlled laboratory chamber evaluations (Long et al., 2023) and national policy-screening assessments (Krupnick et al., 2025; McClure et al., 2026) with direct field-network metrological evidence.

Three scientific conclusions and two practical implications follow directly from the data:

1. **Both Baseline Sensitivity and Smoke-Specific Amplification Matter:** Critics might reasonably ask whether optical threshold divergence is simply a generic artifact of baseline positive bias near any cutoff. Our concentration-matched non-smoke controls show that both mechanisms operate, but in sharply distinct regimes. On elevated non-smoke days (`15–25 µg/m³`, primarily humid winter inorganic haze), residual baseline T640 bias (`+0.5` to `+1.6 µg/m³`) produces modest, partly two-sided Tier-1 discordance (`+3.1` to `+9.0 pp`). During surface-impacted wildfire smoke (confirmed independently by `FRM > site-month p90` and `OC ≥ 5 µg/m³`), however, optical over-response amplifies three- to fivefold (`+3.5` to `+8.0 µg/m³`, $p < 10^{-4}$ to $10^{-15}$ vs. matched non-smoke days) and drives overwhelmingly one-sided Tier-1 crossings (`36` vs. `2` official; `26–33` vs. `0` out-of-time). Consequently, air-quality interpretation under EPA-457/R-24-001 cannot assume post-alignment optical–gravimetric equivalence during intense biomass-burning plumes.
2. **Retrospective and Prospective Out-of-Time Screening Agree in Sign and Asymmetric Structure:** Reconstructing EPA's exact Appendix N site-month formula on strictly historical certified windows (`2018–2022 → 2023`, `2019–2023 → 2024`, `2020–2024 → 2025`) confirms statistically significant, unidirectional excess Tier-1 triggering (`+5.57` to `+7.07 pp`, `26–33` vs. `0`, all two-way $p \le 0.002$) without any look-ahead leakage. At the same time, our out-of-time audit highlights a subtle operational sensitivity of EPA's 5-year rolling rule: because public AirData records omit individual-day `I`-qualifier flags, retaining unflagged 2023 Canadian wildfire days in the `2020–2024` baseline raises reconstructed 2025 thresholds and attenuates the 2025 excess from `+8.08 pp` (official `R`+`I` excluded) to `+3.08`–`+3.59 pp`. Publishing versioned historical snapshots of EPA's official `for_posting.csv` archive alongside each May 1 certification rollover would allow transparent prospective auditing by air agencies and researchers alike.
3. **Practical Guidance for Monitoring Agencies and Calibration Research:** First, where a candidate wildland fire reading from an optical FEM falls within `±20%` of the site-month Tier-1 or Tier-2 threshold—the band containing `32/36` (`88.9%` by FRM; `36/36` = `100%` by midpoint) of T640-only Tier-1 discordances—air agencies and EPA regional offices should cross-check collocated gravimetric FRM or nearby mass-based samplers when available. Second, while our ambient speciation associations (`OC+`, `OC × HMS Heavy+`, `EC−`, `RH+`) reinforce the optical-scattering mechanisms identified by Long et al. (2023), observational field regressions cannot by themselves parameterize regulatory firmware corrections; controlled multi-site physical calibration and smoke-chamber experiments across aerosol aging states remain necessary before designing smoke-aware optical correction functions.

**Scope, Epistemic Boundaries, and Supporting Information Context.** Four explicit boundaries govern the interpretation of this study. First, our primary analysis quantifies *screening-level threshold crossings* under EPA-457/R-24-001; it does not link discordant site-days to formal state Exceptional Event demonstrations, EPA concurrence dockets, or Clean Air Act § 107(d) area designations, which depend on multi-factor weight-of-evidence reviews and regulatory significance criteria. Second, although common-support matching widens the T640 − BAM differential (`+7.93` to `+11.24 pp`), sparse Heavy-smoke triple collocation ($n = 4$) prevents a powered within-rooftop T640-versus-BAM smoke trial; `BAM–FRM` is therefore interpreted strictly as a background reference comparator. Third, public AirData daily files lack raw `I`-qualifier day flags, motivating our four-specification out-of-time bracket (`OOT-A` through `OOT-C`). Fourth, auxiliary screening-level 3-year annual Design Value sensitivity analyses—specifically the `2023–2025` same-rooftop paired DV discrepancy (`+0.468 µg/m³`, `19` vs. `2` sites crossing `9.0 µg/m³` across `178` rooftops vs. `-0.021 µg/m³` on `56` BAM rooftops), the `2021–2023` monitor Design Value threshold reclassification waterfall (`47` counties / `18.39M` residents shifted from $\ge 9.1$ to $\le 9.0\;\mu\text{g/m}^3$ by T640 software alignment alone—explicitly screening-level monitor DV cross-overs, not EPA § 107(d) designations), and archival observational Exceptional Event flagging regressions (where a within-county-year fixed-effects test finds no causal instrument-filing effect: `+0.81` pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.931$, $N = 981$)—are placed cleanly in the **Supporting Information** (`supplementary_j6_est.pdf`) so the main paper remains focused strictly on collocated metrology and screening-threshold sensitivity.
