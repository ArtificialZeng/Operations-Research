## Abstract

Public product recall notices issued by the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) formally allocate corrective duties between recalling firms and consumers—specifying whether households may return a hazardous item to a store or by prepaid mail, or must photograph, mark, physically disable, and arrange local disposal of the product themselves. Using the complete 27-year official CPSC SaferProducts Recall Census (2000–2026, $N = 8,386$ unique notices; strict post-emergence contract era 2022–2026, $N = 1,822$ classified notices with complete price and unit data across $G = 1,414$ firm clusters), we construct an auditable verbatim channel taxonomy and a five-dimensional remedy-duty dataset separating literal photographic proof, mandatory versus optional/alternative proof pathways, physical disablement, permanent marking, and direct physical returns. Auditing prior marketplace coding exposes substantial asymmetric channel conflation: 51/391 common-support notices (13/264 China-direct vs. 38/127 U.S.-entity) listed additional flagged marketplaces, and 100/391 (26/264 vs. 74/127; 162/601 across 2022–2026, including the Anker and OCOOPA recalls) listed other marketplaces or brand direct-to-consumer websites alongside Amazon. We therefore report all channel definitions side by side. Across the full 2022–2026 all-channel census ($N = 1,822$), China-direct marketplace notices listing no U.S. entity (`1_Mkt_FD_China`) exhibit +35.33 pp higher literal photo-evidence wording ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$) and -35.07 pp lower direct-return options ($p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$) relative to brick-and-mortar recalls—including +28.68 pp (`photo_language`) and -13.78 pp (`direct_return`) within marketplaces over verified U.S.-entity sellers (`3_Mkt_True_US`), and +41.42 pp (within-marketplace +36.27 pp) inside strict durables priced $\ge \$100$ ($N = 685$). Inside calendar-aligned (Jan 1–Nov 19) fine-product-noun $\times$ year common-support Amazon comparisons, however, estimates are **specification-sensitive**: moving from broad online-marketplace notices mentioning Amazon ($N = 391$, 38 cells: photo +13.72 pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.027$; direct return -8.20 pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.021$) to notices excluding other flagged platforms ($N = 313$, 34 cells: photo +10.89 pp, 95% CI [-3.03, +24.80], $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.121$; return -7.00 pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.097$) and verbatim strict Amazon-exclusive notices ($N = 236$, 25 cells: photo +10.70 pp, 95% CI [-5.56, +26.96], $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.226$; return -10.76 pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.044$; physical destruction +15.24 pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.035$) keeps point estimates directionally consistent while widening photo-language confidence intervals across zero. An automated rule-plus-author structured audit of $N = 150$ stratified notices (explicitly benchmarking Jugader's optional photo-or-order-number alternative and UVIPC's non-photo proof of destruction) documents higher parser false-negative rates on heterogeneous U.S.-entity wording, demonstrating via bounded misclassification sensitivity that measurement error attenuates the direct-return deficit (making the adjusted gap more negative) while explaining much of the raw cross-border gap in narrow physical-disablement and marking clauses. Across 67 China-direct hazardous-product notices assigning consumer self-disposal (0.0% firm direct return), notice-listed affected inventory totals 3,947,390 units—67.34% concentrated in two multi-channel recalls (OCOOPA and Anker), with 54/67 complete notices carrying local hazardous-waste warnings (3,910,951 units, 99.08%). Public notices capture written remedy responsibilities rather than realized consumer completion rates or observed waste streams; independent double-blind human adjudication and matched CPSC progress reports are required to measure realized operational fulfillment.

**Keywords:** Consumer product recalls; CPSC recall notices; Cross-border e-commerce; Consumer verification burden; Channel classification; Measurement error bounds; *In re Amazon.com*

## 1. CPSC Recall Notice Duties, E-Commerce Disintermediation, and Legal Context

When a hazardous consumer product reaches households, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC) publishes an official recall notice under Section 15 of the Consumer Product Safety Act specifying what consumers must do to obtain a refund, repair, or replacement. For four decades, empirical and analytical scholarship on product recalls in law, economics, marketing, and operations management studied corrective actions executed by domestically anchored manufacturers, importers, and retail chains operating physical store counters, prepaid domestic return networks, and toll-free customer-service hotlines (Jarrell and Peltzman 1985; Chen et al. 2009; Hora et al. 2011; Thirumalai and Sinha 2011; Freedman et al. 2012; Kalaignanam et al. 2013; Ni et al. 2014; Wowak and Boone 2015; Eilert et al. 2017; Liu et al. 2017; Shah et al. 2017; Ball et al. 2018; Mukherjee and Sinha 2018; Astvansh et al. 2022; Mao et al. 2022). In that traditional domestic architecture, reverse logistics and eligibility verification remained primarily firm-led responsibilities.

Over 2015–2026, global third-party (3P) e-commerce marketplaces (`Amazon.com`, `Walmart.com`, `Temu.com`, `Shein.com`, `Wayfair.com`, `AliExpress`, `eBay`, and `TikTok Shop`) transformed the traditional agency-versus-reseller retail structure (Hagiu and Wright 2015; Tian et al. 2018) by enabling overseas manufacturers and trading firms to sell directly to U.S. households without a U.S. importer, physical retail storefront, domestic warehouse return hub, or U.S. telephone support line. Across the complete 27-year CPSC recall census (2000–2026, $N = 8,386$), Marketplace-Only recalls expanded 22-fold from 2.62% in 2015 to 58.30% in 2026, while China-based cross-border notices listing zero U.S. legal entities rose from 0.00% in 2015 to 41.70% in 2026. Parallel computer-science work curates multimodal CPSC text and image repositories for hazard prediction (Bolanos et al. 2025), consumer-advocacy analyses track rising unilateral CPSC safety warnings when overseas vendors fail to cooperate (Consumer Federation of America 2025), marketing scholars link 217 FOIA-obtained CPSC compliance progress reports to realized recall effectiveness (Raithel et al. 2024), and field-experimental research measures consumer behavior under returnless partial refunds on a cross-border e-commerce platform (Fang et al. 2026).

This study examines the distinct regulatory and consumer-policy stage that precedes household participation: **the formal allocation of corrective duties written into published federal recall notices.** Notice wording matters directly for consumer protection because different clauses shift fundamentally different effort, safety, privacy, and disposal burdens onto households. Asking a consumer to submit an identification photograph as an optional alternative to an online order number is operationally distinct from requiring a consumer to sever power cords, dismantle baby loungers, write "RECALLED" in permanent marker across an explosive lithium-ion device, email photographic proof to an unverified free webmail address, and transport defective batteries to a municipal household hazardous waste (HHW) facility without any corporate take-back channel.

Conceptually, three structural forces—formalized in a closed-form principal–agent and reverse-logistics model in **Supplementary Appendix A**—govern how firms and regulators allocate notice-level duties: (i) prohibitive trans-Pacific reverse-logistics costs ($c_{\text{rev}}$) that deter physical returns for asset-light overseas sellers; (ii) platform intermediation that limits third-party sellers' first-party buyer-record observability ($\theta \ll 1$) relative to direct-to-consumer (DTC) brands ($\theta \to 1$), creating fraud and unsafe-retention concerns under unverified refunds; and (iii) asymmetric domestic legal accountability—the classic *judgment-proof problem* (Shavell 1986)—coupled with declining institutional template costs ($a_t$) as CPSC normalizes photographic verification wording across recalls. Crucially, however, public CPSC recall notices record **assigned written responsibilities and remedy options**, not realized consumer completion rates, firm processing costs, or downstream waste destinations.

To establish what the public CPSC archive can and cannot rigorously identify, this paper makes three contributions aimed at consumer-policy scholarship and open empirical data infrastructure:
1. **Verbatim Channel Audit and Three-Definition Comparison:** We uncover a severe asymmetric misclassification in broad marketplace indicators (`amazon_only_mkt`), build an auditable three-tier verbatim Amazon channel classification (`strict_amazon_exclusive`, `multi_channel_with_amazon`, and `amazon_channel_ambiguous`), and estimate every remedy-duty comparison side by side—candidly distinguishing robust all-channel associations from specification-sensitive narrow Amazon-exclusive common-support estimates whose confidence intervals cross zero.
2. **Structured Five-Dimensional Remedy-Duty Coding and Bounded Misclassification Sensitivity:** We separate literal photo wording, mandatory versus optional/alternative proof pathways (benchmarking **Jugader**, Recall 24-199), non-photo destruction proof (benchmarking **UVIPC**, Recall 25-122), physical disablement, permanent marking, duty bearers, and direct physical returns; publish a verifiable $N = 150$ stratified rule-plus-author audit with verbatim CPSC quotes and source URLs; quantify group-specific parser error rates; and propagate differential misclassification into re-estimated bounds.
3. **Disciplined Institutional Measurement:** We separate notice-listed affected hazardous-product inventory (3,947,390 units, explicitly disclosing 67.34% two-batch concentration and 54/67 complete-notice warnings) from unobserved household disposal streams, and evaluate *In re Amazon.com* (U.S. CPSC 2024, 2025; *Amazon.com, Inc. v. CPSC* 2026) strictly as an institutional case study given zero explicit "Fulfilled by Amazon" fulfillment-mode labeling in public notices.

## 2. Data, Sample Flow, Verbatim Channel Classification, and Structured Remedy-Duty Coding Audit

### 2.1 Official CPSC Recall Census (2000–2026) and Sample Flow

We retrieve the complete universe of published U.S. consumer product recalls from the open HTTP-200 CPSC SaferProducts REST Web Services API (`https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json`) spanning 2000-01-04 through 2026-11-19. Deduplicating by `RecallID` yields **$N = 8,386$ unique notices** (`data/cpsc_recalls_2000_2026.json`, SHA-256 `db445bb9199c...`). Across the 27-year census, photographic verification wording stood at 0.00% in 2000–2017, 0.39% in 2018, 0.00% in 2019, 0.39% in 2020, and 2.74% in 2021, before rising sharply to 12.67% in 2022 and 62.45% in 2026. All primary cross-sectional contract comparisons therefore restrict strictly to the post-emergence era (2022–2026, $N = 1,871$ notices) to avoid pooling zero-variation pre-2022 cohorts.

As detailed in `scripts/parser_lib.py`, our entity parser reads across all four structured CPSC fields (`Importers`, `Manufacturers`, `Distributors`, and `Retailers`) to rescue late-2026 notices storing legal entities inside `Retailers`, excludes platform corporate-office co-listings (`AliExpress`, `Woot`, distributor-only `Amazon` co-listed alongside foreign 3P sellers) while retaining genuine Amazon first-party private labels (*Amazon Basics*) as U.S. entities, and strips web domains and non-store phrases (`TikTok Shop`, `App Store`, `not sold in stores`) prior to brick-and-mortar store matching. Every classified 2022–2026 notice ($N = 1,833$, excluding 38 unclear giveaways/unlocated notices) maps to six mutually exclusive supply-chain archetypes: `1_Mkt_FD_China` ($N = 448$: 3P marketplace-only listing a China/Hong Kong entity and zero U.S. entity), `2_Mkt_FD_NonChina` ($N = 26$), `3_Mkt_True_US` ($N = 233$: 3P marketplace-only with a verified U.S. legal entity on record—note that public notices cannot verify whether sellers also hold undisclosed foreign or U.S. affiliates), `4_DTC_Online_Only` ($N = 102$: pure first-party brand websites), `5_Omnichannel` ($N = 397$), and `6_Brick_and_Mortar` ($N = 627$). Requiring non-missing retail price and affected-unit counts yields our all-channel regression sample of **$N = 1,822$ notices across $G = 1,414$ firm clusters**. Figure 1A and Table 1 trace the exact sample flow from the complete census to every empirical subsample.

### 2.2 Resolving the `amazon_only_mkt` Classification Defect: Three Verbatim Channel Definitions

Forensic review of earlier drafts revealed a critical channel-labeling error: the legacy indicator `amazon_only_mkt` (`scripts/parser_lib.py`) merely required a recall to fall into `Marketplace_Only` (no physical brick-and-mortar stores) and mention Amazon anywhere in its retailer string. It did **not** verify that Amazon was the sole sales channel. Auditing all 601 classified 2022–2026 notices flagged `amazon_only_mkt = 1`—and all 391 notices in the calendar-aligned common-support comparison (264 `1_Mkt_FD_China` vs. 127 `3_Mkt_True_US`)—uncovers severe **asymmetric multi-channel contamination** (Figure 1B):

1. **Other Flagged 3P Marketplaces (51/391 in Common Support; 86/601 in Classified Universe):** Inside the $N = 391$ common-support sample, exactly **51/391 (13.0%)** notices also list at least one other flagged 3P platform (`Walmart.com`, `eBay`, `Etsy`, `Temu`, `Shein`, `Wayfair`, `AliExpress`, `TikTok Shop`, `Wish`, or `Target.com`), heavily skewed toward U.S.-entity sellers (**38/127 [29.9%]** vs. **13/264 [4.9%]** China-direct). For example, the 1.158-million-unit **Anker PowerCore 10000** notice (`RecallID 10311`, Recall 25-338) explicitly states *"Online at Anker, Amazon, Newegg and Ebay"*, and the 1.500-million-unit **OCOOPA** notice (`RecallID 10895`, Recall 26-659) states *"Online at Amazon.com, Ocoopa.com, Ocoopa.net and Walmart.com"*.
2. **Additional Brand DTC Websites and Other Online Portals (100/391 Total Multi-Channel in CS; 162/601 in Classified Universe):** Parsing verbatim URLs, named retailers (`Newegg`, `Costco.com`, `Nordstrom.com`, `DicksSportingGoods.com`, `QVC`, `HSN`, brand `.com` storefronts such as `Babytrend.com`, `BedsureHome.com`, `Brother-USA.com`, `NordicTrack.com`), and *"other websites/online retailers"* phrases exposes **49 additional multi-channel notices** inside the 391 sample (and 76 additional notices inside 601). Altogether, **74/127 (58.3%)** of U.S.-entity notices in the 391 common-support sample (112/186 or **60.2%** across all 2022–2026 `3_Mkt_True_US` `amazon_only_mkt` notices) were **multi-channel online sellers**, compared to only **26/264 (9.8%)** of China-direct notices (41/389 or 10.5% overall).
3. **Non-Retail Mentions and CPSC Field Conflicts (5 Ambiguous Notices in 2022–2026):** One legacy `amazon_only_mkt` notice (`RecallID 9234`, Shop LC space heaters) aired on *"Amazon Fire"* TV apps alongside DirecTV and Roku—not on Amazon.com retail—while four additional 2022–2026 notices exhibit clerical contradictions between CPSC's `Title`/`Remedies` fields and `Retailers` field (e.g., `RecallID 10276` child safety gates titled *"Sold on Amazon.com"* while `Retailers` reads *"Exclusively online at www.FidgetThings.com"*, and `RecallID 10494` magnetic fidget spinners titled *"Sold on Amazon by Anzmtosn"* while `Retailers` lists *"Spartan dealers nationwide... \$16,400 to \$19,700"*).

To eliminate label distortion permanently, `classify_amazon_channel_3way()` in `scripts/parser_lib.py` parses verbatim `Retailers` / `Sold At` strings across all 8,386 notices into three mutually exclusive, auditable Amazon-related categories (plus `no_amazon_mention`):
- **`strict_amazon_exclusive`** (441 in 2022–2026): verbatim `Retailers` explicitly states *"exclusively at/on Amazon.com"* (31 notices) OR lists Amazon as the sole named retailer with zero other marketplaces, online portals, DTC websites, or brick-and-mortar stores (410 notices).
- **`multi_channel_with_amazon`** (440 in 2022–2026): lists Amazon alongside other online marketplaces, brand DTC sites, or *"other online retailers"* (164 online multi-channel notices, 162 classified) OR physical brick-and-mortar stores (276 omnichannel notices).
- **`amazon_channel_ambiguous`** (5 in 2022–2026): non-retail *"Amazon Fire"* mention (9234) or direct clerical conflict between `Retailers` and `Title`/`Remedies` (10238, 10245, 10276, 10494).

In Section 3, we report **every channel definition side by side**: All-Channel ($N = 1,822$), **Def 1** (legacy broad `amazon_only_mkt`, CS $N = 391$), **Def 2a** (excluding other flagged 3P platforms—reproducing the exact peer-review benchmark $N = 313$), **Def 2b** (verbatim `strict_amazon_exclusive`, rebuilt CS $N = 236$, full calendar-aligned $N = 386$), and **Def 3** (`multi_channel_online_with_amazon`, full $N = 138$, with its underpowered fine-cell common-support subsample $N = 55$ transparently moved to Supplementary Appendix B).

### 2.3 Structured Five-Dimensional Remedy-Duty Coding, Edge Cases (Jugader, UVIPC), Stratified Audit ($N = 150$), and Bounded Misclassification Sensitivity

Equating any mention of photography with mandatory consumer product destruction conflates distinct regulatory duties. Integrating and extending our literal-text coding architecture (`parse_remedy_duties_v4()` in `scripts/parser_lib.py`), we decompose every CPSC recall notice along **five auditable dimensions**:
1. **Literal Photographic Proof (`photo_language`):** Requires both the remedy-verification screen and literal photo/photograph/image/picture wording in `Remedies`.
2. **Mandatory vs. Optional/Alternative Pathway (`proof_status`, `proof_alternative_path`, `photo_mandatory`):** Sentence-level screening identifies notices offering consumers an explicit non-photo pathway (e.g., an online order number, sales receipt, or physical return instead of submitting a photograph).
3. **Non-Photo Destruction Proof (`destruction_proof`, `destruction_proof_without_photo`):** Separately detects explicit *"proof/evidence of destruction or disablement"* even when no photograph is specified.
4. **Physical Disablement vs. Permanent Marking (`physical_destruction`, `physically_disabled`, `permanent_marking`, `photo_only_no_action`):** Distinguishes instructions that physically render the product unusable (cutting cords/straps/garments, breaking, disassembling, submerging, puncturing, or destroying) from permanent-marker labeling ("RECALLED", initials/date) and photo-only identification/disposal notices.
5. **Duty Bearer (`duty_bearer`):** Classifies whether corrective execution falls primarily on consumers without firm return (`consumer_action_required`), is shared via alternative return/proof pathways (`shared_consumer_and_firm`), or is firm/retailer-led (`firm_or_retailer_led`).

Two benchmark edge cases verify these boundaries directly in code and unit tests (`tests/test_f7_boost.py`):
- **Jugader Zipline Kits (`RecallID 9924`, Recall 24-199, `1_Mkt_FD_China`, `strict_amazon_exclusive`):** *"Consumers must submit a photo of the cable and the trolley or their online purchase order number to receive a free repair kit."* Our parser classifies Jugader as `photo_language = 1`, `proof_alternative_path = 1`, `proof_status = optional_or_alternative_proof`, `photo_mandatory = 0`, `physically_disabled = 0`, `remedy_duty_4cat = photo_optional_or_alternative`—preventing misclassification as mandatory photo-only destruction.
- **UVIPC Baby Gates (`RecallID 10148`, Recall 25-122, `1_Mkt_FD_China`, `strict_amazon_exclusive`):** *"contact Xianshengyouli via email ... for information on how to dispose of the product and submit proof of destruction to obtain a full refund."* Because UVIPC contains zero literal "photo" words, `photo_language = 0`, yet `destruction_proof = 1`, `destruction_proof_without_photo = 1`, `proof_status = mandatory_proof`, and `physically_disabled = 1`—preventing false-negative leakage on unspecified destruction-proof demands (catching all 7 such 2022–2026 notices).

To evaluate parser accuracy rigorously and measure group-specific misclassification, we draw a deterministic stratified sample of **$N = 150$ recalls (2022–2026, `seed = 20261128`)** spanning channel $\times$ entity group (55 `1_Mkt_FD_China`, 49 `3_Mkt_True_US`, 46 DTC/Omnichannel/Brick-and-Mortar) $\times$ year (29 in 2022, 28 in 2023, 32 in 2024, 34 in 2025, 27 in 2026) $\times$ rare remedy and channel clauses (all 5 ambiguous Amazon notices, all 7 non-photo destruction-proof notices, 10 optional/alternative proof notices, 12 permanent-marking-only, 12 mandatory photo-only, 10 hazardous self-disposal, plus the 5 benchmark cases: Jugader, UVIPC, Anker, OCOOPA, TADAKAZU). Every notice's verbatim `Retailers` text, verbatim `Remedies` text, CPSC URL, parser flags, adjudicated labels, and discrepancy notes are published in `tables/table_remedy_duty_coding_audit.csv`. **Transparency disclosure:** Because this validation reflects an **automated rule-plus-author structured audit** (line-by-line inspection by the author against frozen `parser_lib.py` outputs), it is **not** an independent double-blind multi-coder adjudication; independent double-blind human coding remains necessary prior to operational or causal deployment.

Reading all 150 notices verbatim uncovered **24/150 genuine parser discrepancies** (5/55 in `1_Mkt_FD_China`, 11/49 in `3_Mkt_True_US`, and 8/46 in other channels; Table 2A):
- **Channel classification (`amazon_channel_3way`):** 0.9933 accuracy (149/150), with a single false multi-channel assignment caused by a missing space typo in CPSC's source text (`RecallID 10064`: *"Online exclusively atwww.Amazon.com"*).
- **Photo wording (`photo_language` and `photo_mandatory`):** `photo_language` achieves 0.9933 accuracy (F1 = 0.9946; 0 FP, 1 FN in `1_Mkt_FD_China` from `RecallID 10269`, *"email the Amazon order number and a photo showing the remote was destroyed"*). `photo_mandatory` achieves 0.9667 (F1 = 0.9682), catching 2 China-direct FPs on `proof_alternative_path` (10054, 10934, where *"or"* joined order/receipt details alongside a mandatory photo) and 2 other-channel FNs on multi-sentence store-return alternatives (9426, 9445).
- **Differential False Negatives on Action & Return Clauses in `3_Mkt_True_US`:** Critically, heterogeneous domestic legal wording creates markedly higher false-negative rates inside `3_Mkt_True_US` ($n = 49$) than inside standardized `1_Mkt_FD_China` templates ($n = 55$): `direct_return` has 0/4 FNs in China-direct (FNR = 0.0%) vs. **3/7 FNs (FNR = 42.9%)** in `3_Mkt_True_US` (e.g., `RecallID 10108` *"prepaid FedEx shipping label"*, 9432, 9736); `physically_disabled` has 1 FP (10571: *"destroy the toy by writing 'RECALLED'"*) and 1 FN (10269) in China-direct (FPR = 3.1%, FNR = 4.3%) vs. **5/14 FNs (FNR = 35.7%)** in `3_Mkt_True_US` (9430 *"nets cut"*, 9736, 10187, 10238 *"destroyed product"*, 10639 *"cut the USB cable"*); and `permanent_marking` has 0 errors in China-direct vs. **4/9 FNs (FNR = 44.4%)** in `3_Mkt_True_US` (9430, 10478, 10619, 10963).

### 4.1 Affected Hazardous-Product Notice Coverage (3,947,390 Units), Batch Concentration, and Full-Notice Warnings

A major consumer-policy concern arises when recalls involving fire-prone or toxic materials—specifically **hazard-gated lithium-ion batteries, button/coin cells, and lead/phthalate/chemical products** (excluding mechanical brake or frame e-bike defects)—omit corporate take-back options. Across 2022–2026, 74 `1_Mkt_FD_China` notices fall into these three hazardous categories, and **0.0% offer a firm direct-return or prepaid hazmat mail-back program** (compared to 17.0% in brick-and-mortar and 22.2% in omnichannel). Our text screen flags **67/74 notices** assigning consumer self-disposal without a direct return option, covering **3,947,390 notice-listed affected units** (65/67 notices containing literal "dispose/disposal" wording cover 3,944,590 units; Table 5 and Figure 4A).

**Three strict measurement boundaries govern interpretation of this 3,947,390 figure:**
1. **Notice Coverage, Never Observed Waste Stream:** The total sums affected inventory printed on CPSC recall notices. Public notices do not report how many units remained in firm inventory versus consumer homes, how many households participated in the recall, or where discarded items ultimately entered the waste stream. It is strictly **affected hazardous-product notice coverage under consumer self-disposal terms**, **not** an observed municipal waste flow or confirmed trash leakage.
2. **Extreme Two-Batch Concentration (67.34%):** Two large multi-channel electronics recalls account for two-thirds of the entire total: **OCOOPA Direct rechargeable hand warmers** (`RecallID 10895`, Recall 26-659, 1,500,000 units, 38.00%, sold on Amazon, Ocoopa.com, Ocoopa.net, and Walmart.com) and **Anker PowerCore 10000 power banks** (`RecallID 10311`, Recall 25-338, 1,158,000 units, 29.34%, sold on Anker, Amazon, Newegg, and eBay) together total **2,658,000 units (67.34%)**, and adding Charmast (488,000) and Baseus (132,000) reaches 83.04%.
3. **Complete-Notice Disposal Warnings (54/67 Covering 99.08% of Units):** Searching only the `Remedies` field detects explicit local/state HHW or trash-prohibition warnings on 52/67 notices (2,697,571 units, 68.34%). Expanding the screen to the **complete displayed notice (`Remedies + Description + Hazards`)** captures **54/67 notices (80.60%)** covering **3,910,951 affected units (99.08%)**—because Anker's notice (10311) places its specific lithium-ion trash prohibition outside the primary remedy block while OCOOPA's notice (10895) explicitly forbids placing defective lithium-ion warmers in household trash, curbside recycling bins, or retail battery-drop boxes and directs consumers to contact municipal HHW centers. What the census establishes is therefore not careless omission of disposal warnings, but rather the complete absence (0.0%) of firm-operated take-back channels across nearly four million recalled hazardous units, leaving lawful HHW transport entirely to household execution.

### 4.2 *In re Amazon.com* (Docket No. 21-2) as an Institutional Case Study

In *In re Amazon.com, Inc.* (CPSC Docket No. 21-2), the CPSC issued its unanimous **July 30, 2024 Decision and Order** classifying Amazon as a statutory "distributor" specifically for **Fulfilled-by-Amazon (`FBA`)** third-party merchandise (U.S. CPSC 2024), followed by its **January 16, 2025 Decision and Order Approving Notification and Action Plan** specifying product-specific direct consumer notice and refund protocols for the adjudicated FBA items (U.S. CPSC 2025; upheld on summary judgment November 24, 2026 in *Amazon.com, Inc. v. CPSC* 2026).

We treat *In re Amazon.com* strictly as an **institutional case study**—and **not** as a causal natural experiment—for a decisive measurement reason verified directly in our archive: across all 601 Amazon-mentioning online-marketplace notices in 2022–2026, **explicit "Fulfilled by Amazon" or "Fulfillment by Amazon" phrasing appears in 0/601 notices (0.00%)**. A separate acronym search finds exactly 1/601 notice containing a standalone "FBA" token—solely inside product model-name suffixes on the **TADAKAZU baby lounger** recall (`RecallID 10317`, Recall 25-345), which does not establish fulfillment status. Because public CPSC announcements pool FBA and merchant-fulfilled listings, time-series shifts cannot isolate the causal impact of an FBA-scoped order from concurrent statutory rule sweeps (STURDY Act, Reese's Law) or broader platform growth.

With that scope caveat explicit, Figure 4B and Table 5 Panel B verify that the post-2024 descriptive volume expansion is **not** an artifact of our earlier multi-channel mislabeling. Across all $T = 23$ complete quarters (2021Q1–2026Q3; excluding partial 2026Q4) using Newey–West (1987) HAC inference ($L = 4$), annual **verbatim `strict_amazon_exclusive`** notices expanded more than fivefold from 42 (2024) to 115 (2025) and 218 (2026, through November 19), producing a sharp descriptive post-July-2024 slope break relative to brick-and-mortar of **+0.1734/qtr** ($t = +4.99$, $p = 5.98e-07$)—closely tracking the broad `amazon_only_mkt` series (+0.1831/qtr, $t = +8.00$; Supplementary Appendix C confirms persistence under STURDY Act and Reese's Law dummies and monthly frequency). Critically, however, expanded notice volume did not restore firm-operated physical returns: inside `1_Mkt_FD_China`, `photo_verify` wording rose from 60.2% pre-January 16, 2025 to 89.4% post-order (FE +31.01 pp, $t = +5.73$). The *In re Amazon.com* order clarifies why notice wording varies with product-specific administrative remedies—and demonstrates that intermediary notice obligations alone do not automatically replace consumer-side verification and self-disposal duties with domestic reverse logistics.

## 5. Institutional Implications, Open Data Contribution, and Methodological Limitations

### 5.1 Consumer-Policy Implications for CPSC Notice Standardization and Platform Governance

1. **Structured Machine-Readable Notice Duty Schemas:** Today, critical consumer-protection distinctions—whether a photograph is mandatory or optional alongside an order number (Jugader), whether physical disablement is required without specifying proof format (UVIPC), whether permanent marking alone suffices (Anker, OCOOPA), and whether lithium-ion trash prohibitions appear in the `Remedies` block versus the `Description` block—are buried inside unstructured prose. CPSC should add standardized, structured fields to its SaferProducts schema explicitly recording: (a) *proof modality and mandatory vs. alternative pathways*; (b) *whether physical product disablement is required*; (c) *whether a prepaid return or domestic drop-off option exists*; (d) *fulfillment mode (FBA/WFS vs. merchant-fulfilled)*; and (e) *verbatim retailer channel exclusivity*. Standardizing these fields would immediately make consumer compliance burdens auditable across platforms.
2. **Unlocking Platform First-Party Transaction Records to Lower Consumer Friction:** Individual 3P sellers often lack direct access to buyer identities, motivating photographic verification to deter duplicate claims. Yet the **platform itself holds verified first-party order histories** ($\theta_{\text{Platform}} \to 1$). As *In re Amazon.com* illustrates for adjudicated FBA items, authorizing or requiring marketplaces to pair automated account-linked refund crediting with seller reserve/escrow clawbacks can reduce reliance on cumbersome consumer verification steps—particularly for low-cost non-durable items where optional order-number confirmation suffices, while reserving physical disablement strictly for high-recirculation safety hazards.
3. **Addressing Hazardous-Product Take-Back Gaps and Traceability Infrastructure:** For recalled lithium-ion batteries, button cells, and toxic chemicals, relying exclusively on consumer self-transport to municipal HHW facilities (0.0% corporate take-back across 74 China-direct notices covering 3,947,390 affected units) shifts safety and logistical burdens onto households and local municipalities. Consumer-policy frameworks should explore mandatory cross-border seller recall bonds, platform-funded hazmat collection partnerships, verifiable GS1 UPC/GTIN registration (0.2% today vs. 7.3% omnichannel), verified domestic customer-service phone/domain requirements (replacing free consumer webmail), and automated CPSC–Health Canada–PROFECO recall synchronization (2.0% joint recalls vs. 34.8% brick-and-mortar).

### 5.2 Reusable Open Measurement Dataset

Beyond its empirical findings, this package contributes a fully reproducible, SHA-256-verified public data resource suitable for consumer-policy analysis and operational recall data-set extensions: (i) the frozen 27-year CPSC census (`data/cpsc_recalls_2000_2026.json`, $N = 8,386$); (ii) the enriched notice-level table (`data/cpsc_parsed_2000_2026.csv`) containing verbatim 3-way Amazon channel classifications, 5-dimensional structured remedy-duty labels, full-notice HHW warning indicators, and CPSC source URLs; (iii) the verifiable $N = 150$ stratified coding audit (`tables/table_remedy_duty_coding_audit.csv`) with verbatim retailer/remedy quotes and explicit adjudication notes; and (iv) reusable Python code implementing full-rank CR1/WCB econometrics and Clopper–Pearson bounded misclassification sensitivity (`scripts/parser_lib.py`, `scripts/econometrics_lib.py`, `scripts/09_remedy_duty_audit_and_channel_v4.py`).

### 5.3 Methodological Limitations and Research Agenda

Four disciplined limitations bound our conclusions and define the next stage of empirical research:
1. **Notice-Level Duties vs. Realized Fulfillment and Compliance Costs:** Public CPSC notices reveal written duties and options—they contain zero observations of 30-day or 90-day consumer completion rates, firm remedy-processing costs, or verified compliant disposal. Extending from notice design to operational performance requires matching CPSC recall numbers to corrective-action progress reports obtained via Freedom of Information Act requests—following Raithel et al. (2024), who obtained 217 CPSC progress reports and documented statutory confidentiality selection—or partnering with platforms to evaluate pre-scheduled take-back rollouts or randomized communication and regulator-approved remedy pathways (cf. Fang et al. 2026), with pre-registered minimum meaningful differences.
2. **Automated Rule-Plus-Author Audit vs. Independent Double-Blind Human Adjudication:** Our stratified $N = 150$ audit transparently documents 24/150 parser discrepancies and propagates group-specific error rates into bounded sensitivity intervals, showing clearly where differential U.S.-entity false negatives explain raw action-clause gaps. Nevertheless, because initial labels came from frozen parser outputs inspected by a single author, independent double-blind multi-coder adjudication (with inter-rater reliability $\kappa$ and blind tie-breaking) remains essential before deploying these text flags for formal regulatory evaluation or causal mechanism tests.
3. **Observational Channel Associations and Small-Sample Precision:** All regressions estimate conditional observational associations among published recalls rather than causal treatment effects; we lack a denominator of total SKUs sold by channel and cannot rule out differential CPSC enforcement intensity across domestic versus overseas sellers. Moreover, once multi-channel sellers are separated out, the strictly Amazon-exclusive common-support comparison retains only 52 U.S.-entity notices ($N = 236$), rendering narrow photo-language contrasts imprecise with wide CIs crossing zero.
4. **Unobserved Entity Affiliations and Fulfillment Mode:** Structuring seller origin from public notice fields classifies what regulators disclose on the record, not undisclosed parent-subsidiary networks, U.S. warehouses, or FBA versus merchant-fulfilled status (0/601 explicit FBA mentions). Future administrative disclosure reforms capturing fulfillment channel and beneficial corporate ownership would directly resolve both blind spots.
