## Abstract

Classical operations, marketing, and law-and-economics scholarship on consumer product recalls assumes a domestic supply chain: a U.S.-domiciled brand or importer operates physical store return counters, toll-free hotlines, and reverse-logistics repair networks. Over `2015–2026`, cross-border third-party (3P) e-commerce platforms disintermediated the domestic importer. Using the complete 27-year U.S. CPSC recall census (`2000–2026`, $N = 8{,}386$), we document a structural regime shift: Marketplace-Only recalls expanded 22-fold from $2.62\%$ in 2015 to $58.30\%$ in 2026, while Foreign-Direct China recalls listing zero U.S. legal entities surged from $0.00\%$ in 2015 to $41.70\%$ in 2026. To explain how asset-light cross-border sellers execute recalls without U.S. reverse logistics, we develop a closed-form principal–agent and reverse-logistics contract model incorporating buyer-identity observability ($\theta$), consumer compliance hassle ($k_P$), seller legal liability ($\lambda$), and potential consumer-side hazardous-disposal risk ($e_{\text{haz}}$). We test the model's four comparative-static predictions strictly inside the post-emergence contract era (`2022–2026`, $N = 1{,}822$, $G = 1{,}414$ firm clusters) using conditional observational regressions under full-rank ultra-saturated fixed effects (`10 Price-Decile + 10 Unit-Decile + 15 Product-Category + 9 Hazard + Made-in-China + Year-Quarter FE`) with CR1 $t(G-1)$ and Rademacher Wild Cluster Bootstrap ($B = 999$) inference. First, conditional on observables, `Mkt_FD_China` is associated with a $+35.35\text{ pp}$ higher rate of photographic **Proof-of-Destruction** contracts (`photo_verify`, $t = +8.83$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$; Logit $\text{OR} = 5.41\times$), a $+25.48\text{ pp}$ higher rate of explicit physical mutilation/marking ($t = +7.07$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), and a $-35.07\text{ pp}$ drop in direct returns ($t = -9.19$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.001$), including a $+29.03\text{ pp}$ ($t = +7.23$) within-marketplace gap over verified U.S.-entity 3P sellers (`3_Mkt_True_US`). Disaggregating the $371$ cross-border `photo_verify` notices shows that $253/371$ ($68.2\%$) pair photography with physical destruction ($202$, $54.4\%$) or permanent marking/defacing ($51$, $13.8\%$), while $118/371$ ($31.8\%$) require photographic proof without triggering the physical-mutilation rule ($26$ local hazardous-waste/recycling instructions only; $92$ photo-only). Consistent with moral-hazard value monotonicity, the `photo_verify` association reaches $+45.17\text{ pp}$ ($t = +6.72$, within-marketplace $+40.70\text{ pp}$) across all products priced $\ge \$100$ ($N = 794$, including apparel and chemical categories) and remains $+41.39\text{ pp}$ ($t = +5.44$, within-marketplace $+36.57\text{ pp}$) in a strict durables ($\ge \$100$) subsample ($N = 685$) excluding apparel/clothing, textiles/bedding, personal care, and chemical/consumable categories; it also survives `Fine-Product-Noun` $\times$ `Year FE` ($+30.47\text{ pp}$), within `Made in China` ($+27.88\text{ pp}$), shell-front LLC adjustment ($+30.79\text{ pp}$ within-marketplace), and mediation for contact collapse ($+29.95\text{ pp}$). Second, consistent with our first-party buyer-observability shield, pure `DTC_Online_Only` brands—which also lack physical store counters—show an imprecisely estimated null on `photo_verify` ($+3.38\text{ pp}$, $95\%\text{ CI }[-6.48, +13.24]\text{ pp}$, $t = +0.67$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.523$; Logit $\text{OR} = 1.04\times$), turning negative in both products priced $\ge \$100$ ($-6.09\text{ pp}$) and strict durables $\ge \$100$ ($-4.41\text{ pp}$) and significantly negative on free webmail ($-4.73\text{ pp}$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.047$). Third, Proof-of-Destruction generalizes across every 3P platform (even stronger off-Amazon at $+43.36\text{ pp}$ vs. $+34.23\text{ pp}$), diffuses sequentially from cross-border pioneers (`46.7%` in 2022 $\to$ `87.8%` in 2026) into domestic brick-and-mortar recalls (`9.6%` $\to$ `34.8%`, advancing sevenfold faster in China-made imports at $+11.33\text{ pp/yr}$ vs. $+1.55\text{ pp/yr}$ non-China), and coincides with severe contact-infrastructure ($+61.01\text{ pp}$ free webmail; $+57.07\text{ pp}$ zero phone hotline) and traceability collapse ($-6.06\text{ pp}$ GS1 UPCs; $-15.25\text{ pp}$ joint Canada/Mexico recalls). Fourth, around *In re Amazon.com* (July 30, 2024 Distributor Decision and January 16, 2025 Final Remedy Order, upheld November 24, 2026—legally governing Fulfilled-by-Amazon [`FBA`] products, whereas public CPSC notices pool all Amazon-exclusive announcements), Newey–West HAC time-series models document a sharp descriptive structural acceleration in `log(Amazon_Only / Brick_and_Mortar)` ($+0.1831\text{/qtr}$, $t = +8.00$; $+0.1999\text{/qtr}$, $t = +8.18$ controlling for STURDY Act and Reese's Law; $+0.0525\text{/mo}$, $t = +4.40$ on $T = 70$ complete months), while `photo_verify` *entrenched* post-order (`60.2%` $\to$ `89.4%`, FE $+31.01\text{ pp}$)—with $3{,}947{,}390$ recalled hazardous-product units (lithium-ion, button-cell, and lead/chemical items; $67.3\%$ concentrated in OCOOPA and Anker) assigned to consumer self-disposal remedies and $0.0\%$ firm direct-return programs.

## 1. Introduction

For four decades, empirical and analytical research in operations management, marketing, and economics has studied consumer product recalls within a vertically integrated domestic supply chain (Jarrell and Peltzman 1985; Chen et al. 2009; Hora et al. 2011; Thirumalai and Sinha 2011; Freedman et al. 2012; Kalaignanam et al. 2013; Ni et al. 2014; Wowak and Boone 2015; Eilert et al. 2017; Liu et al. 2017; Shah et al. 2017; Ball et al. 2018; Mukherjee and Sinha 2018; Astvansh et al. 2022; Mao et al. 2022). In that canonical architecture, a U.S.-domiciled brand, importer, or national retailer serves as the legal and physical counterparty to both federal regulators and injured households. When a defect is detected, the recalling firm leverages physical store counters, prepaid domestic mail-back networks, and toll-free call centers to retrieve defective units, verify eligibility, and neutralize safety hazards.

Over the past decade, global e-commerce marketplaces (`Amazon.com` 3P/FBA, `Walmart.com` 3P, `Temu.com`, `Shein.com`, `Wayfair.com`, `AliExpress`, and `TikTok Shop`) have reorganized consumer retail by replacing the traditional agency/reseller intermediary structure (Hagiu and Wright 2015; Tian et al. 2018) with **direct cross-border seller fulfillment**. Today, thousands of overseas factories and trading firms located in Shenzhen, Guangzhou, Hangzhou, Ningbo, Yiwu, and Hong Kong list directly to U.S. households without establishing a U.S. subsidiary, brick-and-mortar retail partner, domestic reverse-logistics hub, or U.S. customer-service phone center. While recent computer-science work curates CPSC text and imagery for multimodal hazard prediction (Bolanos et al. 2025) and consumer-advocacy analyses document rising CPSC unilateral safety warnings when overseas vendors refuse to cooperate (Consumer Federation of America 2025), **no prior empirical or analytical study examines how cross-border platform disintermediation reshapes the equilibrium contract design, contact infrastructure, regulatory enforcement, and environmental incidence of formal U.S. product recalls.**

Using the complete 27-year official U.S. CPSC SaferProducts Recall Census (`2000–2026`, $N = 8{,}386$ recalls), we uncover a striking institutional transformation. Between 2000 and 2014, Marketplace-Only recalls averaged below $1.5\%$ of federal actions and cross-border Foreign-Direct China recalls—listing zero U.S. importer, distributor, or manufacturer—were virtually nonexistent ($0.0\%$ in 2015). By 2026, Marketplace-Only recalls rose 22-fold to **$58.30\%$** ($309/530$ recalls), and Foreign-Direct China recalls surged to **$41.70\%$** ($221/530$ recalls). Crucially, this shift spawned a new equilibrium remedy contract that barely existed before 2022 ($0.0\%$ in 2015–2017 and 2019; $0.39\%$ in 2018 and 2020; $2.74\%$ in 2021): **Consumer Self-Destruction with Photographic Verification ("Proof-of-Destruction")**. Under this contract, households are instructed to sever power cords or restraint straps, cut garments or mattresses in half, write *"RECALLED"* in permanent marker across the product, photograph the mutilated object, email the image to a free consumer webmail address (`@gmail.com`, `@outlook.com`, `@qq.com`, `@163.com`), and dispose of the defective—and frequently toxic or flammable—item themselves. By 2026, **$62.45\%$** of *all* U.S. consumer product recalls employed photographic verification, led by **$87.8\%$** of Foreign-Direct China marketplace recalls.

Why did Proof-of-Destruction emerge specifically among cross-border 3P sellers, persist inside both all products priced $\ge \$100$ and strict durables ($\ge \$100$), bypass pure online-only direct-to-consumer (DTC) brands, and subsequently diffuse into U.S. brick-and-mortar chains (`9.6%` in 2022 $\to$ `34.8%` in 2026)? Section 2 develops a tractable principal–agent and reverse-logistics contract model demonstrating that Proof-of-Destruction solves a three-way contracting squeeze: (i) prohibitive trans-Pacific reverse-logistics costs ($c_{\text{rev}}$) rule out physical returns; (ii) platform intermediation masks first-party buyer identity (low $\theta$), exposing unverified refunds to anonymous moral-hazard fraud and hazardous item retention ($\alpha(1-\theta)p$); and (iii) limited domestic tort and reputational exposure—the classic *judgment-proof problem* (Shavell 1986)—lowers the foreign seller's private penalty ($\lambda_{\text{FD}} < \lambda_{\text{US}}$) for hassle-induced consumer non-participation ($q_P < q_R < q_0$). Conversely, first-party DTC online-only brands ($\theta \to 1$) verify buyer records directly without physical stores, eliminating anonymous moral hazard without shifting destruction hassle onto consumers.

We examine every comparative-static prediction of the model across four empirical pillars, strictly respecting era discipline (`2022–2026` main contract estimation, avoiding zero-variation pre-2022 pooling), checked via a dual author-reviewed parser consistency audit (150-recall stratified census sample plus a completely disjoint 50-recall out-of-sample `2025–2026` consistency batch; independent double-blind human annotation remains required prior to journal submission), and transparently reporting every empirical null alongside our headline findings.

## 2. Formal Principal–Agent and Reverse-Logistics Contract Model

### 2.1 Model Setup

Consider a firm recalling a defective product held by a unit mass of affected U.S. consumers, each owed refund or replacement value $p > 0$. The recalling firm selects a remedy contract $m \in \{R, D/U, P\}$ to minimize expected per-unit total recall cost $C_m$:

1. **Consumer Compliance and Hassle Cost:** Completing remedy $m$ imposes private consumer effort, mutilation, and disposal hassle $k_m \ge 0$, yielding recall participation probability $q(k_m) = q_0 - \gamma k_m \in (0, 1]$, where $q_0 \in (0, 1]$ is frictionless response and $\gamma > 0$. Direct first-party or unverified refunds entail minimal hassle ($k_{D/U} \approx 0 \Rightarrow q_{D/U} = q_0$). Physical store or prepaid mail-back returns impose return hassle $k_R > 0 \Rightarrow q_R = q_0 - \gamma k_R$. Proof-of-Destruction requires physical mutilation, photography, email submission, and local disposal, generating the highest hassle ($k_P > k_R > 0 \Rightarrow q_P = q_0 - \gamma k_P < q_R < q_0$).
2. **Buyer-Identity Observability and Moral Hazard:** Let $\theta \in [0, 1]$ denote the share of buyers whose identity and order history are directly verifiable by the recalling firm. Direct-to-consumer online-only brands own first-party customer databases ($\theta_{\text{DTC}} \to 1$), whereas physical cash/store retailers and platform-intermediated 3P sellers face low buyer observability ($\theta \ll 1$). Under an unverified/direct refund without physical return or destruction ($D/U$), unobserved buyers create moral-hazard claim inflation $\alpha(1-\theta)p$ (fraudulent claims or claiming refunds while retaining/reselling hazardous units) and residual unsafe-retention hazard $\phi(1-\theta)$, where $\alpha > 0, \phi > 0$. Notice that as $\theta \to 1$, contract $D/U$ seamlessly converges to a frictionless **Direct First-Party Automated Remedy ($D$)** with zero moral-hazard leakage.
3. **Reverse Logistics, Audit Friction, and Seller Liability:** Physical return ($R$) eliminates moral hazard and hazardous household disposal at per-unit seller reverse-logistics cost $c_{\text{rev}} \ge 0$ (low for U.S. brick-and-mortar and omnichannel chains with store return counters; prohibitive for asset-light overseas sellers lacking U.S. warehouses or facing lithium-ion hazmat shipping bans). Proof-of-Destruction ($P$) eliminates $c_{\text{rev}}$ and neutralizes item retention via photographic verification at per-claim seller audit and institutional compliance cost $a > 0$. Unremedied units expose the firm to expected legal, regulatory, and reputational liability $\lambda > 0$ per unit left in consumer homes, where domestic U.S. firms face substantial asset-backed liability ($\lambda_{\text{US}}$) while cross-border asset-light sellers are partially judgment-proof ($\lambda_{\text{FD}} < \lambda_{\text{US}}$; Shavell 1986).

Expected per-unit seller costs across the three contract regimes are therefore:

$$C_R = q_R (p + c_{\text{rev}}) + \lambda (1 - q_R)$$

$$C_{D/U}(\theta) = q_0 p + \alpha (1 - \theta) p + \lambda \big[(1 - q_0) + \phi(1 - \theta)\big]$$

$$C_P = q_P (p + a) + \lambda (1 - q_P)$$

### 2.2 Equilibrium Cutoffs and Four Falsifiable Propositions

Solving the pairwise indifference conditions $C_P = C_R$ and $C_P = C_{D/U}(\theta)$ yields exact closed-form adoption thresholds:

$$c_{\text{rev}}^*(p, a, \lambda) \equiv \frac{q_P}{q_R} a + \frac{q_R - q_P}{q_R}(\lambda - p)$$

$$\theta^*(p, a, \lambda) \equiv 1 - \frac{q_P a - (q_0 - q_P)(p - \lambda)}{\alpha p + \lambda \phi}$$

Proof-of-Destruction ($P$) is the unique private equilibrium whenever **both** $c_{\text{rev}} > c_{\text{rev}}^*$ and $\theta < \theta^*$. Four sharp comparative statics follow immediately and map directly to our four empirical pillars:

- **Proposition 1 (Cross-Border Reverse-Logistics Cutoff & Value Monotonicity):** Because $\partial c_{\text{rev}}^* / \partial \lambda = (q_R - q_P)/q_R > 0$, judgment-proof cross-border sellers ($\lambda_{\text{FD}} < \lambda_{\text{US}}$) face a strictly lower cutoff $c_{\text{rev}}^*$ simultaneously with prohibitive trans-Pacific $c_{\text{rev}}$. Moreover, $\partial (C_{D/U} - C_P)/\partial p = \alpha(1-\theta) + (q_0 - q_P) > 0$ and $\partial c_{\text{rev}}^*/\partial p = -(q_R - q_P)/q_R < 0$: higher item value $p$ *amplifies* both anonymous moral hazard and hassle-induced refund breakage, strengthening Proof-of-Destruction inside high-value items ($p \ge \$100$), including strict durables.
- **Proposition 2 (First-Party Buyer-Observability Shield — The DTC Shield):** Holding physical store infrastructure absent ($c_{\text{rev}}$ elevated), high first-party buyer observability ($\theta \to 1 > \theta^*$) allows `DTC_Online_Only` brands to execute verified direct remedies ($D/U$) without physical store counters. Consequently, `DTC_Online_Only` avoids the cross-border Proof-of-Destruction surge—remaining statistically indistinguishable from (and on high-value or mandatory-rule recalls, below) Brick-and-Mortar despite zero physical stores.
- **Proposition 3 (Institutional Audit-Cost Diffusion & Contact-Infrastructure Disinvestment):** As CPSC standardizes Proof-of-Destruction templates over `2022–2026`, lowering per-claim institutional/audit friction $a_t$, $\partial c_{\text{rev}}^*/\partial a = q_P/q_R > 0$ and $\partial \theta^*/\partial a < 0$ monotonically expand the $P$ equilibrium region—pulling first U.S.-entity 3P sellers (`3_Mkt_True_US`) and then domestic brick-and-mortar retailers (especially for lower-margin China-sourced SKUs) across the threshold (**Pioneer-to-Domestic Diffusion**). Simultaneously, asset-light foreign sellers underinvest in fixed domestic customer-service capital ($F_{\text{phone}}, F_{\text{domain}}$), relying on free consumer webmail and omitting U.S. hotlines.
- **Proposition 4 (Intermediary Liability Surge & Consumer Self-Disposal Wedge):** Treating refund payouts $q_m p$ as a pure pecuniary transfer between seller and household, let true social harm per unremedied unit be $H > \lambda$ and let $e_{\text{haz}} \ge 0$ denote expected downstream environmental and safety risk per hazardous-product unit assigned to consumer self-disposal (e.g., if defective lithium-ion batteries, button cells, or toxic chemicals are improperly discarded outside specialized hazardous-waste channels). With average per-complying-consumer hassle $k_m$, the net social cost difference between $P$ and $R$ is:

$$W_P - W_R = (q_P a - q_R c_{\text{rev}}) + (q_P k_P - q_R k_R) + H(q_R - q_P) + q_P e_{\text{haz}}$$

Whenever private logistics savings fall short of consumer hassle, unremedied safety loss, and consumer-side hazardous-disposal risk, private adoption of $P$ is socially excessive. Critically, imposing intermediary distributor liability on a marketplace (*In re Amazon.com*, 2024–2025, governing FBA merchandise) raises recall-initiation enforcement probability $\pi_{\text{enf}}$—expanding observed recall volume—yet without a domestic reverse-logistics funding mandate, leaves $c_{\text{rev}}$ prohibitive and $P$ privately optimal, thereby scaling up hazardous-product recalls assigned to consumer self-disposal remedies ($N_{\text{recalls}} \cdot q_P e_{\text{haz}}$).

Under calibrated durable-product parameters ($p = \$60$, $\lambda_{\text{US}} = \$140$, $\lambda_{\text{FD}} = \$35$, $q_0 = 0.780$, $q_R = 0.684$, $q_P = 0.540$, $\alpha = 0.35$, $\phi = 0.25$, $H = \$240$, $e_{\text{haz}} = \$18$), reducing institutional audit cost from $a_{2022} = \$18$ to $a_{2026} = \$6$ shifts the domestic cutoff $c_{\text{rev}}^*/p$ from $0.517$ to $0.360$ and raises $\theta^*$ from $0.484$ to $0.599$ (Figure 2A), pulling `Mkt_True_US` into $P$ while keeping `DTC_Online_Only` ($\theta = 0.88$) shielded inside $D/U$. For a judgment-proof foreign seller ($\lambda_{\text{FD}} = \$35 < p$), $c_{\text{rev}}^*/p = -0.009 < 0$ and $\theta^* = 1.093 > 1$: Proof-of-Destruction privately dominates across the entire domain while generating a positive social cost wedge ($W_P - W_R = +\$22.07\text{/unit}$ vs. private seller savings $+\$31.14\text{/unit}$ at $c_{\text{rev}} = \$45$).

## 3. Data, Clean 6-Archetype Taxonomy, and Author-Reviewed Parser Consistency Audit

### 3.1 Complete 27-Year Official CPSC Census (`2000–2026`)

We retrieve the universe of official U.S. consumer product recalls from the open HTTP-200 CPSC SaferProducts REST Web Services API (`https://www.saferproducts.gov/RestWebServices/Recall?format=json`) across `2000-01-04` through `2026-11-19`, strictly respecting all federal access controls. Deduplicating by `RecallID` yields **$N = 8{,}386$ unique federal recalls** (`data/cpsc_recalls_2000_2026.json`, `23.41 MB`, SHA-256 `db445bb9199cd84ef52d83ef2715dccb96cd8b1667f47c06fdebf95c420efffc`).

### 3.2 Seven Forensic Parser Upgrades and Clean 6-Archetype Supply-Chain Taxonomy

Constructing reliable contract and entity variables from administrative recall text requires addressing seven structural pitfalls caught via forensic schema inspection:

1. **Four-Field Entity Extraction & Platform Co-Listing Guard (Zero Late-2026 Schema Failure):** Beginning in late 2026, $24$ of $530$ CPSC recalls (`4.53%`) populated `Importers = Manufacturers = Distributors = []` while storing the recalling foreign or domestic legal entity inside `Retailers` (e.g., `RecallID 11067` *"Zhongshan Haimao Cross-border Information Technology Co., Ltd., dba Kidsview, of China"*; `RecallID 11068` *"Guangzhou Shian Trading Co., Ltd., dba CHE-US/CHESLICR, of China"*). Our frozen parser (`scripts/parser_lib.py`) extracts entity locations across **all four structured fields** (`Importers + Manufacturers + Distributors + Retailers`), rescuing $23$ late-2026 recalls cleanly, handles multi-comma and `"of the United Kingdom"` foreign addresses (`Laval, QC, Canada`) and `"of"`-less U.S. addresses (`Moulton, Alabama`), and prevents pure 3P platform corporate offices (`AliExpress, of San Mateo, CA`; `Woot, of Carrollton, TX`; distributor-only `Amazon, of Seattle, Wash.`) co-listed alongside Chinese 3P sellers from masking cross-border foreign-direct origin (while preserving genuine Amazon 1P private-label recalls such as *Amazon Basics* as `3_Mkt_True_US`).
2. **Domain-Stripped Physical Store Classification & Pure DTC Isolation:** Naive keyword matching classifies `Target.com`, `Walmart.com`, `HomeDepot.com`, or `Costco.com` as brick-and-mortar stores, while overly narrow store regexes dump branded physical stores (`Helly Hansen clothing stores`, `MoMA Design Stores`, `Rooms To Go stores`, `Independent bike shops nationwide`) into online-only DTC. We exclude late-2026 pure entity entries from channel strings, strip all URL domains and online-store phrases (`TikTok Shop`, `online retailers`, `App Store`, `not sold in stores`), detect physical chains, branded stores, independent shops/outlets/galleries, and commercial distributors cleanly, and separate multi-brand online retail portals (`Costco.com`, `HomeDepot.com`, `BestBuy.com`, `QVC`/`HSN`) from pure first-party brand DTC websites.
3. **Clean 6-Archetype Taxonomy + Shell-Front Sensitivity Audit:** Every classified recall maps cleanly into one of six mutually exclusive supply-chain archetypes (with only $38$ recalls—$2.03\%$ of `2022–2026`—falling into `7_Other_Unclear`, such as promotional giveaways or unlocated unilateral alerts):
   - `1_Mkt_FD_China` ($N = 448$ in `2022–2026`): 3P Marketplace-Only with China/Hong Kong legal entity and **zero verified U.S. seller/importer entity**.
   - `2_Mkt_FD_NonChina` ($N = 26$): 3P Marketplace-Only with non-China foreign legal entity (Canada, UK, EU, Sweden, South Korea, Japan, Australia, Taiwan, Armenia, etc.) and zero U.S. entity.
   - `3_Mkt_True_US` ($N = 233$, $100.0\%$ verified U.S. entities): 3P Marketplace-Only with a verified U.S. importer, distributor, or manufacturer on record. For sensitivity testing, we split `3_Mkt_True_US` into verified legacy U.S. firms (`shell_suspected = 0`, $N = 181$) vs. suspected U.S.-registered cross-border shell LLCs (`shell_suspected = 1`, $N = 52$: U.S. address paired with free consumer webmail or phone-less generic e-commerce/trading LLC names).
   - `4_DTC_Online_Only` ($N = 102$): Pure first-party direct-to-consumer brand website only (zero 3P/multi-brand portals, zero physical brick-and-mortar stores).
   - `5_Omnichannel` ($N = 397$): Sold via both 3P marketplaces/online retail portals and physical brick-and-mortar stores.
   - `6_Brick_and_Mortar` ($N = 627$, reference base): Sold via physical stores/dealers without 3P marketplaces.
4. **Strict Era Discipline (`2022–2026` Main Contract Estimation):** Across the 27-year census, `photo_verify` stood at **$0.00\%$** in `2000–2017`, `0.39%` in 2018, `0.00%` in 2019, `0.39%` in 2020, and `2.74%` in 2021 before jumping to `12.67%` in 2022 and `62.45%` in 2026. Pooling zero-variation pre-2022 years into contract regressions confounds channel composition with temporal emergence. Therefore, **all primary contract, contact, traceability, and safety regressions run strictly on `2022–2026`** ($N = 1{,}822$ non-missing price & unit recalls across $G = 1{,}414$ firm clusters, covering $97.4\%$ of all $1{,}871$ recalls in `2022–2026`).
5. **Negation-Aware Return Parsing & Double-Count-Free Injury Accounting:** Our sentence-level `direct_return` parser rejects explicit return negations (`"Consumers should not return the recalled pajamas to the stores"`) while capturing store, dealer, manufacturer, and prepaid mail-back returns across multi-sentence remedies. Our sentence-level `parse_injuries_clean` parser protects abbreviations (`U.S.`, `Inc.`), strips subordinate breakdown clauses (`"including"`, `"consisting of"`, `"of which"`) and co-occurring property-damage subclauses without erasing burn/shock/cut injuries, guards against cumulative restatements and generic epidemiology headers, and retires uninformative small-batch injury-per-100k ratios in favor of clean `has_injury` and $\log(1 + \text{clean\_injuries})$.
6. **Hazard-Gated Hazardous-Product Self-Disposal & Traceability Indicators:** We code structured `ProductUPCs` (`has_upc`), joint Health Canada / PROFECO Mexico coordination (`has_inconjunction`), pre-recall duration (`months_on_sale`), mandatory federal safety-rule violations (`mandatory_rule`: STURDY Act, Reese's Law, VGB Pool & Spa Safety Act, Flammability, Infant Sleep, Lead/Phthalates, CPSIA/FHSA/PPPA), and three hazardous-product classes (`haz_lithium`, `haz_button`, `haz_chemical_lead`) where lithium-ion classification is hazard-gated (explicit lithium-ion mention or thermal/battery hazard—excluding mechanical brake/bolt/seat-post e-bike recalls!) and consumer self-disposal captures both verb and noun forms (`dispose`/`disposal`) crossed with `direct_return = 0`.
7. **Dual Author-Reviewed Parser Consistency Audit (150-Recall Stratified + 50-Recall Disjoint OOS `2025–2026`):** We conduct a seeded 150-recall stratified author-reviewed parser consistency audit across all six `2022–2026` archetypes, suspected shell LLCs, late-2026 retailer-nested rescues, multi-injury recalls, and `2000–2021` baselines (`tables/table_parser_audit_150.csv`), PLUS a completely disjoint fresh 50-recall out-of-sample `2025–2026` consistency stress-test (`seed = 9992026`) against raw CPSC JSON text. Crucially, because `scripts/02_parse_and_audit.py` initializes reference columns (`gt_*`) from frozen `parser_lib.py` outputs before line-by-line author inspection and override editing, these metrics quantify **author-reviewed parser consistency** rather than independent blind coding; independent double-blind human annotation of remedy categories and seller legal entities is required prior to formal journal submission. As summarized in Table 7, 150-recall 6-archetype consistency is **$99.33\%$** (`Macro-F1 = 0.9933`, 1/150 commercial fitness-dealer edge case), `photo_verify` / `physical_mutilate` / `direct_return` / `free_webmail` / `no_phone` / `mandatory_rule` achieve F1 **$1.0000$**, and `has_injury` achieves F1 **$0.9730$** (`97.33%` exact `clean_injuries`; `100.0%` exact `price`/`units`). ### 4.1 Ultra-Saturated Full-Rank Specification

To estimate conditional observational associations between supply-chain archetype and recall contract design controlling flexibly for item value, batch scale, product category, hazard type, Chinese factory origin, and macro regulatory time trends, we estimate linear probability models strictly on `2022–2026` ($N = 1{,}822$, $G = 1{,}414$ firm clusters):

$$Y_i = \sum_{a \in \mathcal{A} \setminus \{\text{BM}\}} \beta_a \mathbf{1}\{\text{Archetype}_i = a\} + \delta \cdot \text{ChinaMfr}_i + \mu_{d(p_i)} + \nu_{d(u_i)} + \gamma_{c(i)} + \eta_{h(i)} + \tau_{q(i)} + \varepsilon_i$$

Because `3_Mkt_True_US` sells through the *exact same 3P marketplaces* as `1_Mkt_FD_China` while maintaining a verified domestic U.S. legal entity, the exact linear contrast $\Delta_{\text{FD}-\text{US}} \equiv \beta_{\text{Mkt\_FD\_China}} - \beta_{\text{Mkt\_True\_US}}$ cleanly holds marketplace intermediation constant and isolates cross-border asset-lightness.

## 7. Managerial, Platform Governance, and Regulatory Policy Implications

Our theoretical and empirical findings yield three actionable implications for platform operations, supply-chain risk management, and consumer-safety regulation:

1. **Why Intermediary Liability Alone Is Insufficient (Proposition 4):** The CPSC's orders in *In re Amazon.com* (governing FBA third-party merchandise; upheld on summary judgment on November 24, 2026) coincided with a more than fourfold descriptive expansion in pooled public `Amazon_Only` recalls from `66` (2024) to `271` (2026). Yet absent explicit **reverse-logistics and escrow-funding obligations**, platforms and cross-border sellers continue executing recalls predominantly through Proof-of-Destruction (`89.4%` post-Jan 2025 inside `1_Mkt_FD_China`), assigning consumer self-disposal remedies across `3,947,390` recalled hazardous-product units with `0.0%` firm direct-return programs. Policy frameworks should pair intermediary distributor liability with **mandatory seller recall bonds or platform-operated domestic hazmat collection** specifically for lithium-ion batteries, button cells, and toxic-chemical SKUs.
2. **Leveraging First-Party Platform Order Observability ($\theta_{\text{Platform}} \to 1$):** Our DTC Shield mechanism (Proposition 2)—consistent with the imprecisely estimated null on `4_DTC_Online_Only` (`+3.38 pp`, $95\%\text{ CI }[-6.48, +13.24]\text{ pp}$) relative to the large `1_Mkt_FD_China` gap (`+35.35 pp`)—highlights how first-party buyer records reduce the moral-hazard justification for consumer self-destruction. While individual 3P sellers lack buyer identities ($\theta_{\text{3P}} \approx 0$), **the platform itself possesses complete verified transaction records ($\theta_{\text{Platform}} \approx 1$)**. Empowering or requiring marketplaces to execute automated, account-linked refund/replacement crediting—coupled with seller escrow clawbacks—can unlock the DTC observability shield across 3P retail, raising consumer participation from $q_P$ toward $q_0$ without forcing households to mutilate hazardous devices.
3. **Closing Traceability, Contact, and Cross-Border Coordination Gaps:** Requiring verifiable GS1 UPC/GTIN registration (`0.2%` today vs. `7.3%` Omnichannel), domestic service-of-process agents with verified corporate domains and U.S. telephone support (replacing `@gmail/@qq/@163` webmail addresses), and automated CPSC–Health Canada–PROFECO API triggers for 3P ASINs would directly repair the institutional gaps exposed by cross-border platform disintermediation.

**Limitations:** Four methodological caveats define the scope of our evidence and an agenda for future work. First, the CPSC public recall census does not report campaign-level consumer completion rates $q_m$ (unlike NHTSA automotive quarterly filings) or downstream waste-stream routing; therefore, consumer compliance depression under Proof-of-Destruction is derived analytically from Propositions 1–4, and actual improper disposal or trash leakage across the `3,947,390` recalled hazardous-product units assigned to consumer self-disposal (of which `67.3%` stem from OCOOPA and Anker, with OCOOPA explicitly prohibiting household trash and curbside recycling bins) cannot be quantified from CPSC notices alone. Second, Pillar 1 regressions estimate **conditional observational associations** controlling for rich observable attributes (`price`, `units`, `prod_cat`, `fine_noun`, `hazard_cat`, `is_china_mfr`, and `yq`) rather than quasi-experimental causal effects, and the `4_DTC_Online_Only` estimate (`+3.38 pp`, $95\%\text{ CI }[-6.48, +13.24]\text{ pp}$) is an imprecisely estimated null rather than proof of an exact zero. Third, *In re Amazon.com* legally governs **Fulfilled-by-Amazon (`FBA`)** merchandise, whereas public CPSC notices almost never report fulfillment mode (`1/601` explicit FBA mentions); accordingly, our Newey–West HAC `log(Amazon_Only / Brick_and_Mortar)` slope (`+0.1831/qtr`, $+0.1999\text{/qtr}$ controlling for STURDY Act and Reese's Law) represents a descriptive structural acceleration / associative time-series break rather than a clean causal estimate of the FBA ruling, and adding those statutory dummies attenuates Amazon's differential slope over non-Amazon 3P marketplaces (`+0.0281/qtr`, $t = +0.56$), reflecting shared statutory rule sweeps across all 3P platforms. Fourth, our `150`-recall stratified and `50`-recall out-of-sample checks (`Table 7`) are **author-reviewed parser consistency audits** whose reference labels were initialized from parser outputs prior to manual edits; independent double-blind human annotation of remedy classifications and seller legal entities is required before formal journal submission.
