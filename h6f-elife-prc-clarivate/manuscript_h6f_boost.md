# Voluntary Revision and Asymmetric Impact-Factor Dependence: Evidence from the *eLife* Publish-Review-Curate Experiment and Clarivate's 2024 Delisting, 2021–2026

**Date:** November 26, 2026 (Major Revision `v2`)  
**Target Journals:** *Nature Human Behaviour* | *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences (PNAS)* | *Science Advances*  
**Data & Replication Package:** Complete self-contained pipeline (`scripts/01`–`08`), `29/29` Crossref-verified peer-reviewed DOIs, live *eLife* article metrics (`N = 2,653`), and `30`-journal strict (`35`-journal expanded) OpenAlex benchmark panel (`2021H1–2026H1`).

## Abstract

Does abolishing binary post-review rejection erode scientific self-correction, and how asymmetrically do national research systems respond when an incumbent indexer strips a reforming journal of its Journal Impact Factor (JIF)? We examine two coupled institutional shocks at *eLife*: its January 2023 transition to the Publish-Review-Curate (PRC) model—which eliminates post-review rejection and replaces gatekeeping with public two-dimensional assessments (*Significance* and *Strength of Evidence*)—and Clarivate's late-2024 interventions, placing *eLife* on hold on October 24, 2024, revoking its JIF, and announcing on December 19, 2024 that only Versions of Record (VoRs) rated *Solid* or higher would be indexed in the Emerging Sources Citation Index (ESCI) (Behrens et al., 2026). Reconstructing true initial (`v1`) assessments for `99.78%` (`4,541 / 4,551`) of the complete `2023–2026` *eLife* PRC census, linking live *eLife* reader telemetry (`page-views` and `downloads`) for `100.00%` (`2,653 / 2,653`) of mature `2023–2024` `v1` cohorts (strictly guarding against the API's rolling `2023-11` monthly left-truncation window), and assembling an audited `30`-journal (`35`-journal expanded) clean life-science OpenAlex panel (`2021H1–2026H1`), we establish four findings. **First**, voluntary revision remains extensive without binary editorial rejection: across the complete mature `2023–2024` funnel (`4,541 / 4,551` recovered `v1` $\to$ `964` mature `v1` *Incomplete/Inadequate* preprints $\to$ `793` converted to VoR [`82.3%`] $\to$ `644` upgraded), **`66.8%` of all `964` `v1` *Incomplete/Inadequate* preprints (`81.2%` of the `793` converted VoRs)** upgraded their *Strength of Evidence* (`621` crossing to *Solid+*: `64.4%` of all `v1` / `78.3%` of VoRs) across a mean **`325.4`-day calendar-date interval between `v1` posting and VoR publication**. Aligning Clarivate's ESCI *Solid*-threshold rule strictly to `v1_dt` $\ge$ `2024-12-19` (excluding the Oct 1–Dec 18, 2024 transition donut) and estimating **unconditional Intention-to-Treat (ITT)** outcomes across *all* `v1` *Incomplete* preprints (avoiding VoR collider conditioning), fixed-horizon VoR conversion declined (`-7.35` pp at `365d`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.009$; `-6.17` pp at `450d`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.015$), unconditional *Solid+* upgrades did not surge (`-3.58` pp at `365d`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.212$; `-3.75` pp at `450d`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.202$), and Aalen–Johansen competing-risks cumulative incidence (handling right-censoring across all `1,675` `v1` *Incomplete* preprints) shows a `-5.03` pp drop in unrevised `<Solid` VoR declarations at `450d` (`14.0%` $\to$ `9.0%`, $p = 0.0073$) while *Solid+* cumulative incidence converges by `540d` (`54.4%` vs. `54.4%`). Because single-journal before-after ESCI comparisons lack an unexposed control group, they cannot definitively rule out indexing-incentive effects (such as selective deferral of unrevised VoRs); rather, high voluntary revision rates were established from `2023` onward and persist despite zero or at most modest measurable external badge returns. **Second**, we document a **Double Evaluation-Market Paradox**: initially *Incomplete* `v1` VoRs suffer a `-14.5%` strictly post-`v1` OpenAlex citation penalty ($t = -5.11$, $p = 8.67 \times 10^{-5}$; `18/18` subjects negative; completely erased to `+0.7%`, $p = 0.915$ under flawed `VoR`-date alignment), while live readers respond at `v1` to headline *Significance* (`+10.0%` views, $p = 0.00077$; `+9.6%` downloads, $p = 0.00217$) rather than *Strength* (`+2.9%`, $p = 0.580$). Within `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs (observed `22.8–46.0` months post-`v1`, median `32.7` months; `0.2–40.2` months post-VoR, median `23.0` months), upgrading *Incomplete* $\to$ *Solid+* yields no statistically significant gain in post-`v1` OpenAlex citations (`+3.2%`, 95% CI `[-10.7%, +19.2%]`, $p = 0.654$; TOST $\pm 20\%$ $p = 0.0125$, whereas the stricter $\pm 15\%$ bound sits just above `0.05` at $p = 0.0508$), untruncated `v1` $\ge$ `2023-11` monthly rate DiD (`+0.058`, 95% CI `[-0.049, +0.164]`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.256$, TOST $\pm 15\%$ $p = 0.0426$ for views; `+0.054`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.405$ for downloads), or a clean local VoR event study (`N = 651`, identical month-0 spike `+1.11` vs. `+1.11`, FE DiD `+0.001`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.984$; post `t = +1..+6`: `-0.103`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.171$ views; `+0.030`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.720$ downloads); modest cumulative view bumps reflect **version-release events** (`+0.234` per posted version, $p = 0.0116$) rather than the upgraded badge. **Third**, separating submissions from published output, sequential manuscript-ID submission velocity (`sentForReview` Spearman $r = 0.9988$) fell `-56.3%`—reconstructing at quarterly resolution *eLife*'s established full-year submission drop (`6,396` $\to$ `2,853`, `-55.4%`; Rodgers and Pattinson, 2026; Behrens et al., 2026)—while editors cushioned published intake via a `+4.63` pp jump in peer-review acceptance (`15.44%` $\to$ `20.06%`, HAC $t = +3.41$, Permutation $p = 0.0002$, Sup-Wald $F = 53.14$) with invariant `v1` quality (`+0.54` pp, $p = 0.756$). **Fourth**, on **published output**, unique strictly convex Abadie–Diamond–Hainmueller Synthetic Control shows total *eLife* output fell `-0.552` log pts (`-42.4%`, Rank `1/30`, $p = 0.0333$; Expanded Rank `1/35`, $p = 0.0286$), while China-affiliated output surged during Phase-1 PRC (`+4.86` pp share; Senior-PI share `13.0%` $\to$ `20.5%`) before dropping `-1.091` log pts (`-66.4%`, Rank `1/30`, $p = 0.0333$; Expanded `-1.098`, Rank `1/35`, $p = 0.0286$; Senior-PI share `9.1%` overnight). In a small-sample national policy-label comparison across `10` classified systems, `3` JIF-codified nations (`CN, ES, IT`: mean `-57.8%`) contracted `-21.2` pp deeper than `7` DORA-reformed nations (`GB, FR, US, CH, NL, DE, CA`: mean `-36.6%`, exact permutation $p = 0.0167$), closely tracking *eLife*'s published country-level submission drops (Spearman $\rho = 0.683$, $p = 0.0424$).

## 1. Introduction

Traditional scientific publishing bundles dissemination, peer review, and prestige certification into a single binary accept/reject gate (Stern and O'Shea, 2019; Card and DellaVigna, 2020). Critics have argued for decades that this bundling delays knowledge diffusion, induces arbitrary gatekeeping bias across institutional networks (Tomkins et al., 2017; Teplitskiy et al., 2018), and entrenches reliance on journal-level prestige shortcuts—above all the Clarivate Journal Impact Factor (JIF)—that poorly summarize individual article quality (Seglen, 1997; Hicks et al., 2015; McKiernan et al., 2019). Yet reform proposals have long confronted two fundamental, untested counter-hypotheses from the economics of science. First, if a selective journal abolishes post-review rejection, will authors free-ride on guaranteed publication and abandon costly revision—or do high voluntary revision rates persist even when acceptance is unconditional and measurable downstream badge returns are absent or modest? Second, if an incumbent commercial indexer strips a reforming journal of its JIF, how elastic is global scientific participation across national research-assessment systems that formally endorse the San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment (DORA) versus those that codify JIF quartiles into promotion, tenure, and cash-bonus rules (Quan et al., 2017; Baccini et al., 2019; Shu et al., 2020; Zhang and Sivertsen, 2020)?

Between January 2023 and November 2026, *eLife* executed the largest real-world institutional experiment in modern scientific publishing (Eisen et al., 2020, 2022; Avissar-Whiting et al., 2024; Behrens et al., 2024, 2026). Under *eLife*'s Publish-Review-Curate (PRC) model, every manuscript selected for peer review by editors is published as an open *Reviewed Preprint* (`v1`) accompanied by public referee reports and a structured *eLife Assessment* scoring both *Significance* (`Useful` to `Landmark`) and *Strength of Evidence* (`Inadequate` or `Incomplete` up to `Solid`, `Convincing`, `Compelling`, and `Exceptional`; Hardwicke et al., 2024). Critically, authors are never rejected after peer review: they may revise iteratively to improve their assessment or declare an unrevised *Version of Record* (VoR) at any time. Nineteen months into this experiment, Clarivate placed *eLife* "on hold" in the Web of Science (WoS) on October 24, 2024, revoked its JIF from the Science Citation Index Expanded (SCIE), and on December 19, 2024 confirmed a selective indexing rule under which only *eLife* VoRs whose final assessment reached at least *Solid* evidence would be indexed in the non-JIF Emerging Sources Citation Index (ESCI) (Rodgers and Pattinson, 2026; Behrens et al., 2026).

Prior empirical appraisals of *eLife*'s PRC transition have focused on the psychometric clarity of its assessment vocabulary (Hardwicke et al., 2024), descriptive preprint-to-publication links (Abdill and Blekhman, 2019; Fu and Hughey, 2019; Fraser et al., 2020), or *eLife*'s own published three-year submission and editorial summaries (Rodgers and Pattinson, 2026; Behrens et al., 2026). Crucially, cross-sectional analyses aligned by *final VoR publication date* suffer from severe **date-alignment and collider contamination**: because papers rated *Incomplete* at `v1` experience a mean `325.4`-day calendar-date interval between `v1` posting and VoR publication before receiving a VoR date, aligning by VoR date compares late-arriving upgraded papers against rapidly finalized papers and conflates initial assessment with endogenous revision time. Similarly, evaluating post-ESCI revision responses strictly among converted VoRs conditions on a post-treatment conversion outcome (collider bias). We resolve both measurement and conditioning barriers by recovering the true initial `v1` Reviewed Preprint assessment and exact `v1` publication date for **`4,541 / 4,551` (`99.78%`)** PRC articles (`2023–2026`), linking complete Crossref and OpenAlex citing-year records (isolating strictly post-`v1` citations and pre-`v1` bioRxiv baselines), harvesting **live monthly *eLife* article-level `page-views` and `downloads`** for `100.00%` (`2,653 / 2,653`) of mature `2023–2024` `v1` cohorts (with strict rolling-window truncation guards), and building a strictly audited **`30`-journal (`35`-journal expanded) peer life-science OpenAlex benchmark panel (`2021H1–2026H1`)** spanning `12` major research nations.

Our empirical architecture advances the science-of-science literature along four clearly bounded pillars:

1. **Voluntary Revision Funnel, Unconditional ESCI ITT, and Competing-Risks Survival (Pillar 1):** Across the full mature `2023–2024` funnel (`4,541 / 4,551` recovered `v1` $\to$ `2,653` mature `v1` $\to$ `964` initially *Incomplete/Inadequate* `v1` preprints $\to$ `793` converted to VoR [`82.3%`] $\to$ `644` upgraded), **`66.8%` of all `964` `v1` *Incomplete/Inadequate* preprints (`81.2%` of converted VoRs)** upgraded their *Strength of Evidence* (`621` crossing to *Solid+*: `64.4%` of all `v1` / `78.3%` of VoRs) across a mean `325.4`-day calendar-date interval between `v1` posting and VoR publication. Aligning Clarivate's ESCI *Solid*-threshold rule cleanly to `v1_dt` $\ge$ `2024-12-19` (excluding the Oct 1–Dec 18, 2024 transition donut, with sensitivities at `>= 2025-01-01` and the `2024-10-24` indexing suspension date) and estimating **unconditional Intention-to-Treat (ITT)** effects across *all* `v1` *Incomplete* preprints removes VoR collider conditioning: fixed-horizon VoR conversion fell (`-7.35` pp at `365d`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.009$; `-6.17` pp at `450d`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.015$), unconditional *Solid+* upgrades did not jump upward (`-3.58` pp at `365d`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.212$; `-3.75` pp at `450d`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.202$), and Aalen–Johansen competing-risks cumulative incidence reveals a `-4.95` to `-5.80` pp decline in unrevised `<Solid` VoR declarations (`p <= 0.0073` across `365d–540d`) alongside *Solid+* convergence (`54.4%` vs. `54.4%` by `540d`). Because before-after ESCI comparisons lack an unexposed control group, we do *not* claim they prove zero indexing-incentive effect; instead, high voluntary revision rates persisted from `2023` onward despite zero or modest measurable downstream badge returns.
2. **The Double Evaluation-Market Paradox and Methodological Autopsy (Pillar 2):** Aligning mature VoRs strictly by true `v1` date exposes a steep first-impression citation gradient: `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs incur a **`-14.5%`** strictly post-`v1` OpenAlex citation penalty ($t = -5.11$, $p = 8.67 \times 10^{-5}$; `18/18` subjects negative), whereas *Compelling/Exceptional* VoRs earn `+19.3%` ($p = 0.0146$)—a gradient completely erased to `+0.7%` ($p = 0.915$) under flawed `VoR`-date alignment. Live *eLife* readers, by contrast, respond at `v1` to headline **`v1` *Significance*** (`+10.0%` page-views, $p = 0.00077$; `+9.6%` downloads, $p = 0.00217$) rather than *Strength* (`+2.9%`, $p = 0.580$). Once we eliminate rolling-window API left-truncation (`v1` $\ge$ `2023-11`), **upgrading `v1` *Incomplete* $\to$ *Solid+* yields no statistically significant sustained return in either citations or post-VoR reader attention**: OpenAlex citations (`+3.2%`, 95% CI `[-10.7%, +19.2%]`, $p = 0.654$; TOST $\pm 20\%$ $p = 0.0125$, $\pm 15\%$ $p = 0.0508$ just above `0.05`), untruncated monthly rate DiD (`+0.058`, 95% CI `[-0.049, +0.164]`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.256$, TOST $\pm 15\%$ $p = 0.0426$), and a clean local VoR event study (`N = 651`) all show null differential payoffs—even though the **VoR release event itself** triggers a uniform `+1.11` log-view / `+1.79` log-download month-0 spike identical across Upgraded and Unchanged VoRs (`FE DiD = +0.001`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.984$), and each additional posted version (`v2`/VoR) adds `+0.234` ($p = 0.0116$) to `+0.301` ($p = 0.00069$) log views.
3. **Separating Submission Velocity, Editorial Acceptance, and Published-Output Synthetic Control (Pillar 3):** Contextualizing against *eLife*'s established full-year submission contraction (`6,396` in `2024` $\to$ `2,853` in `2025`, `-55.4%`; Rodgers and Pattinson, 2026; Behrens et al., 2026), calibrating *eLife*'s sequential manuscript identifiers (`eid`) against `200` exact `sentForReview` timestamps (Spearman $r = 0.9988$, $p = 1.53 \times 10^{-262}$) locates October 24, 2024 at `eid` $\approx 103,784$ and reconstructs a **`-56.3%`** quarterly submission velocity drop (`2,579` $\to$ `1,127` manuscripts/quarter). Published output fell less steeply (`-0.552` log pts [`-42.4%`] under Synthetic Control; `-44.1%` DiD) because editors buffered intake via a **`+4.63` pp jump in peer-review acceptance** (`15.44%` $\to$ `20.06%`, HAC $t = +3.41$, Permutation $p = 0.0002$, Andrews 1993 Sup-Wald $F = 53.14$) while holding `v1` assessment quality invariant (`v1` *Incomplete* shift `+0.54` pp, $p = 0.756$).
4. **Published-Output Synthetic Control and Small-Sample National Policy-Label Groupings (Pillar 4):** Expanding Abadie–Diamond–Hainmueller Synthetic Control (Abadie et al., 2010, 2015; Abadie, 2021) with strictly convex minimum-$L_2$-norm tie-breaking to `30` strict ($\ge 90\%$ true country coverage every half-year) and `35` expanded clean life-science journals breaks the small-donor inference floor cleanly below `0.05`: total published output drops `-0.552` log pts (**Rank `1/30`, $p = 0.0333$; Expanded Rank `1/35`, $p = 0.0286$**). China-affiliated published output exhibited a two-phase inverted-V—surging during Phase-1 PRC (`+4.86` pp share; Senior-PI share `13.0%` $\to$ `20.5%`) before contracting **`-1.091` log pts (`-66.4%`, Rank `1/30`, $p = 0.0333$; Expanded `-1.098`, Rank `1/35`, $p = 0.0286$)** and halving its Senior-PI share overnight (`20.5%` $\to$ `9.1%`). In a small-sample national policy-label comparison across `10` classified nations, `3` JIF-codified systems (`CN, ES, IT`: mean `-57.8%`) contracted `-21.2` pp deeper than `7` DORA-reformed systems (`GB, FR, US, CH, NL, DE, CA`: mean `-36.6%`, exact combinatorial permutation $p = 0.0167$), tracking *eLife*'s published country-level submission drops (Spearman $\rho = 0.683$, $p = 0.0424$).

## 2. Institutional Setting, Data Architecture, and Alignment Invariants

### 2.1 The *eLife* PRC Census and Complete True-`v1` Longitudinal Recovery

We harvested the universe of *eLife* PRC outputs published between January 1, 2023 and November 25, 2026 from the official *eLife* REST API (`api.elifesciences.org/search`), deduplicating by manuscript identifier (`eid`) to retain `N = 4,551` unique PRC articles (`3,264` finalized VoRs and `1,287` Reviewed Preprints). Rather than relying on the search endpoint's overwritten final assessment, we queried `api.elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/{eid}v1` for every manuscript, achieving **`99.78%` (`4,541 / 4,551`) exact true-`v1` recovery** of initial `v1` *Significance* (5-point ordinal scale: *Useful* = 1 to *Landmark* = 5), initial `v1` *Strength of Evidence* (6-point ordinal scale: *Inadequate* = 1, *Incomplete* = 2, *Solid* = 3, *Convincing* = 4, *Compelling* = 5, *Exceptional* = 6), original preprint DOIs (bioRxiv/medRxiv), subject classifications (`18` mature primary disciplines), and exact `v1` review/publication dates.

Strict programmatic alignment audits confirm **zero date-alignment anomalies**: `0` missing `v1` dates across all `4,551` manuscripts and `0` VoRs with `fin_date < v1_date` across all `3,264` VoRs. All citation and reader-attention analyses restrict to the **mature `2023–2024` `v1` cohort (`2023-01-25` to `2024-12-31`, `N = 2,653`)**, providing **`22.8` to `46.0` months** of post-`v1` observation exposure (median `32.7` months; and among `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs, `0.2` to `40.2` months post-VoR, median `23.0` months) while eliminating right-censoring distortion from recent submissions.

### 2.2 Multi-Source Citations, Live Monthly *eLife* Reader Telemetry, and Rolling-Window Audit

To separate pre-existing manuscript popularity from post-assessment scientific uptake, we link every PRC manuscript to three independent attention ledgers:
1. **OpenAlex Citing-Year Trajectories:** Batch-querying all eLife base, versioned (`10.7554/eLife.{eid}.*`), and original bioRxiv/medRxiv preprint DOIs yields `99.4%` manuscript-level coverage and exact annual citation counts (`counts_by_year`). We define **strictly post-`v1` citations** (`oa_post_v1_cites`) exclusively as citations occurring in calendar years strictly after `v1_year` ($t_{\text{cite}} > t_{v1}$), eliminating pre-review citation leakage while explicitly controlling for **strictly pre-`v1` preprint citations** ($t_{\text{cite}} < t_{v1}$).
2. **Crossref Versioned Citation & Release Ledger:** Complete cursor harvesting of prefix `10.7554` (`2023–2026`) provides independent `cr_cites` (maximum across DOI versions), `cr_cites_sum`, and the exact count of distinct published DOI versions (`cr_n_versions`, distinguishing single-step `v1` $\to$ VoR articles from multi-round `v1` $\to$ `v2` $\to$ VoR revisions).
3. **Live Monthly *eLife* Article-Level Reader Metrics & Rolling-Window Guard:** Querying `api.elifesciences.org/metrics/article/{eid}/page-views` and `/downloads` (`?by=month&per-page=100`) returned HTTP `200` for **`2,653 / 2,653` (`100.00%`)** mature `2023–2024` `v1` papers. Crucially, our programmatic metadata audit discovered a vital feature of the *eLife* metrics API: while cumulative `totalValue` (`pv_total`, `dl_total`) is untruncated for all `2,653` manuscripts, the monthly `periods` array retains a **rolling `37`-month window (`2023-11` through `2026-11`)**, leaving `801` pre-November-2023 articles (`30.2%`) left-truncated in `periods` and `2026-11` partially elapsed. Silently treating missing `2023-01..2023-10` months as zeros would manufacture severe upward bias in pre-vs-post VoR monthly rate comparisons (`27` Upgraded vs. `0` Unchanged `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs would receive zero pre-VoR views). We enforce a strict **anti-truncation invariant**: (i) cumulative lifetime `pv_total`/`dl_total` analyses use all `2,653` mature papers; (ii) monthly rate DiD and balanced `22`-month post-`v1` trajectory models (`tau = 0..21`) restrict strictly to **untruncated cohorts (`v1_date` $\ge$ `2023-11-01`, `N = 1,852` mature; `N = 563` `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs)** and complete calendar months ($\le$ `2026-10`); and (iii) our **local monthly VoR event study (`t = -3..+6`)** restricts strictly to VoRs whose entire `10`-month window `[fin_m - 3, fin_m + 6]` lies inside `[2023-12, 2026-10]` with at least a `4`-month gap after `v1` (`N = 1,701` mature; `N = 651` `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs).

### 2.3 Expanded Clean Peer Life-Science OpenAlex Benchmark Panel (`2021H1–2026H1`)

Single-treated-unit comparative case studies require a large, metadata-clean donor pool to support exact permutation inference below conventional significance thresholds (Abadie et al., 2010; Abadie, 2021). We verified `55` flagship and society life-science journals in OpenAlex `/sources` and harvested complete half-yearly **published article volumes** (`2021H1–2026H1`, `11` complete half-years, zero YTD truncation) across `16` major research nations via exact single-call `group_by=authorships.countries:include_unknown`. Using `:include_unknown` directly measures **true country-affiliation completeness** ($n_{\text{has\_geo}} = n_{\text{all}} - n_{\text{unknown}}$, verified to the exact integer against `institutions.country_code:!null`).

This automated quality audit caught and excluded `20` candidate journals plagued either by magazine/front-matter items lacking affiliations (e.g., *Science* `29.9%` min coverage, *Nature* `52.2%`, *Nature Genetics* `64.8%`, *Nature Neuroscience* `63.6%`, *Development* `73.7%`) or incomplete `11`-period cells under unauthenticated OpenAlex IP throttling. Our **Primary Strict Panel** comprises **`N = 30` clean journals** (`29` peer biology donors + *eLife*, every journal satisfying $\ge 90.0\%$ true affiliation coverage in every single half-year `2021H1–2026H1`, $\ge 25$ articles/half-year, and zero indexing breaks; minimum exact 1-sided permutation $p = 1/30 = 0.0333$). Our **Expanded Clean Panel** adds five flagship Cell/Nature research titles with mean coverage `91.6%–93.9%` and minimum coverage `85.9%–88.7%` (*Cell Metabolism*, *Molecular Cell*, *Neuron*, *Nature Microbiology*, *Nature Metabolism*), yielding **`N = 35` journals** (minimum 1-sided permutation $p = 1/35 = 0.0286$). For *eLife*, `2021H1–2022H2` comes from identical OpenAlex `group_by` counts (`99.9%` min geo coverage) and `2023H1–2026H1` from the deduplicated true-`v1` PRC census (`VoR + RP`, `98.8%` min geo coverage), preventing VoR-only indexing-lag bias while explicitly excluding `2023H1` as the legacy-to-PRC transition seam.

## 3. Econometric Identification Strategy

### 3.1 Unconditional ESCI ITT, Competing-Risks Survival, and Fixed-Effects PPML/Event Studies

To evaluate behavioral responses around Clarivate's December 19, 2024 ESCI *Solid*-threshold rule without inducing post-treatment **VoR collider bias**, we define all primary Pillar 1 ESCI estimands as **unconditional Intention-to-Treat (ITT)** contrasts over *every* `v1` *Incomplete/Inadequate* Reviewed Preprint (`v1_str_min <= 2`), aligned strictly to **`v1_dt` $\ge$ `2024-12-19`** with the **October 1–December 18, 2024 transition donut (`n = 135`)** excluded from primary pre (`2023-04-01..2024-09-30`) vs. post (`>= 2024-12-19`) comparisons (supplemented by cutoff sensitivities at `>= 2025-01-01`, the `>= 2024-10-24` indexing suspension date, and a clean `2023`-only pre-cohort). Across exact right-censoring-free follow-up horizons relative to the `2026-11-25` snapshot—`365` days (`v1_dt <= 2025-11-25`), `450` days (`v1_dt <= 2025-09-01`), and `540` days (`v1_dt <= 2025-06-03`)—we estimate Subject-FE linear probability models with CR1 standard errors and restricted Wild Cluster Bootstrap ($B = 1,999$, `G = 18`) for all three unconditional ITT outcomes: `P(Converted to VoR within W days)`, `P(Upgraded to Solid+ VoR within W days)`, and `P(Unrevised <Solid VoR within W days)`. To utilize all `1,675` `v1` *Incomplete* preprints (`v1_dt >= 2023-04-01`) while handling right-censoring transparently, we pair fixed-window ITT with nonparametric **Kaplan–Meier** VoR conversion ($1 - \hat{S}(t)$) and **Aalen–Johansen competing-risks cumulative incidence functions** ($\widehat{\text{CIF}}_{\text{Solid+}}(t)$ and $\widehat{\text{CIF}}_{\text{Unrev}}(t)$, satisfying $\widehat{\text{CIF}}_{\text{Solid+}}(t) + \widehat{\text{CIF}}_{\text{Unrev}}(t) \equiv 1 - \hat{S}(t)$ identically), with `1,000`-draw bootstrap standard errors and 95% confidence intervals. Conditional-on-VoR upgrade shares `P(Solid+ | VoR within W days)` are retained strictly as a **secondary descriptive decomposition** explicitly flagged as conditioning on a post-treatment conversion outcome.

For Pillar 2 attention outcomes, because citations and article views are non-negative, right-skewed count outcomes for which `log(1 + Y)` OLS can exhibit scale sensitivity (Chen and Roth, 2024), our primary cross-sectional estimator is **scale-invariant Poisson Pseudo-Maximum Likelihood (PPML)** with Discipline $\times$ `v1`-Quarter fixed effects ($\gamma_{s(i), q(i)}$; Santos Silva and Tenreyro, 2006):

$$E\big[Y_i \mid X_i, \gamma_{s,q}\big] = \exp\!\Big(\beta \cdot \text{Treat}_i + \mathbf{X}_i'\boldsymbol{\theta} + \gamma_{s(i),q(i)}\Big)$$

where $\mathbf{X}_i$ controls for exact `v1` *Significance* rank, continuous `v1` age in days, $\log(1 + \text{authors})$, and $\log(1 + \text{Pre-}v1\text{ Cites})$ to absorb pre-assessment paper visibility. We triangulate every PPML estimate against `log(1 + Y)` OLS, within-cell percentile ranks, top-decile indicators, `99`th-percentile winsorization, outlier-excluded checks, exact 95% CIs (using $t_{0.975,\,G-1}$), and Lakens (2017) Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence bounds reported transparently at **both** $\pm 15\%$ and $\pm 20\%$. Standard errors are clustered consistently at the **Discipline (`G = 18`)** level across all primary specifications with exact CR1 small-sample correction and exact LSDV-equivalent restricted **Wild Cluster Bootstrap** ($p_{\text{WCB}}$, Rademacher weights, $B = 1,999$; Cameron et al., 2008), alongside finer `subj_v1q` clustering and within-FE randomization permutation tests ($B = 2,000$).

To eliminate unobserved time-invariant article heterogeneity when estimating reader responses to upgrading `v1` *Incomplete* $\to$ *Solid+* VoR, we combine two complementary zero-truncation panel designs:
1. **Clean Untruncated Local VoR Event Study (`fin` in `2024-03..2026-04`, lag $\ge 4$ months, `N = 651`):** Measuring each article's log-traffic change at the VoR release month ($t = 0$) and post-VoR horizon ($t \in \{+1,\dots,+6\}$) relative to its immediately preceding `3`-month pre-VoR baseline ($t \in \{-3,-2,-1\}$):

$$\Delta \log M_{i,W} \equiv \log\!\big(1 + \overline{M}_{i,W}\big) - \log\!\big(1 + \overline{M}_{i,[-3,-1]}\big) = \delta_W \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{Upgraded to Solid+})_i + \mathbf{Z}_i'\boldsymbol{\phi} + \gamma_{s(i),q(i)} + \varepsilon_i$$

2. **Clean Untruncated Balanced `22`-Month Post-`v1` Trajectory (`v1` $\ge$ `2023-11`, `N = 563`) & Monthly Rate DiD:** Comparing initial `v1` baseline traffic (months `0–2`), active revision-window traffic (months `3–11`), and sustained post-revision traffic (months `12–21`) both before and after conditioning on the exact number of published DOI versions (`cr_n_versions`), alongside clean untruncated pre-vs-post VoR monthly rate DiD.

### 3.2 Structural Break Testing and Strictly Convex Expanded Synthetic Control

To identify editorial selection responses after Clarivate's October 24, 2024 hold, we partition the sequential manuscript ledger (`eid 87,500–112,000`) into `49` fixed-width `500`-`eid` blocks, compute each block's exact peer-review acceptance rate ($100 \times N_{\text{RP}} / 500$), and perform an endogenous Quandt–Andrews **Sup-Wald structural break test** with `15%` trimming (Andrews, 1993) using Newey–West HAC variance ($L = 3, 4$), a `5,000`-draw exact block permutation test, and late-block censoring sensitivity (`eid < 111k, 110k, 109k`).

For cross-journal **published-output** inference across `N = 30` strict (`N = 35` expanded) clean biology journals, we demean each journal's outcome $Y_{jt}$ relative to its pre-treatment mean and solve the exact Abadie–Diamond–Hainmueller convex simplex program (Abadie et al., 2010, 2015) with an infinitesimal scale-invariant minimum-$L_2$-norm tie-breaker ($\epsilon = 10^{-5} \cdot \overline{Y_{0,\text{pre}}^2}$) so that whenever $J > T_0$ creates a flat zero-loss face, the simplex solution is strictly convex, globally unique, and independent of optimizer initialization:

$$\mathbf{w}^* = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \Delta^{J-1}} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_0} \left(Y_{1t} - \sum_{j=2}^{N} w_j Y_{jt}\right)^2 + \epsilon \|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2, \qquad w_j \ge 0,\;\; \sum_{j=2}^{N} w_j = 1$$

We evaluate three complementary pre-treatment windows—Phase-2 Clarivate incremental (`2023H2–2024H2`), Total Cumulative (`2021H1–2022H2`), and Full Pre-Clarivate (`2021H1–2022H2` + `2023H2–2024H2`, $T_0 = 7$)—compute exact in-space placebo signed-gap and RMSPE-ratio ranks across all `N` journals, verify sensitivity under pairwise-penalized Synthetic Control ($\lambda = 0.05$; Abadie, 2021), run Leave-One-Journal-Out (LOJO) sensitivity across both SC and DiD, and execute a `2022H2` Placebo-in-Time falsification test.

### 4.3 Pillar 3: Separating Submissions, Editorial Acceptance, and Published-Output Synthetic Control

How did Clarivate's October 24, 2024 hold and JIF removal reshape *eLife*'s submission pipeline, editorial screening, and published output? To avoid conflating distinct margins, we separate (i) **submission intake velocity**, (ii) **peer-review acceptance rates**, and (iii) **cross-journal published-output Synthetic Control**.

**Submission Velocity Contextualized Against Official Baseline & Editorial Acceptance Cushioning (Figure 3):** *eLife*'s own three-year transparency reports establish the full-year submission baseline directly: total annual submissions fell **`-55.4%`**, from **`6,396` in `2024` to `2,853` in `2025`**, while editorial peer-review selection rose from `27%` to `35%` (Rodgers and Pattinson, 2026; Behrens et al., 2026). Rather than claiming discovery of this contraction, we calibrate *eLife*'s sequential manuscript identifiers (`eid`) against `200` stratified `sentForReview` dates spanning `eid 85,069–112,193` (near-perfect monotonicity: **Spearman $r = 0.9988$, $p = 1.53 \times 10^{-262}$**, placing October 24, 2024 at **`eid` $\approx 103,784$**, with median review turnaround steady at `78` days pre-delisting vs. `82` days post-delisting) to reconstruct its exact quarterly timing and test for structural breaks in editorial screening. Implied quarterly submission velocity dropped **`-56.3%`** (`2,579` manuscripts/quarter in `2024Q1–Q3` $\to$ `1,127`/quarter in `2025Q2–2026Q2`), closely matching the published `-55.4%` annual baseline. Yet **published Reviewed Preprints** fell less steeply (`-42.4%` Synthetic Control / `-44.1%` DiD) because editors buffered intake along a second margin: across `49` sequential `500`-`eid` blocks (`eid 87,500–112,000`), the peer-review acceptance rate jumped discontinuously by **`+4.63` pp** from **`15.44%` pre-Clarivate to `20.06%` post-Clarivate** (HAC $SE = 1.36$, $t = +3.41$, $p = 0.00065$; `5,000`-draw permutation $p = 0.0002$; endogenous Andrews 1993 Sup-Wald $F_{\text{HAC}} = 53.14$). Excluding late-2026 blocks leaves this break intact (`eid < 111k`: `+3.78` pp, HAC $t = +3.22$, $p = 0.0013$; `eid < 110k`: `+3.48` pp, $p = 0.0040$; `eid < 109k`: `+2.84` pp, $p = 0.0067$). This discontinuity reversed a slight pre-delisting tightening trend (`-0.113` pp per `500` `eid`, $p = 0.0029$) and quantitatively matches the `+30%` proportional jump in *eLife*'s reported `27%` $\to$ `35%` review-selection rate. Critically, **published `v1` assessment quality did not dilute**: across `4,256` manuscripts, post-delisting `v1` *Incomplete* share moved by just `+0.54` pp ($t = +0.31$, $p = 0.756$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.876$; `+1.12` pp with geographic controls, $p = 0.504$), mean `v1` *Strength* rank moved `+0.004` ($p = 0.927$), and `v1` *Significance* rank moved `+0.013` ($p = 0.682$). Because exiting submissions came disproportionately from geographic pools with higher baseline `v1` *Incomplete* rates within disciplines (China `+10.96` pp, $p = 0.00028$ vs. US `-4.58` pp, $p = 0.0018$ and UK `-7.01` pp, $p = 0.00016$), compositional improvement among remaining submissions enabled higher acceptance at stable peer-review standards.

## 5. Discussion, Mechanism, and Limitations

### 5.1 Reconciling High Voluntary Revision with Double Evaluation-Market Inertia

Our four empirical pillars sharpen two central debates in metascience and institutional economics while drawing strict causal boundaries. First, **abolishing post-review editorial rejection did not trigger widespread free-riding**: across `964` mature `2023–2024` `v1` *Incomplete/Inadequate* preprints, `793` (`82.3%`) converted to VoR and `644` genuinely upgraded their *Strength of Evidence* (**`66.8%` of all `964` `v1` *Incomplete* preprints**; **`81.2%` of converted VoRs**, with `621` [`64.4%` of all `v1` / `78.3%` of VoRs] crossing into *Solid+* across a mean `325.4`-day calendar-date interval between `v1` posting and VoR publication). Crucially, aligning Clarivate's ESCI *Solid*-threshold rule properly to `v1_dt` $\ge$ `2024-12-19` (excluding the Oct 1–Dec 18, 2024 transition donut) and removing VoR collider conditioning shows that unconditional `365d–450d` VoR conversion actually slowed (`-7.35` to `-6.17` pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} \le 0.015$) and unrevised `<Solid` VoR declarations fell `-4.95` to `-5.80` pp (`p <= 0.0073` in Aalen–Johansen competing-risks CIF), while unconditional *Solid+* upgrades did not jump upward (`-3.58` to `-3.75` pp at `365d–450d`, converging to `54.4%` vs. `54.4%` by `540d`). Because this single-journal before-vs-after contrast lacks an unexposed control group, we **explicitly do not claim it rules out indexing-incentive effects**—in fact, the decline in unrevised `<Solid` VoRs is fully consistent with authors withholding unrevised VoRs after ESCI excluded `<Solid` VoRs, alongside post-delisting author recomposition.

Second, our **Double Evaluation-Market Paradox** highlights why high voluntary revision rates—already prevalent from `2023` onward—are striking: authors invest substantial calendar intervals upgrading `v1` *Incomplete* preprints **despite zero or at most modest measurable marginal returns in either citations or sustained post-VoR reader traffic for crossing the *Solid* threshold**. At `v1`, citers penalize *Incomplete* evidence (`-14.5%`, $p = 8.67 \times 10^{-5}$) while readers click headline *Significance* (`+10.0%` views, $p = 0.00077$). Upon finalizing a VoR, **the publication event itself** drives an identical `+1.11` log-view / `+1.79` log-download burst for Upgraded and Unchanged VoRs (`FE DiD = +0.001`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.984$), and posting extra intermediate versions (`v2` Reviewed Preprints) adds `+0.234` ($p = 0.0116$) to `+0.301` ($p = 0.00069$) log views—yet after accounting for version releases, sustained post-VoR traffic (`t = +1..+6`: `-0.103`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.171$; months `12–21`: `+0.061`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.336$) and post-`v1` citations (`+3.2%`, 95% CI `[-10.7%, +19.2%]`, $p = 0.654$; TOST $\pm 20\%$ $p = 0.0125$, $\pm 15\%$ $p = 0.0508$) show no statistically significant differential reward to the upgraded *Solid+* badge. Two complementary mechanisms fit this pattern: (i) public, permanently indexed peer-review criticism activates professional reputational incentives and self-policing even without large short-run citation or traffic payoffs (Eisen et al., 2022; Hardwicke et al., 2024), while (ii) slow-moving citation networks remain anchored to first impressions over `2–3.5` years (Azoulay et al., 2014) and discovery feeds broadcast *release events* (`v2`/VoR) rather than assessment upgrades.

### 5.2 The DORA Implementation Gap and Commercial Indexer Market Power

Even across the `7` DORA-reformed nations, losing the Clarivate JIF coincided with a substantial `-36.6%` mean relative published-output decline (mirroring *eLife*'s published submission drops of `-40%` in the US, `-52%` in Germany, and `-47%` in the Netherlands), reinforcing Rodgers and Pattinson's (2026) observation of a persistent **"implementation gap" between university/funder DORA pledges and departmental hiring/tenure practice** (McKiernan et al., 2019). Yet our small-sample `3`-vs.-`7` national policy-label comparison indicates quantitatively sharper sensitivity where bibliometric tiers remain codified: the `3` JIF-codified systems (`CN, ES, IT`) suffered `-21.2` pp deeper published-output contractions ($p = 0.0167$), while the UK (`-18%` submissions, `+2.14` pp published share rebound) and France (`-23%` submissions, `+0.85` pp share) proved most resilient in Europe. Meanwhile, China's stark two-phase inverted-V (`9.5%` $\to$ `20.0%` $\to$ `15.1%` published share; Senior PI `20.5%` $\to$ `9.1%` overnight) suggests that even China's landmark 2020 "break the five-only" (*po wuwei*) assessment reform (Zhang and Sivertsen, 2020) has not fully decoupled laboratory submission choices from Clarivate SCIE indexing—though CAS's retention of *eLife* on its top-journal list appears to have stabilized output in `2025H2–2026H1` (`13.9%–14.7%` Senior-PI rebound).

### 5.3 Limitations and Identification Boundaries

Five explicit limitations bound our conclusions:
1. **Compound Policy Bundle & Single-Journal Before-After Limits:** Because *eLife* is a single journal undergoing overlapping institutional changes without an unexposed PRC control journal, **cleanly separating the distinct causal effects of (a) abolishing post-review rejection, (b) Clarivate's October 24, 2024 indexing pause, (c) JIF revocation, and (d) selective December 19, 2024 ESCI *Solid*-threshold coverage requires internal submission/peer-review logs and multi-journal institutional benchmarks** not observable in public API records. In particular, our before-vs-after ESCI comparison (Table 1B) cannot definitively rule out indexing-incentive effects on author revision or VoR timing.
2. **Observational Upgrade Selection & Calendar-Interval Measurement:** Within-tier revision upgrades (`Incomplete` $\to$ *Solid+* vs. unrevised *Incomplete*) are observational author choices rather than randomized assignments; although pre-`v1` citations ($p = 0.467$), untruncated first-3-month page-views ($p = 0.768$) and downloads ($p = 0.940$), and median `v1`-to-VoR intervals (`272d` vs. `274d`) are balanced—and our local VoR event study eliminates time-invariant article unobservables—unobserved quality differences could remain (noting that positive selection into upgrading would bias badge estimates *upward*, reinforcing our conclusion of zero or modest returns). Moreover, our `325.4`-day lag measures the **calendar-date interval between `v1` posting and VoR publication**, not directly observed author bench effort.
3. **Observation Horizons & Equivalence-Bound Precision:** Our mature `2023–2024` citation horizon spans `22.8–46.0` months post-`v1` (median `32.7` months; median `23.0` months post-VoR), which cannot rule out longer-run (`5–10` year) citation convergence. On strictly post-`v1` OpenAlex citations (`+3.2%`, 95% CI `[-10.7%, +19.2%]`), TOST formally rejects effects $\ge \pm 20\%$ ($p = 0.0125$) while the tighter $\pm 15\%$ bound sits just above `0.05` ($p = 0.0508$). Similarly, *eLife*'s rolling `37`-month metrics API restricts monthly trajectory analyses strictly to `2023-11` onward (`N = 1,852` mature; `N = 563` `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs).
4. **Small-Sample Country Groupings & Any-Author Attribution:** Our national policy-label comparison contrasts `3` JIF-codified countries against `7` DORA-reformed countries; while exact permutation across all `120` partitions yields $p = 0.0167$, small-country-sample groupings cannot control for every unobserved national funding or disciplinary confounder. In addition, OpenAlex country-level `group_by` counts reflect *any-author* co-authorship rather than corresponding-author ownership, attenuating published-output drops for internationally collaborative European systems like Italy and the Netherlands relative to Senior-PI or domestic submission counts.
5. **OpenAlex Donor Panel Exclusions:** Out of `54` candidate peer biology journals audited alongside *eLife*, `20` (including *Nature* and *Science*) were programmatically excluded because OpenAlex tags front-matter items without affiliations (`< 85%` coverage) or missed single cells under unauthenticated API throttling, while `5` additional journals entered only the `35`-journal expanded panel (`mean >= 90%`, `min >= 85%`); identical Rank-1 findings across both `N = 30` strict and `N = 35` expanded panels confirm donor-pool robustness.

## 6. Conclusion

Combining complete true-`v1` assessment recovery (`4,541 / 4,551`, `99.78%`), untruncated live *eLife* reader telemetry (`100.00%` lifetime coverage with strict rolling-window monthly guards), and a strictly audited `30`-journal (`35`-journal expanded) life-science OpenAlex panel, this study evaluates both the micro-level revision dynamics and macro-level geographic consequences of *eLife*'s Publish-Review-Curate experiment. Abolishing post-review rejection did not collapse voluntary self-correction: across `964` mature `v1` *Incomplete/Inadequate* preprints, `793` (`82.3%`) converted to VoR and `644` (`66.8%` of all `964` preprints; `81.2%` of converted VoRs) upgraded their *Strength of Evidence* across a mean `325.4`-day calendar interval—and these high voluntary revision rates persist despite zero or at most modest measurable returns in post-`v1` citations or sustained post-VoR readership. Properly aligned unconditional ESCI contrasts (`v1` $\ge$ `2024-12-19`, transition donut excluded) further reveal a `-6.2` to `-7.4` pp slowdown in fixed-horizon VoR conversion and a `-5.0` pp drop in unrevised `<Solid` VoR declarations without an unexposed control group, cautioning against overclaiming zero indexing-incentive effects. At the macro level, separating submissions (`-56.3%` sequential `eid` velocity, matching *eLife*'s official `-55.4%` drop from `6,396` to `2,853`), editorial screening (`+4.63` pp acceptance jump at invariant `v1` quality), published-output Synthetic Control (`-0.552` total, Rank `1/30` / `1/35`; `-66.4%` China-affiliated output, Rank `1/30` / `1/35`), and small-sample national policy-label groupings (`3` JIF-codified vs. `7` DORA-reformed nations: `-21.2` pp gap, $p = 0.0167$) shows that commercial journal metrics still exert potent, geographically asymmetric pull—underscoring that durable publishing reform requires aligning transparent article-level evaluation with binding departmental decoupling from proprietary journal metrics.
