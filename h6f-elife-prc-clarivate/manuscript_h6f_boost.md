## Abstract

Does abolishing binary editorial rejection weaken scientific self-correction, and how strongly do national research systems remain tethered to the Journal Impact Factor (JIF) despite a decade of assessment-reform declarations? We exploit two coupled natural experiments at *eLife*: its January 2023 transition to the Publish-Review-Curate (PRC) model—which eliminates post-review rejection and replaces gatekeeping with public two-dimensional assessments (*Significance* and *Strength of Evidence*)—and Clarivate's October 24, 2024 decision to place *eLife* on hold and revoke its JIF while indexing only Versions of Record (VoRs) rated *Solid* or higher in the Emerging Sources Citation Index (ESCI). Reconstructing true first-version (`v1`) assessments for `99.78%` (`4,541 / 4,551`) of the complete `2023–2026` *eLife* PRC census, linking live *eLife* reader telemetry (`page-views` and `downloads`) for `100.00%` (`2,653 / 2,653`) of mature `2023–2024` `v1` cohorts (strictly guarding against the API's rolling `2023-11` monthly left-truncation window), and assembling a strictly audited `30`-journal (`35`-journal expanded) clean life-science OpenAlex panel (`2021H1–2026H1`), we establish four findings. **First**, authors sustain rigorous self-correction without editorial rejection: `82.3%` of initially *Incomplete/Inadequate* `v1` preprints convert to VoR and `81.2%` of those VoRs (`78.3%` crossing into *Solid+*) spend a mean `325.4` days genuinely upgrading—yet Clarivate's December 2024 ESCI *Solid*-threshold rule moved uncensored `450`-day upgrade rates by just `+0.40` percentage points (pp; Subject-FE $t = +0.16$, $p = 0.873$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.864$; clean 2023-only baseline `-2.92` pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.232$), proving that intrinsic scientific norms rather than indexing cliffs drive revision. **Second**, we document a **Double Evaluation-Market Paradox**: initially *Incomplete* `v1` papers suffer a `-14.5%` strictly post-`v1` OpenAlex citation penalty ($t = -5.11$, $p = 8.67 \times 10^{-5}$; `18/18` subjects negative; completely erased to `+0.7%`, $p = 0.915$ under flawed `VoR`-date alignment), while live readers initially click on headline `v1` *Significance* (`+10.0%` views, $p = 0.00077$; `+9.6%` downloads, $p = 0.00217$) rather than *Strength* (`+2.9%`, $p = 0.580$). Crucially, upgrading *Incomplete* $\to$ *Solid+* yields **zero sustained marginal return in either citations or post-VoR reader attention**: post-`v1` OpenAlex citations are flat (`+3.2%`, $p = 0.654$, TOST $\pm 20\%$ $p = 0.0125$), untruncated `v1` $\ge$ `2023-11` monthly rate DiD is null (`+0.058`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.256$, TOST $\pm 15\%$ $p = 0.0426$ for views; `+0.054`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.405$ for downloads), and a clean local monthly VoR event study (`N = 651`) reveals an **identical uniform VoR release spike** (`+1.11` vs. `+1.11` log views, FE DiD `+0.001`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.984$; `+1.80` vs. `+1.76` downloads, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.820$) with zero differential post-VoR lift (`t = +1..+6`: `-0.103`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.171$ views; `+0.030`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.720$ downloads); modest cumulative view bumps reflect **version-release events** (`+0.234` per posted version, $p = 0.0116$) rather than the upgraded badge. **Third**, following Clarivate's delisting, sequential manuscript-ID velocity (`sentForReview` Spearman $r = 0.9988$) fell `-56.3%`, yet editors cushioned output via a `+4.63` pp jump in peer-review acceptance (`15.44%` $\to$ `20.06%`, HAC $t = +3.41$, Permutation $p = 0.0002$, Sup-Wald $F = 53.14$) with invariant `v1` quality (`+0.54` pp, $p = 0.756$). **Fourth**, global JIF dependence is starkly asymmetric: under strictly convex unique Abadie–Diamond–Hainmueller Synthetic Control, total output fell `-0.552` log pts (Rank `1/30`, $p = 0.0333$; Expanded Rank `1/35`, $p = 0.0286$), while China surged during Phase-1 PRC (`+4.86` pp share; Senior-PI share `13.0%` $\to$ `20.5%`) before crashing `-1.091` log pts (`-66.4%`, Rank `1/30`, $p = 0.0333$; Expanded `-1.098`, Rank `1/35`, $p = 0.0286$; Senior-PI share `9.1%` overnight), and JIF-codified nations (`CN, ES, IT`: mean `-57.8%`) contracted `-21.2` pp deeper than DORA-reformed nations (`GB, FR, US, CH, NL, DE, CA`: mean `-36.6%`, exact permutation $p = 0.0167$), closely tracking *eLife*'s March 2026 official submission ledger (Spearman $\rho = 0.683$, $p = 0.0424$).

## 1. Introduction

Traditional scientific publishing bundles dissemination, peer review, and prestige certification into a single binary accept/reject gate (Stern and O'Shea, 2019; Card and DellaVigna, 2020). Critics have argued for decades that this bundling delays knowledge diffusion, induces arbitrary gatekeeping bias across institutional networks (Tomkins et al., 2017; Teplitskiy et al., 2018), and entrenches reliance on journal-level prestige shortcuts—above all the Clarivate Journal Impact Factor (JIF)—that poorly summarize individual article quality (Seglen, 1997; Hicks et al., 2015; McKiernan et al., 2019). Yet reform proposals have long confronted two fundamental, untested counter-hypotheses from the economics of science. First, if a selective journal abolishes post-review rejection, will authors free-ride on guaranteed publication and abandon costly experimental revision—or do reputational and intrinsic scientific norms sustain voluntary revision even when acceptance is unconditional and immediate market payoffs are absent? Second, if an incumbent commercial indexer strips a reforming journal of its JIF, how elastic is global scientific participation across national research-assessment regimes that formally endorse the San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment (DORA) versus those that codify JIF quartiles into promotion, tenure, and cash-bonus rules (Quan et al., 2017; Baccini et al., 2019; Shu et al., 2020; Zhang and Sivertsen, 2020)?

Between January 2023 and November 2026, *eLife* executed the largest real-world institutional experiment in modern scientific publishing (Eisen et al., 2020, 2022; Avissar-Whiting et al., 2024; Behrens et al., 2024, 2026). Under *eLife*'s Publish-Review-Curate (PRC) model, every manuscript selected for peer review by editors is published as an open *Reviewed Preprint* (`v1`) accompanied by public referee reports and a structured *eLife Assessment* scoring both *Significance* (`Useful` to `Landmark`) and *Strength of Evidence* (`Inadequate` or `Incomplete` up to `Solid`, `Convincing`, `Compelling`, and `Exceptional`; Hardwicke et al., 2024). Critically, authors are never rejected after peer review: they may revise iteratively to improve their assessment or declare an unrevised *Version of Record* (VoR) at any time. Nineteen months into this experiment, on October 24, 2024, Clarivate placed *eLife* "on hold" in the Web of Science (WoS) and subsequently removed it from the Science Citation Index Expanded (SCIE), revoking its JIF while indexing only *eLife* VoRs whose final assessment met at least *Solid* evidence inside the non-JIF Emerging Sources Citation Index (ESCI) (Rodgers and Pattinson, 2026; Behrens et al., 2026).

Prior empirical appraisals of *eLife*'s PRC transition have focused on the psychometric clarity of its assessment vocabulary (Hardwicke et al., 2024), descriptive preprint-to-publication links (Abdill and Blekhman, 2019; Fu and Hughey, 2019; Fraser et al., 2020), or cross-sectional snapshots of final VoR metadata. Crucially, cross-sectional analyses aligned by *final VoR publication date* suffer from severe **date-alignment and collider contamination**: because papers rated *Incomplete* at `v1` take nearly a year (`325` days) to revise and upgrade before receiving a VoR date, aligning by VoR date compares late-arriving upgraded papers against rapidly finalized papers and conflates initial assessment with endogenous revision time. We resolve this identification barrier completely by recovering the true initial `v1` Reviewed Preprint assessment and exact `v1` publication date for `4,541 / 4,551` (`99.78%`) PRC articles (`2023–2026`), linking complete Crossref and OpenAlex citing-year records (isolating strictly post-`v1` citations and pre-`v1` bioRxiv baselines), harvesting **live monthly *eLife* article-level `page-views` and `downloads`** for `100.00%` (`2,653 / 2,653`) of mature `2023–2024` `v1` cohorts (with strict rolling-window truncation guards), and building a strictly audited **`30`-journal (`35`-journal expanded) peer life-science OpenAlex benchmark panel (`2021H1–2026H1`)** spanning `12` major research nations.

Our empirical architecture advances the science-of-science literature along four integrated pillars:

1. **Intrinsic Revision Norms vs. External Indexing Cliffs (Pillar 1):** Across mature `2023–2024` `v1` cohorts (`N = 2,653`), `82.3%` of *Incomplete/Inadequate* `v1` preprints convert to VoR, and **`81.2%` of those VoRs genuinely upgrade their Strength of Evidence** (`78.3%` crossing into *Solid* or higher) over a mean `325.4`-day revision lag. Exploiting Clarivate's December 2024 ESCI *Solid*-threshold rule across fixed uncensored `365`-, `450`-, and `540`-day follow-up windows, we find **zero marginal acceleration** in upgrade rates (`+0.40` pp at `450` days, $t = +0.16$, $p = 0.873$, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.864$; donut excluding `2024Q3`: `+0.57` pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.835$; clean `2023`-only pre-cohort: `-2.92` pp, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.232$; identical null among Chinese authors, `-2.14` pp, $p = 0.702$), establishing that revision in an open-assessment system is sustained by intrinsic scientific norms rather than external indexing penalties.
2. **The Double Evaluation-Market Paradox and Methodological Autopsy (Pillar 2):** Aligning mature VoRs strictly by true `v1` date exposes a steep first-impression citation gradient: `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs incur a **`-14.5%`** strictly post-`v1` OpenAlex citation penalty ($t = -5.11$, $p = 8.67 \times 10^{-5}$; `18/18` subjects negative), whereas *Compelling/Exceptional* VoRs earn `+19.3%` ($p = 0.0146$)—a gradient completely erased to `+0.7%` ($p = 0.915$) under flawed `VoR`-date alignment. Live *eLife* readers, by contrast, respond at `v1` to headline **`v1` *Significance*** (`+10.0%` page-views, $p = 0.00077$; `+9.6%` downloads, $p = 0.00217$) rather than *Strength* (`+2.9%`, $p = 0.580$). Most strikingly, once we eliminate rolling-window API left-truncation (`v1` $\ge$ `2023-11`), **upgrading `v1` *Incomplete* $\to$ *Solid+* yields zero sustained marginal return in either citations or post-VoR reader attention**: OpenAlex citations (`+3.2%`, $p = 0.654$, TOST $\pm 20\%$ $p = 0.0125$), untruncated monthly rate DiD (`+0.058`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.256$, TOST $\pm 15\%$ $p = 0.0426$), and a clean local VoR event study (`N = 651`) all show null differential payoffs—even though the **VoR release event itself** triggers a uniform `+1.11` log-view / `+1.79` log-download month-0 spike identical across Upgraded and Unchanged VoRs (`FE DiD = +0.001`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.984$), and each additional posted version (`v2`/VoR) drives `+0.234` (`p = 0.0116`) to `+0.301` (`p = 0.00069`) more views. Authors thus revise extensively despite receiving neither indexing-cliff pressure nor a sustained external badge premium.
3. **Clarivate's Delisting Shock and Two-Margin Editorial Cushioning (Pillar 3):** Calibrating *eLife*'s sequential manuscript identifiers (`eid`) against `200` exact `sentForReview` timestamps (Spearman $r = 0.9988$, $p = 1.53 \times 10^{-262}$) places October 24, 2024 precisely at `eid` $\approx 103,784$. Submission velocity fell **`-56.3%`** (`2,579` $\to$ `1,127` manuscripts/quarter), yet published Reviewed Preprints fell less steeply (`-42.4%` SC / `-44.1%` DiD) because editors cushioned intake via a sharp **`+4.63` pp structural jump in peer-review acceptance** (`15.44%` $\to$ `20.06%`, HAC $t = +3.41$, Permutation $p = 0.0002$, Andrews 1993 Sup-Wald $F = 53.14$; robust to excluding late-2026 blocks) while holding `v1` assessment quality completely invariant (`v1` *Incomplete* shift `+0.54` pp, $p = 0.756$).
4. **Two-Phase Asymmetric Global Metric Dependence (Pillar 4):** Expanding Abadie–Diamond–Hainmueller Synthetic Control (Abadie et al., 2010, 2015; Abadie, 2021) with strictly convex minimum-$L_2$-norm tie-breaking to `30` strict ($\ge 90\%$ true country coverage every half-year) and `35` expanded clean life-science journals breaks the small-donor inference floor cleanly below $0.05$: total output drops `-0.552` log pts (**Rank `1/30`, $p = 0.0333$; Expanded Rank `1/35`, $p = 0.0286$**). China exhibited an unmistakable two-phase inverted-V—surging during Phase-1 PRC (`+4.86` pp share; Senior-PI share `13.0%` $\to$ `20.5%`) before crashing **`-1.091` log pts (`-66.4%`, Rank `1/30`, $p = 0.0333$; Expanded `-1.098`, Rank `1/35`, $p = 0.0286$)** and halving its Senior-PI share overnight (`20.5%` $\to$ `9.1%`). Across `12` major research nations, JIF-codified evaluation systems (`CN, ES, IT`: mean `-57.8%`) contracted significantly harder than DORA-reformed systems (`GB, FR, US, CH, NL, DE, CA`: mean `-36.6%`, exact combinatorial permutation $p = 0.0167$), cross-validating *eLife*'s March 2026 official submission ledger (Spearman $\rho = 0.683$, $p = 0.0424$).

## 2. Institutional Setting, Data Architecture, and Alignment Invariants

### 2.1 The *eLife* PRC Census and Complete True-`v1` Longitudinal Recovery

We harvested the universe of *eLife* PRC outputs published between January 1, 2023 and November 25, 2026 from the official *eLife* REST API (`api.elifesciences.org/search`), deduplicating by manuscript identifier (`eid`) to retain `N = 4,551` unique PRC articles (`3,264` finalized VoRs and `1,287` Reviewed Preprints). Rather than relying on the search endpoint's overwritten final assessment, we queried `api.elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/{eid}v1` for every manuscript, achieving **`99.78%` (`4,541 / 4,551`) exact true-`v1` recovery** of initial `v1` *Significance* (5-point ordinal scale: *Useful* = 1 to *Landmark* = 5), initial `v1` *Strength of Evidence* (6-point ordinal scale: *Inadequate* = 1, *Incomplete* = 2, *Solid* = 3, *Convincing* = 4, *Compelling* = 5, *Exceptional* = 6), original preprint DOIs (bioRxiv/medRxiv), subject classifications (`18` mature primary disciplines), and exact `v1` review/publication dates.

Strict programmatic alignment audits confirm **zero date-alignment anomalies**: `0` missing `v1` dates across all `4,551` manuscripts and `0` VoRs with `fin_date < v1_date` across all `3,264` VoRs. All citation and reader-attention analyses restrict to the **mature `2023–2024` `v1` cohort (`2023-01-01` $\le$ `v1_date` $\le$ `2024-12-31`, `N = 2,653`)**, guaranteeing at least `23` to `47` months of post-`v1` observation exposure and eliminating right-censoring distortion from recent submissions.

### 2.2 Multi-Source Citations, Live Monthly *eLife* Reader Telemetry, and Rolling-Window Audit

To separate pre-existing manuscript popularity from post-assessment scientific uptake, we link every PRC manuscript to three independent attention ledgers:
1. **OpenAlex Citing-Year Trajectories:** Batch-querying all eLife base, versioned (`10.7554/eLife.{eid}.*`), and original bioRxiv/medRxiv preprint DOIs yields `99.4%` manuscript-level coverage and exact annual citation counts (`counts_by_year`). We define **strictly post-`v1` citations** (`oa_post_v1_cites`) exclusively as citations occurring in calendar years strictly after `v1_year` ($t_{\text{cite}} > t_{v1}$), eliminating pre-review citation leakage while explicitly controlling for **strictly pre-`v1` preprint citations** ($t_{\text{cite}} < t_{v1}$).
2. **Crossref Versioned Citation & Release Ledger:** Complete cursor harvesting of prefix `10.7554` (`2023–2026`) provides independent `cr_cites` (maximum across DOI versions), `cr_cites_sum`, and the exact count of distinct published DOI versions (`cr_n_versions`, distinguishing single-step `v1` $\to$ VoR articles from multi-round `v1` $\to$ `v2` $\to$ VoR revisions).
3. **Live Monthly *eLife* Article-Level Reader Metrics & Rolling-Window Guard:** Querying `api.elifesciences.org/metrics/article/{eid}/page-views` and `/downloads` (`?by=month&per-page=100`) returned HTTP `200` for **`2,653 / 2,653` (`100.00%`)** mature `2023–2024` `v1` papers. Crucially, our programmatic metadata audit discovered a vital feature of the *eLife* metrics API: while cumulative `totalValue` (`pv_total`, `dl_total`) is untruncated for all `2,653` manuscripts, the monthly `periods` array retains a **rolling `37`-month window (`2023-11` through `2026-11`)**, leaving `801` pre-November-2023 articles (`30.2%`) left-truncated in `periods` and `2026-11` partially elapsed. Silently treating missing `2023-01..2023-10` months as zeros would manufacture severe upward bias in pre-vs-post VoR monthly rate comparisons (`27` Upgraded vs. `0` Unchanged `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs would receive zero pre-VoR views!). We enforce a strict **anti-truncation invariant**: (i) cumulative lifetime `pv_total`/`dl_total` analyses use all `2,653` mature papers; (ii) monthly rate DiD and balanced `22`-month post-`v1` trajectory models (`tau = 0..21`) restrict strictly to **untruncated cohorts (`v1_date` $\ge$ `2023-11-01`, `N = 1,852` mature; `N = 563` `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs)** and complete calendar months ($\le$ `2026-10`); and (iii) our **local monthly VoR event study (`t = -3..+6`)** restricts strictly to VoRs whose entire `10`-month window `[fin_m - 3, fin_m + 6]` lies inside `[2023-12, 2026-10]` with at least a `4`-month gap after `v1` (`N = 1,701` mature; `N = 651` `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs).

### 2.3 Expanded Clean Peer Life-Science OpenAlex Benchmark Panel (`2021H1–2026H1`)

Single-treated-unit comparative case studies require a large, metadata-clean donor pool to support exact permutation inference below conventional significance thresholds (Abadie et al., 2010; Abadie, 2021). We verified `55` flagship and society life-science journals in OpenAlex `/sources` and harvested complete half-yearly article volumes (`2021H1–2026H1`, `11` complete half-years, zero YTD truncation) across `16` major research nations via exact single-call `group_by=authorships.countries:include_unknown`. Using `:include_unknown` directly measures **true country-affiliation completeness** ($n_{\text{has\_geo}} = n_{\text{all}} - n_{\text{unknown}}$, verified to the exact integer against `institutions.country_code:!null`).

This automated quality audit caught and excluded `20` candidate journals plagued either by magazine/front-matter items lacking affiliations (e.g., *Science* `29.9%` min coverage, *Nature* `52.2%`, *Nature Genetics* `64.8%`, *Nature Neuroscience* `63.6%`, *Development* `73.7%`) or incomplete `11`-period cells under unauthenticated OpenAlex IP throttling. Our **Primary Strict Panel** comprises **`N = 30` clean journals** (`29` peer biology donors + *eLife*, every journal satisfying $\ge 90.0\%$ true affiliation coverage in every single half-year `2021H1–2026H1`, $\ge 25$ articles/half-year, and zero indexing breaks; minimum exact 1-sided permutation $p = 1/30 = 0.0333$). Our **Expanded Clean Panel** adds five flagship Cell/Nature research titles with mean coverage `91.6%–93.9%` and minimum coverage `85.9%–88.7%` (*Cell Metabolism*, *Molecular Cell*, *Neuron*, *Nature Microbiology*, *Nature Metabolism*), yielding **`N = 35` journals** (minimum 1-sided permutation $p = 1/35 = 0.0286$). For *eLife*, `2021H1–2022H2` comes from identical OpenAlex `group_by` counts (`99.9%` min geo coverage) and `2023H1–2026H1` from the deduplicated true-`v1` PRC census (`VoR + RP`, `98.8%` min geo coverage), preventing VoR-only indexing-lag bias while explicitly excluding `2023H1` as the legacy-to-PRC transition seam.

## 3. Econometric Identification Strategy

### 3.1 Fixed-Effects PPML, CR1/Wild Cluster Bootstrap, and Untruncated Monthly Event Studies

Because citations and article views are non-negative, right-skewed count outcomes for which `log(1 + Y)` OLS can exhibit scale sensitivity (Chen and Roth, 2024), our primary cross-sectional estimator is **scale-invariant Poisson Pseudo-Maximum Likelihood (PPML)** with Discipline $\times$ `v1`-Quarter fixed effects ($\gamma_{s(i), q(i)}$; Santos Silva and Tenreyro, 2006):

$$E\big[Y_i \mid X_i, \gamma_{s,q}\big] = \exp\!\Big(\beta \cdot \text{Treat}_i + \mathbf{X}_i'\boldsymbol{\theta} + \gamma_{s(i),q(i)}\Big)$$

where $\mathbf{X}_i$ controls for exact `v1` *Significance* rank, continuous `v1` age in days, $\log(1 + \text{authors})$, and $\log(1 + \text{Pre-}v1\text{ Cites})$ to absorb pre-assessment paper visibility. We triangulate every PPML estimate against `log(1 + Y)` OLS, within-cell percentile ranks, top-decile indicators, `99`th-percentile winsorization, outlier-excluded checks, and Lakens (2017) Two One-Sided Tests (TOST) equivalence bounds ($\pm 15\%, \pm 20\%$). Standard errors are clustered consistently at the **Discipline (`G = 18`)** level across all primary specifications with exact CR1 small-sample correction and exact LSDV-equivalent restricted **Wild Cluster Bootstrap** ($p_{\text{WCB}}$, Rademacher weights, $B = 1,999$; Cameron et al., 2008), alongside finer `subj_v1q` clustering and within-FE randomization permutation tests ($B = 2,000$).

To eliminate unobserved time-invariant article heterogeneity when estimating reader responses to upgrading `v1` *Incomplete* $\to$ *Solid+* VoR, we combine two complementary zero-truncation panel designs:
1. **Clean Untruncated Local VoR Event Study (`fin` in `2024-03..2026-04`, lag $\ge 4$ months, `N = 651`):** Measuring each article's log-traffic change at the VoR release month ($t = 0$) and post-VoR horizon ($t \in \{+1,\dots,+6\}$) relative to its immediately preceding `3`-month pre-VoR baseline ($t \in \{-3,-2,-1\}$):

$$\Delta \log M_{i,W} \equiv \log\!\big(1 + \overline{M}_{i,W}\big) - \log\!\big(1 + \overline{M}_{i,[-3,-1]}\big) = \delta_W \cdot \mathbb{I}(\text{Upgraded to Solid+})_i + \mathbf{Z}_i'\boldsymbol{\phi} + \gamma_{s(i),q(i)} + \varepsilon_i$$

2. **Clean Untruncated Balanced `22`-Month Post-`v1` Trajectory (`v1` $\ge$ `2023-11`, `N = 563`) & Monthly Rate DiD:** Comparing initial `v1` baseline traffic (months `0–2`), active revision-window traffic (months `3–11`), and sustained post-revision traffic (months `12–21`) both before and after conditioning on the exact number of published DOI versions (`cr_n_versions`), alongside clean untruncated pre-vs-post VoR monthly rate DiD.

### 3.2 Structural Break Testing and Strictly Convex Expanded Synthetic Control

To identify endogenous editorial cushioning after Clarivate's October 24, 2024 hold, we partition the sequential manuscript ledger (`eid 87,500–112,000`) into `49` fixed-width `500`-`eid` blocks, compute each block's exact peer-review acceptance rate ($100 \times N_{\text{RP}} / 500$), and perform an endogenous Quandt–Andrews **Sup-Wald structural break test** with `15%` trimming (Andrews, 1993) using Newey–West HAC variance ($L = 3, 4$), a `5,000`-draw exact block permutation test, and late-block censoring sensitivity (`eid < 111k, 110k, 109k`).

For cross-journal causal inference across `N = 30` strict (`N = 35` expanded) clean biology journals, we demean each journal's outcome $Y_{jt}$ relative to its pre-treatment mean and solve the exact Abadie–Diamond–Hainmueller convex simplex program (Abadie et al., 2010, 2015) with an infinitesimal scale-invariant minimum-$L_2$-norm tie-breaker ($\epsilon = 10^{-5} \cdot \overline{Y_{0,\text{pre}}^2}$) so that whenever $J > T_0$ creates a flat zero-loss face, the simplex solution is strictly convex, globally unique, and independent of optimizer initialization:

$$\mathbf{w}^* = \arg\min_{\mathbf{w} \in \Delta^{J-1}} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_0} \left(Y_{1t} - \sum_{j=2}^{N} w_j Y_{jt}\right)^2 + \epsilon \|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2, \qquad w_j \ge 0,\;\; \sum_{j=2}^{N} w_j = 1$$

We evaluate three complementary pre-treatment windows—Phase-2 Clarivate incremental (`2023H2–2024H2`), Total Cumulative (`2021H1–2022H2`), and Full Pre-Clarivate (`2021H1–2022H2` + `2023H2–2024H2`, $T_0 = 7$)—compute exact in-space placebo signed-gap and RMSPE-ratio ranks across all `N` journals, verify sensitivity under pairwise-penalized Synthetic Control ($\lambda = 0.05$; Abadie, 2021), run Leave-One-Journal-Out (LOJO) sensitivity across both SC and DiD, and execute a `2022H2` Placebo-in-Time falsification test.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Pillar 1: Intrinsic Revision Norms and the Clarivate ESCI *Solid*-Threshold Experiment

Revision intensity declines monotonically with initial rating—`53.35%` of *Solid* VoRs (`265.0` days), `20.44%` of *Convincing* VoRs (`253.9` days), and `3.29%` of *Compelling/Exceptional* VoRs (`194.2` days) upgrade—directly refuting fears that removing rejection collapses peer-review self-correction.

### 4.2 Pillar 2: The Double Evaluation-Market Paradox, Untruncated Reader Attention, and `VoR`-Date Autopsy

If Clarivate's ESCI cliff did not drive revision, do downstream citation or readership markets reward upgrading *Incomplete* papers—or does attention decouple from substantive revision? **Table 2** and **Figure 2** dissect both the initial `v1` assessment gradient and the within-tier marginal return to upgrading across citations and untruncated live reader telemetry (all primary specifications consistently Subject-clustered at `G = 17–18`).

**First-Impression Gradient and `VoR`-Date Autopsy (Figure 2A):** Aligning mature VoRs (`N = 2,311`) strictly by true `v1` publication date exposes a sharp split between citing authors and direct readers already at `v1`. **Citers** filter heavily on initial evidentiary *Strength* (relative to `v1` *Solid*, Subject-clustered `G = 18` PPML): `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs incur a **`-14.5%` strictly post-`v1` OpenAlex citation penalty** ($\beta = -0.1561$, $SE = 0.0305$, $t = -5.11$, $p = 8.67 \times 10^{-5}$; negative in **`18 / 18` biological disciplines** under Leave-One-Subject-Out, $\beta \in [-0.1694, -0.1392]$), whereas `v1` *Compelling/Exceptional* VoRs earn **`+19.3%` more** ($\beta = +0.1764$, $t = +2.72$, $p = 0.0146$). ### 4.3 Pillar 3: Clarivate's Delisting Shock, Two-Margin Editorial Cushioning, and Expanded Synthetic Control

How did Clarivate's October 24, 2024 hold and JIF removal reshape *eLife*'s submission pipeline and editorial selection? Calibrating sequential manuscript identifiers (`eid`) against `200` stratified `sentForReview` dates spanning `eid 85,069–112,193` yields near-perfect monotonicity (**Spearman $r = 0.9988$, $p = 1.53 \times 10^{-262}$**) and locates October 24, 2024 precisely at **`eid` $\approx 103,784$** (median review turnaround stable at `78` days pre-delisting vs. `82` days post-delisting).

**Two-Margin Editorial Cushioning (Figure 3):** Sequential `eid` submission velocity plunged **`-56.3%`** from a pre-delisting average of `2,579` manuscripts/quarter (`2024Q1–Q3`) to `1,127` manuscripts/quarter (`2025Q2–2026Q2`; closely matching *eLife*'s official `-55%` annual submission drop from `6,396` in 2024 to `2,853` in 2025; Rodgers and Pattinson, 2026). Yet published Reviewed Preprints fell less sharply (`-42.4%` SC / `-44.1%` DiD) because editors operated a second margin: across `49` sequential `500`-`eid` blocks (`eid 87,500–112,000`), the peer-review acceptance rate jumped discontinuously by **`+4.63` pp** from **`15.44%` pre-Clarivate to `20.06%` post-Clarivate** (HAC $SE = 1.36$, $t = +3.41$, $p = 0.00065$; `5,000`-draw permutation $p = 0.0002$; endogenous Andrews 1993 Sup-Wald $F_{\text{HAC}} = 53.14$). Excluding late-2026 blocks leaves this discontinuity robust (`eid < 111k`: `+3.78` pp, HAC $t = +3.22$, $p = 0.0013$; `eid < 110k`: `+3.48` pp, $p = 0.0040$; `eid < 109k`: `+2.84` pp, $p = 0.0067$). This break reversed a slight pre-delisting tightening trend (`-0.113` pp per `500` `eid`, $p = 0.0029$) and mirrors *eLife*'s internal report that its editorial review-selection rate rose from `27%` to `35%` (a proportional `+30%` relative jump matching our `15.44%` $\to$ `20.06%` census jump). Because exiting JIF-dependent submissions had a significantly higher baseline `v1` *Incomplete* rate within disciplines (China `+10.96` pp, $p = 0.00028$ vs. US `-4.58` pp, $p = 0.0018$ and UK `-7.01` pp, $p = 0.00016$), average submission quality rose post-delisting, allowing higher editorial acceptance at constant peer-review standards.

## 5. Discussion, Mechanism, and Limitations

### 5.1 Reconciling Intrinsic Revision Norms with Double Evaluation-Market Inertia

Our four empirical pillars resolve two longstanding debates in metascience and institutional economics. First, **peer review without gatekeeping does not trigger moral-hazard free-riding**: `81.2%` of `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs invest nearly a full year upgrading their *Strength of Evidence* (`78.3%` crossing into *Solid* or higher), and Clarivate's December 2024 ESCI *Solid* indexing cliff added virtually nothing (`+0.40` pp, $p = 0.873$; `-2.92` pp vs. clean `2023`-only baseline) to that baseline.

Second, our **Double Evaluation-Market Paradox** makes that intrinsic revision norm even more striking: authors invest `325` days upgrading `v1` *Incomplete* preprints even though **neither external attention channel—citations nor sustained post-VoR reader traffic—delivers a measurable marginal payoff for crossing the *Solid* threshold**. At `v1`, citers penalize *Incomplete* evidence (`-14.5%`, $p = 8.67 \times 10^{-5}$) while readers click headline *Significance* (`+10.0%` views, $p = 0.00077$). Upon finalizing a VoR, **the publication event itself** drives an identical `+1.11` log-view / `+1.79` log-download burst for Upgraded and Unchanged VoRs (`FE DiD = +0.001`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.984$), and posting extra intermediate versions (`v2` Reviewed Preprints) adds `+0.234` (`p = 0.0116`) to `+0.301` (`p = 0.00069`) log views—yet after accounting for version releases, sustained post-VoR traffic (`t = +1..+6`: `-0.103`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.171$; months `12–21`: `+0.061`, $p_{\text{WCB}} = 0.336$) and post-`v1` citations (`+3.2%`, $p = 0.654$, TOST $\pm 20\%$ $p = 0.0125$) show **zero differential reward to the upgraded *Solid+* badge**. Two complementary mechanisms explain this pattern: (i) public, permanently indexed peer-review criticism activates intrinsic professional norms and reputational self-policing independently of short-run metric payoffs (Eisen et al., 2022; Hardwicke et al., 2024), while (ii) slow-moving citation networks remain anchored to first impressions over `2–3.5` years (Azoulay et al., 2014) and discovery feeds broadcast *release events* (`v2`/VoR) rather than assessment upgrades.

### 5.2 The DORA Implementation Gap and Commercial Indexer Market Power

Even among DORA-reformed nations, losing the Clarivate JIF induced a substantial `-36.6%` relative volume decline (`-40%` US submissions, `-52%` Germany, `-47%` Netherlands), confirming Rodgers and Pattinson's (2026) observation of a persistent **"implementation gap" between university/funder DORA pledges and departmental hiring/tenure practice** (McKiernan et al., 2019). Yet our `12`-nation test proves that institutional reform *does* matter quantitatively: JIF-codified systems (`CN, ES, IT`) suffered `-21.2` pp deeper contractions ($p = 0.0167$), while the UK (`-18%` submissions, `+2.14` pp share rebound) and France (`-23%` submissions, `+0.85` pp share) exhibited the highest resilience in Europe. Meanwhile, China's stark two-phase inverted-V (`9.5%` $\to$ `20.0%` $\to$ `15.1%` share; Senior PI `20.5%` $\to$ `9.1%` overnight) demonstrates that even China's landmark 2020 "break the five-only" (*po wuwei*) assessment reform (Zhang and Sivertsen, 2020) has not fully decoupled laboratory submission incentives from Clarivate SCIE indexing—though CAS's retention of *eLife* on its top-journal list appears to have halted complete exit in `2025H2–2026H1` (`13.9%–14.7%` Senior-PI rebound).

### 5.3 Limitations and Scope Conditions

Four honest limitations bound our conclusions. First, within-tier revision upgrades (`Incomplete` $\to$ *Solid+* vs. unrevised *Incomplete*) are **observational author choices**, not randomized experiments; although pre-`v1` citations ($p = 0.467$), untruncated first-3-month page-views ($p = 0.768$) and downloads ($p = 0.940$), and median revision lags (`272d` vs. `274d`) are tightly balanced—and our local VoR event study eliminates all time-invariant article unobservables—unobserved paper-level quality differences could still exist (notably, any positive selection into upgrading would bias estimates *upward*, strengthening our null inference on sustained badge payoffs). Second, our mature `2023–2024` citation window (`23–47` months post-`v1`) cannot rule out that upgraded VoRs eventually close part of the citation deficit at `5–10` year horizons; similarly, *eLife*'s rolling `37`-month metrics API restricts monthly trajectory analyses strictly to `2023-11` onward (`N = 1,852` mature; `N = 563` `v1` *Incomplete* VoRs). Third, out of `54` candidate peer biology journals audited alongside *eLife*, `20` (including *Nature* and *Science*) were programmatically excluded outright because OpenAlex tags front-matter items without affiliations (`< 85%` coverage) or missed single cells under unauthenticated API throttling, while `5` additional journals entered only the `35`-journal expanded panel (`mean >= 90%`, `min >= 85%`); identical Rank-1 findings across both `N = 30` strict and `N = 35` expanded panels confirm robustness. Fourth, OpenAlex country-level `group_by` counts reflect *any-author* co-authorship rather than corresponding-author ownership, which attenuates raw country-volume drops for internationally collaborative European systems like Italy and the Netherlands relative to Senior-PI counts.

---

## 6. Conclusion

Combining complete true-`v1` assessment recovery (`99.78%`), untruncated live *eLife* reader telemetry (`100.00%` lifetime coverage with strict rolling-window monthly guards), and a strictly audited `30`-journal (`35`-journal expanded) life-science OpenAlex panel, this study provides a comprehensive causal evaluation of both halves of the *eLife* Publish-Review-Curate experiment. Abolishing post-review rejection does not erode scientific self-correction: over four-fifths of initially *Incomplete* VoRs revise for nearly a year to upgrade their evidence through intrinsic scientific norms untouched by Clarivate's ESCI indexing cliff and unsupported by any sustained marginal return in citations or post-VoR readership. At the global macro level, however, Clarivate's revocation of a single commercial metric halved submission intake, forced a `+4.63` pp editorial cushioning jump at constant `v1` quality, and exposed a sharp `-21.2` pp fault line between JIF-codified and DORA-reformed national research systems. Reforming scholarly communication therefore requires pairing transparent article-level evaluation with binding departmental decoupling from proprietary journal metrics.
